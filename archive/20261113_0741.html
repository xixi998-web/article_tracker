Amelioration of Saline Alkali–Soil and Improvement of Soybean Performance by Organically Modified Coal GangueCORE4
Authors: Qi-Quo Zhao, Zhi-Peng Yao, Yu-Sheng Niu, Xian-Ji Guo, Kun-Peng Cui
Venue: Nanomaterials
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Growing global concern over soil salinization has drawn considerable attention to the remediation and amelioration of saline–alkali soils. Coal gangue (M) can be applied to improve saline–alkali soils; nevertheless, its amelioration performance is subject to certain limitations. Organically modified coal gangue (GM) was prepared using alcohol ethoxylate-7 (AEO-7) as the primary modifier, and both M and GM were mixed with a saline–alkali soil at 10%, 20%, 30% and 40% (w/w) in a 28-day incubation and a 28-day soybean pot experiment. At 28 d, compared with the CK group, the GM40% treatment significantly increased the available potassium and alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen in the saline–alkali soil by 49.91% and 97.10%, respectively, and increased soil organic matter by 10% compared with M40%. The GM groups decreased soil pH relative to CK, alleviated M-induced available phosphorus deficiency, and increased soil urease, peroxidase and catalase activities by 467.77%, 140.37% and 76.29%, respectively. Plant height, shoot dry weight and root dry weight increased by 23.24%, 102.22% and 208.05%. Amplicon sequencing showed that the modified material increased bacterial richness and shifted bacterial community composition. Thus, the organically modified gangue improved soil nutrient pools and soybean performance relative to raw gangue.
Biostimulation of Crude Oil-contaminated Soil Using Plantain (Musa paradisiaca) Pseudo-stem Residues: Effects on Total Petroleum Hydrocarbon Degradation and Soil Physicochemical PropertiesCORE4
Authors: Ayi Richard Inimgba, D. Davis, E. Ukoha-Onuoha
Venue: Current Journal of Applied Science and Technology
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs · PDF
Abstract
The Niger Delta of Nigeria faces a major environmental problem: crude oil pollution. Agricultural soils have been adversely affected by many years of petroleum activities. The study examined the potential of plantain (Musa paradisiaca) pseudo-stem residues as a low-cost biostimulant for the restoration of crude-oil-contaminated loam soil. Ten treatments, including an unamended control, were incubated for 56 days in a Completely Randomized Design with dry pseudo-stem (DPS), fresh pseudo-stem (FPS), and the combination (DPS + FPS) added to soil spiked with 18,920 mg/kg total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH). Soil pH, electrical conductivity (EC), moisture, organic matter, nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and residual TPH were analysed at 14-day intervals. Degradation kinetics, ANOVA, and Pearson correlation analysis were used to evaluate the treatment effects and relationships among variables. TPH removal was better in pseudo-stem amendments compared to natural attenuation (F(3,6) = 15.79, p = 0.003). The joint application of 500 g DPS + 500 g FPS (T10) achieved TPH removal of 83.4% (3,150 mg/kg) with a degradation constant of 0.025349 day-1 and a half-life of 27.34 days, whereas 21.0% removal and a half-life of 218.4 days were recorded for the control. Amendments also improved soil quality by increasing the pH from 3.92 to 6.03 and decreasing EC from 6,590 to 115 µS/cm in T10, while N, P, and K increased with amendment dosage. There was a positive correlation between the concentrations of nutrients and TPH removal (N: r=0.748; P: r=0.793; K: r=0.769; p<0.05). These results suggest that plantain pseudo-stem residues, in particular DPS and FPS combination, are an efficient and sustainable strategy for petroleum hydrocarbon remediation in tropical soils.
Microbiome structure, function, and drivers across different soil groups in an agricultural region of SerbiaCORE3
Authors: Ana Kuzmanović, Dragana Tamindžija, Jordana Ninkov, Jovica Vasin, M. Djan...
Venue: SOIL

Abstract
Soil is the largest reservoir of biodiversity, with distinct physical, chemical, and biological properties. Microorganisms play essential roles in soil formation and fertility. This study aimed to analyse the microbiomes of three selected soil groups (Chernozem, Solonchak, and Vertisol) in an important agricultural region of Vojvodina (Serbia) by 16S rRNA gene metabarcoding and explore their association with soil properties, including soil pH, texture, organic matter, nutrient status, cation exchange capacity, and selected macro- and micronutrients. Soil samples from a total of 26 field plots (in 5 replicates) were analysed using Illumina MiSeq paired-end sequencing and processed through the QIIME2 pipeline. The obtained results indicate that the analysed soils generally exhibit physicochemical properties typical for the respective soil groups. Alpha diversity indices revealed the highest microbiome diversity in Chernozem, consistent with its favourable physicochemical characteristics. Based on beta diversity, clear separation of soil groups according to their properties was determined. Proteobacteria, Acidobacteriota, and Actinobacteriota dominate the microbial community composition at the phylum level. Redundancy analysis revealed that soil properties account for 53.8 % of the variation in community composition, with pH value, iron availability, and CaCO 3 content having the strongest influence, pH being particularly significant. The functional potential of microbial communities showed dominance of functions related to metabolism, with significant representation of functions belonging to the following groups: genetic information processing, environmental information processing, and cellular processes. The analysis of genes involved in nitrogen cycling using Kruskal–Wallis test showed no statistically significant differences in their abundances across different soil groups ( p  value > 0.05). This study provides the first detailed analysis of soil microbial communities across Serbia and highlights factors shaping them. These findings underscore the importance of microbial diversity for ecosystem functioning and offer a framework for soil health monitoring, while providing insights relevant for sustainable agriculture.

Authors: M. Allegrezza, Mara Gabbrielli, Nicola Alessi, Armadoro Luca, Marco Perfetto...
Venue: Nutrient Cycling in Agroecosystems
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Nitrous oxide (N2O) emissions from fertilised agricultural soils are often dominated by short-lived emission pulses, but their regulation under contrasting tillage systems remains uncertain. This study combined high-frequency automated chamber measurements, process-based estimates of mineral nitrogen availability and Random Forest analysis to investigate N2O emission dynamics after cattle slurry application under conventional tillage, minimum tillage and no-tillage. The experiment was conducted during a maize–barley sequence in northern Italy. Daily N2O fluxes, soil temperature, water-filled pore space, carbon dioxide fluxes and mineral nitrogen dynamics were analysed to identify the main controls of post-fertilisation emission pulses. Most crop-period N2O emissions occurred within the first 40 days after slurry application, accounting for 91–97% of total emissions during maize and 78–89% during barley. In absolute terms, cumulative N2O–N emissions averaged 4.68 ± 0.74 kg N ha-1 during maize and 2.04 ± 0.74 kg N ha-1 during barley. This difference was not explained by water-filled pore space alone, which was broadly comparable between crop periods, but by the interaction among soil temperature, slurry-derived ammonium and nitrate availability, microbial activity and tillage-related soil physical conditions. Random Forest models showed crop- and system-specific driver importance, with soil temperature dominant during barley and stronger roles of water-filled pore space and mineral nitrogen during maize. No-tillage reduced area-scaled emissions but caused a strong yield penalty during maize, whereas it showed a more favourable agro-environmental balance during barley. These findings highlight the need to evaluate slurry and tillage management through both emission intensity and crop productivity.
Effects of Uprooting and Impala Herbicide Control of Black Wattle on Soil Physicochemical Properties in Nyika National-ParkCORE3

Abstract
Black wattle (Acacia mearnsii) threatens montane grasslands and soil functioning in Nyika National Park, Malawi, but comparative evidence on the soil effects of alternative control methods is limited. This four-year study evaluated the effects of mechanical uprooting and cutting followed by Impala herbicide application on selected soil physicochemical properties, using untreated invaded plots as controls in a randomised complete block design. Soil samples were collected at a depth of 0–15 cm before and after treatment and analysed for pH, soil organic carbon (SOC) stocks, total nitrogen (TN), available phosphorus (P), exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP), exchangeable calcium (Ca), exchangeable magnesium (Mg), and electrical conductivity (EC). Linear Mixed-Effects Model (LMEM) was then performed in Minitab (version 21) to test for significant differences among treatments (α=0.05). Uprooting increased pH by 7.3% (4.1 to 4.4) and SOC stocks from 68.88 to 74.95 Mg C ha⁻¹, whereas cutting/Impala increased SOC by 43.8% (64.82 to 93.18 Mg C ha⁻¹). TN declined from 2.52% to 0.61% after uprooting and from 2.51% to 0.62% after cutting/Impala. Available P increased most under cutting/Impala, from 4.3 to 45.3 mg kg⁻¹, while EC declined by 59.4% (1.92 to 0.78 mmho cm⁻¹) after uprooting. Treatment effects were significant for TN (P = 0.014), exchangeable Ca (P = 0.045), exchangeable Mg (P = 0.028), and EC (P = 0.001). ESP declined by 60.5% (3.8% to 1.5%) following mechanical uprooting. Therefore, both methods altered soil properties, indicating that black-wattle control should integrate soil monitoring with vegetation restoration.

Abstract
Declining soil fertility remains a major cause of reduced crop production among smallholder farmers in sub-Saharan Africa. This is partly attributed to the blanket application of inappropriate fertilizers. Most fertilizer recommendations are based on soil analyses conducted in the 1990s, which have since become obsolete. In addition, the adoption of minimum tillage without considering the differential impacts of structural deformation has increased soil erosion, resulting in reduced nutrient levels as reflected by productivity indices (PI). To characterize the soil nutrient status of smallholder farms, six hundred soil samples were collected from thirteen counties in Kenya: Embu, Bungoma, Trans Nzoia, Kitui, Nandi, Machakos, Makueni, Tharaka Nithi, Nakuru, Kakamega, Kilifi, Kwale, and Taita Taveta. The samples were analyzed for soil pH, organic matter, and nutrient content. The results showed variation in the productivity index across the study regions. The Coastal region had a PI of 59.1%, while the Eastern and Western regions had PIs of 64.9% and 61.3%, respectively. The most limiting factors were soil pH, nitrogen, organic carbon, bulk density, and zinc. The mean nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) contents were 0.10%, 20.14 ppm, and 0.95%, respectively, in the Eastern region; 0.13%, 2.5 ppm, and 0.49%, respectively, in the Coastal region; and 0.20%, 11.20 ppm, and 0.61%, respectively, in the Western region. Nitrogen, phosphorus, and zinc were below the critical threshold levels in all three regions, whereas copper and potassium were within adequate ranges. Nitrogen showed a significant correlation (p < 0.001) with bulk density in the Eastern, Coastal, and Western regions, respectively, as follows: −0.1279x + 0.2753 (R² = 0.365), 0.1124x + 0.2462 (R² = 0.189), and −0.3292x + 0.5921 (R² = 0.462). These findings provide essential information for making informed and sustainable decisions regarding agricultural production in the study regions.

Abstract
Red mud (RM), a highly alkaline solid waste, remains a global environmental disposal bottleneck, yet its mineralogy offers untapped soil formation potential—if alkalinity and structure can be overcome rapidly. We report a 10-day protocol using acidic distiller's grains (DG), Maotai-flavor liquor wastewater (MFW), and sawdust (SD) to rapidly transform bauxite residue into a plant growth substrate, drastically shortening a process that conventionally takes weeks to months. Single and combined treatments were assessed using pH, electrical conductivity (EC), organic matter (OM), excitation-emission matrix (EEM) fluorescence, X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM-EDS), particle-size distribution (PSD), X-ray fluorescence (XRF), and inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) of water extracts. At the 10-day endpoint, DG and MFW-containing treatments lowered RM pH from 11.23 to 8.87-9.33, while EC in every amended group decreased below 600 µS·cm-1 by day 15. OM increased from 10.50 to 24.82-49.29 g·kg-1, and six amended groups exceeded 30 g·kg-1. EEM results showed that MFW supplied readily soluble fluorescent organics, whereas DG promoted humification (humification index, HIX, up to 0.987); the adjusted EEM coordinate grid placed the two DG-containing combined-treatment peaks at excitation/emission (Ex/Em) coordinates of 382/470 and 384/472 nm. SEM-EDS, particle-size, and normalized XRF patterns were consistent with mineral-organic association and a shift from fine particles (<20 µm) toward aggregate-sized assemblages (>50 µm). The best-performing SD-containing treatment achieved 65-70% ryegrass survival, whereas DG-containing formulations showed greater EC-related stress. Under HJ 557-2010 water-extraction conditions, maximum total Cr, Mn, Cu, and Ni concentrations were 0.142, 0.187, 0.780, and 0.114 mg·L‑1, respectively, and Zn, Pb, and Cd were below the instrument detection limits used in this analysis. This work establishes a 10-day, waste-treats-waste route for red-mud soilization—shifting rehabilitation from a slow process to a practical timescale while co-valorizing bauxite residue and Maotai-flavor liquor wastewater.

Interactive Effects of Soil Texture and Salinity Levels on Nitrification-Related Nitrogen Transformations in SoilsPROXY2
Authors: Fei-Rong He, Hai-Bin Gu, Xu-Nuo Zhao, Cheng Ji, Bo Sun...
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-11-06

Abstract
Soil salinity substantially modulates soil nitrogen (N) transformation processes, yet how soil texture mediates the salinity-induced changes in soil nitrogen transformation remains poorly quantified. To evaluate the interactive effects of soil identity and salt stress on inorganic N concentrations, a 56-day column incubation experiment was conducted using three typical soil textures (loamy sand, silt loam, and clay loam) with a salinity gradient (0, 3, and 6 g kg−1). Uniform urea application (215 mg N kg−1 soil) was performed at 25 ± 1 °C and approximately 60% water-holding capacity, and dynamic variations in soil inorganic N fractions and urease activity were monitored throughout incubation. The results revealed distinct soil-specific inorganic N concentration patterns under salt perturbation. Loamy sand soil exhibited prominent NO3--N accumulation, while clay loam soil showed stronger NH4+-N retention and more significant transient NO2--N accumulation. In the non-saline control treatment, the final concentration of NO3--N reached 202.14 mg kg−1 in loamy sand, which was 48.4% higher than that in clay loam soil. Increasing soil salinity generally prolonged NH4+-N retention and transient NO2--N accumulation while limiting NO3--N formation, with the magnitude of such salt-induced changes highly dependent on soil texture and incubation stage. Specifically, soil salinity at 3 and 6 g kg−1 reduced the final nitrate content of clay loam soil by 14.3% and 15.4%, respectively. Urease activity consistently ranked as clay loam > silt loam > loamy sand across treatments, and this enzymatic difference was decoupled from soil nitrate accumulation capacity. RDA further indicated date-dependent associations of EC with NH4+-N and NO2--N retention, while pH was more closely related to NO3--N accumulation. The results indicate that soil texture is associated with heterogeneous inorganic N responses to salt stress, although initial chemical differences preclude attribution to texture alone. These findings describe measured inorganic N pools and urease activity rather than directly measured nitrification rates or microbial mechanisms. Validation with plants, mixed salts, and field conditions is required before management recommendations can be established.
Quantification of sources and risks of groundwater fluorine pollution in Guanzhong Basin, China.PROXY2
Authors: Qi-Ying Zhang, Hui Qian, Yi-Lin Li, Kun-Yuan Li, Ai-Hui Wei
Venue: Journal of environmental management
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs
Abstract
This study developed an integrated framework combining self-organizing maps (SOM), positive matrix factorization (PMF), and two-dimensional Monte Carlo simulation (2D MCS) to investigate fluoride sources, enrichment mechanisms, and probabilistic health risks. The analysis used 245 groundwater samples from a typical semi-arid irrigation district with high-fluoride groundwater in the Guanzhong Plain, China. Classification by SOM yielded five hydrochemical groups with clear spatial heterogeneity, F- concentration were low (<1 mg/L) south of the Wei River, whereas a distinct west-to-east gradient occurred north of the river, with peak values > 3 mg/L in the east. Five sources were identified by PMF: fluoride mineral dissolution, silicate weathering, evaporite dissolution, agricultural pollution, and domestic pollution. Geochemical and isotopic evidence confirmed fluorite dissolution under alkaline pH as the primary natural control, with calcite precipitation and cation exchange promoting this process. Evaporative concentration, intensified by prolonged irrigation, was a secondary enrichment mechanism. Probabilistic risk assessment using 2D MCS showed significantly higher non-carcinogenic risks for children than for adults. Approximately 30% of children and 10% of adults had a hazard quotient (HQ) > 1. Sensitivity analysis identified water intake rate (IR) and body weight (BW) as the dominant factors, explaining ∼92% of the variance. HQ equaled 1 at IR/BW ratios of 0.0630 L/day/kg for adults and 0.0632 L/day/kg for children. Substitution of these parameters into the HQ equation yielded a fluoride concentration of 0.95 mg/L for both groups. Thus, groundwater fluoride concentrations above 0.95 mg/L place both adults and children at risk of fluoride-induced health effects. The integrated framework effectively distinguishes natural and anthropogenic fluoride contributions, overcoming limitations of previous single-method approaches. Recommended mitigation measures include adjusting irrigation patterns, transitioning to organic fertilizers, and installing point-of-use defluoridation devices. These findings provide a robust scientific basis for sustainable groundwater management and public health protection in fluoride-affected irrigation districts globally.

Abstract
Heavy metals are recognized as a growing environmental concern due to their toxicity and long-term stability. In this study, a magnetic composite biochar material (PDFMB) was synthesized using iron-based sludge and Chinese medicine residue, with zinc chloride and polyaniline serving as modifiers, to adsorb and reduce Cr(VI) in water. The optimal preparation conditions were determined through preliminary experiments, and the adsorption performance was evaluated through material characterization and Cr(VI) adsorption tests. The removal of Cr(VI) by PDFMB was highly pH-dependent, with an optimal pH of 2, indicating that acidic conditions favored adsorption. The maximum adsorption capacity for Cr(VI) at 308 K and pH 5 was 111.11 mg/g. The adsorption kinetics followed a pseudo-second-order model, while the Langmuir isotherm provided a good fit to the adsorption isotherm data. Thermodynamic analysis revealed that the removal process was spontaneous and endothermic. The anions Cl‒, SO42‒, and PO43‒ inhibited the removal efficiency of Cr(VI), with inhibition strength following the order Cl‒ < SO42‒ < PO43‒. The key mechanisms involved in Cr(VI) removal included physical and chemical adsorption, chelation, and the reduction of Cr(VI) to Cr(III). The PDFMB composite exhibited high stability and reusability, achieving an 85.25% removal efficiency after six cycles. Owing to its high efficiency, magnetic separability, and excellent recyclability, PDFMB represents a highly promising candidate for addressing Cr(VI) pollution.

Venue: Microorganisms

Abstract
Mountain ecosystems play a crucial role in global carbon cycling and host a series of processes that degrade and modify soil dissolved organic matter (DOM). Understanding the succession of soil DOM with elevation in mountain ecosystems is helpful for predicting and managing the turnover of soil organic matter in response to future climate warming. In the present study, we investigated the characteristics of soil DOM and its relationship with microbial communities along an altitudinal gradient in the Luyashan Mountains using ultrahigh-resolution Fourier transform ion cyclotron resonance mass spectrometry and high-throughput sequencing. The chemical stability of soil DOM increased with increasing elevation, and the changes in composition of DOM extracted from forestland soil were more significant than those of DOM extracted from grassland soil along an altitudinal gradient. Potential transformations among soil DOM molecules were predominantly thermodynamically limited, suggesting that external energy inputs may be needed, potentially through microbial metabolism. Additionally, intragroup molecular transformations that require less energy occur more frequently than intergroup molecular transformations. Ecological null models revealed that the compositional assembly of the DOM molecules was controlled by deterministic processes; however, their molecular reactivity strongly influenced the assembly mechanisms of the DOM molecules. The bacterial community affected the composition of DOM, while bacteria tended to degrade labile DOM components that did not contain heteroatoms. The results of the present study provide a deep understanding of the basic principles that control the complex dynamics of soil DOM in natural ecosystems, emphasizing that the application of concepts and methods in the metacommunity ecology may provide a new avenue for understanding natural DOM dynamics.

Abstract
Flow diagram showing rainfall-driven flood modelling using HEC-RAS, comparing baseline, nature-based solutions, structural measures, and hybrid scenarios, with reductions in flood depth, velocity, and discharge across the catchment. Rapid urban expansion and informal settlements are increasing exposure to flood hazards in urbanising catchments such as the Nairobi River Catchment. The 2024 El Niño event caused widespread flooding, affecting critical infrastructure and highlighting the urgent need for effective mitigation strategies. This study evaluates three flood management approaches using the HEC-RAS model: (1) modified channels as grey infrastructure, (2) soil organic matter enhancement and reforestation as Nature-based Solutions, and (3) a hybrid strategy. Model performance showed strong agreement with observed flows (R2 = 0.88; NSE = 0.75). Spatial datasets, including Global Forest Change and Land Productivity Dynamics, were used to identify intervention zones and parameterise scenarios. Results show that NbS reduced peak discharge by 37–75% and delayed peak flow by about one hour. Grey infrastructure achieved the greatest impact, reducing flood depths by 55–60% and peak discharge by 48–75%, but increased flow velocities. The hybrid strategy provided the most balanced hydraulic performance, combining runoff attenuation with improved channel conveyance to deliver consistent reductions in flood magnitude and enhanced flow regulation. These findings demonstrate that integrated approaches combining engineered and nature-based interventions offer a resilient pathway for managing urban flood risk under increasing climatic and developmental pressures.
