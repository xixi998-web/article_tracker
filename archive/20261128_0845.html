Responses of fungal communities in roots and soil to sulfur-induced acidification in a meadow steppe: A pH threshold for soil fungal richness.CORE9
Authors: Xiao-Meng Ma, He-Yong Liu, Yue-Heng Wang, Wen-Yao Wang, Shui-Xia Li...
Venue: Journal of environmental management
Published: 2026-11-21
Abs
Abstract
Although atmospheric sulfur (S) deposition has declined substantially in China in recent decades, it remains an important contributor to soil acidification, with its contribution comparable to or greater than that of nitrogen (N) deposition in Asia; whilst substantial S inputs persist in northeastern China and Inner Mongolia. Yet how reduced soil pH would affect fungal diversity loss and the driving factors underlying diversity changes remains poorly understood. Based on a 6-year field experiment with eight S addition rates ranging from 0 to 50 g S m-2 yr-1 in a meadow steppe in Inner Mongolia, we elucidated how S addition modulates root and soil fungal communities. Our results show that the effects of S addition on fungal α-diversity were niche-specific. S15 and S20 increased root symbiotic fungal richness, possibly in association with changes in plant-fungal mutualisms, whereas soil fungal α-diversity was lowest at S50, possibly reflecting soil acidification induced by S addition. Soil fungal richness at S50 did not differ significantly from the control. Segmented regression estimated pH breakpoints of 5.88 (95% CI 4.51-6.29) for soil fungal richness and 5.62 (95% CI 4.89-6.42) for root fungal richness. The Davies test supported a change in slope in soil (P = 0.042), with limited evidence for a slope change in roots (P = 0.14). Variance partitioning analyses showed that below the soil pH threshold, the largest fraction of variation in soil fungal richness was jointly explained by soil properties, plant factors, and fungal network attributes. Overall, root and soil fungal communities responded differently to S addition, and the factors associated with fungal richness differed above and below the fitted pH breakpoints.
Mitigation of Cd Accumulation in Rice by the Combination of Mg-modified Biochar/attapulgite Materials and Foliar InhibitorsCORE5
Authors: Ji-Lei Fang, Wen-Xuan Luo, Ji-Zhuang Wu, Kun-Peng Yang, Ya-Ping Zhang
Venue: Water, Air, & Soil Pollution
Published: 2026-11-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Cadmium (Cd) is a highly toxic heavy metal that poses serious threats to both human health and plant growth. It exhibits several environmental risks, including high enrichment potential, mobility, chemical stability, long persistence in soil, and resistance to microbial degradation. In this study, a novel Mg-modified biochar/ attapulgite composite material was developed and applied in field experiments to evaluate its effects on soil pH, organic matter content, as well as the speciation and concentration of Cd. Furthermore, the effectiveness of combining silicon-based foliar regulators to reduce Cd accumulation in different rice tissues was also investigated. The experimental results demonstrated MgCl2/BA and MgO/BA increased soil pH by 0.46 and 0.53 units, respectively, resulting in a 41.02% and 38.83% increase in soil organic matter content. At the same time, the available Cd content in the soil significantly decreased, as the acid-soluble and reducible fractions of Cd were transformed into oxidizable and residual forms. Besides, Cd content in rice grains treated with MgCl2/BA and MgO/BA decreased by 66.22% and 72.90% respectively. After the foliar application of the remediation agent, the Cd content in rice grains further decreased by 20.54% and 13.22% respectively. Moreover, the application of the remediation agent contributed to an increase in rice yield. This may be attributed to the elevated pH and soil organic matter content, along with the reduced Cd toxicity, which collectively promoted the growth and development of rice.
Interactive effects of gasification biochar and humanure on physiological and biochemical responses of Raphanus sativusCORE4
Authors: Mahalakshmi Sekar, V. M. Jaganathan
Venue: Discover Plants
Published: 2026-11-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Early seedling growth and physiological responses are key indicators for assessing the agricultural suitability of soil amendments. This study investigated the biological compatibility of gasification-derived biochar from cashew nutshells and coconut shells, both individually and in combination with humanure, using a radish (Raphanus sativus) seedling assay. Biochar characterization found no detectable polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and measured heavy metal concentrations complied with International Biochar Initiative guidelines. Humanure was odourless, E. coli and Salmonella were below detection limits, and heavy metal concentrations were within the European Union threshold. A comprehensive phytotoxicity assessment evaluated germination indices, seedling growth, photosynthetic pigments, soluble protein content, and post-harvest soil pH and electrical conductivity (EC). Germination index values ranged from 96.5 to 131.4%, indicating responses from non-phytotoxic to phytostimulatory under the tested conditions. The emergence rate index (ERI) and peak value ranged from 88 to 121.5% and from 97.5 to 135.8%, respectively, relative to the control. Two-way ANOVA revealed significant main and interactive effects of biochar and humanure on germination, ERI, shoot length, fresh weight, and EC. Pearson correlation analysis showed a strong positive association between soil pH and EC (r = 0.90), whereas chlorophyll (r = − 0.65, − 0.74) and carotenoid contents (r = − 0.63, − 0.71) showed negative associations with both soil parameters. Overall, integrating gasification biochar from agricultural residues with humanure transforms waste into plant-safe, growth-promoting inputs for sustainable agriculture.

Effect of an Amino Acid-Balanced Low-Protein Diet throughout the Rearing Period on Broiler Performance under Semi-Practical ConditionsECO1

Venue: The Journal of Poultry Science
Published: 2026-11-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Abstract Reducing greenhouse gas emissions from the livestock industry is essential for mitigating climate change. Nitrogen excreted by poultry is a key contributor to nitrous oxide emissions during composting. Hence, strategies that improve nitrogen utilization efficiency should be developed. Under controlled conditions, amino acid-balanced low-protein (AALP) diets lower nitrogen excretion without compromising growth performance. This study aimed to evaluate the effect of AALP diets on growth performance, nitrogen utilization, physiological status, meat quality, and sensory attributes of broiler chickens under semi-practical conditions. In total, 300 day-old Ross 308 broiler chicks (150 males and 150 females) were randomly allocated to two dietary treatments, with three replicate pens per treatment, and reared until they were 45 days old. Subsequently, growth performance, blood biochemical parameters, and meat quality traits, including taste attributes, were evaluated.Nitrogen excretion in representative excreta was consistent with previous findings, and the AALP diet did not adversely affect growth performance or muscle yield. Abdominal fat deposition increased, whereas blood biochemical parameters remained within the physiological range. Drip loss at 72 h postmortem declined in the AALP group, indicating improved water-holding capacity. The free amino acid composition of breast muscle was not substantially altered by the dietary treatment. Consistent with these findings, electronic taste-sensing analysis showed no differences between dietary groups in the sensory attributes of breast or thigh meat. In conclusion, AALP diets maintained growth performance, physiological status, meat quality, and taste under semi-practical rearing conditions, supporting their potential application in sustainable broiler production.
Esterified Banana Peel Waste as a Low-Cost Biosorbent for Heavy Metal Removal from Real Paint Manufacturing Wastewater: Effects of Concentration, pH, Contact Time, Dosage, and RegenerationECO1
Authors: M. Bilal
Venue: Water, Air, & Soil Pollution
Published: 2026-11-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Heavy metal discharge from paint manufacturing is a significant contributor to industrial water pollution in industrialising economies, yet most biosorption studies validate candidate materials against synthetic, single-metal solutions rather than authentic multi-metal effluent. This study evaluated methanol/hydrochloric acid esterified banana peel waste (E-BPW) as a low-cost biosorbent for chromium, nickel, cobalt, and lead, using both dilute single-variable screening solutions and real multi-metal wastewater from an operating paint manufacturer. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy of E-BPW showed hydroxyl/carboxyl, aliphatic C–H, ester carbonyl, and C–O functional group signatures consistent with a carboxylate-rich lignocellulosic biosorbent. In dilute single-metal-variable screening (1 g · dm⁻3 dose), biosorption capacity followed the order Co(II) > Ni(II) > Pb(II) ≫ Cr(III), increasing with initial concentration (4.3–24 mg · dm⁻3) and with solution pH up to approximately pH 5.5–7.5; overall metal removal increased with contact time to a maximum of 77% at 80 min before declining slightly, and with adsorbent dosage to a plateau of ~ 82% at and above 4 g · dm⁻3. When applied to real paint-industry wastewater at 5 g · dm⁻3 dose, E-BPW achieved removal efficiencies of 80.3% (Cr), 68.9% (Co), 63.1% (Pb), and 56.0% (Ni), corresponding to biosorption capacities of 83.8, 39.4, 50.2, and 36.2 mg · g⁻1 respectively — a different metal-selectivity ranking from the dilute screening series, which we attribute to the very different concentration regimes involved (24 mg · dm⁻3 versus 286–522 mg · dm⁻3) rather than to a single consistent mechanism. Regeneration with 2 mol · dm⁻3 HNO3 recovered 99.3% of bound metals, indicating reuse potential. This study reports single-determination measurements without replication, and several mechanistic interpretations (metal speciation effects, diffusion-limited kinetics) are offered as hypotheses rather than confirmed mechanisms, since no isotherm, kinetic, or surface-characterisation studies were performed. Within these limits, the results indicate that E-BPW has genuine, if not yet fully characterised, potential as a low-cost biosorbent for real industrial effluent, and we set out the isotherm, kinetic, replication, and pilot-scale studies needed before that potential can be considered established.
