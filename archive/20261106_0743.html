Legume Cover Crops Enhance Nutrient Cycling and Biomass Production in a Lettuce–Lettuce–Oat Cropping Sequence in a Pot ExperimentCORE9
Authors: Peltier Aguiar, M. Arrobas, M. Rodrigues
Venue: Horticulturae
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study evaluates the performance of seven cover crop species, five legumes, one grass, and one brassica, and their residual effects on a lettuce–lettuce–oat rotation. A completely randomised design with eight treatments and three replicates was established in 12 L pots to assess biomass production, nutrient accumulation, and the effects of cover crops on subsequent crops and soil properties. Legumes produced significantly higher biomass (97.0–152.9 g pot−1) than non-legumes (7.5–11.1 g pot−1). Nitrogen (N) accumulation was also significantly higher in legumes (2.2–3.1 g pot−1) than in non-legumes (0.1 g pot−1). More than 96% of the N accumulated by legumes was derived from the atmosphere, as estimated using the difference method, highlighting the importance of atmospheric N fixation to legume N accumulation under the conditions of this experiment. Differences among legume species were also observed. Although legumes showed lower concentrations of phosphorus (P) and other nutrients in plant tissues, this was attributed to a dilution effect associated with greater biomass production rather than restricted nutrient uptake. Cover crops had marked effects on subsequent cash crops. Crops grown after legumes showed significantly higher biomass, N status, and photosynthetic performance than those following non-legumes or the control. These effects were strongest in the first crop but persisted throughout the rotation. In contrast, non-legumes did not differ from the control. N recovery proved to be a sensitive indicator of these differences, confirming the key role of legumes in supplying N through residue mineralisation. Legumes also influenced soil properties by increasing nitrate availability and promoting slight soil acidification, potentially associated with enhanced nitrification. Overall, the results highlight the importance of species selection in cover cropping, with legumes playing a central role in improving subsequent crop productivity.
Spatial Variability of the Physicochemical Properties of Cotton-growing Soils at Six Locations in Northern Côte d’Ivoire (Boron, Boundiali, Diawala, Ferkessédougou, Kasséré and Korhogo)CORE9
Authors: Kouadio Konan-kan Hippolyte, Koné Ismail, N'goran Kouadio Emmanuel
Venue: International Journal of Plant &amp; Soil Science
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Cotton-growing soils in northern Côte d’Ivoire differ in their physical and chemical properties, potentially affecting soil fertility management. This study assessed the spatial variability of selected physicochemical indicators at six locations: Boron, Boundiali, Diawala, Ferkessédougou, Kasséré and Korhogo. Composite soil samples were collected from the 0–20 cm layer under cotton cultivation. Laboratory analyses included particle size distribution, soil pH, soil organic carbon, total nitrogen, available phosphorus, exchangeable bases, cation exchange capacity (CEC) and base saturation. Differences among locations were examined using statistical and multivariate analyses. Soil pH ranged from 6.45 at Korhogo to 7.12 at Diawala, indicating slightly acidic to neutral conditions. Mean soil organic carbon ranged from 0.7 to 1.8 g kg⁻¹, total nitrogen from 0.07 to 0.16 g kg⁻¹ and available phosphorus from 9 to 30 mg kg⁻¹. CEC ranged from 5.64 to 15.32 cmol₊ kg⁻¹, while base saturation ranged from 22.9% to 59.4%. Sandy textures predominated at four locations; Diawala had a sandy loam texture and Kasséré a loam texture. The first two principal components explained 71.84% of the observed variability. Coarse silt, fine sand, pH, total nitrogen and CEC were important differentiating variables, and four relatively homogeneous soil groups were identified. These findings indicate that fertility management should consider the contrasting soil properties of the locations studied, particularly nutrient availability, soil texture and exchange capacity.

Effect of Biochar Application on Soil Chemical Properties, Nutrient Dynamics and Plant Nutrient UptakeCORE6
Authors: A. Innazent, K. T. Rao, Vikas Kumar, Jince Mary M. Joy, D. K. D. Deekshitha...
Venue: Oriental Journal Of Chemistry
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil degradation, nutrient depletion, and declining nutrient-use efficiency are major constraints to sustainable agricultural production, creating a need for environmentally compatible soil amendments. Biochar, a stable carbon-rich material produced through pyrolysis of biomass under limited oxygen conditions, has emerged as a promising strategy for improving soil fertility, nutrient retention, and long-term carbon storage. The present study evaluated the effects of biochar application on soil chemical properties, nutrient dynamics, and plant nutrient uptake, with particular emphasis on soil pH, electrical conductivity, organic carbon, nutrient availability, and the influence of different biochar feedstocks and production conditions. Biochars derived from agricultural residues, woody biomass, and animal manure were considered, together with the effects of pyrolysis temperature, residence time, and physicochemical characteristics. Biochar application generally increased soil pH and buffering capacity, particularly in acidic soils, owing to the presence of alkaline minerals and basic cations. It also influenced soil electrical conductivity through the release and retention of soluble mineral nutrients. A prominent response was the enrichment and stabilization of soil organic carbon due to the recalcitrant aromatic carbon structure of biochar. Furthermore, its porous structure, large surface area, and reactive functional groups enhanced cation exchange capacity and promoted the retention of ammonium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus within the root zone. These improvements created favorable conditions for nutrient availability, root development, microbial activity, and plant nutrient acquisition. The effectiveness of biochar depended considerably on feedstock type, pyrolysis conditions, soil characteristics, and application rate. Overall, biochar represents a multifunctional and sustainable soil amendment capable of improving soil fertility, nutrient-use efficiency, plant nutrition, and carbon sequestration while reducing nutrient losses from agricultural systems.

Authors: Ze-Yu Yang, Jing-Jie Zhang, Ying-Ying Li, Bei-Bei Tao, Shi-Chen Zhu...
Venue: Forests
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Forest soil organic carbon (SOC) and nutrient dynamics are critical for terrestrial carbon cycling, yet full-profile soil property characterization at regional scale remains limited in southern subtropical China. Based on 0–100 cm soil profile data from 443 forest sampling points in Maoming City—6645 field observations aggregated to 2215 point × layer records for statistical modelling—this study investigated vertical distribution patterns of SOC, total N, total P, total K, pH, and molar stoichiometric ratios, and quantified the relative explanatory contributions of stand type and topographic factors using linear mixed-effects models (LMMs), random forest (RF), and geostatistical kriging. SOC and total N exhibited pronounced surface accumulation, declining by approximately 50% and 44% from the surface (12.75 g kg−1 and 941 mg kg−1) to the 80–100 cm layer (6.35 g kg−1 and 530 mg kg−1), respectively. Total P showed minimal vertical variation (≈5%), total K remained broadly stable, and pH increased slightly with depth (4.55 → 4.66). Stand type was significantly associated with SOC, total N, total K, pH, and most stoichiometric ratios (all p < 0.05); surface SOC was higher in broadleaf mixed and coniferous forests than in eucalypt, economic tree species, and litchi/longan plantations. All stoichiometric ratios declined significantly with depth. RF models showed that soil depth had the highest permutation importance for SOC and total N (0.44–0.59), while stand type ranked first for pH and total K (0.40–0.49); however, out-of-bag R2 values were modest (–0.06 to 0.23), indicating that substantial variation remains unexplained by the four measured predictors. All properties exhibited moderate spatial autocorrelation (nugget ratio 0.48–0.67). Total K showed moderate interpolation accuracy under random leave-one-out validation (R2CV = 0.458), whereas spatially structured block cross-validation yielded negative R2 for four of the five properties; the kriging maps accordingly visualise the spatial arrangement of the measured properties. These results provide regional baseline evidence for 0–100 cm nutrient vertical patterns and soil carbon density in southern subtropical China.

Abstract
Despite growing evidence that land-use change and altitudinal variation influence soil organic carbon (SOC) and soil pH, integrated assessments examining their combined effects across multiple land-use systems and environmental gradients remain limited in semi-arid landscapes of northern Tanzania. This study assessed SOC (%) and pH across four land use types i.e., conserved land, cropping land, grazing land, and abandoned land in Arusha and Monduli Districts, northern Tanzania, encompassing lowland (< 1400 m), midland (1400–1600 m), and highland (> 1600 m) zones altitude categories. A total of 586 soil samples were collected across 2 Soil depth classes and analyzed for SOC (%) and pH. Results demonstrated that land use type was the dominant driver of both SOC and pH variability (one-way ANOVA: FSOC = 107.19, P = 2.95 × 10− 55; FpH = 48.68, P = 4.43 × 10− 28). Conserved land recorded the highest mean SOC (2.15 ± 0.35%) and lowest mean pH (6.48 ± 0.38), while abandoned land showed the lowest SOC (0.84 ± 0.20%) and highest pH (7.27 ± 0.42). Monduli District consistently outperformed Arusha District across all land use types, with overall mean SOC of 1.87% versus 1.18%. A strong negative correlation was observed between SOC and pH (Pearson r = -0.637; Spearman ρ = -0.653; P < 0.001), and altitude positively predicted SOC (r = + 0.59). Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc comparisons confirmed statistically significant differences in SOC between all six pairwise land use combinations. These findings show the critical role of land conservation, highland ecosystem protection, and active restoration of degraded soils in sustaining carbon stocks and soil health across semi-arid landscapes of northern Tanzania. Effects of chemical fertilizer application on soil properties, banana (Musa paradisiaca) yields, and soil microbiomes in a long-term cultivation fieldCORE3
Authors: Sasipa Pruksangkul, T. Termsaithong, W. Mhuantong, T. Limpiyakorn, Prinpida Sonthiphand
Venue: PeerJ
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Due to climate change and increasing food demand, conventional agriculture relies heavily on chemical fertilizers. This study investigated the effects of chemical fertilizer application on soil properties, banana (Musa paradisiaca) yields, and soil microbiomes in a long-term cultivation field. Metagenomic analysis of the 16S rRNA gene provided a comprehensive overview of soil microbial diversity and functional potential. Nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium (NPK) fertilizers at 0, 119, 238, and 476 kg ha−1 were applied monthly for one year. Fertilizer application significantly altered soil properties and microbial community dynamics but had no significant effect on banana yields. Higher fertilizer rates reduced soil pH and increased available phosphorus and exchangeable potassium, while total nitrogen and organic matter remained relatively unchanged. Elevated NPK inputs increased the abundance of specific microbial taxa, including Bacillus, Streptomyces, uncultured bacterium JG30-KF-AS9, and Nitrospira, while Xanthobacteraceae, bacteriap25, Micromonosporaceae, Nocardioides, Subgroup 6, and Gemmatimonadaceae consistently represented core taxa across all treatments. Pearson’s correlation analysis revealed that soil total nitrogen and exchangeable potassium were positively correlated with the abundance of key plant growth-promoting and nitrifying taxa, including Pseudomonas and Nitrospira, whereas soil pH was negatively correlated with Acidothermus, Nitrospira, and Bacillus, but positively correlated with Subgroup 6. Functional gene profiles further indicated enhanced anaerobic processes associated with carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur cycling. The findings suggest that long-term nutrient accumulation resulting from repeated fertilization and crop residue inputs may sustain banana crops throughout the 10-month production period without additional fertilization. Together, these findings highlight the need for fertilizer regimes tailored to long-term soil nutrient status, contributing to both sustainable agriculture and microbiome-informed crop management.

Abstract
Cover crops have garnered increased attention as a conservation strategy to increase soil organic carbon (SOC) and nitrogen (N) while improving overall soil health in agricultural systems. However, their effect on SOC and N dynamics in sandy soils of the US southeastern (SE) Coastal Plain is poorly understood. This study evaluated the response of SOC and N pools to cover crops in a Norfolk loamy sand under long‐term (>40 years) conventional (CvT) and conservation (CnT) tillage systems and short‐term (5 years) winter cover cropping. Treatments included: Tillage (two levels: CvT and CnT) crossed with winter cover crops (two levels: cover crop and fallow) in a factorial design. Soil samples were collected from each treatment in spring and fall 2022–2024, and analyzed for soil pH, electrical conductivity, total SOC and soil total nitrogen (STN), dissolved organic carbon (DOC), hot water‐extractable carbon (HWEC), microbial biomass carbon (MBC), inorganic N, and potentially mineralizable nitrogen (PMN). The CnT increased total SOC and STN by 64% and 72%, and DOC and HWEC by 22% and 52% over CvT, respectively. There were measurable, yet modest, improvements in SOC and N fractions with cover crops, suggesting ecosystem enhancement services were underway, albeit modest at this stage. Our results demonstrated that cover cropping should be considered a long‐term conservation strategy to gradually improve soil health and productivity, not as a “quick fix” for low‐fertility soils in the SE Coastal Plain.
A Lightweight Full-Spectrum Embedding Model for Multi-Property Prediction from Soil Vis-NIR SpectraCORE3
Authors: Xiao-Hui Cheng, Min-Tao Duan, Yan-Jun Wang, Yun Deng, Lu-Gui Qiu
Venue: Symmetry
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil visible–near-infrared (Vis-NIR) spectroscopy enables rapid, high-throughput estimation of soil physicochemical properties, but complex deep learning models can increase computational cost and reproducibility burden. This study developed a lightweight full-spectrum embedding multilayer perceptron (LSE-MLP) for simultaneous prediction of soil pH, organic carbon (OC), and total nitrogen (N). LSE-MLP maps 4200-dimensional spectra into a compact, learnable representation and uses residual MLP modules for subsequent nonlinear regression. Using the LUCAS2009 dataset, LSE-MLP was compared with PLSR, SVR-RBF, 1D-CNN, ResNet-1D, and Transformer-1D under a POINT_ID-grouped cross-validation framework. Model performance was assessed using internal split sensitivity analysis, ablation experiments, multi-random-seed analysis, and an independent blind test set. On this test set, LSE-MLP achieved R2 values of 0.9437, 0.9519, and 0.9363 for pH, OC, and N, respectively. Its computational efficiency was primarily reflected in low floating-point operations (FLOPs) and fast batch CPU inference, rather than the smallest parameter count or model storage footprint. Supplementary experiments indicated that band compression reduced input dimensionality with limited performance loss, whereas direct transfer of frozen LUCAS-trained models to KSSL and AUT showed substantial performance degradation associated with spectral and target-distribution differences. Overall, LSE-MLP provides a practical and computationally efficient option for soil property prediction using Vis-NIR spectroscopy under the evaluated conditions, while reliable cross-dataset transfer requires further investigation.
Soil carbon storage and stability controlled by site properties more than forest management in a humid boreal forestCORE3
Authors: Véronique Rouleau, É. Thiffault, Jérôme Laganière, D. Paré, C. Martineau
Venue: Soil Science Society of America Journal
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soils of managed boreal forests are a significant carbon (C) reservoir, and maintaining or increasing their ability to store C could play an important role for climate change mitigation. We investigated the effects of forest logging through clearcut and partial cut compared to forest conservation on total soil C stocks, particulate organic matter, and mineral‐associated organic matter (MAOM) physical fractions down to 55 cm in the mineral soil in a humid boreal balsam fir forest of Eastern Canada. We analyzed the variables that best explained the observed variations in soil C stocks, including topographic variables, deadwood, and soil physicochemical properties. Our results showed that harvesting treatments had no significant effect on soil C stocks for the entire soil profile. The variables that contributed most to explaining variations in soil C stocks for the FH horizons were exchangeable manganese, C stocks in buried wood, aboveground woody debris and mosses, slope inclination, and pH; pH, metal oxide, and rock content for mineral horizon C stocks; and pH, metal oxide content, and exchangeable iron for the MAOM fraction of mineral horizons. This study highlighted the complex and indirect role of deadwood and the significant influence of local site and soil properties (mainly metal oxide content) on soil C stocks in managed boreal forests.
Study on rhizosphere soil bacterial communities of dominant plant species in the island forest of Dachen Island, China based on high-throughput sequencingCORE3
Authors: Shu-Xu Yue, Zhen-Hua Yang, Hai-Sheng Chen
Venue: 14th Annual International Conference on Material Science and Environmental Engineering

Abstract
In the island forest vegetation of Dachen Island, Casuarina equisetifolia, Ligustrum lucidum, Miscanthus floridulus, and Melia azedarach are the four dominant plant species with the largest distribution areas. In this study, high-throughput sequencing technology was used to investigate the structure and diversity of rhizosphere soil bacterial communities associated with these four plant species, and correlation analyses were conducted in combination with rhizosphere soil nutrient contents. The results showed that the rhizosphere soils of the four dominant plant species in the island forest of Dachen Island harbored rich bacterial compositions. A total of 40 phyla, 110 classes, 198 orders, 324 families, and 641 genera of soil bacteria were identified across all soil samples. The highest bacterial diversity index was observed in the rhizosphere soil of Melia azedarach, while the lowest was found in the rhizosphere soil of Casuarina equisetifolia. The highest bacterial richness index occurred in the rhizosphere soil of Ligustrum lucidum, whereas the lowest richness index was recorded in the rhizosphere soil of Melia azedarach. A high proportion of operational taxonomic units (OTUs) was shared among the rhizosphere soils of different plants, while the proportion of plant-specific OTUs was relatively low. Among the four plant species, Casuarina equisetifolia had the highest proportion of unique OTUs in its rhizosphere soil, whereas Miscanthus floridulus had the lowest. Proteobacteria, Acidobacteria, Actinobacteria, Bacteroidetes, Verrucomicrobia, Planctomycetes, and Firmicutes were the dominant bacterial phyla in the rhizosphere soils of vegetation in this region. The rhizosphere soil bacterial communities of the four plant species showed similar structures at the phylum and class levels, although significant differences were observed in the relative abundances of some bacterial taxa. At the phylum level, the bacterial community compositions in the rhizosphere soils of Melia azedarach and Miscanthus floridulus were similar, whereas that of Casuarina equisetifolia differed markedly from those of the other three plant species. Soil total nitrogen, available potassium, organic matter content, soil moisture, and soil pH were the main factors influencing bacterial diversity indices and dominant bacterial communities in the rhizosphere soils of the constructive plant species. These results provide a scientific basis for exploring the use of soil microorganisms in vegetation restoration and reconstruction of seabird island ecosystems.

Abstract
Experimental studies on the effect of industrial effluent contaminated clayey soil and the impact of charcoal as barrier material was investigated. The physico-chemical properties of the effluent were characterized, and soil parameters (pH, particle size distribution, consistency limits, and hydraulic conductivity) were determined before and after four-weeks of effluent-soil interaction. Venue: Vadose Zone Journal
Published: 2026-10-30

Abstract
Soil infiltration is a major component of the terrestrial hydrological cycle related to land management practices within agroecosystems. This study examined the nexus between field‐measured average infiltration rates (AIRs) and key soil health properties determined via the Comprehensive Assessment of Soil Health. Physical soil health properties analyzed included particle size, water‐stable aggregates, available water capacity (AWC), penetration resistance (PR), and soil organic carbon as an additive biogeochemical indicator. The analysis covered 10 sites with diverse land uses at Ohio State University's Grace Drake Agricultural Laboratory, including organic and conventional croplands, forests, pastures, and hay fields. Results indicated that AIR was significantly correlated ( r = −0.87) with the degree of soil compaction as determined by PR. Among croplands, certified organic management showed the highest infiltration rates, likely due to macropores developed by soil organic matter accumulation, while the conventional corn–soybean rotation had the lowest infiltration rates induced by soil compaction issues. Forested sites had lower infiltration rates compared to pasture and cropland, presumably due to higher initial soil water content and larger root mass impeding the infiltration process. Additionally, a pedotransfer function was established using a multiple linear regression model based on four soil properties (PR, sand, clay, and AWC). This model provides preliminary connections between multiple physical soil health properties and infiltration rates across the landscape mosaic for this and similar research locations.

Authors: Akram Bayoumi, Megan L. Hill, A. Berbille, Ashutosh Rana, Mobina Masdari...
Venue: Langmuir
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs
Abstract
Electrogenerated chitosan films, commonly described as electrodeposited, underpin a broad class of electrochemical biosensors, but they suffer from irreproducibility and film-to-film heterogeneity. Film deposition necessitates an electrogenerated alkaline environment near the electrode surface, and the alkalinity can be obtained through either water electrolysis or the reduction of benzoquinone at milder potentials. Classically, films are characterized by electrochemical measurements, which lack spatial resolution, as well as by optical, force, and electron micrographs, which do not detail the chemical microenvironment within the films with appropriate spatiotemporal resolution. Here, we introduce electrochemiluminescence (ECL) microscopy via luminol/H2O2 to probe film homogeneity, and mass transport within the film, owing to the diffusive nature of molecules involved in the ECL mechanism. Because ECL emission requires luminol to reach the electrode, the film locally suppresses the emission, generating negative contrast that reports local differences in electrode access across the entire electrode surface. ECL microscopy resolves the comparatively uniform response of films obtained via benzoquinone-mediated processes and the pronounced heterogeneity of one obtained via the hydrogen-evolution pathway. Because benzoquinone retained in the chitosan film quenches the ECL signal, we demonstrate that a reductive potential step enhances the ECL signal by 13.2 ± 2.6-fold. This potential step allowed us to differentiate the quenching of the ECL signal resulting from hindered mass transport to that caused by chemical quenchers retained in the film, offering previously unreported mass transport insights. Our work extends ECL imaging to the characterization of continuous, hydrated polymer coatings, allowing one to carefully characterize and control the quality of chitosan-modified electrodes in real time.

Abstract
Desulfurization ash is a solid waste generated during the flue gas desulfurization process in the steel industry. Currently, if left untreated, it can pose environmental hazards. The desulfurization ash from semi-dry sintering (SDDA) in this study was used as the primary raw material to prepare a composite stabilizer suitable for soft soil stabilization. The stabilizer formulations with varying SDDA content were designed, and the engineering performance and ecological safety of the stabilized soft soils were comprehensively evaluated. This study provided a feasible technical approach for the large-scale resource utilization of semi-dry desulfurization ash.

Published: 2026-10-30

Abstract
The behavior of ions at the electric double layer interfacial region between solid and liquid phases is well known in surface and material science. However, this is not fully the case in soil science, where the electric double layer develops due to the inherent complexity and diversity of negatively charged soil organic matter molecules that sorb at the mineral surface. This lack of knowledge opens questions regarding the interaction mechanisms at the molecular scale that lead to the stabilization of such soil building blocks, the so-called organo-mineral aggregates. By using classical molecular dynamics simulations, we studied the interactions between Kaolinite and four standardized soil organic matter samples with different aromatic and carboxylic content and Ca2+ cations clustering OM molecules and stabilizing them to the surface. We assessed the influence of (i) organic content, (ii) water content, and (iii) competing Cl− ions. Cluster aggregates are favored in soils with a high aliphatic fraction at low-water content, whereas aggregation is favored in soils with high aromatic fraction at high-water content and in the presence of Cl− ions. Moreover, samples with a low acid fraction favor clustering due to non-bonded interactions and hydrogen bonds. All of these environments are reflected as unique charge-density “fingerprints” and surface coverage for each OM sample. This work represents a step towards the characterization and study of natural soils tunable to specific local conditions.
