Ammonia-oxidizing bacteria dominate active ammonia oxidation during nitrogen fertilizer-induced acidification of a maize-cultivated black soilCORE8
Authors: Yu-Xiang Song, Xiang-Xin Sun, Yuan-Feng Cai, Wei-Wei Cao, Yu-Fang Wang...
Venue: ISME Communications
Published: 2026-10-14
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Nitrogen fertilization accelerates soil acidification by stimulating ammonia oxidation, yet the dominant active ammonia oxidizers and their acid-adaptation strategies remain unresolved. We combined DNA stable isotope probing (DNA-SIP) with enrichment cultivation, metagenomics, and metatranscriptomics to identify active ammonia oxidizers and characterize acid-tolerance responses in a maize-cultivated black soil from Northeast China. Repeated urea and NH4Cl additions stimulated nitrification and acidified the soil, with pH decreasing from 7.35 to 4.73 under NH4Cl addition. DNA-SIP showed that ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB), rather than ammonia-oxidizing archaea (AOA), dominated active ammonia oxidation during acidification. Phylogenetic analysis of amoA sequences from isotope-labelled higher-density DNA fractions identified Nitrosospira-related D11 and D12 clusters among active AOB. An acid-tolerant enrichment culture retained nitrification activity at pH 5.0 and yielded a D11-affiliated Nitrosospira MAG, designated Nitrosospira sp. NT. Its amoA sequence showed 98.2% nucleotide identity to a SIP-derived D11 amoA sequence, linking the enrichment-derived population to an active soil D11 cluster. Under acidic conditions, the enrichment culture showed pronounced aggregation with increased fungal occurrence, suggesting that fungal occurrence may be associated with the aggregated phenotype. Genomic and transcriptomic analyses indicated coordinated responses involving substrate acquisition and acid buffering, respiratory energy metabolism, intracellular pH homeostasis, envelope stress responses, and candidate systems potentially related to surface-associated growth and stress responses. These findings link community-level SIP evidence with genomic and transcriptional insights from an enrichment-derived representative of an active D11 cluster and show that acid-tolerant AOB can dominate active ammonia oxidation and sustain nitrification during nitrogen fertilizer-induced acidification in the black soil examined.
Influence of Carbon-Input Level on Cadmium Immobilisation by Wheat Straw-Derived Biochar in Contaminated SoilCORE7

Venue: Sustainability
Published: 2026-10-14
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study evaluated how wheat-straw input modifies the effects of 2% (w/w) wheat straw-derived biochar on Cd availability, soil physicochemical properties, enzyme activities, N transformation and microbial communities during a 49-day incubation. Combining 2% biochar with 6% straw reduced soil-available Cd by 7.14–14.28% relative to the biochar-only treatment, whereas increasing straw input from 6% to 9% did not further improve Cd immobilisation. Compared with straw-only treatments, biochar-amended soils maintained higher pH and lower available Cd, indicating that biochar buffered straw-induced acidification and Cd mobilisation. High carbon input increased β-glucosidase activity and AOB-amoA abundance, which was consistent with enhanced nitrification-related processes and may indirectly influence Cd mobility through soil-pH dynamics. These effects were partly buffered by the alkalinity of biochar. Biochar alone supported the highest microbial alpha diversity, whereas increasing carbon input reduced community richness. The BS6 treatment also maintained relatively high microbial biomass during incubation. Soil pH, mineral N and enzyme activities were associated with a variation in microbial community structure. Overall, moderate wheat-straw input combined with biochar improved Cd immobilisation while maintaining microbial activity and soil biochemical stability, suggesting potential for the remediation of Cd-contaminated agricultural soils.

Authors: Reuben K. Bulungu Bulungu, F. Muyekho, Vitalis Kalor Ogemah, Y. G. Kidane, J. Omollo...
Venue: African Journal of Empirical Research
Published: 2026-10-14

Abstract
Poor soil fertility, characterized by soil acidity, low organic carbon and nutrient imbalances, constrains smallholder crop production in western Kenya. This exploratory study characterized soil-chemical changes between pre-planting and post-harvest sampling within a field experiment comprising sole crops and two- and three-component shrub–cereal–legume intercrops under rainfed, minimum-tillage and low-external-input management. The experiment comprised 12 treatments arranged in three blocks. However, soil subsamples collected in a zig-zag pattern across the treatment plots were composited within each block, resulting in an effective sample size of three paired block-level observations and precluding assessment of treatment-specific soil effects. After one cropping season, mean soil pH changed from 4.63 to 5.20 and soil organic carbon from 1.09% to 1.78%. Available phosphorus changed from 11.37 to 17.07 mg kg⁻¹ and exchangeable potassium from 95.75 to 132.99 mg kg⁻¹, whereas total nitrogen, exchangeable calcium and EDTA-extractable zinc decreased numerically. None of the paired differences was statistically significant at p < 0.05, and all 95% confidence intervals included zero, reflecting substantial uncertainty associated with the small sample. Because no independently sampled temporal reference was included, the findings represent experiment-wide temporal associations rather than treatment-specific effects. Nevertheless, the contrasting estimates demonstrate why regenerative performance should be evaluated across multiple soil indicators rather than inferred from a single property. They provide preliminary evidence of short-term soil dynamics and generate hypotheses concerning soil acidity, carbon cycling, nutrient redistribution and adaptive nutrient stewardship. Treatment-specific sampling, appropriate temporal controls, greater replication, multi-season monitoring, plant-tissue analysis, nutrient budgets and integrated field-based soil-health assessments are required to test the observed patterns and evaluate the regenerative potential of shrub–cereal–legume intercropping.
Darkroom-Free pH Planar Optode via Image Color Correction for Portable Imaging of Extreme AcidityCORE5
Authors: Hai-Tao Sun, Chao Han, Hai-Yi Chen, Yu Sun, Yu-Ling Han...
Venue: Analytical Chemistry
Published: 2026-10-14
Abs
Abstract
Classical pH planar optodes (POs) rely on a darkroom to reduce analytical interference from ambient light fluctuations, a requirement that severely limits portability and field applicability. To overcome this, we report a darkroom-free PO strategy integrating three key components: (i) a pixel-level image color correction (CC) technique to compensate for lighting and device variations; (ii) a robust quantitative parameter Huev, which outperforms conventional RGB ratios in stability against exposure and photobleaching; and (iii) a Congo red-nylon sensing film with a multicolor reflective-light response, tailored to the requirements of both the CC workflow and the Huev parameter. The as-prepared PO-sensing film exhibits a blue-to-red color transition in the extremely acidic pH range of 0.5−1.5. While a pH imaging method covering this range was previously reported, our method shows markedly improved sensitivity in this range. Following CC, the mean pH analytical error for aqueous solutions under varying light sources and imaging devices was reduced to 0.087 ± 0.055, compared with 0.504 ± 0.420 without CC. In soil pH imaging, the corrected pH maps acquired under multiple lighting and camera conditions exhibit high consistency, with maximal deviations in pH depth profiles of ∼0.2 pH units. Only a smartphone, a color card, and a white light source (e.g., sunlight) are required to construct the imaging system, dramatically lowering the cost and technical barriers for field deployment. This work not only achieves high-sensitivity PO for extremely acidity detection but also provides a generalizable strategy for developing darkroom-free PO for other pH intervals and target analytes.
Dynamics of Soil Microbial Biomass Carbon and Total Nitrogen Under Different Land Use in Indo-Myanmar Hotspot Region, NagalandCORE5
Authors: Gyati Yam, Vieneite-O Koza, O. P. Tripathi, A. A. A. Mohamed
Venue: Journal of Landscape Ecology
Published: 2026-10-14

Abstract
Abstract Soil microbial biomass and nitrogen, a vital nutrient pool for ecosystem nutrient cycling is affected by several factors including climate, edaphic, and land-use change. Focusing on Phek, India which lies within the Indo-Myanmar Biodiversity Hotspot, this study investigates how land use system (forest, permanent agriculture, horticulture, and jhum cultivation) season and soil depth influence soil microbial biomass carbon (MBC) and total nitrogen (TN). Soil samples were collected from three depths up to 30 cm across four seasons. Soil physical and chemical properties varied significantly with season, land-use and soil depth (p < 0.001), with forest soils maintaining the highest water holding capacity and lowest bulk density. In contrast, agricultural soils exhibited significant compaction (BD 1.0–1.5 g cm⁻³). Forest soils recorded highest MBC (~520 µg g⁻¹) in the topsoil (0–10 cm), whereas agricultural soils reached a minimum of 40–50 µg g⁻¹ at depth (20–30 cm). TN stocks were highest in surface Jhum soils during winter (~310 Mg ha⁻¹) but declined drastically with depth. Statistical analysis revealed that SOC, TN, and MBC are strongly positively correlated, while BD and soil temperature negatively affect microbial pools. The findings highlight that forest-to-agriculture conversion significantly degrades microbial-driven nutrient cycling, emphasizing the need for sustainable fallow management and reduced disturbance to preserve soil health in this ecologically sensitive landscape.

Abstract
Chicken manure is widely used in vegetable production, yet its concurrent effects on soil nutrient status, heavy-metal behavior, and soil bacterial communities remain insufficiently understood. A field experiment was conducted to evaluate five chicken-manure application rates (0, 7.5, 15, 30, and 60 t ha−1) in a pakchoi production system. Bacterial communities were characterized by 16S rRNA gene sequencing. Compared with the control, chicken manure significantly increased soil organic carbon, total nitrogen, available nitrogen, total phosphorus, and available phosphorus, whereas the two highest rates significantly decreased soil pH (P < 0.05). Only the highest rate significantly increased pakchoi aboveground fresh weight, whereas soluble sugar content increased significantly at 15–60 t ha−1 (P < 0.05). Chicken manure significantly increased total soil Cd, Cr, Cu, and Zn concentrations (P < 0.05), with application-rate-dependent increases in their TCLP-extractable fractions (P < 0.05 or P < 0.01), whereas Pb remained unchanged (P > 0.05). In edible tissues, the two highest rates significantly increased Cd and Zn concentrations, and the highest rate increased Cr and Cu concentrations (P < 0.05), although all concentrations remained below the applicable food-safety limits. Bacterial richness and diversity increased significantly under selected treatments (P < 0.05). Community analyses identified TN, Zn, and Cd as major environmental drivers, with heavy metals explaining more variation in bacterial communities than soil nutrients. These findings reveal a rate-dependent trade-off between improved soil fertility and increased heavy-metal accumulation, emphasizing the need to control application rates for the sustainable and safe recycling of manure.

Abstract
Phosphorus (P) fertiliser application significantly influences soil quality by modifying key physicochemical attributes and regulating P dynamics, which are essential for sustaining nutrient supply and crop productivity. A field experiment was conducted from July 2021 to November 2021 using a randomised block design (RBD) comprising eleven treatments with three replications to evaluate the impact of different P sources and levels on soil properties and P fractions after pearl millet harvest. Implementation of different nutrient management practices decreased the soil pH (p = 0.001) as compared to initial value, while soil electrical conductivity (EC) increased (p = 0.069). Integrated application of farmyard manure (FYM) + single super phosphate (SSP) showed the highest increases in available nitrogen (N), P and sulphur (S), as well as significant increases in key inorganic P fractions (saloid-P (Sa-P), aluminum-phosphorus (Al-P), iron-phosphorus (Fe-P), calcium-phosphorus (Ca-P), total inorganic phosphorus (Tpi) and total-P) and biological yield (114.36 q ha-1) compared to control. However, application of FYM + di-ammonium phosphate (DAP) showed highest increase in available potassium (K) (p = 0.001). In control plots, inorganic P fractions followed the order Ca-P (66.5 %) > Fe-P (19.02 %) > Al-P (11.83 %) > Sa-P (2.61 %). Overall integrating 7.5 t FYM ha-1 along with SSP superiorly modulates P transformation and availability in semi-arid soils compared to chemical fertilisers alone. These findings demonstrate that integrated nutrient management (INM) is vital for optimising P efficiency, restoring soil health and ensuring sustainable agricultural productivity in semi-arid regions.

Abstract
Cover cropping is an essential conservation management practice to mitigate overwinter nitrogen leaching and soil degradation in hemiboreal agroecosystems. However, short-term nitrogen dynamics and agrochemical responses across contrasting soil types during the winter-fallow period remain under-quantified. This study evaluated the six-month overwintering impact of a four-species cover crop mixture (Helianthus annuus, Raphanus sativus, Fagopyrum esculentum, and Vicia sativa) on topsoil nitrogen pools and related agrochemical properties in Northern Lithuania. The research was conducted on a commercial agricultural scale (73 ha), utilizing a paired design across four spatially replicated mineral soil fields (medium-heavy loam Cambisols, n = 4) and an exploratory, unreplicated observational zone in an organic soil (Histosol, n = 1). Due to regulatory restrictions prohibiting bare fallow, the observed agrochemical shifts represent the combined influence of cover crop biological activity and natural seasonal hemiboreal weathering, rather than isolated causal mechanisms. Despite an extended autumn vegetation period facilitating substantial aboveground fresh biomass accumulation (mean 549 ± 187 g m−2), the mineral Cambisols exhibited pronounced chemical inertia. Paired-sample analyses revealed no statistically significant changes in total nitrogen, total organic matter, pH, mobile phosphorus, or water-extractable organic carbon over the overwintering period (p > 0.05). Given the high risk of winter leaching, the stability of the total nitrogen pool (−7.08%, p = 0.333) demonstrates the efficacy of the combined cover crop and weathering system in retaining residual nitrogen. A non-significant trend toward mobile potassium accumulation (+9.20%, p = 0.063) was also observed, which is presented strictly as an unverified exploratory hypothesis regarding biological nutrient pumping. In contrast, exploratory observations in the Histosol zone indicated rapid turnover, characterized by numerical surges in total nitrogen (+42.7%) and water-extractable organic carbon (+50.3%). These findings reflect observed changes during the overwintering cover-cropping period, demonstrating that multi-species mixtures assist in maintaining baseline soil chemical equilibria in well-buffered mineral soils, while highlighting potential, unverified organic matter turnover trends in organic Histosols.

Abstract
Soil microbes are crucial in maintaining soil and plant health, underpinning agronomic productivity. This study investigates antimicrobial copper- and zinc-laden cow and pig slurry applications on soil microbial community structures in an approximately 50-year long-term slurry experiment on a permanent grassland located in County Down, Northern Ireland. Illumina and PacBio rRNA sequencing (analysing 16S and ITS genera) were used to investigate soil samples (to 15 cm depth) from the experiment’s treatments. Slurry type and application rate were found to alter the soil microbial community structure. The highest slurry application rate resulted in the greatest log-2-fold changes in most relative abundances. Soil pH (ranging from ~6.5 to ~5.8) and carbon content (ranging from ~7% to ~5%) were also significantly elevated in the highest slurry treatments relative to the non-amended control. Soil pH, copper, and zinc concentrations were found to be significant key drivers of soil microbial community shifts (permutation test, p  < 0.001). More specifically, copper primarily influenced bacterial (16S; F  ≈ 25) and zinc has the greatest influence on fungal (ITS; F  ≈ 13) genera. A key finding was that these two community types responded differently to slurry management: Canonical analysis of principal components showed 16S community structure diverged by both slurry type and application rate, whereas ITS community structure was distinguished by application rate alone. Despite this, there is no sign that it has detrimental impacts on grass yield, and indeed, some promoted microbes ( Nitrospira spp.) were associated with increased productivity. However, the potentially pathogenic organism (i.e., Fusarium spp.) observed in all treatments is of concern, given the contaminating mycotoxins they produce. The findings illustrate soil microbial responses to sustained and settled long-term organic-amended soils that could not be observed without the long-term nature of this experiment. These findings indicate that long-term Cu and Zn-loaded slurry application restructures the soil microbial community in significant ways with implications for antimicrobial resistance selection and a hypothesised link to mycotoxin-related food safety for further investigation, within a One Health framework linking soil, plant, animal, and human health.

Abstract
This study evaluated the effects of chemical and organic fertilization on aboveground biomass and soil properties across three natural grassland regions in the middle section of the Greater Khingan Mountains. Field experiments were conducted in Keyouqian Banner, Keyouzhong Banner, and Zhalaite Banner using three treatments: fencing enclosure (control), fencing enclosure + chemical fertilizer, and fencing enclosure + organic fertilizer, each with three biological replicates. Both chemical and organic fertilization significantly increased aboveground biomass by 30.41% and 24.56%, respectively (p < 0.05), with no significant difference between treatments. Chemical fertilization significantly reduced soil available phosphorus by 9.01% (p < 0.05), whereas organic fertilization had no significant effect on the measured soil properties (p > 0.05). Aboveground biomass and soil properties differed among the three regions, and these differences were associated with variations in soil pH and background soil conditions. These findings indicate that fertilization effectively enhanced grassland productivity, whereas biomass and soil responses varied among regions with different soil conditions, highlighting the importance of considering regional soil characteristics when interpreting fertilization responses and developing grassland management strategies.

Abstract
Soil chemical composition is a key indicator of soil fertility, yet conventional physicochemical analysis is time-consuming and costly. Although near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy enables rapid, non-destructive detection, traditional linear models struggle with the non-linear nature of high-dimensional spectral data, and deep-learning models often suffer from a "black-box" problem. A one-dimensional residual convolutional neural network (1D-Res-CNN) was constructed, and the output feature maps of intermediate convolutional layers were visualized to reveal how the model learns spectral features. One-dimensional spectral sequences were mapped into twodimensional images via Gramian Angular Summation Field (GASF) and Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT); after systematically comparing encoding performance across spectral sub-intervals, the optimal range was selected to feed a two-dimensional residual CNN (2D-Res-CNN) regression model. Using SNV-preprocessed Fourier-transform NIR spectra and an SPXY-partitioned dataset, the proposed framework was benchmarked against partial least squares regression (PLSR), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), and random forest (RF). First-layer kernels in the 1D-Res-CNN automatically captured spectral features corresponding to functional-group absorption peaks, supporting the physical interpretability of feature extraction. GASF-encoded images in the 5880~4000 cm -1 range exhibited the highest feature density and outperformed CWT encoding. The 2D-Res-CNN achieved prediction-set R p 2 of 0.87, 0.91, 0.91, 0.93, and 0.88 for soil pH, labile organic carbon, total nitrogen, organic matter, and available phosphorus, respectively, with lower RMSEP than both the 1D-CNN and PLSR. By enhancing spectral feature expression through visual encoding and providing preliminary insight into model behavior via layer-wise feature-map visualization, this work offers an interpretable technical strategy for rapid quantitative analysis of soil chemical composition by NIR spectroscopy.
Rotation system reshapes soil profile environmental gradients to reorganize bacterial communities and decouple potential nitrogen cycling functionsCORE3

Abstract
Cropping systems reshape soil profile environmental gradients, thereby driving the vertical reorganization of bacterial communities and regulating the coupling between microbial communities and predicted nitrogen-cycling (N-cycling) potential. This study integrated soil chemical analyses, 16S rRNA gene sequencing, co-occurrence network analysis, PICRUSt2-based functional prediction, and structural equation modeling (SEM) across three long-term cropping systems: unclaimed land (U), tobacco–maize rotation (R), and continuous tobacco cropping (C), sampled at three soil depths (0–20, 20–40, and 40–60 cm; n  = 3). Environmental drivers exhibited distinct vertical shifts, with readily available nutrients (AN, AK, and AP) dominating surface soils (0–20 cm), microbial biomass (MBC and MBN) governing subsurface soils (20–40 cm), and total nutrient pools (OM, TN, and TP) becoming increasingly important in deeper soils (40–60 cm). Rotation buffered vertical environmental changes, increased bacterial richness in the 0–40 cm profile, enriched nitrifying and nitrogen-fixing taxa (e.g., Nitrospira, Sphingomonas, and MND1), and maintained higher predicted N-cycling potential throughout the soil profile, accompanied by enhanced network modularity and robustness. In contrast, continuous cropping reduced surface soil pH, OM, MBC, and MBN (with MBN declining by>60%), promoted stress-associated taxa (e.g., Bacillus and Lactobacillus), and weakened network connectivity and robustness, particularly at 20–40 cm where network fragmentation was most evident. SEM revealed a stronger statistical coupling among soil environment, bacterial communities, microbial networks, and predicted N-cycling potential under rotation (GOF > 0.7), whereas continuous cropping showed weakened associations among these components. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that crop rotation promotes the stability of soil microbial ecological functions by preserving environmental gradient continuity and enhancing network resilience, whereas continuous cropping disrupts microbial network organization and weakens the linkage between resource availability and predicted functional potential. This study highlights the importance of maintaining multiscale environmental and microbial connectivity for improving the sustainability of agricultural soil ecosystems.

Nonlinear threshold responses of agroecosystem water use efficiency to increasing vapor pressure deficitECO1
Authors: You-Li Ma, Ming-Chang Wang, Xun-Hua Zheng, Hong-Chao Fan, Feng-Yan Wang
Venue: Ecological Processes
Published: 2026-10-14
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Increasing vapor pressure deficit (VPD) alters ecosystem carbon and water exchange, but agroecosystem water use efficiency (WUE) may respond nonlinearly to rising atmospheric evaporative demand. The VPD levels at which these nonlinear transitions occur, their spatial consistency, and the factors associated with spatial variation in breakpoint magnitude remain poorly understood. We therefore examined nonlinear WUE responses to increasing VPD and assessed how breakpoint magnitude varied across croplands in relation to environmental and agricultural characteristics. Using multisource observations from 111 sub-basins of the Songhua River Basin during 2002–2024 and independent eddy covariance observations from cropland sites, we identified a nonlinear WUE–VPD relationship. The first derivative of the generalized additive model fit indicated a point of maximum WUE sensitivity to VPD at approximately 0.27 kPa, whereas piecewise linear fitting identified the primary basin-scale breakpoint at approximately 0.41 kPa. Repeated subsampling indicated that the basin-scale breakpoint was robust to sampling variation, while local breakpoint estimates varied across sub-basins. Statistically supported breakpoints were detected in 59 of the 111 sub-basins, and site-level screening retained breakpoint estimates for 20 of the 28 evaluated cropland flux sites. The mean breakpoint estimate across the 20 retained flux sites was approximately 0.43 kPa. Sub-basin breakpoint magnitude was negatively associated with leaf area index, soil bulk density, and mean growing-season precipitation total, and positively associated with C4 crop proportion. Above the basin-scale breakpoint, the sensitivity of gross primary productivity to VPD increased significantly, whereas the sensitivity of evapotranspiration showed no significant change, indicating an asymmetric carbon–water response. At the retained flux sites, higher Bowen ratios and lower evaporative fractions above the site-specific breakpoints were consistent with a relative shift in energy partitioning toward sensible heat. The approximately 0.41-kPa breakpoint characterizes the basin-scale WUE response to increasing VPD but does not represent a universal local threshold. Spatial variation in breakpoint magnitude was associated with climate, vegetation structure, soil properties, and crop composition, although the observational design precludes direct causal attribution to individual management factors. These findings clarify spatial variation in nonlinear agroecosystem WUE responses to increasing VPD and underscore the importance of local environmental context when interpreting carbon–water responses to atmospheric drying.
