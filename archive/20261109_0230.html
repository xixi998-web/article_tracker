OctreeSplat: Hierarchical Octree-Guided Feed-Forward Gaussian SplattingCORE8
Authors: Yi-Bin Zhao, Yi-Han Pan, Wen-Li Yang, Jun Nan, Jian-Jun Yi
Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-11-02
Abs
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has shown considerable potential for novel view synthesis (NVS) and 3D reconstruction. Feed-forward methods avoid costly per-scene optimization, but fixed image or spatial resolutions limit their ability to allocate primitives according to local complexity. We present OctreeSplat, a hierarchical octree-guided feed-forward Gaussian splatting. OctreeSplat extracts multi-view features and geometric priors from a frozen visual foundation model (VFM), constructs a hierarchical octree, and predicts densification scores. The resulting representation allocates Gaussian primitives at spatially varying densities according to local complexity. Stochastic solids volume rendering improves depth rendering, strengthens geometric supervision, and supports surface reconstruction. When camera parameters are available, an optional test-time optimization (TTO) module improves agreement between predicted and ground-truth parameters. Training updates only 76 million additional parameters and requires 35 GPU-hours. Experiments on Replica, TUM, Tanks and Temples, and DTU demonstrate competitive RGB view synthesis, depth synthesis, and surface reconstruction. OctreeSplat reduces the number of Gaussian primitives while maintaining high reconstruction quality.

Abstract
Depth perception is fundamental for autonomous navigation, industrial inspection, and mixed-reality systems. It often operates under strict constraints on power and form factor. In such cases, monocular depth estimation (MDE) is often used. Even as recent advances continue to improve monocular depth quality, conventional frame-based MDE systems remain challenged by fast motion and high-dynamic-range scenes. This work introduces eSharp, an MDE framework that addresses these problems using an event camera. eSharp repurposes pretrained diffusion priors for event-only MDE and produces accurate metric depth with unparalleled sharpness on real data. Real-world event datasets provide only sparse LiDAR depth maps for ground-truth supervision, making dense prediction challenging. We address this limitation through cross-modal distillation, using LiDAR-calibrated dense pseudo-labels generated from a spatially co-registered frame camera to train eSharp. Experiments show that eSharp outperforms prior event-to-depth baselines in both metric-depth accuracy and spatial fidelity, establishing diffusion models as a promising foundation for event-driven 3D perception.
Local-Global Joint Modeling for Point Cloud Super-Resolution Guided by Multi-Scale Spatial PriorsCORE5
Authors: Wang Yang, Wen-Yi Ge
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-11-02
Abs
Abstract
Range-image-based point cloud super-resolution provides an effective way to reconstruct sparse outdoor point clouds by projecting low-resolution LiDAR scans into a regular 2D representation. However, current methods still face three challenges. First, range images exhibit pronounced vertical nonuniformity, with sparse observations in distant regions and denser observations in nearby regions. Some cross-scale fusion mechanisms do not account for the varying reliability of features at different vertical positions, allowing invalid values and noise from distant sparse regions to propagate into the decoder. Second, a single convolutional or sequence-modeling architecture often struggles to preserve both local high-frequency geometric details and global long-range dependencies, resulting in over-smoothed reconstructions. Third, smooth transition depths near foreground-background boundaries can produce spike artifacts when the 2D range image is back-projected into 3D. To address these issues, we propose a point cloud super-resolution method based on multi-scale spatial priors and joint local-global modeling. The design comprises a spatially gated fusion module, a multi-granularity local-global cooperative perception module, and 2D median-residual denoising. Experiments on KITTI-360 and nuScenes demonstrate effective reconstruction, with IoU scores of 0.4703 and 0.3593, respectively, supporting the feasibility of the proposed method for range-image super-resolution.
SCARF: A Scene-Adaptive Depth-Guided G-3DGS Encoder Accelerator with Semantic Reuse and Fused DataflowCORE5
Authors: Zi-Rui Ma, Zhi-Hua Fan, Hai-Bin Wu, Wen-Xing Li, Fu-Lin Zhang...
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-11-02
Abs
Abstract
Depth-guided, cost-volume-based Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (G-3DGS) encoders reconstruct scenes as sets of Gaussian primitives directly from multi-view images without requiring per-scene optimization, thereby facilitating feed-forward novel-view synthesis on edge computing platforms. However, their shared encoder architecture presents two primary bottlenecks. First, the per-pixel depth search disregards semantic similarity within the 2D feature space, causing depth search operations to account for over 65% of memory traffic due to redundant exhaustive candidate scans. Second, the sequential pipeline of depth prediction followed by Gaussian generation neglects local 3D geometric continuity, resulting in more than 52% of generated Gaussians being redundant prior to instantiation. In this paper, we introduce SCARF, a dataflow-co-designed accelerator that aligns each bottleneck with a corresponding scene-level regularity. To mitigate the first bottleneck, Feature Similarity Depth Reuse reconceptualizes depth search as a problem of semantic locality: depth consistency is determined by affinity in feature space rather than physical address proximity. It transforms exhaustive per-pixel depth searches into anchor-centered local searches. To overcome the second bottleneck, Scene-Adaptive Early Sparsification disrupts the conventional serial depth-to-materialization boundary by probing each tile to infer local scene regularity. It then selectively activates only the minimally sufficient generation pathway, eliminating redundant Gaussian computations before primitive instantiation. On a 27.8 square millimeter 28 nm post-layout design, SCARF delivers a 2.94x geometric-mean end-to-end speedup with maximum PSNR degradation below 0.072%, 2.23 inferences per joule, and 34.5x GPU energy efficiency.
Flex-RSI: Benchmarking Recursive Self-Improvement of Frontier AgentsCORE4
Authors: Jingdong Zhang, Jionghao Wang, Zhengming Yu, Yancheng Cai, Wei Chow...
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Frontier language models are increasingly deployed as agents, and how well an agent performs depends not only on its model but also on its harness: the prompts, tools, memory, and control loop through which the model acts. When an agent is applied to a new problem, a natural question is whether it can use the feedback of the task to improve the way it works, and not merely its answer. Most existing benchmarks keep the agent's harness fixed and score the solution it produces, or target large-scale model training. We introduce Flex-RSI, a benchmark for the recursive self-improvement of agents on vision and robotics tasks under a fixed budget. In each round, an executor improves the task solution, which is kept only if it scores higher on held-out validation data, and an improver then revises the harness, including the executor and itself. Every run starts from the same general-purpose harness, and the final solution is scored on hidden test data. Evaluating closed-source and open-weight models on tasks spanning perception, reasoning, robot action, 3D reconstruction, visual creation, and infrastructure, we find that self-improvement helps most when the agent builds its own solution, that feedback recording only success or failure holds back open-weight models, and that open-weight models fall furthest behind where outputs must pass strict checks of physical stability or exact correctness. Paired runs further show that editing the harness itself contributes to the gains.
What Limits mmWave Human-State Sensing for Robot Perception?CORE4
Authors: Shuntian Zheng, Guangming Wang, Hongyang He, Shuai He, Yu Guan
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Assistive robots and search-and-rescue platforms require reliable human state sensing in smoke, darkness, and privacy-sensitive environments, where cameras and LiDAR may be unreliable Millimeter-wave radar offers an alternative that works without ambient light, penetrates smoke, and does not capture visual appearance. However, the compact arrays that fit robot size, power, and cost budgets have limited apertures, producing joint-localization errors on the order of decimeters at a few meters, large enough to compromise the safety margins and tracking accuracy a robot depends on. Existing pipelines reduce pose error through frame fusion, multiple viewpoints, return selection, or stronger estimators, but examine one technique at a time, without relating its effect to a shared measurement limit or separating gains from added information from gains from estimator completion. We address this gap with a framework that models the human and environment as one time-varying scattering field and states a Cramer-Rao type error floor for joint localization as a function of bandwidth, aperture, signal-to-noise ratio, fused frame count, phase coherence, and visibility of retained returns. We then use existing frame fusion, multi viewpoint, return selection, and pose estimation methods as matched probes of this floor rather than as competing pipelines. Under the evaluated conditions, experiments on three datasets and a live deployment show these strategies move joint localization error in the predicted directions, for example lowering mean per-joint position error from about 97 to 65 mm with a second viewpoint, while a stronger estimator mainly lowers overall error by completing joints the measurement cannot resolve. These results give the robotics community a principled basis for deciding whether to prioritize observation design or estimator capacity when deploying reliable mmWave human sensing.
Dynamic GPU Voxel Grids and Voxel-sequential Processing in NanoVDBCORE4
Authors: Eftychios Sifakis, Francis Williams, Christopher Horvath, Rohan Sawhney, Mark Harris...

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce two fundamental advances to the NanoVDB data structure: dynamic GPU-native topological updates, and a voxel-sequential access paradigm that dramatically accelerates point-wise and stencil-based operators on sparse grids. NanoVDB's linearized memory layout has delivered strong GPU performance, and the Index-Grid concept introduced in the fVDB framework [Williams et al 2024] provided topology/data separation and optimal compactness of storage in sparse machine learning workloads. However, all these prior iterations of NanoVDB assume a static topology that has precluded use of this data structure in simulation and dynamic geometric processes. We eliminate this limitation by introducing a suite of dynamic topology operators -- morphological dilation, grid merging, pruning, and cross-resolution coarsening and refinement. These operators use bit-parallel algorithms to reconstruct grids containing hundreds of millions of voxels in just a few milliseconds. We also introduce a voxel-sequential access paradigm and data structure for GPU kernels operating on sparse data. We stream active voxels as a flat linear sequence, minimizing or eliminating the need for tree traversal in the process. This achieves compute density and regularity that allow even complex stencil-based operators to approach peak memory bandwidth, with minimal storage overhead. Together, these contributions remove the read-only constraint from NanoVDB, establishing it as a high-performance primary representation for dynamic GPU simulation and geometry manipulation. We evaluate the efficacy of our newly introduced operators in benchmarks sourced from applications such as 3D reconstruction, fluid simulation, and large-scale mesh voxelization.

Abstract
Recent robot systems combine wrist and fixed cameras, requiring camera-to-robot transforms for both. Most hand-eye calibration methods rely on targets or robot-body observations. Scene-based methods using 3D reconstruction require neither but address only wrist cameras. We introduce AnchorHEC, a targetless hand-eye calibration framework for wrist and fixed cameras in a single procedure using RGB images, forward kinematics (FK), and known camera intrinsics. We initialize both camera types using FK and a shared 3D foundation model reconstruction. FK-constrained wrist-image bundle adjustment reduces the impact of reconstructed pose and scale errors. If this refinement fails or retains too few inliers, we relax the constraints on potentially inaccurate FK poses and lens parameters. We use the refined wrist transform and FK to triangulate metric points and estimate fixed-camera poses. Across three backbones, calibration errors in simulation decrease by 86.4% in translation and 71.6% in rotation, averaged over both camera types, relative to reconstruction-based baselines. Extrapolation Matters: Sparse-Only Depth Completion Beyond LiDAR CoverageCORE3
Authors: Maximilian Jansen, Gasser Elazab, Praveen Kulkarni
Published: 2026-11-05
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Extrapolation beyond LiDAR coverage is an important yet under-evaluated challenge of depth completion. In real camera--LiDAR systems, partial sensor overlap, occlusions, and limited field coverage often leave large image regions weakly observed or completely unobserved by LiDAR. Thus, we investigate depth completion in this beyond-coverage regime using only sparse supervision from raw single-sweep LiDAR, without dense depth labels, LiDAR accumulation, or pseudo-LiDAR generation. Our approach leverages a frozen monocular depth foundation model, lightweight per-frame alignment to sparse LiDAR geometry, and a refinement module that fuses the aligned prior with sparse LiDAR anchors to predict dense metric depth. To regularize learning under raw sparse supervision, we introduce a high-frequency normal-consistency loss defined as the image-space gradient of the per-pixel normal cosine discrepancy. This term penalizes spatial variation in scalar angular error; it does not directly match changes in neighboring normal vectors. We further introduce an evaluation protocol that explicitly separates LiDAR-supported and beyond-coverage regions, revealing extrapolative behavior that conventional depth-completion metrics can obscure. Our method achieves strong depth accuracy beyond LiDAR coverage while remaining competitive in LiDAR-covered regions, providing a practical way to deploy monocular foundation priors in multi-sensor systems.

Venue: Applied Sciences

Abstract
Polarization 3D imaging has gained attention due to its simple hardware, low cost, and suitability for weakly textured and long-range scenes; however, its inherent 180° azimuth ambiguity restricts practical deployment. This paper proposes a monocular polarization 3D reconstruction method based on multi-criteria azimuth correction, which exploits only the physical and statistical information contained in the polarization images themselves, requiring no external priors or training data. Specifically, Stokes sign consistency is utilized as a physical validity gate, while local continuity and distribution concentration are combined to form a comprehensive scoring function to perform pixel-wise azimuth correction. Subsequently, Frankot–Chellappa frequency-domain integration and Poisson integration are separately employed to recover the 3D surface shape. Experiments on an indoor chair and an outdoor lighthouse demonstrate that the proposed method achieves improved reconstruction quality under both integration frameworks, effectively suppressing depth fluctuations in smooth regions and preserving sharp contours at edges. Quantitative profile fitting and ablation studies confirm its robustness, while its generalizability is validated within the two tested representative scenes.

Abstract
Autobiographical memory is not merely a record of past events but a dynamic reconstruction shaped by context, emotion, and social interaction. In this hypothesis paper, we propose that empathic interactions can transform autobiographical memory, altering memory's content, emotional tone, and integration into the self. We begin by outlining the social foundations of autobiographical memory and the neural systems involved in its transformation, focusing on the hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and amygdala. We then explore how empathic exchanges may lead to changes in memory specificity and coherence, emotional reappraisal, and self-concept, particularly within psychotherapy and close relationships. Abstract How to enable autonomous vehicles to sustain high safety and rapid response capability in complex, dynamic environments remains a critical and urgent challenge. This study aims to elevate the learning efficiency of multi-sensor feature fusion in autonomous-driving tasks, thereby strengthening system safety and responsiveness. To this end, we present an innovative multi-modal, multi-sensor feature-fusion model that integrates two highly effective mechanisms (Sparse Channel Pooling and Residual Triplet-Attention) to refine the fusion process. Solv3R: 3D Looped Transformer as a Multi-view SolverECO1
Authors: Jeong-gi Kwak, Andrea Tagliasacchi, Sho Kagami, Yuki Ono, Kwang Moo Yi
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-11-02
Abs
Abstract
Classical structure-from-motion reconstruction proceeds iteratively, refining camera poses and scene geometry from a current estimate. This allows maps to easily expand without recomputing already computed views, or geometry estimates to improve as more observations are provided. Most geometric foundation models, however, predict entire geometry in a single forward pass or update a recurrent state as frames arrive sequentially, making this expansion and refinement non-trivial. We introduce Solv3R, a looped transformer that learns to continue solving multi-view geometry from its current state. Importantly, its update operator is independent of the iteration count, allowing previously refined and newly introduced views to be jointly updated regardless of how ``solved''they are. We train the operator to preserve accurate reconstructions under continued iteration and correct inaccurate states, enabling refinement of fixed image collections and integration of new observations. Representations of solved views can also be cached once and reused as geometric context across iterations, enabling compact and efficient key-value caching. We analyze the solver dynamics and compare it with the state-of-the-art, demonstrating that a single learned solver supports offline reconstruction, incremental reconstruction, relocalization, and streaming without task-specific training or modules. The code and models will be made publicly available at https://ubc-vision.github.io/solv3r/.
