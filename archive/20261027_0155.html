Real-time 3D Gaussian scene reconstruction based on multisensor fusion SLAM in dynamic scenesCORE10
Authors: Jia-Ni Liu
Venue: International Conference on Algorithms, Machine Learning, and Image Processing (AMLIP 2026)
Published: 2026-10-20
Abs
Abstract
To address the problem that moving objects such as pedestrians and vehicles easily cause ghost artifacts, floating Gaussians, and incorrect textures in 3D Gaussian maps under dynamic scenes, this paper proposes a real-time 3D Gaussian scene reconstruction method based on multi-sensor fusion SLAM. The proposed method integrates LiDAR, camera, and IMU information, and uses a continuous-time multi-sensor fusion odometry front-end to provide stable pose estimation. A dynamic object detection and point cloud removal module is introduced between the odometry front-end and the 3D Gaussian mapping back-end. First, YOLO-seg is used to perform instance segmentation on potential dynamic objects in images, and 2D-3D object matching is completed by combining LiDAR point cloud projection and PointPillars-based 3D detection results. Then, multi-object tracking is employed to determine the true dynamic or static state of targets, remove dynamic point clouds, and generate static region masks. Finally, static colored point clouds are used to initialize 3D Gaussians, and static-region loss constraints are introduced in the back-end optimization so that dynamic regions do not participate in image and depth losses. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively reduces ghost artifacts and floating Gaussians, improving the quality and robustness of static scene reconstruction in dynamic environments.
Latent Bridge: From Video Latents to 3D Geometry via Feature AlignmentCORE8

Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-10-20
Abs · Code1
Abstract
Video generation and feed-forward 3D reconstruction typically use separate representations, requiring RGB decoding and visual re-encoding to recover geometry from generated latents. We propose Latent Bridge, which connects video generative latent spaces with geometric feature spaces to directly predict 3D geometry from video latents. We develop representation-specific bridges for Wan2.1 and V-RAE, enabling feed-forward prediction of cameras, depth, and 3D point clouds without the intermediate RGB pathway. Each bridge is trained on RGB videos through feature distillation, while the video encoder and geometry model remain frozen. Training requires neither ground-truth geometry nor execution of the downstream geometric aggregator or prediction heads. At inference, the same video latents support both RGB decoding and geometry prediction. The same trained bridge can be reused for image-to-video and text-to-video generation within the same video latent space, without bridge retraining. Experiments on Co3Dv2 and WildRGB-D demonstrate competitive geometric reconstruction quality, efficient end-to-end generation and reconstruction, and faster latent-to-geometry decoding, highlighting latent alignment as a practical and reusable interface between video generation and 3D reconstruction. Our code is available at: https://github.com/hustvl/Latent-Bridge.
GeoLift: Grounding Video Spatial Reasoning in Explicit 3D MapsCORE7

Published: 2026-10-24
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video spatial reasoning requires multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to understand how objects are arranged and related across viewpoints. While these models can recognize objects in individual frames, integrating these observations to solve complex spatial tasks remains challenging. We examine route planning as a representative task and observe that existing models become substantially less accurate as the number of required turns increases, with several models achieving accuracy close to random guessing on routes requiring three or more turns. This finding suggests that recognizing landmarks alone is insufficient: models must also connect observations across viewpoints and track how landmarks are arranged in a shared space. Motivated by this observation, we introduce GeoLift, a training-free framework that uses feed-forward 3D reconstruction to map objects detected in individual frames into a shared metric 3D scene. GeoLift combines a frozen MLLM for 2D grounding with pretrained geometry models for scene reconstruction. It then uses shared geometric regions, called superpoints, to associate observations of the same object across views without a learned matcher. The reconstructed 3D map enables explicit computation of spatial relations and multistep routes, and provides geometric evidence for estimating distances and sizes. With all model weights frozen, GeoLift improves Qwen3-VL-4B's average score on VSI-Bench from 55.4% to 64.1%, including an increase in route-planning accuracy from 30.9% to 53.6%, and achieves 59.8% accuracy on MindCube. These results show that connecting frame-level object grounding through a shared 3D map can improve complex spatial reasoning without task-specific training.

Abstract
Language-aware 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has advanced language-driven 3D scene understanding by incorporating semantic and relational cues into reconstructed scenes. However, relation-aware grounding depends not only on representing such information, but also on whether the underlying scene-side evidence is reliable and sufficient. Multi-view semantic aggregation can incorporate weak observations, while object relations can remain under-observed along the available camera trajectory, leading to ambiguous evidence for target disambiguation. We present RelateGS, a structured language-aware Gaussian representation that selectively refines semantic and relational evidence for 3D visual grounding. RelateGS suppresses unreliable observations during semantic feature uplifting and consolidates multi-view object observations into a persistent 3D graph. The graph further identifies under-observed object relations and guides targeted re-querying of informative existing frames to complement their evidence. Experiments on Sr3D+ and Nr3D demonstrate competitive 3D grounding performance, improving the previous best by 7.9 and 1.7 percentage points at Acc@0.25, respectively.

Abstract
Real-world environments undergo continual semi-static changes, requiring a memory representation that captures both the spatial and temporal evolution of a scene. Existing spatio-temporal memories for robots typically rely either on structured representations, which are limited to the entities and attributes captured during construction, or on image-based memories, which are constrained to previously observed viewpoints. We introduce GEMS, a framework that combines a persistent spatio-temporal 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) map with an agentic reasoning pipeline. The 3DGS map is reconstructed once and subsequently updated only in regions where the scene changes, storing only these modifications while preserving visual detail across time. The agentic pipeline leverages this map to render novel views on demand, iteratively accumulating evidence to answer spatio-temporal questions and ground objects from natural-language queries. To advance future research, we introduce GEMS-Bench, a spatiotemporal question-answering benchmark focused on semi-static environments. It comprises 3 real-world and 12 synthetic environments, spanning 473 question-answer pairs. We evaluate GEMS on novel-view synthesis quality across scene updates, 3D object grounding, spatiotemporal question answering, and storage efficiency. GEMS outperforms recent baselines in 3D object grounding and spatiotemporal question answering, while achieving rendering quality on par with dedicated scene-update methods. Abstract
Real-world 3D reconstruction pipelines often rely on multi-view images degraded by lossy compression. While compression artifacts have been extensively studied in single-image or video restoration, their impact on multi-view geometry remains largely overlooked. In this work, we identify compression-induced cross-view geometric inconsistency as a critical failure mode, where view-dependent artifacts disrupt feature matching, dense correspondence, camera parameter estimation, and downstream 3D reconstruction tasks such as novel view synthesis. To address this problem, we propose CompMVR, a compression-aware multi-view diffusion restoration framework that jointly refines compressed views while preserving cross-view consistency, and construct compressed datasets for systematic evaluation. Unlike conventional restoration methods that optimize each image independently for 2D perceptual quality, CompMVR leverages learned compression priors and cross-view correspondence to recover visual details while improving geometric reliability across views. Experiments across diverse datasets, codecs, and compression levels demonstrate gains in restoration quality and downstream 3D tasks, including novel-view synthesis, camera pose estimation, and view matching. These results establish compressed multi-view restoration as a distinct problem and highlight its importance for 3D reconstruction under lossy compression. KoalaGS: Complexity-Aware Continuous Level of Detail for Gaussian SplattingCORE5
Authors: Radu Beche, Raoul de Charette
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-10-20
Abs
Abstract
Deploying a trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene under shrinking compute budgets requires one primitive set to remain effective when rendered at smaller prefixes. In this continuous level of detail (LoD) setting, the challenge is to decide which Gaussians should retain capacity as the budget decreases. A pilot study on 3DGS-MCMC reconstructions shows that image complexity, computed from the input views before optimization, predicts where reconstruction error concentrates in image and 3D space. It also reveals that simple regions contain too many Gaussians and degrade as much as complex ones at lower budgets, despite being inherently cheaper to represent. Subsequently, in KoalaGS, during training we use this complexity prior to bias relocation toward poorly reconstructed areas while ensuring smooth degradation across LoDs. A primitive importance score then enables inference at low-capacity, while preserving the scene structure. Tested across three datasets, KoalaGS maintains competitive full-capacity quality while significantly improving quality across the budget spectrum, yielding smoother transitions across LoDs, and enabling faster progressive streaming.
OV-Foam: How 3D Representations Shape Semantic Feature LiftingCORE5
Authors: Yousef Rajeh, Alejandra Esparza Pelcastre, Ivan Viola, Dominik Engel King Abdullah University of Science, Technology...
Venue: Science Robotics
Published: 2026-10-20
Abs
Abstract
Existing feature lifting approaches for open-vocabulary segmentation rely on simple backprojection to assign features to primitives. In the case of 3D Gaussian splatting reconstruction, the primitives overlap heavily, leading to suboptimal reconstructions, governed by many primitives per ray. Backprojection, however, reduces to a least-squares solvable problem, exactly when no two primitives share a ray. Motivated by this insight, we propose OV-Foam, which uses PowerFoam as the underlying representation. This representation uses a power-diagram to partition the scene, resulting in zero overlap between primitives and a substantial reduction in primitives per ray, which ultimately simplifies the lifting problem and allows for closer-to-optimal feature assignment. We show that the space partitioning primitives of OV-Foam reach near-optimal assignment, and our experiments demonstrate improved open-vocabulary segmentation over Gaussian baselines.
Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Network for Single-Shot 3D Reconstruction from Fringe PatternsCORE3
Authors: Xinjie Zhou, XiaoLei Li
Published: 2026-10-25
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Deep learning facilitates rapid single shot three dimensional (3D) reconstruction using fringe projection profilometry (FPP), but recovering accurate surface height from a single high frequency fringe image remains challenging because both long-range spatial consistency and fine geometric details must be preserved. Convolutional networks are limited in modeling long range dependencies, whereas window-based Transformers establish global interactions only progressively with increasing depth. To address these limitations, a hybrid Mamba Transformer network is proposed for fringe to height regression. The network adopts a Swin based U shaped architecture and introduces visual state space modeling at the intermediate resolution encoder stage. An axial and dilated vision state space (ADVSS) block is further developed to enhance prescan feature encoding through local, dilated, and axial depthwise convolutions while retaining four directional selective scanning. This design combines linear complexity long range modeling with direction aware preservation of local fringe structures. Experiments on a publicly available FPP dataset show that the proposed method outperforms hNet, SwinConvUNet, UHRNet, and HiDNet in mean absolute error(MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), absolute relative error, and structural similarity index measure (SSIM). Using only one deformed fringe image, the proposed method achieves an RMSE of 0.548 mm and an SSIM of 0.9985, reducing RMSE by 65.2% and 59.6% relative to UHRNet and HiDNet, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of combining selective state space modeling with hierarchical window based attention for accurate single-shot FPP reconstruction.

Abstract
Feed-forward 3D foundation models have recently reshaped multi-view reconstruction, yet dynamic 4D understanding still often relies on large-scale motion supervision or hand-crafted, architecture-specific heuristics. We ask whether frozen 3D foundation model tokens already encode motion-relevant information that can be exposed by lightweight readout functions, rather than re-learning motion-specific features with high-capacity decoders. Through probing on three representative backbones, we find that static--dynamic separation is decodable from frozen token representations. Our rank-restricted and spectral analyses on $π^3$X reveal that the recovered motion cues are concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace. Building on this, we propose Probing-based Motion Distillation (PMD), which converts pairwise motion-state compatibility scores against a fixed anchor token into dense motion masks. Trained on only 160 YouTube-VOS videos using a single 16 GB GPU, PMD transfers zero-shot to DAVIS, SegTrackv2, and FBMS-59 without target-benchmark fine-tuning. With multi-view pretrained geometric backbones, PMD outperforms training-free heuristics and approaches substantially heavier supervised and self-supervised methods, suggesting that motion decoding from frozen 3D foundation models can be lightweight and data-efficient.

Abstract
Reconstructing a coherent 3D scene from uncurated image collections remains challenging due to the presence of irrelevant or non-overlapping views. Recent work has shown that feed-forward reconstruction models contain internal view-consistency signals that support distractor rejection. However, reference-conditioned filtering relies on a curated clean reference, which is not guaranteed in practical image collections. To address this limitation, we introduce FreeView, a training-free framework that jointly selects a filtering reference and rejects distractor views from an uncurated collection. FreeView leverages collection-level consensus to identify a representative view that serves as a reliable reference for filtering. It then adapts view rejection to each input collection by combining complementary visual and geometric evidence. This approach preserves useful views under substantial appearance and viewpoint variations while rejecting distractors. FreeView integrates with both VGGT and pi^3, removing the need for a manually curated reference or manual calibration of view rejection. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate high clean-view retention and distractor rejection rates, leading to reliable 3D reconstruction from uncurated views.

Abstract
Crack propagation provides richer information than static crack observations for evaluating structural conditions of concrete infrastructure and planning maintenance strategies. Unlike static crack detection, which can now be largely automated using computer vision, vision-based crack tracking is a coupled cross-view and cross-time problem: the same physical crack must retain its identity as camera viewpoints change, while newly developed growth must be distinguished from existing crack segments in a unified reference view. Existing segmentation and 3D reconstruction methods can recover crack masks or scene geometry, but they do not preserve crack identity across views or quantify temporal extension. To address this gap, this study formulates crack growth monitoring as a cross-view and cross-time crack-instance tracking task and proposes a geometry-assisted, morphology-aware framework. The framework first uses image-based geometric registration to align observations from different viewpoints into a shared structural reference, then applies morphology-aware instantiation to assign physically interpretable crack identities, and finally performs temporal mapping in the unified view to distinguish newly developed growth from existing crack segments. To enable evaluation, a temporally annotated dataset was established and used, covering three representative cracking scenarios: single dominant crack propagation, mixed-mode branching, and multiple-crack interaction, with pixel-level masks and cross-time instance labels. Experimental results show that the proposed framework can establish loose cross-view identity correspondence under changing viewpoints, while strict spatial overlap and growth localization remain limited by early micro-crack segmentability and projection drift, highlighting directions for future crack-monitoring research.

Abstract
Rapid illumination transitions can disturb Visual SLAM (VSLAM) by saturating images before the Automatic Exposure (AE) policy adapts, removing the visual correspondences needed to maintain tracking. Our proposed methods mitigate this disturbance at two levels: B-Adapt acquires adaptive exposure brackets to cover the scene dynamic range while promoting photometric overlap, and BVSLAM directly uses the multi-exposure sequence as constraints in a factor graph state estimator without relying on High Dynamic Range (HDR) merging. We evaluate both methods against state-of-the-art approaches in nominal environments and during a repeated tunnel-exit transition at an emulated vehicle speed of 32 m s-1. These results suggest that bracketing efficiently improves visual pose estimation robustness to rapid illumination disturbances while retaining low error under nominal conditions. Abstract
While feed-forward models excel in static 3D geometry estimation, extending them to dynamic 4D scenes requires modeling visual geometry as a causally evolving state. Current methods typically rely on unordered temporal aggregation, which lacks the directional structure of physical time. Furthermore, existing supervision often treats geometry and motion as disjoint objectives, failing to exploit their inherent physical coupling. Consequently, they lack a unified constraint to effectively supervise temporal state transitions, leading to inconsistent 4D scene representations. To address this, we propose a feed-forward framework for causal 4D visual geometry learning from monocular videos. At its core, Causal Spatiotemporal Mamba augments temporal aggregation with directional state propagation. Through complementary Temporal-Spatial and Spatial-Temporal Scans, CSM continuously propagates geometry-aware states across frames to model spatiotemporal evolution. To align this causal state evolution with observable dynamics, we introduce Unified Motion Supervision. Rather than treating motion as an independent objective, UMS leverages geometry-aware predictions from the camera and depth heads to construct a unified forward motion field that is consistent with the underlying 3D structure. This geometry-grounded supervision explicitly constrains inter-frame state transitions, enabling stable states propagation across frames. Extensive evaluations on dynamic benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across video depth, camera pose, and optical flow estimation. These results validate that the synergy between the directional state propagation of Causal Spatiotemporal Mamba and Unified Motion Supervision effectively captures continuous scene evolution, significantly reducing temporal drift and reinforcing spatial-temporal consistency in 4D representation learning.

Purpose: To mitigate parameter degeneracy and improve robustness of IVIM fitting, a multimodal physics-informed neural network was developed to leverage anatomical information through the combined use of cross attention, selective spatial gating, and anatomy-weighted spatial prior. Methods: T1w and FLAIR anatomical images were co-registered to IVIM signal in native space and segmented by tissue type. Abstract
Feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction from sparse posed monocular frames requires reasoning over observations that vary jointly in viewpoint and time. Existing methods either predict Gaussian primitives from observation-aligned features or aggregate multi-frame information through compact latent queries. However, the former tightly couples Gaussian allocation to individual frames, while the latter does not explicitly structure observed evidence according to its temporal relation to the requested timestamp. Consequently, evidence across spatial scales, viewpoints, and timestamps can be difficult to align and integrate. We present \textbf{HiQGS}, a query-based hierarchical framework for feed-forward Gaussian scene reconstruction from sparse monocular frames. HiQGS introduces a \textbf{Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Encoder} that organizes multi-resolution geometry-appearance visual features according to image location, observation time, and camera pose, thereby preserving complementary appearance and structural cues. Our \textbf{Time-Adaptive Decoder} use target timestamp queries the resulting hierarchical representation to retrieve relevant spatiotemporal evidence and predicts the corresponding Gaussian scene state. Experiments on static and dynamic scene reconstruction benchmarks demonstrate strong rendering quality and geometric accuracy, validating the effectiveness of hierarchical visual encoding and target-time-aware Gaussian querying.

Abstract
Simulation is a powerful tool for robotics, but building diverse, simulation-ready scenes at scale remains difficult. Reconstructing scenes from single images is a promising alternative, but visual similarity alone is insufficient: objects must also be recovered, accurately placed, and physically grounded. Existing methods that rely on post-hoc physics settling often misplace objects, producing scenes that look correct but behave poorly under manipulation. We present SceneRig, an agentic system that turns a single image into an editable, simulation-ready scene. SceneRig combines geometric, visual, and physical tools to reconstruct a faithful 3D scene. An agent then performs edit-and-validate loops inside a physics simulator to achieve physical stability. Across diverse inputs, SceneRig improves instance segmentation, object-centric appearance, and 3D placement while keeping the reconstructed scenes physically stable. Furthermore, SceneRig is effective for downstream robotics: its scenes predict real-robot policy outcomes more accurately than the baseline method and more reliably support replay of recorded manipulations without trajectory adaptation.
