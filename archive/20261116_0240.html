Authors: Zi-Xin Zhang, Kang-Hao Chen, Lu-Tao Jiang, Zi-Dong Cao, Yuanhuiyi Lyu...
Venue: Proceedings of the 34th ACM International Conference on Multimedia
Published: 2026-11-09
Abs · PDF
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has revolutionized the field of 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis, emerging as a promising representation for 3D scenes. Registering multiple 3D Gaussians across different coordinate systems is a crucial step for various downstream tasks, such as large-scale scene reconstruction. However, existing methods have either underutilized the scene information in 3D Gaussians or struggled with degraded 3D Gaussian point clouds with wrong geometry. To address these issues, we introduce SemRegGS, a novel and robust registration approach that incorporates semantic information to guide the registration process so as to bring the 3DGS models into the same coordinate. Specifically, to address the error-prone geometry of the scene in 3D Gaussians, we propose an uncertainty-aware semantic unprojection module that leverages the 2D foundational vision models to assign high-level semantic information to off-the-shelf 3DGS models. Building on this, we introduce a semantic-guided point cloud registration module that is resilient to the less accurate geometry in the 3D Gaussian point clouds. Moreover, to fully exploit the scene information in the 3D Gaussians, we propose an image-guided refinement module that enhances registration precision. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method not only outperforms existing approaches in standard scenarios but also achieves a high success rate in extreme cases where other methods fail.

Abstract
We present DynamicDriver, a generalized Gaussian splatting framework tailored for reconstructing dynamic driving scenes and synthesizing novel views across multiple lanes. In real-world autonomous driving data collection, videos are typically captured from a single trajectory, limiting scene coverage. To enrich training data with diverse viewpoints and road positions, we virtually shift the vehicle's trajectory to generate novel lane-level views. However, rendering views from adjacent or unseen lanes remains highly challenging due to limited coverage and occlusions in the original observations. Generalized Gaussian splatting presents a promising solution when trained on large-scale driving datasets. Previous works in this field mainly focus on static scenes, while DynamicDriver enables the reconstruction of dynamic scenes by integrating LiDAR and optical flow, which also benefits static scene modeling. To address the limitations of depth estimation in both dynamic and static regions, we propose a Cross-Cue LiDAR Fusion module that injects accurate LiDAR depth into the image feature space. Additionally, we introduce a Flow-Based Gaussian Optimizer that improves the representation of dynamic objects by enhancing temporal consistency, allowing their Gaussians to be more clearly separated from the background. Experiments demonstrate that DynamicDriver achieves state-of-the-art performance in generating high-fidelity and temporally consistent novel views for complex lane-switching scenarios involving both static and dynamic elements.
Stage-wise Lineage-aware Dropout for Sparse-View 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE7
Authors: Yan Xing, Zhi Chen, Fanglue Zhang, Junhong Zhao, Bing Xue...
Published: 2026-11-13
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is prone to overfitting because only a limited number of images are available to constrain scene geometry. Dropout is one regularization strategy for alleviating this problem. We introduce the concept of a lineage family to describe a set of Gaussian primitives derived from the same founder through cloning or splitting operations. The founder Gaussians refer to all Gaussian primitives generated during initialization. Descendants within the same family often jointly contribute to the refinement of adjacent local regions. However, dropping individual Gaussians can be less effective due to the compensation effect from neighboring Gaussians for the dropped ones. To address this issue, we propose Stage-wise Lineage-aware Dropout(SLD), which applies lineage-family dropout during the structural regularization stage and switches to individual dropout during the later refinement stage. We further propose Lineage-Balanced Densification(LBD), which operates during densification by constructing an expanded candidate pool and reallocating the densification budget according to the mean gradient scores and existing descendant sizes of different families. Experiments on the LLFF, MipNeRF360, and Blender datasets show that the proposed framework achieves competitive performance in sparse-view reconstruction
Joint Orientation and Weight Optimization for Robust Watertight Reconstruction from Unoriented PointsCORE7
Authors: Jia-Ze Li, Dai-Sheng Jin, Fei Hou, Jun-Hui Hou, Zheng Liu...
Venue: ACM Transactions on Graphics (TOG)
Published: 2026-11-09
Abs · PDF · Code1
Abstract
We propose Dirichlet Winding Reconstruction (DiWR), a method for watertight surface reconstruction from unoriented point clouds affected by non-uniform sampling, noise, and outliers. DiWR represents geometry using a generalized winding number (GWN) field whose point-based discretization depends on point orientations and per-point contributions. Instead of treating each point's contribution as fixed, DiWR alternates between updating point orientations, area weights that compensate for local sampling density, and confidence coefficients that reduce the influence of unreliable samples. The optimization combines a sampled Dirichlet-energy regularizer with winding-number surface constraints, area-stability regularization, and confidence regularization. This joint treatment reduces reliance on separate denoising or outlier-removal stages and helps stabilize reconstruction on corrupted inputs. We evaluate the method using controlled stress tests and point clouds produced by 3D Gaussian Splatting and vision pipelines. In our experiments, DiWR improves robustness over classical multi-stage pipelines and several recent joint orientation-reconstruction methods on many challenging cases, while still assuming that the input sufficiently samples an underlying watertight manifold surface. The source code and test models are available at https://github.com/LaoChui999/DiWR.

Abstract
Efficient solutions for monocular depth estimation (MDE) constitute fundamental components in robotics, autonomous driving, and 3D reconstruction, particularly for resource-constrained platforms requiring real-time processing. Recent research has explored leveraging knowledge distillation to generate more lightweight methods. Despite progress in this learning paradigm, distilling knowledge to compact networks faces multiple challenges: 1) The reliability of teacher supervision often fails to remain consistent across all pixel locations. 2) Wide depth value ranges and scale inconsistency complicate the implementation of normalization. 3) Heterogeneous teacher-student architectures may compromise knowledge transfer effectiveness. To overcome these limitations, we propose the unified knowledge distillation framework RD-KD. This framework integrates three core components: Teacher Reliability-Boosted Mask (TRM) filters low-reliability regions, Soft Segment Normalization (SSN) aligns depth while suppressing boundary artifacts, and Distribution-Level Semantic Alignment Module (DSAM) reduces feature discrepancies. By synergistically leveraging these components, we achieve significant performance gains during distillation. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that our framework consistently outperforms existing lightweight depth estimators across multiple benchmarks, delivering dual improvements in accuracy and generalization while maintaining compact parameters and computational efficiency. Abstract
High-fidelity surface reconstruction from sparse RGB images remains a significant challenge, where classical optimization-based approaches typically fail to resolve geometric ambiguity under insufficient epipolar constraints. While recent feed-forward methods have achieved remarkable progress in directly regressing point clouds and 3D Gaussians from sparse inputs, extending such paradigms to explicit surface reconstruction often results in suboptimal geometric quality. We identify the primary obstacle in feed-forward surface reconstruction as an inherent structural misalignment between 2D visual inputs and 3D surface targets, which creates a learning bottleneck and leads to degraded performance. In this work, we propose MeRM, a large-scale feed-forward Mesh Reconstruction Model that leverages a novel Neural Geometry Token (NGT) technique to address this challenge. Our key insight is that intermediate tokens of a multi-view Transformer—pretrained to predict dense depth maps, normal maps, and masks—can serve as a powerful geometric pre-aggregator to bridge the structural bottleneck. Benefiting from the highly informative geometric cues distilled by NGT, our MeRM delivers exceptional surface reconstruction quality while maintaining impressive training and inference efficiency. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our MeRM significantly outperforms prior feed-forward methods and even exceeds the performance of state-of-the-art optimization-based approaches on dense views, despite using only sparse inputs. We believe our approach establishes a novel framework for large-scale feed-forward mesh reconstruction, paving a new path for future research.

Abstract
Multimodal image alignment is an essential step for vision tasks that rely on multiple modalities. Most existing methods use deep networks to warp one image to match another. Although effective, such warping is not 3D-aware and often alters the image structure, causing distortion. To address this problem, we propose ThermalGaussian-X, a 3D-aware method that aligns multimodal images via 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). Our model constructs a shared 3DGS model from unaligned multiview RGB and thermal images, automatically learning the relative camera poses between modalities. Abstract
Thermal infrared novel view synthesis (TIR-NVS) is important for nighttime robotics, infrastructure inspection, aerial monitoring, and adverse-weather perception. However, sparse-view TIR-NVS remains highly under-constrained. Existing thermal reconstruction methods often become unstable under sparse views, while directly transferring RGB-centric diffusion priors to TIR is also problematic because TIR synthesis requires radiometric faithfulness rather than appearance-driven edits. We propose ThermalDiffusion, a two-stage diffusion framework for sparse-view TIR-NVS. To bridge the domain gap between RGB-centric video diffusion and thermal generation, we construct filtered pseudo thermal trajectories using multiple nearby-view synthesis strategies, and combine them with real thermal trajectory clips to adapt a pretrained latent video diffusion backbone to the TIR domain via lightweight low-rank adaptation (LoRA) tuning. At inference time, we reconstruct a coarse 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) proxy from the sparse inputs and derive trajectory-aligned controls, including thermal intensity, depth, and a reliability confidence map. These controls enable confidence-aware dual guidance during reverse diffusion: depth-aware screened-Poisson guidance regularizes uncertain regions, while radiometric evidence anchoring preserves reliable thermal evidence in high-confidence regions. The generated trajectory is further distilled into a refined 3DGS representation for efficient free-view rendering. Experiments on TI-NSD show that ThermalDiffusion consistently outperforms the strongest baseline under sparse-view settings, improving PSNR by up to 2.30 dB while also achieving better SSIM and LPIPS. The pseudo dataset and code will be released at https://github.com/JingyuanGao0919/ThermalDiffusion.

Abstract
Infrared and visible image fusion (IVIF) combines robust infrared sensing with the rich structural details of visible imagery. Existing IVIF methods are predominantly developed in 2D image space, limiting them to fixed viewpoints and hindering explicit modeling of modality discrepancies at the scene level. Although recent works introduce multimodal cues into 3D representations, they focus primarily on cross-modality rendering conflicts or modality-dominant reconstruction rather than fusion-oriented selective preservation and suppression. Abstract
3D single object tracking is a key technology in robotic vision and intelligent perception systems. In complex scenarios, common challenges such as target absence, occlusion, and deformation often lead to tracking drift or failure. For LiDAR point clouds, their inherent sparsity and texture-less nature often result in insufficient target feature representation, further exacerbating tracking instability in the aforementioned complex scenarios. Although existing Siamese tracking paradigms introduce memory mechanisms to propagate geometric and mask features, they are still confined to pure geometric information and lack the utilization of intrinsic material properties, leading to insufficient discriminability when facing geometrically similar distractors and sparse appearances. Wide field-of-view panorama stitching is important for computational photography and immersive visual applications, but often faces some challenges like severe geometric disparities. Abstract
Multisensory grasp stability prediction estimates whether a planned grasp will remain stable by combining visual context with tactile contact evidence. Vision provides global object geometry and appearance but is ambiguous under sparse viewpoints, occlusion, and challenging surface properties; touch reveals local geometry and contact deformation but can be acquired only after choosing a grasp. Existing visuo-tactile methods generally assume that tactile observations are already available, while tactile-aided Gaussian representations primarily use touch to improve reconstruction rather than grasp decisions. We instead study single-touch active grasp stability prediction: given sparse visual observations and many feasible two-finger actions, the robot first selects one action to execute and then uses its tactile images to estimate grasp success. To support this task, we first establish a benchmark simulated by PyBullet using 19 objects from ObjectFolder, with aligned multi-view observations, candidate actions, tactile images, and lift-success labels under shape-family-disjoint evaluation. On this benchmark, we propose a Gaussian-guided two-stage framework in which object-level 3D Gaussian Splatting constructs reliable candidate actions and local contact-and-corridor geometry. Stage 1 scores all candidates from canonical RGB, grasp-action, and Gaussian evidence and outputs the Top-1 action for execution. Stage 2 then outputs its grasp-success probability from bilateral GelSight images and the same pre-contact context, thereby verifying the selected action after contact. Experiments are performed on our constructed benchmark against visual, geometric, Gaussian-quality, depth, and point-cloud alternatives, and the results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed single-touch decision pipeline. 3D visual perception and scene reconstruction are critical for autonomous robots and intelligent transportation, yet real-world deployments often generate long, redundant image sequences that hinder downstream processing. Abstract
High-fidelity multi-view 3D hair reconstruction from casual handheld captures remains a formidable challenge due to severe self-occlusions and the intricate topology of individual strands. A critical bottleneck in existing 3D hair representations—both volumetric grids and continuous implicit fields—is the reliance on deterministic spatial queries that assign a single local orientation. This unimodal assumption inevitably collapses and averages crossing or layered strands into erroneous directions, leading to a loss of internal structural details. To overcome this, we introduce SODF-Hair, a novel framework that represents local hair structures using Spherical Orientation Distribution Functions (SODFs). Finally, rather than prematurely committing to a single streamline, we assemble explicit scalp-connected strands via a field-guided beam search and constrained graph optimization. Extensive evaluations suggest that our approach faithfully preserves subject-specific visible details while generating structurally coherent internal strand geometry, demonstrating robust performance across diverse hairstyles.
