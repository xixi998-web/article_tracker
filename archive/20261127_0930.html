Geospatial assessment of soil quality using remote sensing and GIS in Jamtara district Jharkhand IndiaCORE6
Authors: Debaaditya Mukhopadhyay, Kambam Boxen Meetei, Kanti Hansda, Ajijul Mir, Sk Mujibar Rahaman...
Venue: Discover Soil
Published: 2026-11-20
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study assesses the spatial variability of soil quality in Jamtara district, Jharkhand, using field sampling and geospatial techniques to support regional-scale soil and agricultural management. Nineteen parameters were analyzed, including macro- and micronutrients, soil physical properties, soil organic carbon (SOC), pH, electrical conductivity (EC), moisture, bulk density, NDVI, slope, rainfall, elevation, and temperature. Ordinary kriging was applied to generate spatial prediction surfaces, while parameter importance was determined through the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) using a consistent weighting structure (CR = 0.048). A Soil Quality Index (SQI) was developed through weighted summation and classified into five grades. The results indicate substantial spatial heterogeneity: Grade I (13.6%), Grade II (19.6%), Grade III (61.1%), Grade IV (3.00%), and Grade V (2.7%). Validation using field-based error matrix produced an overall accuracy of 0.80 and a Kappa coefficient of 0.75, indicating substantial agreement between observed and classified soil-quality categories. Land-use/land-cover analysis suggested that high-quality soils were generally associated with agricultural and fallow lands, whereas lower grades corresponded to barren and degraded areas. Block-wise assessment indicated relatively higher soil quality in Jamtara, Narayanpur and Kundahit blocks, while Fatehpur exhibited predominantly moderate to low soil quality. Overall, the integration of field observations with Remote Sensing (RS), Geographic Information Systems (GIS), kriging and AHP provided a systematic regional-scale framework for soil quality assessment. The study highlights the importance of sustainable soil-management practices, including targeted nutrient application, liming of acidic soils, enhancement of organic matter, and zone-specific interventions to support long-term agricultural productivity. The generated outputs provide useful spatial insights that may support informed land-management planning within the district.
Nutrient Release Behaviour of POME-Derived Organo-Mineral Fertilisers in Acidic UltisolCORE6
Authors: Fadeila Mohamed Hashim Ali, Syaharudin Zaibon, S. Kasim
Venue: Pertanika Journal of Tropical Agricultural Science
Published: 2026-11-20
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study investigated the nutrient release behaviour of organic and organo-mineral fertilisers made from palm oil mill effluent (POME) in an acidic Ultisol under regulated incubation. The experiment used a completely randomised design with eight treatments and 400 g of soil, incubated for 60 days at 25 °C. The treatments comprised soil only, mineral fertiliser, POME only (one formulation), and blends of POME and minerals (two formulations). The first Ultisol was strongly acidic (pH 4.46), low in cation exchange capacity (10.16 cmol (+) kg−1), and severely deficient in available phosphorus (P), calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), and potassium (K), while POME sludge was indicative of nutrient contribution potential. The study showed that the fertilised treatments had significant improvement in the soil pH and nutrients. In terms of short-term nutrient release (particularly phosphorus and several base cations), treatment T3 (120% POME) produced the best results, suggesting that POME sludge has a high nutrient-supply capacity. However, T5 (80% POME + 20% mineral fertiliser) exhibited the most balanced, slow nutrient-release pattern throughout the incubation. Further, T5 also managed to moderate soil pH better than the single-source treatment. The results show that organo-mineral fertiliser based on POME could improve nutrient availability in acidic tropical soil. It could reduce the reliance on synthetic fertiliser. Furthermore, it offers a more sustainable nutrient management strategy in low-fertility Ultisol systems with practical value for sustainable recycling of agro-waste.
Effects of zinc-enriched and acid-modified biochar on soil physicochemical properties and wheat growth in calcareous soilsCORE4
Authors: M. R. Soba, Z. Demir, Tuğçe Ayşe Kardeş, Ahsen Ertem, E. Kaya...
Venue: Frontiers in Plant Science
Published: 2026-11-20
Abs · PDF
Abstract
In calcareous soils, where zinc (Zn) fixation restricts crop productivity, modified biochars have been proposed as promising carriers for micronutrient management. This study aimed to characterize the Zn adsorption–desorption behavior of olive pruning waste-derived biochar (BC) produced at 400 °C and modified with 1 mol L -1 HNO 3 (NA), H 3 PO 4 (PA), and H 2 SO 4 (SA), and to evaluate the agronomic performance of Zn-enriched biochars under wheat ( Triticum aestivum L.) cultivation. Adsorption experiments (0–400 mg L -1 Zn) showed that Zn retention depended strongly on modification type. The original BC followed an H-type isotherm and removed 91% of the added Zn at an initial concentration of 100 mg L -1 , whereas the acid-modified materials showed S-type behaviour and removed only 1-10%, reflecting the strongly acidic post-contact solution conditions (pH 1.26–2.03) maintained by residual acid. Desorption in 0.01 M CaCl 2 released 1.6-17.8% of the Zn retained by the original BC but a considerably larger proportion of that retained by the acid-modified materials. In the greenhouse pot experiment, biochar was applied at 1% (w/w) and prepared at nominal target Zn loadings of 30, 300, and 1000 mg kg -1 Zn in a completely randomized design using the Bayraktar 2000 bread wheat cultivar. Wheat dry weight increased from 1.37 g pot -1 in the control to 4.93 g pot -1 under PA+BC+Zn 300 . Plant elemental composition was significantly affected by all treatments, with the highest N concentrations in NA+BC+Zn 300 and NA+BC+Zn 1000 (up to 37.1 g kg -1 ), the highest P in PA+BC+Zn 1000 (7.53 g kg -1 ), and the highest Zn in NA+BC+Zn 1000 (73.0 mg kg -1 ). In soil, the highest available P 2 O 5 was observed in the PA+BC+Zn 1000 treatment (640.5 kg ha -1 ), biochar treatments generally increased organic matter, and Zn enrichment increased soil Zn concentrations by 1.38 mg kg -1 compared with the control. Correlation analysis showed that wheat dry weight was strongly associated with plant N (r ≈ 0.90) and soil organic matter (r ≈ 0.82), whereas multivariate analyses (PCA and hierarchical clustering) demonstrated that acid-modified biochar+Zn treatments clustered along a favorable nutrient–biomass axis. Overall, acid modification altered Zn retention–release behavior and influenced short-term wheat vegetative growth and nutrient status under calcareous soil conditions. The responses varied according to acid modification type and Zn enrichment level, with PA+BC+Zn 300 producing the highest wheat dry biomass and NA-modified treatments generally showing high plant N and Zn concentrations.

Abstract
An experiment was conducted during the Rabi season of 2022–23 at the Agricultural Research Farm, Institute of Agricultural Sciences, Banaras Hindu University, Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh, to evaluate the effects of different mulches on soil physicochemical properties, soil microbial biomass, enzymatic activities, microbial populations, and economics of chickpea. The experiment was laid out in a randomised block design (RBD) with three replications comprising seven treatments, namely no mulch, dust mulch, paddy straw, maize stover, Leucaena twigs, and combinations of paddy straw or maize stover with Leucaena twigs. Among the treatments, Leucaena twigs @ 5 t ha⁻¹ recorded the highest available nitrogen (259.3 kg ha⁻¹), phosphorus (15.23 kg ha⁻¹), potassium (185 kg ha⁻¹), and organic carbon (0.56%). The combination of maize stover @ 2.5 t ha⁻¹ + Leucaena twigs @ 2.5 t ha⁻¹ recorded the highest microbial biomass carbon (178.44 µg g⁻¹ soil), dehydrogenase activity (18.32 µg TPF g⁻¹ soil 24 h⁻¹), alkaline phosphatase activity (115.32 µg p-NP g⁻¹ soil h⁻¹), bacterial population (16.98 CFU g⁻¹ soil), fungal population (14.76 CFU g⁻¹ soil), and actinomycetes population (3.48 CFU g⁻¹ soil). However, the combination of paddy straw @ 2.5 t ha⁻¹ + Leucaena twigs @ 2.5 t ha⁻¹ recorded the highest gross returns (₹100,409.80 ha⁻¹), net returns (₹56,281.54 ha⁻¹), and benefit-cost ratio (2.28). The results indicated that residue-based mulching improved soil fertility and biological activity, with maize stover + Leucaena twigs being particularly effective for soil biological properties, while paddy straw + Leucaena twigs provided the highest economic returns.

Venue: ACS applied materials & interfaces
Published: 2026-11-20

Abstract
Stimuli-responsive prodrug-based controlled-release systems enable precise spatiotemporal regulation of the site-specific activation and release of active ingredients, thereby enhancing overall efficacy while minimizing off-target effects in pesticide application. In this study, cyproconazole (CPZ) was covalently conjugated with cinnamic acid (CAA) via a hydrolyzable ester bond to yield a prodrug (CCP), which spontaneously self-assembled into uniform nanoparticles (CCP NPs) without the addition of any auxiliary adjuvants. Benefiting from its small hydrodynamic diameter (277 nm), narrow size distribution (PDI = 0.139), and high surface charge (-33.2 mV), the resulting CCP NPs exhibited significantly improved foliar retention and spreading capacity on plant leaf surfaces. Compared with commercial CPZ suspension concentrate (CPZ SC), CCP NPs reduced soil leaching by 71.81% and 57.48% in yellowish-brown soil from Beijing and red soil from Yunnan Province, respectively. Furthermore, CCP NPs displayed pH- and esterase-dual-triggered release behavior and provided long-term antifungal activity against plant pathogens with a control efficacy of 74.86%, outperforming CPZ SC (50.36%) after 14 days of foliar application. Comprehensive biosafety evaluations revealed that CCP NPs had no adverse effects on plant growth and exhibited approximately 5-fold lower acute toxicity to earthworms. Overall, this CAA-based prodrug nano assembly provides an effective strategy that simultaneously enhances efficacy and curbs environmental mobility, thereby supporting the development of sustainable crop protection products.

Venue: ACS Omega
Published: 2026-11-20

Abstract
Our recent designs of Janus bimodal peptide nucleic acids (bm-PNA) have nucleobases attached to both sides of the aminoethylglycyl PNA backbone, equipping them for concurrent binding of two complementary DNA strands to form fused double duplexes. On the basis of the design of Janus PNA sequences, we have earlier shown that these form a variety of polyplexes ranging from double duplexes, triplex of duplexes, and tetraduplex of G4-tetraplexes. In this manuscript, we elaborate on this range to demonstrate Janus bm-PNA-Cn with cytosines attached to the PNA backbone via a flexible alkyl triazole linker, forming i-motif tetraplexes through C–H+···C at pH < 5.0, similar to the t-amide link as in standard PNA or via the glycosidic bond in DNA. At pH > 5.0, when cytosine N3 is nonprotonated, C:C duplexes occur by direct C:C base pairing from the triazole face. In such bm-PNA C:C self-duplexes, the mixed base sequence on two outer t-amide sides forms two duplexes with complementary DNA, leading to DNA:PNA-C:C-PNA:DNA triple duplexes, with synergistic stabilization of the constituent duplexes. The pH-dependent i-motif tetraplex (acidic pH) and triple duplexes (neutral pH) extend the repertoire of multistranded assemblies from Janus bm-PNAs. These open up future possibilities to build programmable functional supramolecular assemblies of multiplexes by tuning the sequences of both bm-PNA and DNA/RNA/PNA.
Biogeochemical processes and benthic impacts of submarine mine tailings: A case study from Jøssingfjorden, Norway.ECO1
Authors: L. M. O. Nixon, T. Kutti, H. Haflidason, H. Dahle, I. Thorseth
Venue: Marine pollution bulletin
Published: 2026-11-20
Abs · PDF
Abstract
An interdisciplinary study was conducted on mine tailings deposited in Jøssingfjorden (SW Norway) and at the fjord entrance (Dyngadjupet) between 1960 and 1994 to identify the main biogeochemical processes within the deposits and assessing their impacts on the benthic environment and fauna. The dataset includes sedimentological, (bio)geochemical, microbial community, and benthic fauna analyses of sediment cores collected in 2012, 2017 and 2018, complemented by seabed video transects. The results reveal steep redox gradients and cycling of Mn/Fe, S and C, likely mediated by microbial degradation of residual organic process chemicals in the tailings. These subseabed processes promote mobilisation and upward migration of heavy metals and generate patchy sulphide and methane seepage at the seabed in Jøssingfjorden. Ongoing discharge and resedimentation of tailings particles after 1994 have resulted in sedimentation rates substantially higher than pre-deposition levels. Low oxygen availability, toxic metals, methane and rapid sedimentation presumably prevent succession of benthic communities towards an equilibrium community. This was particularly evident in Jøssingfjorden, where patchily distributed, surface-dwelling aggregations of the pollution-tolerant brittle star Amphiura filiformis dominated. Recovery of the benthic environment and recolonisation by reference communities will likely require accumulation of >15 cm of uncontaminated natural sediments, corresponding to the present bioturbation depth, a process estimated to take several hundred years. The findings underscore the importance of biogeochemical and sedimentological assessments in evaluating the long-term environmental consequences of submarine tailings deposits. They also raise questions regarding the suitability of the Water Framework Directive indices for classifying the ecological quality of such deposits.
