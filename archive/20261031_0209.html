GradSplat: Gradients Inside the Forward Pass for Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE8
Authors: Jingong Chen, Linlian Jiang, Guangyuan Li, Xinxin Zuo, Yang Wang...
Published: 2026-10-28
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting predicts a scene from a few images, but our predictor is less accurate than its own Gaussians optimized per scene on those images. This gap lies mostly in the shape of the Gaussians, their scale and rotation, since optimizing the shape alone recovers almost all of it and opacity and color recover less. Gradient steps on the input views correct this shape, and the model holds these views at inference. Log-anisotropy correction changes the axis ratios of each Gaussian in log space at constant volume, and in-forward gradient refinement renders the Gaussians into the input views and applies the gradient of the photometric loss with a learned recurrent update, first to opacity and color and then to the shape. GradSplat is a feed-forward model that refines its own Gaussians by learned gradient steps inside its forward pass, with no target view. On both complete six-view RealEstate10K protocols, it has the best PSNR and SSIM among nine methods and keeps its accuracy from $6$ to $42$ input views. Without fine-tuning, it is the most accurate on DL3DV and has the best PSNR and SSIM on ScanNet++. The code will be released at https://github.com/Jingong-Chen/GradSplat/.

We present UMI-Live, a full-stack open-source bimanual UMI system for in-the-wild data collection. GeoLift: Grounding Video Spatial Reasoning in Explicit 3D MapsCORE7
Authors: Xing-Lin Li, Ting-Ting Long, Jing-Zhi Zhou, Jia-Jing Chen, Jian Yang...
Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-10-24
Abs
Abstract
Video spatial reasoning requires multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to understand how objects are arranged and related across viewpoints. While these models can recognize objects in individual frames, integrating these observations to solve complex spatial tasks remains challenging. We examine route planning as a representative task and observe that existing models become substantially less accurate as the number of required turns increases, with several models achieving accuracy close to random guessing on routes requiring three or more turns. This finding suggests that recognizing landmarks alone is insufficient: models must also connect observations across viewpoints and track how landmarks are arranged in a shared space. Motivated by this observation, we introduce GeoLift, a training-free framework that uses feed-forward 3D reconstruction to map objects detected in individual frames into a shared metric 3D scene. GeoLift combines a frozen MLLM for 2D grounding with pretrained geometry models for scene reconstruction. It then uses shared geometric regions, called superpoints, to associate observations of the same object across views without a learned matcher. The reconstructed 3D map enables explicit computation of spatial relations and multistep routes, and provides geometric evidence for estimating distances and sizes. With all model weights frozen, GeoLift improves Qwen3-VL-4B's average score on VSI-Bench from 55.4% to 64.1%, including an increase in route-planning accuracy from 30.9% to 53.6%, and achieves 59.8% accuracy on MindCube. These results show that connecting frame-level object grounding through a shared 3D map can improve complex spatial reasoning without task-specific training.

Abstract
We present LucidLyra, a framework that makes generatively reconstructed 3D scenes directly queryable for semantics and instances in a feed-forward manner from video latents. While generative 3D reconstruction can synthesize virtual environments from a single image, downstream applications in robotics and physical AI require not only visual realism but also access to scene semantics and object identities. Existing 3D understanding approaches, however, require captured multi-view scenes, have limited generalization capabilities, or rely on expensive per-scene processing and optimization. To bridge this gap, our method distills complementary knowledge from a camera-controlled video diffusion model and frozen 2D vision foundation models into a unified model to jointly infer 3D Gaussians encoding geometry, appearance, open-vocabulary semantics, and class-agnostic instance cues. The resulting scenes support novel-view rendering, open-vocabulary segmentation, and object grouping without per-scene optimization, while training requires neither manual 3D annotations nor captured multi-view data. Our method outperforms baselines combining state-of-the-art 3DGS segmentation approaches with generative 3D scene reconstruction on curated synthetic and real-image-conditioned benchmarks, while preserving reconstruction quality. Furthermore, our latent formulation naturally extends to monocular video, reconstructing structured dynamic 3D scenes across time. By turning renderable but unstructured outputs into queryable 3D worlds, LucidLyra enables downstream applications including 3D scene editing, 3D asset mining, and robotic simulation.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Smartphone indoor localization requires low-cost, robust positioning across scene transitions and phone-use variations. Pedestrian dead reckoning (PDR) enables infrastructure-free deployment but accumulates errors from heading drift, structural transitions, and carrying-mode changes. Existing methods often treat contextual cues as a single event stream or local correction signal, limiting their ability to address distinct localization errors. This paper presents a framework enhanced by hierarchical multi-sensor context recognition. A jointly data- and model-driven recognition model predicts environment, scene, and activity probabilities, which are converted into scene-event and action-context segments. Scene-event segments guide scene-constrained PDR correction through corridor heading constraints, landmark attraction, fixed-lag backtracking, and vertical-transition handling. Action-context segments drive action-aware RoNIN-based position compensation for carrying-mode-dependent drift. Experiments on cross-floor and multi-action trajectories against LiDAR-SLAM references show that the proposed framework outperforms PDR and representative learned inertial odometry baselines, demonstrating that hierarchical semantics provide reliable, localization-specific correction cues.

Abstract
LiDAR-based 3D perception underpins autonomous driving and robotics, yet deploying these models safely requires stress-testing them against adversarial inputs before real-world use. Although state-of-the-art adversarial attacks exist, their implementations are often fragmented across individual repositories, each tied to a specific architecture or task and requiring manual environment reconstruction and model-specific integration code. We present LIDARSHIELD, a unified and fully Dockerized framework that reverses this workflow: rather than installing the target model inside the toolkit, LIDARSHIELD exposes any model through three lightweight HTTP endpoints, namely /info, /forward, and /gradient As a result, connecting a new model or dataset requires only a single loader function or adapter. The framework supports both 3D semantic segmentation and 3D object detection through the same engine-service protocol, integrating a task-aware suite of state-of-the-art attacks, including PGD and Square Attack for segmentation, and point perturbation, point removal, point injection, and LiDAttack for detection. This enables systematic exposure of corner cases and vulnerabilities across heterogeneous LiDAR backbones. Robust LIDAR Sensor-to-Sensor Domain Generalization via Accumulated-Point Scene CompletionCORE5
Authors: Christoph B Rist, Larissa T Triess, Markus Enzweiler, Dariu M Gavrila
Published: 2026-10-29
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study domain generalization for LIDAR semantic segmentation. Deep neural networks achieve strong performance on 3D point clouds, yet real-world LIDAR exhibits sensor-specific sampling patterns that hinder transfer across datasets. In many applications, performance on completely unseen sensors is crucial. A model should preferably work on a new setup without prior access to its data or labels. To this end, we introduce a source-only training scheme that adds an occupancy prediction auxiliary objective. We aggregate source-domain scans into a simple world model and use it as the supervision signal, replacing single-scan standard segmentation training. A key property of our approach is that it does not alter the input scan. During training and testing the network consumes the same single-frame LIDAR input, only the supervision signal is enriched via the occupancy objective. At deployment, there is no runtime overhead as the auxiliary head can be dropped for pure segmentation, or kept to obtain scene completion alongside semantic segmentation. In an experimental evaluation across distinct LIDAR datasets, we demonstrate that a model's generalization to new domains can be significantly affected by how it is supervised. In certain setups, performance on unseen domains improves without any explicit domain adaptation (no target scans, no target labels, no sensor priors) and without prediction refinement. At the same time, our method maintains competitive source-domain segmentation performance and incurs no additional inference cost.
ACD-GS: Asymmetric Curvature-aware Densification for 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE5
Authors: Houqiang Zhong, Tianchi Zhu, Qiang Hu, Xiaoyun Zhang, Li Song...
Published: 2026-10-29
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for real-time photorealistic novel view synthesis. However, its training-time densification remains largely heuristic, often resulting in redundant Gaussian proliferation and inaccurate geometric reconstruction. Existing methods predominantly rely on first-order screen-space gradients to trigger cloning and splitting, which fail to distinguish true geometric discontinuities from high-frequency textures or noisy residuals. To address this limitation, we propose ACD-GS, an Asymmetric Curvature-aware Densification framework for compact and geometry-aware 3D Gaussian optimization. Our key insight is that second-order depth responses provide a more reliable structural prior for density control, enabling asymmetric regulation of cloning and splitting to better capture geometric discontinuities. Building on this, we introduce a multi-view photometric consistency constraint to suppress transient single-view errors, together with a capacity-aware adaptive regulation strategy that balances densification and pruning during training. Unlike post-hoc compression or quantization-based approaches, ACD-GS directly prevents redundant Gaussian generation during training, yielding inherently compact representations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method reduces nearly 60% of Gaussian count and storage while maintaining competitive rendering fidelity, consistently outperforming existing methods across multiple datasets. Our code is available in https://waveviewer.github.io/ACD-GS/.

Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is increasingly explored as an input representation for 3D perception. Yet it remains unclear how current 3D architectures exploit heterogeneous Gaussian attributes. This ambiguity makes accuracy hard to interpret and limits principled architecture design for 3DGS perception. However, existing object-level 3DGS datasets lack the appearance variation or artifact control needed for systematic attribute-level analysis. We introduce COGS, a large-scale 3DGS dataset with 15,675 artifact-filtered and appearance-rich objects across 40 categories. Built on COGS, we establish COGS-Bench for object classification and attribute-usage diagnosis, complemented by external validation. Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has rapidly become the preferred representation for high-fidelity novel view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction. However, the learned representation encodes sensitive visual information including human faces, license plates, documents, and computer screens, raising significant privacy concerns when trained splats are shared or deployed. In this work, we address the underexplored problem of privacy preservation in Gaussian Splatting by proposing a representation-level anonymization framework that directly anonymizes a trained splat. Given a trained splat and a user-provided reference view identifying sensitive content, our method localizes the corresponding regions in the underlying 3D representation and directly anonymizes them, ensuring that the sensitive information is absent from all subsequently rendered novel views while preserving the overall scene geometry and visual fidelity. We achieve this post-training anonymization in less than 1 second. Furthermore, we extend our framework with a privacy-by-design training strategy that prevents sensitive content from being embedded into the representation during optimization, enabling the generation of privacy-preserving splats directly from training images. Our method achieves a remarkably low Face Similarity Measure (FSM) of 9.79% at a retained Face Detection Rate (FDR) of 39.47% and makes sensitive text completely unreadable with an OCR Character Error Rate of 1.00. Our work aims to serve as the primary stepping stone towards privacy-aware 3D representations and motivates further research along these directions.

Abstract
Novel view synthesis (NVS) in degraded scenes aims to reconstruct 3D scenes from multi-view observations affected by various degradations, and to generate clear and photorealistic images at unseen viewpoints. Despite recent advances, existing methods often rely on degradation-specific models, limiting their generalization across diverse extreme environments. Generative models provide rich visual priors, but their outputs often suffer from pixel misalignment and color deviations, which can propagate into the reconstructed 3D scene and compromise reconstruction quality. Furthermore, the high computational and memory costs of 3D reconstruction hinder the practical application of NVS in extreme environments. To this end, we propose MLLM-GS, a multimodal large language model (MLLM) prior-guided framework for fast NVS in extreme environments. MLLM-GS consists of two branches: MLLM prior-guided view enhancement and fast 3D scene reconstruction. The latter improves 3DGS efficiency by optimizing rasterization, Gaussian memory layout, and parameter updates, reducing redundant computation, memory-access overhead, and optimization costs. Extensive experiments on multiple degraded scenes demonstrate that MLLM-GS effectively handles various severe degradations and achieves high-quality NVS with significantly lower reconstruction time and memory consumption. Abstract
Efficient crowd navigation requires anticipating dynamic collision-free space without relying on fragile object tracking. We propose the Don't Risk It Planner (DRIP), an end-to-end LiDAR-only planning framework that couples occupancy forecasting with trajectory-conditioned clearance and uncertainty estimation. Operating directly on LiDAR-derived occupancy grid histories, DRIP predicts a distribution over minimum robot-obstacle clearance for candidate control sequences to evaluate collision risk. Abstract
4D perception models, which classify 3D point-cloud videos, are being built into robots, assistive monitoring and AR, yet before deploying one a practitioner cannot check which cue it relies on: how a body part moves, or what shape it has. 3D attribution is single-frame, video attribution is confined to RGB pixels, gradient maps need white-box access, attention is absent from state-space backbones, and point-level perturbations assume a point correspondence across frames that real depth and LiDAR data lack. We present Kinematic Causal Attribution (KCA), a black-box, interventional attribution method that queries only the model's outputs. Its freeze operator removes a region's motion while keeping its shape, and a shape-free proxy removes the shape while keeping the motion. Together they span a motion$\times$geometry cube for every tracked region, whose order-symmetric Shapley decomposition is complete and whose interaction is measured rather than assumed away. The operators rewrite per-frame point sets, so they need no correspondences, and a context-averaged estimator handles structures that span several regions. We validate KCA on the official PSTNet, P4Transformer and Mamba4D trained on MSR-Action3D. The decisive test is an audit: two binary tasks are built from real clips whose classes differ only in the motion or only in the shape of the same arm, and the motion share of the arm's importance follows the defining cue on all three backbones (0.83 vs. 0.60 on P4Transformer, 0.86 vs. 0.30 on PSTNet, 0.92 vs. 0.61 on Mamba4D), a distinction no single attribution map can make. The split costs no faithfulness: the necessity maps have the lowest deletion AUC on P4Transformer and PSTNet, tie with the best baseline on Mamba4D, and lose their correlation with the trained model's once the weights are randomised. Skeleton ground truth, re-sampling stability and cost complete the validation.

Abstract
Feed-forward 3D foundation models have recently reshaped multi-view reconstruction, yet dynamic 4D understanding still often relies on large-scale motion supervision or hand-crafted, architecture-specific heuristics. We ask whether frozen 3D foundation model tokens already encode motion-relevant information that can be exposed by lightweight readout functions, rather than re-learning motion-specific features with high-capacity decoders. Through probing on three representative backbones, we find that static--dynamic separation is decodable from frozen token representations. Our rank-restricted and spectral analyses on $\pi^3$X reveal that the recovered motion cues are concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace. Abstract
Crack propagation provides richer information than static crack observations for evaluating structural conditions of concrete infrastructure and planning maintenance strategies. Unlike static crack detection, which can now be largely automated using computer vision, vision-based crack tracking is a coupled cross-view and cross-time problem: the same physical crack must retain its identity as camera viewpoints change, while newly developed growth must be distinguished from existing crack segments in a unified reference view. Existing segmentation and 3D reconstruction methods can recover crack masks or scene geometry, but they do not preserve crack identity across views or quantify temporal extension. To address this gap, this study formulates crack growth monitoring as a cross-view and cross-time crack-instance tracking task and proposes a geometry-assisted, morphology-aware framework. The framework first uses image-based geometric registration to align observations from different viewpoints into a shared structural reference, then applies morphology-aware instantiation to assign physically interpretable crack identities, and finally performs temporal mapping in the unified view to distinguish newly developed growth from existing crack segments. To enable evaluation, a temporally annotated dataset was established and used, covering three representative cracking scenarios: single dominant crack propagation, mixed-mode branching, and multiple-crack interaction, with pixel-level masks and cross-time instance labels. Experimental results show that the proposed framework can establish loose cross-view identity correspondence under changing viewpoints, while strict spatial overlap and growth localization remain limited by early micro-crack segmentability and projection drift, highlighting directions for future crack-monitoring research.

Abstract
Aorto-left ventricular tunnel (ALVT) is a rare congenital extracardiac communication between the ascending aorta and the left ventricle (LV) that originates above the aortic valve leaflets; approximately 177 cases have been published. We report an 11-month-old boy referred after a “to-and-fro” murmur was detected on routine examination. External transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) had suggested a subpulmonary ventricular septal defect (VSD) with an aneurysm of the right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT). At our institution, TTE showed a dilated LV (z-score +4.2) with preserved ejection fraction (EF, approximately 60%) and a tunnel with predominantly diastolic flow from the ascending aorta into the LV. Abstract
Reconstructing a coherent 3D scene from uncurated image collections remains challenging due to the presence of irrelevant or non-overlapping views. Recent work has shown that feed-forward reconstruction models contain internal view-consistency signals that support distractor rejection. However, reference-conditioned filtering relies on a curated clean reference, which is not guaranteed in practical image collections. To address this limitation, we introduce FreeView, a training-free framework that jointly selects a filtering reference and rejects distractor views from an uncurated collection. FreeView leverages collection-level consensus to identify a representative view that serves as a reliable reference for filtering. It then adapts view rejection to each input collection by combining complementary visual and geometric evidence. This approach preserves useful views under substantial appearance and viewpoint variations while rejecting distractors. FreeView integrates with both VGGT and pi^3, removing the need for a manually curated reference or manual calibration of view rejection. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate high clean-view retention and distractor rejection rates, leading to reliable 3D reconstruction from uncurated views.

Abstract
Automated prostate lesion detection from diffusion-weighted MR imaging (DWI) conventionally relies on reconstructed images and does not fully use all acquisition information. We present an end-to-end network that uses complex multicoil DWI data and coil sensitivity information for prostate lesion segmentation. We included 269 fastMRI prostate examinations with additional voxel-level lesion annotations. Of these, 64 examinations contained PIRADS 4 or 5 lesions. The proposed network, VarNet-Seg, consists of a k-space variational network (VarNet) with six variational cascades and an image-space 3D segmentation U-Net. Comparator models used the same segmentation architecture with fully coil-combined images (CC-Seg), fixed root-sum-of-squares reconstruction (RSS-Seg), or fixed SENSE reconstruction (SENSE-Seg). We performed ablations with end-to-end training and data consistency (DC) enabled or disabled. We evaluated lesion segmentation using Dice and patient-level detection using AUROC in five-fold cross-validation with full sampling, simulated undersampling, added noise, or both. VarNet-Seg achieved the highest Dice at every undersampling level. At eight-fold acceleration, Dice was 0.22 for VarNet-Seg, 0.14 for SENSE-Seg and 0.16 for VarNet-Seg (no DC). The paired difference from SENSE-Seg was 0.08 (95% CI, 0.04-0.11). With full sampling, VarNet-Seg and RSS-Seg performed similarly (0.40 vs 0.39). Patient-level AUROC decreased from 0.75 with full sampling to 0.63 at eight-fold acceleration; corresponding RSS-Seg AUROCs were 0.76 and 0.64. The proposed multicoil network improved prostate lesion segmentation under simulated undersampling compared to conventional models. Matched ablations supported contributions from both end-to-end training and data consistency. Prospective evaluation is needed to establish whether this approach can be used clinically in accelerated prostate DWI.
