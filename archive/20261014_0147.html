Active Reconstruction of Radiance Fields with Expanded Silhouette Refinement.CORE11
Authors: Rui-Qi Li, Yiu-Ming Cheung, Meng-Ke Li, Zhi-Kai Hu
Venue: IEEE transactions on visualization and computer graphics
Published: 2026-10-07
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Radiance field representations have achieved notable success in novel view synthesis and 3D geometry reconstruction. Nevertheless, the reconstruction quality is highly sensitive to both the number of input images and the extent of scene coverage, and it often requires manual effort to capture well-conditioned image collections. To reduce acquisition cost while preserving reconstruction fidelity, active view selection methods aim to determine the next-best-view (NBV) that maximizes information gain at each iteration. However, existing approaches typically restrict the camera pose search space during NBV optimization, which can lead to sub-optimal view selection and limited reconstruction quality. In this paper, we expand the NBV candidates sampling space to identify informative image collections that jointly balance local surface detail and global geometric completeness. We propose a two-stage Silhouette Refinement Candidate Generation (SRCG) framework that leverages prior knowledge of well-composed viewpoints to generate candidate views with improved object coverage. We optimize the coarse camera candidates in a differentiable manner through volume rendering, guided by a proposed Expanded Silhouette Loss that encourages effective silhouette capture. Extensive evaluations on synthetic and real-world benchmarks using Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) backbones demonstrate that the proposed approach produces candidate views with superior surface coverage and pixel density and consistently improves novel view synthesis across diverse object categories.
FoundationVIO: Enforcing Metric Scale in Feed-forward Visual-Inertial OdometryCORE9
Authors: Jin Rhee, Yuhao Zhang, Frank Dellaert, Maurice Fallon
Published: 2026-10-10
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Feed-forward geometric foundation models (GFMs) directly predict dense geometry and camera poses from batches of images. Emerging visual SLAM methods chain together consecutive GFM batches to form dense point cloud maps. A core challenge is how to address the ambiguous geometric scale of GFM predictions: independent predictions are locally consistent up-to-scale but not constrained by any direct physical measurements. Models trained on data with known scale improve the consistency of GFM reconstructions but again do not directly measure it. Consequently, trajectories and 3D reconstructions from GFM-enabled SLAM methods inherit this issue. Motivated by parallels with classical visual-inertial odometry, we introduce FoundationVIO which integrates inertial sensing with GFM predictions to recover metric scale reconstruction using a factor-graph based approach. We demonstrate odometry estimates competitive with classical state-of-the-art VIO methods and accurate dense reconstructions that outperform GFM-enabled SLAM methods. We also add a minimal GFM-based loop-closure mechanism to create a full Visual-Inertial SLAM system capable of creating globally accurate reconstructions and pose estimates. We evaluate our method on three datasets with well known sensor systems and demonstrate its compatibility on ubiquitous camera and IMU sensors. The method runs at approximately real-time on a consumer-grade laptop GPU. Code: https://github.com/ori-drs/FoundationVIO.

Abstract
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models have achieved remarkable progress through large-scale training and strong learned priors. However, they remain fragile when applied to dynamic in-the-wild videos, where non-rigid motion, occlusions, and illumination changes are prevalent. A key limitation lies in their reliance on expensive depth and camera pose annotations, which hinders scaling to diverse real-world dynamic data and restricts generalization. To address this issue, we propose \textbf{PURE}, an unlabeled post-training refinement framework that improves depth and camera estimation without requiring ground-truth supervision. Instead of relying on annotations, PURE elegantly distills robust structural knowledge from state-of-the-art 2D priors into 4D feed-forward architectures. PURE leverages relative depth constraints, affine-invariant geometry distillation, and pose refinement based on feature correspondences to stabilize geometric predictions under challenging dynamic conditions. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world dynamic benchmarks demonstrate that PURE delivers consistent improvements across multiple state-of-the-art architectures. Furthermore, our data-efficient paradigm effectively mitigates domain shifts, successfully unlocking the architectural limits of these base models in challenging, in-the-wild dynamic scenes.

Abstract
Robots operating in dynamic environments require reliable detection of how their surroundings change over time. Existing learning-based methods largely rely on pairwise 2D image features, which struggle under large viewpoint changes and occlusions, are sensitive to noise, and show limited generalization across domains, while explicit 3D approaches typically require costly offline optimization. We show that the implicit 3D knowledge of Geometric Foundation Models (GFMs) provides a strong basis for addressing these limitations. We introduce Argos, which adapts GFM features for joint scene change detection and 3D reconstruction. To address data scarcity and take a step toward a foundation model for scene change detection, we introduce a large-scale benchmark comprising two synthetic datasets and one real-world dataset, and train jointly across diverse datasets to improve cross-domain generalization. We further introduce Argos-SLAM, a real-time system designed for robotics, which performs online change detection and change-aware 4D mapping. Across benchmarks, our framework substantially outperforms existing baselines, with gains of up to 42.01% in change IoU and 27.91% in F1, while supporting scalable deployment in changing real-world environments.

Abstract
Sparse-view Cone-Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) reconstruction is essential for reducing radiation dose and scan effort, but remains underconstrained due to limited projection information. Recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)-based CBCT reconstruction methods rely on gradient-driven densification, which becomes unstable under sparse acquisition and often leads to over-smoothed structures. We propose AD-GS, an Anatomical Density-guided 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that formulates Gaussian densification as an attenuation-aware Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling process. By interpreting Gaussians as samples from an underlying anatomical attenuation distribution, AD-GS adaptively concentrates representation capacity in high-attenuation regions, enabling sharper structural boundaries under sparse projections. To further constrain reconstruction, we incorporate a semantic regularization based on zero-shot features from Segment Anything in Medical Images, enforcing multi-view consistency through semantic priors. Experiments on the ToothFairy3 dental CBCT dataset demonstrate that AD-GS achieves superior structural fidelity and boundary preservation compared with existing 3DGS-based approaches under sparse-view settings within the dental and maxillofacial setting. Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent advances in video diffusion enable novel-view video synthesis from a single input video, supporting world modeling, embodied intelligence, and robotic simulation. However, existing methods process camera trajectories independently, lacking the cross-trajectory consistency needed for coherent, navigable 4D worlds. We introduce Demiurge, an autoregressive video rerendering pipeline built on a dynamic 4D point-cloud memory cache. It promotes cross-trajectory consistency through four components: (1) an appearance video diffusion model that synthesizes photorealistic novel-view videos conditioned on the 4D memory; (2) a memory-consistent video depth completion model that aligns known-region depths with cached geometry and completes missing regions, mitigating scale ambiguity and discontinuities during memory updates; (3) memory-aware dual-stream warping that provides high-fidelity visual guidance and occlusion cues for novel-view synthesis; and (4) geometry-aware video refinement that enhances perceptual quality. Extensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance and three key capabilities: coherent spatiotemporal lattice construction over dense multi-view trajectories; large-parallax video rerendering under challenging camera motions, including 180-degree rotations with no input-view overlap; and geometry-faithful outputs that can be directly optimized into high-quality 3D Gaussian Splatting representations with minimal artifacts. Published: 2026-10-09
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Position tracking is a well-studied problem in Euclidean navigation, yet comparatively little attention has been given to navigation on general manifolds, where motion is governed by the underlying geometry of the space. In this work, we formalize the problem of manifold navigation as a velocity-driven tracking task and propose a coordinate-chart-based learning framework in which learning models operate entirely in a Euclidean representation. Predicted trajectories are mapped to the manifold via an immersion function prior to error computation, allowing geometric consistency to be evaluated without explicitly enforcing manifold constraints during learning. Within this framework, we focus on Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) for sequential position prediction on curved manifolds after comparisons with alternative recurrent architectures. We evaluate our GRU models, trained with pertinent custom loss functions, against both a standard MSE loss function and a naive vector-addition baseline. We evaluate our approach on both analytically (and numerically) defined manifolds, as well as a surface reconstructed from a LiDAR point cloud. Our results show that recurrent models can learn to approximate manifold-constrained dynamics from velocity sequences and consistently outperform Euclidean baselines. Our results demonstrate that certain custom loss functions significantly improve long-horizon prediction accuracy compared to conventional point-wise loss functions.

Abstract
Exterior Gaussian-splat twins for real-estate listings are captured to a fixed recipe: a consumer drone orbits a single property in several overlapping passes at stepped altitudes, with occasional vertical ascents, so the imagery is dense, sequential, GPS- and time-tagged, and revisits each facade from many angles. This structure is tantalizing: it suggests camera poses could be recovered by a dense pose-graph optimization (PGO) whose only unknowns are the poses, far fewer than the joint pose-and-point problem bundle adjustment (BA) solves. PGO is mature in robotics, but almost always as post-processing: it redistributes the drift of an already-decent trajectory once loop closures tie it together. Splatting captures push that regime to its limit, every frame closing loops with dozens of others, raising a clean question: does pose-only optimization reach BA accuracy? We built a system specialized to this capture structure, not a general-purpose SfM tool, to test it. It beat COLMAP on speed but never on quality; this paper is the candid post-mortem. The shortfall arises from several interacting causes, hand-set weights, greedy merging and a noisy GPS ruler, together with two limits inherent to the formulation: SE(3) edges carry no scale degree of freedom, so independently scaled sub-maps drift when merged, and a two-view edge stops short of the coupling one 3D point imposes on every frame that sees it. A viable design must add back Sim(3) constraints, absolute GPS priors, retained structure, and uncertainty-aware weighting
MaskGaussian++: Probabilistic Masks for General 3D Gaussian Representation.CORE5
Authors: Yi-Fei Liu, Yuan-Yuan Gao, Yi-Fan Zhan, Sheng-Jing Xu, Yu-Ning Gong...
Venue: IEEE transactions on pattern analysis and machine intelligence
Published: 2026-10-07
Abs
Abstract
While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has demonstrated remarkable performance in novel view synthesis and realtime rendering, the high memory consumption due to the use of millions of Gaussians limits its practicality. To mitigate this issue, improvements have been made by pruning unnecessary Gaussians, either through a hand-crafted criterion or by using learned masks. However, these methods deterministically remove Gaussians based on a snapshot of the pruning moment, leading to sub-optimized reconstruction performance from a long-term perspective. To address this issue, we introduce MaskGaussian++, which models Gaussians as probabilistic entities rather than permanently removing them, and utilize them according to their probability of existence. To achieve this, we propose a masked-rasterization technique that enables unused yet probabilistically existing Gaussians to receive gradients, allowing for dynamic assessment of their contribution to the evolving scene and adjustment of their probability of existence. Beyond vanilla 3DGS, this probabilistic masking is a model-agnostic mechanism for the broader Gaussian family: we adapt it to neural (anchor-based) Gaussians with a shared anchor mask, and to distributed multi- GPU Gaussians with a distributed masked-rasterizer, through which we further uncover a log-linear scaling law of Gaussian Splatting spanning up to 90 million primitives, and to dynamic 4D Gaussians with a single time-invariant per-primitive mask that transfers across two structurally different 4D representations. Extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method: on vanilla 3DGS it prunes over 60% of Gaussians on average with only a 0.02 PSNR decline, halves the storage of neural Gaussians, scales $2\times$ more efficiently than Grendel-GS in large-scale distributed training, and compressing dynamic 4D Gaussians several-fold at on-par or better quality.
Align-VAE: Aligning Attention and Geometry in VecSet VAEs for 3D ReconstructionCORE3
Authors: Huaizhi Qu, Mingxin Yang, Xintong Han, Fangneng Zhan, Chunchao Guo
Published: 2026-10-10
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Set-based variational autoencoders (VecSet VAEs) represent 3D shapes as compact sets of latent tokens. Although each token originates from a query at a sampled surface location, whether these latents truly align with geometry remains unclear. We investigate this alignment and identify four issues: (i) attention-sink-like tokens that attract queries from across the shape; (ii) spatially periodic repetitions in decoder attention responses; (iii) mismatched normal information in encoder and decoder query features; and (iv) missing supervision of the intrinsic relation between signed distance fields and surface normals. To address these issues, we introduce Align-VAE with four corresponding modifications: (i) a shared bank of learnable decoder tokens provides a workspace for global computation; (ii) distance-biased cross-attention guides retrieval toward nearby predicted spatial anchors; (iii) normal-free encoder queries align the two stages' access to normal information while retaining normals in the encoder keys and values; and (iv) a screened Poisson-inspired regularizer supervises surface values and normal-direction derivatives. These changes align latent retrieval and field prediction with geometry without enlarging the per-shape code. On the ABO dataset, Align-VAE reduces Chamfer distance by 26.6% compared to the standard VecSet VAE.

Abstract
4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) has emerged as a powerful dynamic scene representation, supporting real-time high quality rendering but suffers from prohibitive storage overhead. Existing methods typically attempt to solve this by treating compression as a per-scene optimization problem, limiting generalizability and applicability where low-latency compression is required. In this work, we leverage existing image and video codecs and propose a generalized post-training compression framework for 4DGS to address these issues. Specifically, we apply importance-based adaptive quantization to Gaussians according to their importance levels, effectively refining bitrate allocation while remaining fully compatible with existing codecs. To further improve compression efficiency, we utilize the statistical properties of 4DGS and propose motion- and importance-aware packing to reorganize attributes into codec-friendly 2D maps. Our approach can achieve over 40x compression rate in seconds while maintaining high quality, facilitating the practical deployment of 4DGS.

Abstract
Continuous indoor-outdoor navigation connects destination-level reasoning with motion decisions that must respond to current observations. We present VbotNav-0, a cloud-edge dual-policy framework that links these processes through asynchronous policy-to-policy conditioning. The cloud policy produces subgoal guidance from visual history, navigation instructions, and route tokens; the edge policy combines available guidance with current RGB and LiDAR observations to generate local trajectories. This formulation allows the local perception-action loop to continue between cloud updates. A common route representation connects indoor paths and outdoor map routes, and the training recipe combines navigation demonstrations, corrective data, and grounded visual question answering (VQA). Abstract
Robotic platforms combine multi-modal sensors to operate across diverse conditions, but most dense correspondence methods are limited to RGB-RGB or a small set of modality pairs. Training a model for each modality pair scales poorly with the number of sensors and forgoes potential positive transfer across modality pairs. We introduce TartanMatch, a unified model for dense correspondence across all 25 ordered pairs among RGB, event, thermal, depth, and projected LiDAR. TartanMatch adapts an RGB-pretrained correspondence model to all modality pairs within one shared network using lightweight modality adaptation and a mixture of real and synthetic data. Abstract
Guided by life course and linked lives perspectives, this study examines how intergenerational relationships develop as patterns of solidarity, ambivalence, and conflict through negotiation across generational positions within three‐generation Hong Kong Chinese families. Longer shared lifetimes extend the influence of linked lives on intergenerational relationships. Single‐generation perspectives, dyadic frameworks, and static indicators categorize relationships as supportive, mixed, or strained, but they cannot explain how relationship dynamics emerge and evolve through changing needs, roles, and expectations. Crucially, how cultural‐structural realities, often treated as contextual background, operate within this process remains less clear. In‐depth triadic interviews were conducted separately with grandparents, parents, and adult children from 40 families ( N  = 120). Transcripts were analyzed abductively through constant comparison at within‐family dyadic and triadic levels and across families. The analysis developed a triadic relational framework in which alignment and misalignment served as the processual relational mechanism linking family members' recognition, interpretation, and response to patterns of solidarity, ambivalence, and conflict. Across care, obligation, reciprocity, resources, authority, and communication, workable alignment generated solidarity, containable misalignment sustained ambivalence, and intensified misalignment developed into conflict. Relational grounding and cultural‐structural realities infused this mechanism with emotional, historical, moral, and practical weight, driving family members into continuous relational negotiation across linked lives. Intergenerational relationships are dynamic processes continually negotiated and revised across linked lives over the life course.

Abstract
Three-dimensional (3D) modeling of waste pits can support waste pile monitoring and crane-assisted feeding in waste-to-energy (WtE) plants. RGB image-based multi-view stereo (MVS) offers a flexible perception route, but its performance remains limited by non-uniform illumination, weakly textured waste surfaces, local specular highlights, occlusion, and changes in pile morphology. Abstract
Reconstructing 3D Computed Tomography (CT) images from a few X-ray projections is a highly ill-posed inverse problem due to the loss of volumetric information. We propose PhyDiCT, a training-free framework that integrates a differentiable Physics-based forward model, grounded in the Beer-Lambert law, with a text-conditioned Diffusion as a strong prior to reconstruct 3D lung CT images. We refer to our approach as training-free since the prior model is used without fine-tuning, and our goal is to steer the denoising procedure to generate samples consistent with X-ray observations. Hardware-aware Calibrated Clustered Attention for Efficient Visual Geometric TransformersECO1
Authors: Wei-Tian Wang, Shubham Rai, Cecilia De la Parra, Akash Kumar
Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-10-07
Abs
Abstract
The Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) marks a significant leap forward in 3D scene reconstruction, as it is the first model that directly infers all key 3D attributes (camera poses, depths, and dense geometry) jointly in one pass. However, this joint inference mechanism requires global attention layers with extremely long sequences that causes a significant latency bottleneck. In this paper, we propose blockwise clustered attention (BC attention) to accelerate the global attention layers in VGGT. By limiting the clustering within HW-friendly neighborhood blocks, BC attention reduces the computation overhead of query clustering as well as the costly data movement between on- and off-chip memory. This enables BC attention to scale to long sequences and deliver practical latency improvements on GPUs. Moreover, we introduce a hashing hyperplane calibration method and a threshold-based error compensation method to reduce clustering errors efficiently, which is a bottleneck in the current clustered attention mechanism. Overall, our experiments on GPU demonstrate that calibrated BC attention accelerates the global attention layers by 2.10-2.63$\times$ and the whole backbone by 1.77-2.35$\times$ with negligible loss (1%) for large scenes. With a small performance loss (<5%), calibrated BC attention further achieves a 2.26-2.87$\times$ latency improvement on the global attention layers and a 1.90-2.55$\times$ improvement on the backbone.

Abstract
Camera trajectories control viewpoint changes in video generation, scene reconstruction, and robotic perception. Generating them from language requires both scene geometry and target-aware framing. We introduce OmniCam, an autoregressive model that generates camera pose sequences from a single panorama and textual trajectory descriptions. Its geometry-grounded pose token learning combines three components: a panoramic point-cloud encoder for omnidirectional geometric context; hybrid absolute-rotation and relative-translation tokenization with temporally consistent quaternion signs; and separate geometric and semantic conditioning streams with an explicit 3D target anchor. We also construct OmniCaT, containing 267,700 trajectories across four camera behaviors. Component ablations support the use of geometric and target-aware conditioning, while downstream experiments examine camera-controlled video generation and robotic active perception.
