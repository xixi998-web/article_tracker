DPGS-SLAM: Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM for Dynamic Visual SensingCORE8
Authors: Yifan Luo, Takafumi Matsumaru, Vibekananda Dutta, Xin He
Published: 2026-11-09
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper presents DPGS-SLAM (Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM) for dense mapping in environments containing dynamic objects. Objects in dynamic motion remain a significant challenge for dense visual SLAM since dynamic observations can contaminate camera tracking and scene reconstruction. Although 3DGS (3D Gaussian splatting) enables efficient dense mapping, existing 3DGS-SLAM systems may exhibit instability when dynamic objects violate their underlying static-scene assumption. Excluding dynamic observations can reduce their influence on tracking and mapping, but may leave occluded static backgrounds missing or weakly constrained. Our proposed DPGS-SLAM is a monocular RGB SLAM framework that supports provisional representations of insufficiently observed static backgrounds through asynchronous diffusion-based background completion and provenance-aware Gaussian mapping. Dynamic regions are identified through optical-flow-based motion discovery and temporally consistent mask propagation by SAM2. For selected keyframes, a fine-tuned diffusion inpainting model generates plausible background appearance in occluded regions, while metric depth predicted from completed images initializes provisional Gaussian primitives. Provenance labels distinguish generated primitives from observation-supported primitives, excluding generated content from camera-pose estimation and observed-map optimization. When previously occluded backgrounds become visible, eligible generated primitives are removed, and observation-supported primitives are independently initialized from real RGB observations and predicted depth. Experimental results demonstrated competitive camera tracking, with average ATE RMSE values of 1.2 cm on TUM RGB-D and 2.7 cm on BONN RGB-D datasets. On StaDy4D, DPGS-SLAM improved average PSNR by 4.60 dB over WildGS-SLAM using only monocular RGB input.
SpaceMCP: Vision Language Model Spatial Reasoning via Model Context ProtocolCORE7
Authors: Ameen Izhac, Nobuko Yoshida, Ronald Clark
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Despite impressive advances in large language models (LLM), vision-language models (VLM) still exhibit limited spatial reasoning capabilities. Recent progress in feed-forward 3D reconstruction has enabled precise, real-time geometric understanding from visual inputs. In this work, we propose SpaceMCP, a framework that leverages these advances to introduce an MCP-style paradigm for spatial reasoning. Our approach augments a VLM with structured function calls, allowing it to query a scene graph for spatial information such as distances, object positioning, and camera properties. The scene graph is constructed in real time using feed-forward 3D reconstruction to recover metric geometry, combined with open-vocabulary segmentation to identify and localize relevant objects within the scene. Paired with the VLM's strong reasoning capabilities, the VLM is able to combine this primitive information to solve more complex spatial tasks. Without any training, SpaceMCP achieves state-of-the-art results on VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench and VSTI-Bench, outperforming recent spatially post-trained VLMs and improving its base VLM by 15-22 points per benchmark. We argue that, given the contrast between the relatively slow progress in native VLM spatial reasoning and the rapid advances in feed-forward 3D computer vision, this paradigm offers a promising path forward for enabling robust spatial reasoning.
Fog-FWL: A Large-Scale Full-Waveform LiDAR Dataset for Recovering Fog-Hidden ObjectsCORE6
Authors: Kazuma Ikeda, Kotaro Oishi, Ryosei Hara, Ryo Yoshida, Takahiro Kado...
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LiDAR is a core sensor for autonomous driving, yet it is highly vulnerable to fog. Fog scattering produces strong backscatter near the sensor and attenuates reflections from objects behind it, so weak object returns are often discarded by the peak detection that converts received signals into point clouds. Full-waveform LiDAR (FWL), which records the received optical signal before peak detection, retains these weak returns, but the lack of suitable data has prevented research on recovering fog-hidden objects from FWL. We present Fog-FWL, a dataset foundation for this task that consists of a real-world benchmark, Fog-FWL-Real, and a large-scale synthetic training set, Fog-FWL-Sim. Fog-FWL-Real contains 7,527 real foggy FWL frames from 8 scenes and 58 sequences, which is 579x larger than the only existing real foggy FWL data, together with histogram-level Object/Fog/Noise labels, 10,719 3D bounding boxes, and three difficulty levels defined by the attenuation of object reflections. Fog-FWL-Sim contains 12,000 frames generated by our physics-based simulator, Fog-FWL-Simulator, which integrates spatially heterogeneous fog density fields, time-resolved light transport including multiple scattering, and a photon-counting SPAD receiver model. The two subsets share the same sensor format and annotation protocol, enabling a strict sim-to-real evaluation in which no real waveforms are used for training. Our analysis shows that, under the hardest condition, object returns are included in the three strongest peaks for only 34.9% of object rays, underscoring the need for waveform-level recovery. Fog-FWL-Real, Fog-FWL-Sim, and the Fog-FWL-Simulator will be publicly released to facilitate research on fog-robust LiDAR perception.
ReFineR: Single image to dense point cloud registration at pixel level with point splattingCORE6
Authors: Ugo Insalaco, Jean-Emmanuel Deschaud, Pierre Dellenbach
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cameras and LiDARs (Light Detection and Ranging) provide complementary observations of the same scene: cameras capture rich appearance cues while LiDAR sensors provide accurate geometric measurements. In this work, we address the problem of fine image-to-point cloud registration from a single image and a roughly estimated camera pose. On robotic platforms, residual reprojection errors of several pixels are commonly observed even after calibration due to temporal desynchronization, mechanical vibrations, or inaccuracies in point cloud reconstruction. Such errors can lead to decimeter-level geometric discrepancies at moderate distances and significantly affect multiple downstream tasks, such as dense cross-modal supervision, texture mapping, and semantic label transfer. To address this challenge, we introduce ReFineR: an offline framework to finely register individual images to pre-reconstructed point clouds. ReFineR builds upon differentiable point splatting rasterization and a smooth Gradient Orientation Measure (GOM) to iteratively refine the camera pose through analysis-by-synthesis optimization. We evaluated the method on a curated dataset of manually calibrated images collected from heterogeneous data sources. Furthermore, we show that an automatic point cloud rescale factor can be estimated prior to optimization, enabling robust refinement across a wide range of scenarios using a single scale-invariant formulation. The project page can be found at https://refiner-project.github.io/
Reconstruct What You Can: Distractor-free 4D Gaussian SplattingCORE6
Authors: Joel Julin, Liuyue Xie, Ananya Bal, Mose Sakashita, Koichiro Niinuma...
Venue: WACV 2027
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Dynamic reconstruction from casual video must preserve valid subject motion while suppressing transient occluders, shadows, and volumetric effects. Existing methods either suppress valid motion through static-scene assumptions or model all observations jointly, producing ghosting and unstable geometry. We introduce ReCan-GS, a 4D Gaussian Splatting framework that separates coherent scene content from inconsistent observations. It derives a Jacobian-based deformation-sensitivity score from the learned deformation field, using it as an empirical proxy for reconstruction ambiguity. The score guides separation into dynamic Core Gaussians and per-view Distractor Gaussians, or post-hoc pruning of sensitive Core Gaussians. The same criterion supports removal of spatial occluders, photometric artifacts, and evolving smoke or fog without masks, pretrained segmenters, or medium-specific supervision. Experiments on synthetic and real dynamic scenes and synthetic smoke/fog scenes demonstrate its usefulness across the evaluated distractor regimes.
SPEM: Scan-Prior Entropy Modeling for LiDAR Point Cloud CompressionCORE5
Authors: Bojun Liu, Yangzhi Ma, Jie Li, Dong Liu, Li Li
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LiDAR point clouds exhibit scan-induced spatial regularities that are informative for geometry compression. Existing scan-aware approaches often exploit these regularities by transforming coded geometry into spherical or beam-aligned coordinate systems. However, this couples acquisition priors with geometry representation, altering spatial partitioning and quantization, and potentially preventing exact recovery of the original Cartesian geometry. We argue that acquisition priors should guide probability modeling rather than dictate the coding representation. We propose SPEM, a Scan-Prior Entropy Modeling framework that exploits LiDAR scanning regularities within the entropy model without requiring coordinate transformation. We introduce a multi-frequency spherical rotary positional encoding that captures scan-aligned spatial relationships in both backbone self-attention and decoded-context retrieval. Building upon this encoding, we develop a decoded-memory autoregressive predictor that efficiently exploits previously decoded occupancy information through reusable key/value caches. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in both lossy and lossless LiDAR geometry compression, highlighting the effectiveness of scan-prior modeling across different coordinate representations.
GOOSELoc: A Multimodal Benchmark for Ground-to-Aerial Localization in Unstructured Off-Road TerrainCORE5
Authors: Fabian Deuser, Peter Mortimer, Robin-Nico Kampa, Anna Bößendörfer, Mirko Mählisch...
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cross-view pose estimation estimates a ground camera's geographic pose by matching onboard sensor data with geo-referenced overhead imagery. This is particularly relevant when GNSS is unreliable, for example under forest canopy or in urban canyons. Existing metric cross-view benchmarks, such as VIGOR and cross-view KITTI, are dominated by urban and road scenes with strong human-made structure without off-road terrain. We introduce GOOSELoc, a multimodal benchmark for ground-to-aerial localization in unstructured environments, including forests, fields, and rural tracks. GOOSELoc comprises 9,356 scenes recorded across all four seasons around Munich, Germany, pairing geo-referenced aerial orthophotos with surround-view RGB imagery and 3D LiDAR point clouds. Paved/unpaved labels enable analyzing the impact of terrain structure, while the seasonal recordings allow studying the appearance gap to mostly summer-captured orthophotos. To assess the transferability of existing methods, we adapt and fine-tune four state-of-the-art cross-view localization baselines on GOOSELoc. Despite task-specific fine-tuning, the performance degrades substantially in off-road environments. We therefore propose a multimodal baseline combining surround-view imagery and LiDAR geometry, improving localization performance and providing a stronger reference for future work. Data and baselines are released at https://gooseloc.github.io.

Abstract
We present Kosmo-GFM, a scene reconstruction system that integrates a geometry foundation model (GFM), a pose optimization engine (PoseEngine), and reconstruction-derived supervision from KosmoWorld. GFM predicts camera parameters, depth, cross-view correspondences, and dynamic 3D points from images and videos. PoseEngine combines these predictions with geometric optimization to recover scene-level camera poses and geometry for downstream 3D and 4D Gaussian Splatting. KosmoWorld generates images and geometric supervision from reconstructed real-world scenes to improve GFM's geometric predictions. On the Sparse and Medium subsets of SpatialBench, GFM achieves the lowest absolute relative depth error and the highest relative-pose AUC at 5 degrees among the compared methods. It also achieves the lowest mean endpoint errors for dynamic points and scene flow across the five evaluated datasets. Across 34 scenes completed by all compared reconstruction systems, PoseEngine achieves the highest scene-averaged pose AUC at thresholds of 1, 3, and 5 degrees, with a cumulative runtime of 14.07 hours, approximately 20% of GLUEMAP's 69.67 hours. PoseEngine Flash provides a further accuracy-efficiency trade-off, with a measured mixed-precision runtime of 2.62 hours, approximately 4% of GLUEMAP's runtime. Separate scaling experiments show generally favorable trends with increased data, model computation, and training budgets. These results support the use of learned geometric priors within a scene-level reconstruction pipeline.

Abstract
Multi-camera observations in off-road environments vary significantly in reliability, posing challenges when complementing degraded geometric constraints. Motivated by this, we propose Quality-aware View Selection Lidar-Vision Odometry Risk Mapping (QVS-LIVO RiskMap), which decouples visual processing for state estimation from that for semantic mapping. The front end evaluates camera observations through read-only pretracking on a shared lidar-inertial posterior, combining observation health with geometric complementarity. Channel health memory and switching hysteresis govern selection of at most one eligible view for direct photometric correction. The posterior is retained when no view is eligible or the correction fails acceptance. The mapping back end integrates semantic observations from all available views in a shared world grid. Vehicle-scale geometry constrains semantic risk corrections, while spatial and temporal evidence supports risk confirmation. The system also produces a local bird's-eye-view (BEV) risk map and a global 2.5D semantic-terrain risk map with obstacle instance voxels. On two field sequences, QVS-LIVO achieved horizontal root mean square errors of 0.910 m and 1.664 m. It reduced error by 43.3% relative to FAST-LIO2 on the degraded long-route sequence. Across 12 controlled camera-fault configurations involving dropout, black frames and 60 ms timestamp shifts, it maintained complete trajectories with errors of 1.54-2.23 m. When deployed onboard an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, the system generates local and global maps at 9.94 Hz and 2.00 Hz, respectively, with a median local mapping latency of 32.4 ms. These runtime results demonstrate the real-time feasibility of selective visual state updates alongside multi-view semantic mapping under challenging off-road conditions. The code and related resources are available at: https://github.com/zihanPan-HIT/QVS-LIVO.

Abstract
Given a set of LEGO parts and an image of an assembled object, our task is to infer the pose of each part to assemble the object. In contrast with traditional 3D reconstruction, doing so requires satisfying discrete physical constraints, which provide structure but render the problem combinatorial and highly sensitive to small pose errors. To learn a prior over how parts fit together, we train a set-conditioned SE(3) flow model on procedurally generated assemblies. We then finetune this model to inject image conditioning, using 1% as much data. However, while the learned flow captures global structure, it does not enforce connectivity. To address this, we introduce an analytic connectivity-field guidance to pull predictions toward connected assemblies at test time. Through this, we demonstrate improved build quality with model size, pretraining, and guidance. We make our models and data available for research purposes at https://kulits.github.io/BrickFlow/
CT-SGAL-LIO: Causal Continuous Time Refinement of Discrete LiDAR Inertial OdometryCORE3
Authors: Nam Tran, Thu Tran, Toan Nguyen, William J Beksi, Tuan Dang
Venue: ICRA 2027
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
State-of-the-art LiDAR-inertial odometry methods, including those on richer state manifolds, achieve strong global accuracy, yet their pose stream alternates between propagation and correction and can oscillate over short horizons even when globally accurate. Most existing methods follow these approaches: they either modify the filter to enable faster updates or estimate a continuous-time trajectory from raw LiDAR and inertial data. The latter involves extra optimization, delaying state updates and becoming less robust during rapid rotations or accelerations. We propose CT-SGAL-LIO, a continuous-time refinement module that smooths the posterior pose stream of a discrete SGal(3) estimator without modifying the estimator itself. The key novelty is to perform continuous-time trajectory refinement directly on the projected SGal(3) posterior: we fit a cubic trajectory over a bounded window of recent poses and evaluate it at a small fixed lag. This places the query time within the observed interval, so the refined pose is obtained by interpolation rather than extrapolation, while preserving causal operation. CT-SGAL-LIO improves relative trajectory accuracy on 17 sequences from three public benchmarks. Across 17 sequences from GRAND-TOUR, MCD, and GEODE, CT-SGAL-LIO improves short-term motion accuracy while largely preserving global accuracy. RTE improves by up to 70% on GRAND-TOUR and on all seven GEODE sequences, with 9.9-51.1% reductions. A 19.5-min R-Campus run also reduces posterior jumps by 85.4% with only a 4.7 mm change in the end-to-start gap. We plan to release our source code and results to support community validation and reproducibility.

Abstract
Autonomous greenhouse management increasingly requires robotic systems that integrate perception, navigation, environmental regulation, and plant-level intervention. Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Smartphone-distracted pedestrians deviate from social norms unpredictably and cannot be relied upon to yield to approaching robots -- a safety challenge that existing navigation methods are poorly equipped to handle, since they treat all pedestrians uniformly regardless of attention state. We propose an attention-aware navigation framework that classifies each pedestrian as attentive or distracted in real-time using 3D skeletal features and a Random Forest classifier, then dynamically widens cost-map safety margins around distracted individuals to drive proactive avoidance. A within-subjects user study with 12 participants across 252 trials demonstrates significant improvements in perceived smoothness (p = 0.003) and predictability (p = 0.034) over both a LiDAR-only baseline and a trajectory-prediction-only method, without any increase in travel time. Notably, trajectory prediction alone does not improve subjective navigation quality unless coupled with attention-state-aware cost adjustment -- confirming that appropriately responding to who is in front of the robot can matter more than trajectory smoothness alone.

Abstract
Micro aerial vehicles that operate without GPS often run SLAM on ARM-based edge boards, yet there is little published data on how standard RGB-D SLAM systems behave on such hardware. We benchmark RTAB-Map and ORB-SLAM3 on the Raspberry Pi 5 using six TUM RGB-D sequences at three image resolutions, recording tracking coverage, trajectory error, time per frame, memory, CPU load and temperature for every run, and we profile both systems at the stage and function level. RTAB-Map was configured to process and keep every frame, so its back end runs on every frame rather than at its default rate of 1 Hz. On the sequences that every configuration tracked, its front end takes 38-74 ms per frame and its full pipeline 150-462 ms, compared with 13-45 ms and 26-62 ms for ORB-SLAM3. RTAB-Map's main cost is optimizing a pose graph that keeps growing, because its working memory is unbounded by default; ORB-SLAM3's local bundle adjustment costs about as much as its tracking but runs in a parallel thread. At quarter resolution, both systems lose tracking on most sequences, and runs that lose tracking finish sooner and report lower error on the poses they keep, so time and accuracy must be read alongside coverage. Timing is repeatable to within 1-5%, but trajectory error varies by up to 27% between identical runs, so the accuracy differences between the two systems are inconclusive. No thermal throttling occurred.
Topology-Aware 4D Gaussian Splatting for Egocentric VideosCORE3
Authors: Leonardo Vanni, Stefano Gasperini, Federico Tombari, Chiara Plizzari
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reconstructing dynamic scenes and objects from monocular egocentric video is crucial for embodied AI, yet existing 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) approaches struggle with everyday interactions involving topological changes, such as cutting, assembly, opening, or closing. Since 4DGS methods rely on deforming a fixed representation, events that split, merge, or reveal surfaces cause severe visual artifacts. To address this challenge, we introduce TopoEgo, the first benchmark dedicated to topological changes in egocentric video (covering fission, fusion, open, and close), alongside Topo-4DGS, a topology-aware reconstruction method. Topo4DGS jointly models part-level motion and time-varying surface support by assigning independent rigid transformations to interacting parts and dynamically activating or deactivating Gaussians as surfaces appear, disappear, or separate. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Topo-4DGS significantly outperforms state-of-the-art 4DGS approaches across topological changes, especially on post-event frames. Code and data will be made publicly available.

Abstract
Recent large human reconstruction models create remarkable animatable 3D avatars from a single image, but their appearance remains entangled with the illumination and cannot be relighted. In contrast, existing 3D relightable-avatar reconstruction methods typically require person-specific optimization. We present ZARA, a zero-shot feed-forward framework that reconstructs an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar together with its intrinsic attributes from a single image. ZARA couples a pretrained large-scale human reconstruction model with a Material Transformer to inherit geometry priors while learning material-aware Gaussian representations. To preserve localized details, we introduce Gaussian-aligned material feature fusion strategy, which injects multi-scale material features onto individual Gaussians and combines them with geometry tokens. As most human-avatar datasets lack ground-truth material maps, we use pseudo-material labels as conditioning inputs and supervision targets during training. We further introduce MatFace regularization to preserve facial structure and color in albedo prediction under unreliable facial pseudo-material supervision. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ZARA reconstructs animatable and relightable humans within tens of seconds without person-specific optimization or post-processing, outperforming existing baselines in both relighting quality and materials generalization ability. As the ability of recovering the physically-based-rendering attributes of the human, ZARA also enables direct material editing of the avatar's appearance.

Abstract
The computational geometry reconstruction of topology-optimized structures can be conducted by surface-skeleton-based methods. However, skeletons are often non-manifold, which complicates the creation of geometry without topological inconsistencies such as self-intersections. This paper addresses non-manifoldness in a particular geometry reconstruction workflow for 3D topology-optimized structures. Through implicit fusion, robust connections between non-manifold joint regions of the skeleton can be reconstructed. This provides controllable geometric transitions between these entities, which may even improve the structural performance of the resulting solid. From implicit representation, the resulting model is converted back to explicit representation by the marching cubes algorithm, quad and voxel remeshing, and spline-conversion.
