Cereal-legume intercropping for climate-smart livestock production: insights from oat-alfalfa intercropping.CORE7
Authors: A. Alkhtib, M. Tamiru, J. Wamatu
Venue: Journal of the science of food and agriculture
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
BACKGROUND Cereal-legume intercropping can improve forage productivity and quality at the same time as enhancing soil function and reducing environmental impacts, yet integrative evaluations under low-input conditions are scarce. This study assessed oat (Avena sativa L.)-alfalfa (Medicago sativa L.) intercropping for forage yield, nutritive value, soil properties and greenhouse gas emission intensity. A randomized complete block design compared sole oat (80 kg ha-1), sole alfalfa (15 kg ha-1) and oat-alfalfa seed ratios of 3:1, 1:1 and 1:3 without irrigation or fertilisation. Previous intercropping studies examined agronomic productivity and forage quality separately. The current study addresses the gap with an integrated assessment of forage yield, conservation quality, soil health, greenhouse gas emission intensity, livestock production potential and decision metrics of oat-alfalfa under low-input conditions. RESULTS Intercropping increased total forage dry matter yield (by 21%), crude protein and metabolisable energy content, and also reduced neutral detergent fibre, compared to sole oat (P < 0.05). The 1:1 intercrop had significantly the highest land equivalent ratio (1.56) and system productivity index and showed better silage fermentation and dry matter recovery (P < 0.05). At final sampling, intercropping increased soil organic carbon, total nitrogen, cation exchange capacity and available phosphorus, with a small reduction in soil pH (P < 0.05). Predicted global warming potential per unit of potential liveweight gain was lower (37-47%) for intercrops than for monocultures for both hay and silage. Multi-criteria decision analysis consistently ranked the 1:1 intercrop highest across producer-centric, balanced and environment-centric scenarios. CONCLUSION Oat-alfalfa intercropping, particularly at a 1:1 seed ratio, provides a promising climate-smart forage production strategy by simultaneously enhancing productivity, forage quality and soil fertility at the same time as reducing predicted greenhouse gas emission intensity under low-input conditions. © 2026 The Author(s). Divergent responses of soil organic carbon fractions to chronic nutrient additions in alpine meadow vs. alpine steppeCORE6
Authors: Xue-Ying Chen, Pei-Li Shi, Jia-Luo Yu, Ge Hou, N. Zong
Venue: Journal Of Plant Ecology
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Nutrient enrichment profoundly alters soil organic carbon (SOC) dynamics, yet the relative controls of plant versus microbial pathways on specific SOC fractions remains mechanistically unresolved across contrasting grasslands. We investigated the effects of 11-year nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) addition on SOC fractions in a semi-humid alpine meadow (AM) and an arid alpine steppe (AS) on the Northern Tibetan Plateau, aiming to disentangle the plant- and microbe-mediated mechanisms. In AM, where tight plant-microbial coupling prevailed, N addition increased SOC primarily by enhancing plant-derived particulate organic carbon (POC) through increased aboveground net primary productivity (ANPP), but reduced mineral-associated organic carbon (MAOC) via soil acidification-induced microbial suppression. Conversely, in plant-microbial decoupled AS, N addition had no effect on SOC but decreased dissolved organic carbon and slightly increased MAOC, likely mediated by NO3−-N promoting microbial-residue stabilization. P addition reduced SOC in AM by lowering fungal diversity and accelerating POC decomposition, whereas it exerted negligible effects in AS despite increasing ANPP, likely offset by enhanced microbial respiration. N + P additions showed no additive effects on soil carbon fractions in either grassland. Overall, SOC persistence under nutrient enrichment is likely modulated by the balance between plant and microbial nutrient demands, as well as soil resource availability. This suggests that AM may functions as transient sinks while AS acts as slightly conservative reservoirs. Our study underscores the necessity of incorporating ecosystem-specific plant-soil-microbial decoupling mechanisms, driven by inherent water-nutrient coupling and pH shifts, to better understand carbon persistence.

Abstract
The global prevalence of geogenically derived cadmium (Cd) enrichment poses significant risks to agricultural sustainability and food security. Micronutrients such as selenium (Se) usually co-occur with Cd in such regions but exhibit low bioavailability in soils, further causing a “hidden hunger” of nutrient depletion in the food supply chain. Through controlled field experiments, we tested strategically designed biochar-based amendments (clay-, manure-, and iron-enriched variants) at 5–20 t ha−1 on limestone-derived paddy soils to synchronously mitigate Cd mobility while enhancing grain Se accumulation. Biochar amendments decreased grain Cd bioaccumulation by 42.3–85.4% and increased grain Se bioaccumulation by 91.6–357.5% across both growing seasons, with a better effect observed in synergy with yield increase in the second growing season, reflecting longer-term soil conditioning and progressive weathering of the amendments. Among the three amendments, BC-ZVI at 10–20 t ha−1 achieved the fastest and most consistent Cd immobilization, lowering grain Cd below the national safety threshold (0.2 mg kg−1) in both growing seasons while increasing grain Se by 91.6–357.5%. BC-Clay and BC-Manure required the second season to reach comparable Cd performance across all application rates. Elevations in soil pH, CEC, and available phosphorus may have accounted for the (im)mobilization effect. Field and literature data further indicated a decoupling between soil total Cd-Se co-enrichment and their mobile or plant-accumulated pools. Findings from this study demonstrate a practical and effective strategy for producing Se-enriched, low-Cd rice in regions with elevated geogenic Cd-Se background. A field trial was conducted in a karst area specifically characterized by the natural enrichment of cadmium and selenium. Total Cd and Se were co-enriched, whereas their mobile pools showed contrasting responses. Biochar-based amendments successfully stabilized Cd while mobilizing Se, resulting in rice with low Cd and high Se. Contrasting potential mediating roles of DOC and SOM in microplastic effects on soil heavy metals bioavailability: A meta-analysis and machine learning approach.CORE5
Authors: Hai-Bo Zhou, Zhuo Zhen, Ni-Jing Zhong, Chang-Zhou Yan, Yang-Ying Zhao...
Venue: Journal of hazardous materials
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs
Abstract
Microplastics (MPs) and heavy metals (HMs) often co-occur in soil, yet the role of soil organic matter (SOM) fractions in mediating the effects of MPs on HMs bioavailability remains unclear. Here, data from 52 studies were used to investigate whether dissolved organic carbon (DOC) and soil organic matter (SOM) serve as contrasting mediating pathways, by integrating meta-analysis, machine learning, and structural equation modeling (SEM). MPs increased DOC, SOM, and HMs bioavailability by 14.84%, 13.82%, and 5.21%, respectively. DOC changes positively correlated with HMs bioavailability, whereas SOM showed a U-shaped relationship. MPs size, time, and soil pH were identified as common drivers across all responses. Biodegradable MPs and higher water holding capacity promoted DOC increases and HMs mobilization, while higher MPs concentrations enhanced SOM accumulation and HMs immobilization. SEM supported DOC and SOM as two opposing mediating pathways: DOC exerted a positive mobilization effect, while SOM exerted a negative immobilization effect. Their dynamic balance may contribute to the net direction of HMs bioavailability. These findings reveal that MPs influence HMs bioavailability primarily through two contrasting OM pathways, rather than through direct MPs-metal interactions. Distinguishing DOC from SOM is therefore critical for assessing the ecological risks of MPs-HMs co-contamination in soil.

Abstract
Spatial gradients around power plants can vary among environmental matrices and between seasons, making proximity alone an incomplete predictor of contamination. This study examined soil chemistry, seven reported metals, and 16 priority polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) at nominal distances of 0, 300, and 600 m around the Trans-Amadi and Omoku gas-fired power plants, Rivers State, Nigeria. Etche served as a separate control site. Soil and plant sampling covered three dates per season between December 2024 and July 2025; soil was sampled at 0–10 and 10–20 cm layers, and waterleaf and cassava roots and leaves were analysed. Descriptive comparisons combined seasonally tabulated soil properties, heavy metals and soil PAH diagnostic ratios. Wet-season soil pH increased by 1.223–1.856 units at Trans-Amadi and 0.844–1.190 units at Omoku. Heavy metals generally reflected concentrations with distance, except manganese; selected crop plots also showed non-monotonic zinc profiles. PAH concentrations did not follow a distance-decay pattern. Trans-Amadi soil and Omoku waterleaf showed prominent intermediate-distance maxima, while Omoku cassava profiles were comparatively uniform across the three points. At Trans-Amadi, dry-season surface-soil LMW/HMW ratios decreased from 1.11025 at 0 m to 0.31331 at 300 m and 0.01034 at 600 m; at Omoku they were 4.23204, 0.84781, and 2.78897, respectively. Seasonal changes in these profiles further demonstrated that compositional gradients differed by site. The observations identify spatial and seasonal heterogeneity.

Abstract
To investigate the distribution characteristics and driving factors of soil potentially toxic elements after land reclamation in a mining area, this study focused on the soil potentially toxic elements distribution in a Hippophae rhamnoides plantation within the Juxinlong Coal Mine reclamation area in Ordos, Inner Mongolia. Soil sampling gradients were established at distances of 1/8 (0.25 m), 1/4 (0.5 m), and 1/2 (1 m) of the inter-tree distance from the standard tree. Abandoned grassland was used as a control group. The contents and distribution characteristics of potentially toxic elements including Cadmium (Cd), Copper (Cu), Lead (Pb), Chromium (Cr), and Arsenic (As) in the soil were analyzed to assessed their potential ecological risks, and identified the driving factors. The results showed that the contents of Pb, Cu, Cr, Cd, and As in the study area increased as the distance from the tree trunk decreased. The contents of Pb, Cu, Cr, and Cd in the abandoned grassland were generally higher than those in the Hippophae rhamnoides treatment groups, while As showed significant enrichment at the 1/8 plant spacing distance, with its content increasing from 4.03 mg·kg⁻¹ to 9.35 mg·kg⁻¹.The comprehensive potential ecological risk index (RI) increased as the distance decreased, reaching an extremely high-risk level near the standard trees (1/8 plant spacing), with RI values ranging from 434.58 to 581.17, and Cr was identified as the key risk factor. Soil moisture indicators (field water-holding capacity, CWC; saturated water content, SW) were the core driving factors regulating the contents of As, Cd, and Cu, exerting significant negative direct effects (with path coefficients of -0.84, -0.80, and -0.50, respectively). Furthermore, soil moisture indirectly influenced potentially toxic elements distribution by affecting soil pH, available potassium, organic matter, and bacterial community diversity. This study elucidates the distribution pattern of soil potentially toxic elements and their main influencing factors in a coal mine reclamation area, providing a theoretical basis for risk identification and precise management in mining area ecological restoration.
Ecological structuring of biosynthetic potential across terrestrial soil-associated Streptomyces genomesCORE3
Authors: Hajar Mhammedi, Sajida Mhammedi, Said Bouda, Y. El Hilali Alaoui, A. Essarioui
Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Streptomyces populations represent one of the richest reservoirs of microbial specialized metabolites, although the ecological organization of their biosynthetic diversity remains insufficiently resolved at large genomic scales. In the present study, 14,597 publicly available genome assemblies were initially screened. Environmental metadata associated with each genome were subsequently retrieved from the corresponding NCBI BioSample records and systematically curated to retain genomes originating from terrestrial soil-associated environments while excluding marine, freshwater, clinical, laboratory, industrial, and other non-terrestrial sources. Standardized climatic and edaphic variables were examined using principal component analysis (PCA), K-means clustering, and Kruskal–Wallis tests to evaluate ecological differentiation across terrestrial soil-associated Streptomyces populations, with false discovery rate (FDR) adjustment applied to inter-cluster comparisons. PCA revealed pronounced environmental structuring, with Principal Component 1 (PC1) explaining 44.3% of the total variance and Principal Component 2 (PC2) contributing 31.1%, together accounting for 75.4%. K-means clustering resolved three environmentally coherent groups with distinct soil and climatic profiles. Significant differentiation was detected for soil pH, rainfall, temperature, soil organic carbon, and clay content, with adjusted p -values below 0.001. Genome mining using GECCO (GEne Cluster prediction with COnditional Random Fields) revealed extensive biosynthetic diversity dominated by polyketide- and non-ribosomal peptide-associated gene clusters. Total biosynthetic richness also differed significantly among ecological groups (Kruskal–Wallis statistic = 7.28, p  < 0.05). This study provides a large-scale ecological-genomic framework linking environmental differentiation with biosynthetic organization across terrestrial soil-associated Streptomyces populations, advancing understanding of soil microbial and metabolic diversity.

Abstract
The objective of this study was to characterize and identify arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal (AMF) spores associated with economically important vegetable crops in the Acatzingo, El Cristo, and Santa María Actipan localities within the horticultural zone of the Tepeaca Valley, Puebla, Mexico. Arbuscular mycorrhizae are mutualistic symbioses established between fungi of the phylum Glomeromycota and the cortical cells of plant roots. This association contributes to plant adaptation to terrestrial environments by providing the fungus with carbohydrates, while the fungus facilitates the uptake and transport of phosphorus, nitrogen, and water from the soil. Two sampling events were conducted in April and August 2023, during which a total of 90 soil samples and 54 plant samples were collected from six vegetable crops. Spores were extracted from soil, and roots were stained using the Phillips-Hayman technique to detect mycorrhizal structures. Nine AMF genera and 14 morphotypes were identified, with Funneliformis mosseae, Claroideoglomus claroideum, and Glomus sp. being the most frequently observed taxa. Mycorrhizal structures, particularly vesicles and spores, were detected in four of the six crops, whereas no AMF structures were observed in pumpkin roots. Soil physicochemical characterization showed soil moisture contents of 9.4–13 %, water retention capacity of 41.3–50.9 %, pH values of 5.87–6.87, and electrical conductivity of 0.1–0.93 mS cm-1. Soil texture ranged from sand to sandy loam, with sandy particles predominating. These results provide a baseline for the diversity of AMF associated with vegetable crops in the Tepeaca Valley and show the potential influence of soil physicochemical properties on AMF occurrence and diversity.
Reprotonation Kinetics Engineering of Aramid Nanofiber Hydrogel Electrolytes for Aqueous Zinc-Ion Batteries.PROXY2
Authors: Yu-Seung Choi, Gilyong Shin, Ji-Hun Lee, Sang Won Kim, Hoyeon Lee...
Venue: ACS applied materials & interfaces
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs
Abstract
Aqueous zinc-ion batteries are promising next-generation energy-storage systems owing to their intrinsic safety and cost-effectiveness. However, dendrite formation and parasitic reactions at Zn metal anodes limit long-term cycling stability. Here, aramid nanofiber (ANF) hydrogel electrolytes are engineered by controlling the acid identity and solution pH during reprotonation. These variables regulate reprotonation kinetics, thereby shaping hydrogen-bond formation, molecular ordering, and the through-thickness morphology of the ANF network. The selected ANF hydrogel electrolyte exhibits a Young's modulus of 1.1 MPa while maintaining an ionic conductivity of 30.8 mS cm-1. Its mechanically robust and ionically conductive network promotes compact Zn deposition and resists dendrite penetration. Consequently, Zn||Zn symmetric cells sustain stable plating/stripping for over 2000 h at 1.0 mA cm-2 with an areal capacity of 1.0 mAh cm-2. Compared with conventional glass-fiber-based cells, Zn||hydrated vanadium oxide full cells also exhibit improved high-rate capacity retention and retain over 88% of their initial capacity after 2000 cycles at 1.0 A g-1. These findings establish reprotonation kinetics as a practical design parameter for balancing mechanical robustness, ion transport, and interfacial stability in hydrogel electrolytes for durable aqueous zinc-ion batteries.

Venue: ACS Applied Energy Materials
Published: 2026-10-21

Abstract
A carbon xerogel (CX) with a pore size of ∼70 nm was selected as support for a fuel cell electrocatalyst. While carbon xerogels offer excellent mass transport properties, their amorphous nature reduces their resistance to carbon corrosion under the oxidizing conditions of PEMFC operation. To tackle this, a partially graphitized carbon layer was deposited on the xerogel surface using chemical vapor deposition (CVD). This layer covers a large part of the micropores but keeps intact the interconnected meso/macropore network of the CX. The carbon-coated CX was also post-treated at 1500 °C under an inert atmosphere for further crystallization. Pt nanoparticles (20 wt %) were then deposited on both pristine and CVD-treated CXs by reduction of hexachloroplatinic acid in liquid phase; the resulting catalysts were evaluated with respect to their electrochemical activity and compared to a commercial carbon black-supported catalyst. The catalyst on the post-treated supports exhibited initial catalytic activities comparable to those obtained on pristine CX. After accelerated aging in RDE configuration, despite a decrease in the catalytic surface of Pt, specific activities increased while mass activities remained stable. In the Membrane-Electrodes Assembly configuration, the catalyst supported on the carbon xerogel post-treated with chemical vapor deposition and subsequent high-temperature treatment at 1500 °C exhibited encouraging durability behavior. This behavior was attributed to differences in Pt nanoparticle growth on post-treated carbon xerogel supports. Moreover, it was observed that these post-treatments do not negatively affect mass transport inside the electrode, since the meso/macropore texture remained intact.
Monitoring the Environmental Degradation of Biodegradable Mulch Films Using Py-GC-MS and a Multitechnique Analytical ApproachPROXY2
Authors: F. De Falco, T. Nacci, F. Burgevin, W. Courtene-Jones, Allicia Fullarton...
Venue: Environmental Science &amp; Technology
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Biodegradable mulch films have been developed for use in agriculture to reduce costs incurred during the retrieval of conventional mulch films and to prevent plastic pollution of soil caused by their incomplete removal. However, there are knowledge gaps on their degradation rate and potential environmental impact of any residuals. This study investigated the degradation of three commercially available mulch films, one made of polyethylene and two labeled as “biodegradable”. These products were exposed to four conditions in the open environment. After 24 months in soil, air, and a combination of air pre-exposure for 3 months followed by soil burial, all samples retained their overall film structure and were still recoverable, although different levels of physical and chemical changes indicated ongoing degradation. In seawater, no residual fragments of the biodegradable mulch films remained after 3 months of exposure. Pyrolysis-gas chromatography-mass spectrometry detected leaching of a plasticizer from one of the biodegradable films in all environments and other degradation features. The outcome of this study highlights the potential for environmental accumulation of mulch films labeled as “biodegradable” and the need for the development of international environmental testing standards and associated labeling to guide users on appropriate use and disposal of mulch films.
Lewis-acid activation of persulfate enables oxidation of antibiotics: a new green low-cost advanced oxidation processPROXY2
Authors: Anushka Lucas, H. Kornweitz, Y. Albo, D. Meyerstein
Venue: RSC Advances
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Developing advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) that operate efficiently under environmentally relevant conditions remains a key challenge in water treatment. The results presented herein demonstrate that the –O–O– bond in persulfate is activated to react with substrates by forming (H2O)4Mg(OS(O)2OOSO3)/H(OS(O)2OOSO3)− that oxidise antibiotics (ciprofloxacin and ofloxacin). At pH 7.4, Mg2+/PDS achieved 75% degradation of ciprofloxacin and 89% degradation of ofloxacin after 24 h, with pseudo-first-order rate constants of 2.7 × 10−3 and 5.0 × 10−4 min−1, respectively. These values represent over a twentyfold enhancement over PDS alone at the same pH. In addition, for the reaction with DMSO, a novel mechanism was found, in which the oxidation processes are initiated by Lewis acid activation and do not involve initial radical formation; the substrates react directly with the activated O–O bond. Thus, its reactions with sulfoxides form the corresponding sulfones. This mechanism, for the reaction with DMSO is supported by DFT calculations. In contrast, the calculations indicate a radical mechanism for the reaction with phenol, suggesting that the substrate oxidation mechanism depends on the nature of the substrate. The results prove that peroxides can be activated towards oxidation processes not only via energy transfer (e.g., heat or light) or redox processes (e.g., Fenton-like or electrochemistry) but also via Lewis acid activation. The results establish an additional pathway for peroxide activation and provide a mechanistic basis for further exploring the development of potentially cost-effective and environmentally compatible advanced oxidation processes. This study highlights magnesium and protons as effective activators of persulfate for robust contaminant degradation in aqueous systems.

Abstract
Soil organic carbon (SOC) is an important component of forest ecosystem carbon storage, and its distribution may vary with plantation stand composition. However, information on the vertical distribution of SOC stocks among different plantation types on the Bashang Plateau remains limited. In this study, we investigated the vertical distribution of SOC concentration and SOC stock and their relationships with continuous vegetation and soil variables in three mid-aged plantation types: pure Larix principis-rupprechtii stands (LP), mixed Larix principis-rupprechtii–Betula platyphylla stands (LBM), and pure Betula platyphylla stands (BP). Three independent 25 × 25 m plots were established for each stand type, and soil samples were collected from five depth intervals (0–10, 10–20, 20–30, 30–50, and 50–70 cm). SOC concentration, bulk density, soil moisture, pH, sucrase activity, and catalase activity were determined, together with understory aboveground biomass, litter biomass, and fine-root biomass. SOC concentration (g/kg) and SOC stock (t C/ha) generally decreased with increasing soil depth, and more than 50% of the total 0–70 cm SOC stock occurred within the upper 0–30 cm. One-way ANOVA showed that total SOC stock differed significantly among stand types (p < 0.05), with 202.50 ± 4.82 t C ha−1 in BP, 190.25 ± 4.51 t C ha−1 in LBM, and 109.75 ± 4.64 t C ha−1 in LP. Pearson correlation analysis among continuous variables showed that SOC stock was positively associated with understory aboveground biomass, soil moisture, and sucrase activity, whereas bulk density was negatively associated with SOC stock. Neither litter biomass nor fine-root biomass showed significant associations with SOC stock. These results indicate that stand type significantly affected SOC stock on the Bashang Plateau, and broadleaf-containing stands had higher SOC stocks than the pure larch stand under the conditions examined. The findings provide field-based evidence for considering stand composition in forest soil carbon management and ecological restoration in semi-arid plateau ecosystems.

Abstract
Biochar is widely used for heavy metal immobilization in co-contaminated soil, yet the remediation performance varies greatly with feedstock. This study aimed to evaluate the efficiency of four biochars derived from apple wood (A-BC), peanut shell (P-BC), corn straw (C-BC), and wheat straw (W-BC) on Zn and Cd availability, soil enzyme activity, and Zn and Cd accumulation in Brassica chinensis L. A pot experiment was conducted to investigate soil Zn and Cd fractions, metal uptake by Brassica chinensis L., and soil catalase, urease, and sucrase activities under biochar amendments. Two-way ANOVA and correlation analysis were applied to assess the effects of biochar type, application rate, and their interaction. C-BC and W-BC had ash contents nearly three-fold higher than A-BC and P-BC; all biochars were alkaline, with comparable pH values for A-BC and P-BC. The 5% biochar addition significantly decreased water-extractable (WA) Zn (20%-50%) and Cd (15%-35%), while residual Zn and Cd increased by 30%-90% and 110%-330%, respectively ( P < 0.05). Biochar type, application amount, and their interaction exerted extremely significant influences on soil extractable and residual Zn/Cd contents ( P < 0.001). W-BC exhibited the best Zn immobilization, reducing weak acid-extractable Zn by up to 47.8% and increasing residual Zn by up to 91.8% at the 5% application rate. P-BC was optimal for Cd stabilization and significantly increased residual Cd at all application rates (by up to 121%). Biochar type, rate, and their interaction significantly affected plant Zn and Cd uptake ( P < 0.0001). A-BC and C-BC most effectively reduced Zn and Cd accumulation in Brassica chinensis L. and lowered the corresponding dietary health risks of these two metals. Biochar amendment increased catalase, urease, and sucrase activities in a dose-dependent manner, with growth rates of 24%-38%, 17%-29%, and 22%-41%, respectively. Biochar type, rate, and their interaction significantly altered sucrase activity. Correlation analysis showed that changes in weak acid-extractable metal concentrations explained over 50% of the variance in plant metal accumulation and soil enzyme activity. Feedstock source determines the remediation performance of biochar in Zn-Cd co-contaminated soil. Biochar can immobilize soil Zn and Cd, reduce plant metal uptake and associated health risks, and restore soil enzyme functions. These findings provide practical guidance for selecting suitable biochar materials to remediate Zn-Cd co-contaminated farmland while preserving soil biochemical functions.
Herbivory enhances cadmium foraging in the invasive hyperaccumulating plant Phytolacca americanaECO1
Authors: Jing-Ge Kuang, Zhi-Li Pan, Lin He, Zi-Yang Zhang, Evan Siemann...
Venue: Functional Ecology
Published: 2026-10-21

Abstract
Biological invasions and heavy metal pollution are two pervasive environmental challenges threatening global ecosystems. While the elemental defence hypothesis posits that certain plants accumulate heavy metals to deter herbivores, it remains unclear whether invasive hyperaccumulators actively forage for these toxic elements and whether such below‐ground behaviour is dynamically induced by above‐ground biotic stress. Here, we investigated the foraging behaviour of the invasive hyperaccumulator Phytolacca americana in a rhizobox experiment simulating a dual‐stress environment of heterogeneous cadmium (Cd) distribution and Spodoptera litura herbivory. We considered root morphological responses and employed integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses to elucidate the underlying mechanisms. We found that P. americana preferentially allocated root biomass to Cd‐enriched soil patches when exposed to cadmium. Notably, above‐ground insect herbivory significantly amplified this below‐ground Cd foraging response. The interaction between Cd stress and herbivory markedly altered root morphology. Cd accumulated primarily in fine roots, where plants optimized their specific root length and tissue density to efficiently exploit Cd‐rich zones. Integrated molecular analyses revealed that Cd treatment significantly suppressed cytokinin levels in the roots. This hormonal shift likely promotes lateral root formation and accelerates heavy metal sequestration. Our results demonstrate that above‐ground biotic interactions can actively drive below‐ground heavy metal foraging strategies in invasive plants, manifesting as a complex morphological plasticity mediated by cytokinin signalling. More broadly, our study highlights how synergistic responses to multiple environmental stressors can enhance the adaptive capacity and ecological success of invasive species in contaminated habitats. Turning dirt into gold: the business case of soil carbon for agrarian pathways in arid climateECO1
Authors: T. Althalaj, Ross M. Thompson, Tala H. Qtaishat, D. Bumblauskas, F. Al-Shibli
Venue: Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Agricultural carbon sequestration offers considerable potential for mitigating anthropogenic climate change, particularly in the low-organic-carbon soils of the Middle East. This study presents a foundational scenario-based assessment of soil carbon sequestration and economic feasibility. Crop production and market datasets from Jordanian statistics (1998–2023) were used to estimate potential soil carbon sequestration and economic performance. Using benchmarks from literature in comparable agroecological regions, four soil carbon management practices were evaluated across multiple crops for their carbon storage potential using Monte Carlo uncertainty and sensitivity analysis. Sensitivity of economic performance was assessed under optimistic/pessimistic scenarios. Among the evaluated practices, the highest carbon sequestration rate was achieved through increased Soil Organic Carbon (SOC), reaching a maximum of 27.44 metric tons of CO₂ per hectare per year (tCO₂/ha/year) under the high scenario for cucumbers. In contrast, barley showed the highest sequestration rate at 0.45 tCO₂/ha/year under increased SOC in the high scenario. Sensitivity analysis showed that ±20% uncertainty in the management coefficients resulted in practice-specific changes of 1.11 tCO₂/ha/year for increased SOC and 0.46 tCO₂/ha/year for improved cropland management. Horticultural crops, particularly cucumbers and lemons, exhibited the highest sequestration rates and strongest responses to management interventions. Field crops such as wheat, barley, and olives demonstrated lower, more stable sequestration rates. Economic analysis revealed that cucumbers, grapes, potatoes, and Open-field farming (OFF) tomatoes achieved the highest tri-pillar performance (net and adjusted profits and break-even cost) alongside effective carbon sequestration. These findings highlight that crop choice is a primary determinant of both crop profitability and sequestrated carbon potential and feasibility, with significant implications for climate-smart agriculture, carbon credit mechanisms, and intensification strategies in data-scarce regions. While empirical field validation remains a future priority, this model-based study provides a screening-level theoretical baseline to guide targeted research and investment in arid agricultural systems.

Abstract
To address the dual challenges of water scarcity and climate change in rice production, a 2‐year field study was conducted to assess the combined effects of water management practices and γ‐polyglutamic acid (γ‐PGA) application on greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, water productivity, and economic benefits. Three water regimes—conventional flooding (C), alternate wetting and drying (A), and drought management (D)—were tested with and without the addition of 25 kg of γ‐PGA per hectare. Compared to the conventional flooding (C) regime, the water‐saving regimes (A and D) significantly reduced methane (CH 4 ) emissions while increasing nitrous oxide (N 2 O) emissions. γ‐PGA application reduced CH 4 and N 2 O emissions across all water treatments, improving soil aggregation and redox potential. The AAP treatment (alternate wetting and drying irrigation combined with γ‐PGA application) achieved the highest rice yield and crop water productivity, along with the lowest GHG intensity and carbon footprint. Consequently, the net economic benefit to the ecosystem was maximized under the AAP treatment, demonstrating a synergistic effect integrating water conservation, yield enhancement, emission reduction, and economic return. These findings suggest that integrating A irrigation with γ‐PGA amendment is a sustainable approach to balancing productivity and environmental sustainability in rice cultivation. Further research should focus on elucidating the underlying microbial mechanisms and assessing the long‐term adaptability of this approach in different agroecological regions.
