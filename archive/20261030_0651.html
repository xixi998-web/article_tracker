Nutrient-Driven Mechanisms of Soil Acidification in Mountain Tea Plantations Along Elevation Gradients: A Case Study of Lushan Tea Plantations in Jiangxi Province, ChinaCORE15
Authors: Xiao-Ling Zhang, Xiao-Bing Lin, Jian-Fu Wu
Venue: Agriculture
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil acidification and nutrient imbalances threaten mountain tea plantation sustainability, while nutrient-driven acidification mechanisms along elevation gradients in deep soil profiles remain poorly understood. This study examined three tea plantations at different elevations (107, 400, and 711 m) on Mount Lu, Jiangxi Province, sampling soils at five depths to 100 cm. Objectives were to quantify vertical differentiation of acidity, base ions, and nutrients, reveal altitudinal conversion patterns of acidification drivers, and guide fertilizer management. Soils exhibited strongly acidic conditions (pH 3.86–5.46), with the most severe acidification at the mountain foot and surface layer (0–20 cm). Potential acid accumulation in deep soils exceeded 75% (exchangeable aluminum/exchangeable acidity: 80.61%, 77.42%, 80.10% at mountain foot, mountainside, and mountaintop, respectively), and acidification spread was most pronounced at the mountain foot. The mountaintop and surface layer showed strongest acid–base resistance. Cation exchange capacity and base cations decreased with depth; base saturation in surface soil was 47.12% higher than in deeper layers. Organic matter, nitrogen, phosphorus, and available potassium were surface-enriched, whereas total potassium accumulated in deeper layers. Correlation analysis showed that pH was significantly negatively correlated with exchangeable acidity, exchangeable aluminum, organic matter, total nitrogen, and alkaline-hydrolyzable nitrogen, and significantly positively correlated with exchangeable calcium, exchangeable magnesium, and total potassium. Structural equation modeling revealed that nitrogen accumulation was the primary acidification driver, with total effect sizes ranking as total nitrogen (0.549) > organic matter (0.319) > total potassium (0.247) > alkaline-hydrolyzable nitrogen (0.156). Nitrogen drove soil acidification via direct and indirect pathways by increasing exchangeable acidity and depleting base cations dominated by Ca2+. Organic matter exerted bidirectional regulation on soil pH, while total potassium indirectly alleviated soil acidification. These findings underscored the need to adjust the N:K2O ratio from 2:1 to approximately 1.5:1; Organic fertilizers and soil amendments should be applied as base fertilizers, with the mountainside (400 m) being the most suitable elevation for tea cultivation.
Type-Specific Restructuring and Nitrogen–Sulfur Functional Degradation of Black-Soil Bacterial Communities Driven by Different Coal-Based Solid Waste Dumping in an Extremely Cold Open-Pit Coal MineCORE8
Authors: Yi-Fang Long, Zi-Ling Song, Yu Wen
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Coal-based solid waste (CBSW; fly ash, weathered coal, coal gangue) from open-pit coal mining in extremely cold regions differentially releases salinity/alkalinity, heavy metals and acids, altering surrounding black-soil properties and reshaping its bacterial communities. We compared healthy farmland black soil (CK) with three dumping treatments—fly ash (FA), weathered coal (WC) and coal gangue (CG) (six replicates each; n = 24)—using full-length 16S rRNA sequencing (PacBio CCS), physicochemical, phospholipid fatty acid and enzyme-activity analyses. The wastes showed contrasting stress regimes: fly ash was slightly alkaline, saline and heavy-metal-enriched; weathered coal was strongly acidic and saline; and coal gangue was acidic–sulfidic. Dumping significantly altered soil pH, salinity and metal speciation, reducing organic matter by 36–61% and microbial biomass carbon by 45–74%. Alpha diversity responded in a type-specific manner: WC had the lowest Shannon index (4.72, −55.1%) and was dominated by Pseudomonadota (80.6%) with methylotrophy being enriched; FA retained the highest diversity (11.23); and CG was dominated by Actinobacteriota (45.1%). PERMANOVA and ANOSIM confirmed significant community separation. Nitrification was nearly lost under WC, while sulfate respiration was enriched under CG and sulfur respiration under FA, indicating type-specific restructuring of nitrogen and sulfur cycling. These results show that CBSW dumping drives restructuring and nitrogen–sulfur functional degradation of black-soil bacterial communities through type-specific environmental filtering, with weathered coal causing the most severe degradation, informing classified remediation.
Ecological Stoichiometry and Microbial Community Dynamics in the Rhizosphere of Parashorea chinensis: Implications for Ex Situ Conservation of Endangered TreesCORE7
Authors: Wan-Nian Li, Yu-Jun Liu, Ting Li, Yuan-Yuan Xu, Xianyu Yao...
Venue: Plants
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Ex situ conservation of the endangered tree Parashorea chinensis, a national first-class protected species in China, hinges on the development of functional belowground ecosystems, a process that remains mechanistically unclear in restoration ecology. This study evaluated the development of conservation plantations by analyzing rhizosphere soil stoichiometry, enzyme activities, and microbial communities across four growth stages in natural forests and ex situ plantations. Results showed that ex situ plantations exhibited obvious soil acidification and persistently low N:P ratios, with phosphorus acting as the primary limiting nutrient. Fungal α-diversity was strongly correlated with stoichiometric imbalance in ex situ plantations. Artificial management drove microbial community shifts toward fungal-enriched assemblages, forming a distinctive functional microbial portfolio to tolerate combined stresses from acidification and nutrient stoichiometric imbalance. Crucially, these managed ecosystems maintain core nutrient cycling functions, and key microbial indicators show distinct developmental trends toward the conditions observed in natural forests as stands mature. In summary, ex situ conservation alters belowground biogeochemical processes rather than merely supporting plant survival. We propose targeted microbial management, particularly inoculation with native functional taxa from natural forests, to accelerate soil stoichiometry rebalancing and enhance nutrient cycling efficiency. These findings provide both evidence for the ecological feasibility of ex situ conservation and practical strategies for optimizing its long-term success through science-based belowground management.
Nitrogen stimulation of soil carbon content differs between two types of mycorrhizal‐dominated ecosystemsCORE6
Authors: Yuan-Liu Hu, Ji Chen, D. Hui, R. Phillips, Ying-Ping Wang...
Venue: Functional Ecology
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Increasing nitrogen (N) deposition has been suggested to favour arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) plants over ectomycorrhizal (ECM) plants, but how mycorrhizal association mediates N impacts on soil organic carbon (SOC) remains unclear. By synthesizing 315 N addition studies globally, we investigated the effects of N addition on SOC content in ecosystems dominated by ECM and AM plants (ECM and AM ecosystems). Our results showed that SOC increased by 4.1% in ECM ecosystems, likely due to reduced ligninase activity. In AM ecosystems, SOC did not change during the first 5 years of N addition, but increased by 7.3% over long‐term N addition (≥5 years), driven by increased litter inputs and progressive soil acidification. These findings highlight that N addition enhances SOC in both ECM and AM ecosystems via distinct mechanisms and temporal dynamics, implying that the potential of soil C sequestration may differ between the two ecosystems from short‐ to long‐term N addition. Magnesium promotes aluminum tolerance in apple by regulating cell wall remodeling through the MdMYB78-MdHCT/MdXTH2 transcriptional module.CORE6
Authors: Mengxue Lyu, Jing-Quan Liu, Xiu-Zheng Chen, Hao-Nan Cheng, Hong-Hui Liu...
Venue: Journal of hazardous materials
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs
Abstract
Soil acidification-induced aluminum (Al) toxicity severely restricts apple growth and development, threatening apple production and potentially human health. Magnesium (Mg) supplementation can alleviate Al toxicity in plants, but the underlying physiological and molecular mechanisms remain poorly understood. Here, increasing the Mg supply from 0.3 to 3 mM reduced root Al accumulation and increased root dry weight and root length in M26 apple rootstock seedlings exposed to Al stress (1.5 mM). Elemental analysis showed that approximately 60% of the accumulated Al was localized in the root cell wall, with hemicellulose 1 (HC1) exhibiting a greater Al-binding capacity than other cell wall components. Mg supplementation under Al stress reduced the cell wall hemicellulose (HC) and lignin contents, thereby decreasing cell wall thickness and Al accumulation. Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses identified MdHCT and MdXTH2 as key genes involved in Mg-mediated Al tolerance. Overexpression of MdHCT and MdXTH2 negatively and positively regulate Al tolerance in apple seedlings, respectively, by modulating cell wall modification. Transcription factor analysis identified the MYB family member MdMYB78, which directly binds the ACCAAC motif in promoters of MdHCT and MdXTH2, activating and repressing their transcription, respectively. Transgenic validation results indicate that MdMYB78 negatively regulates Al tolerance in apple seedlings. Collectively, these findings establish MdMYB78 as a key regulator of Mg-enhanced Al tolerance in apple seedlings by coordinately regulating MdHCT and MdXTH2 to remodel the root cell wall and alter its Al-binding capacity. Mg-mediated cell wall remodeling provides a potential strategy for mitigating Al toxicity in acidified soils.
Community differentiation and assembly of soil nosZ-type denitrifiers across dominant plant communities in an alpine wetland of Qinghai LakeCORE5
Authors: Shi-Jia Zhou, Ni Zhang, Jiao Tang, Ke-Long Chen
Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Nitrous oxide (N₂O) reduction mediated by nosZ -harboring microorganisms represents the terminal step of denitrification and an important biological sink for N₂O in wetland soils, yet how naturally occurring dominant plant communities are associated with the diversity, taxonomic differentiation, and assembly of nosZ -type communities in alpine wetlands remains poorly understood. We investigated four dominant plant communities in wetlands surrounding Qinghai Lake on the northeastern Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, dominated by Blysmus sinocompressus (BS), Carex regescens (CR), Elymus nutans (EN), and Leymus secalinus (LS), and integrated nosZ -targeted amplicon sequencing with vegetation biomass, soil physicochemical properties, environmental-association analyses, and phylogeny-based null models to evaluate variation in community diversity, taxonomic composition, environmental correlates, and assembly processes. The four plant communities exhibited pronounced differences in vegetation–soil conditions and supported distinct nosZ -type communities. LS-associated soils showed the highest richness and the largest pool of community-specific OTUs, whereas Proteobacteria and Bradyrhizobium dominated the overall taxonomic composition. Environmental associations differed between community diversity and taxonomic structure: diversity was more strongly associated with plant biomass, soil temperature, and carbon and nitrogen status, whereas compositional variation was more closely related to soil C ratio, pH, total carbon, moisture, and nitrate nitrogen. Phylogeny-based null-model analysis further indicated that community assembly was predominantly associated with variable selection, although the relative contributions of dispersal limitation and other stochastic processes differed among dominant plant communities. These findings demonstrate that dominant plant communities in the Qinghai Lake alpine wetland are associated with coordinated differentiation in vegetation–soil environments, nosZ -type community composition, and assembly processes, thereby characterizing the taxonomic differentiation and inferred assembly of the primer-defined soil nosZ community across contrasting plant-community habitats; however, the implications for realized N₂O reduction require direct validation using gene-abundance, transcriptional, enzyme-activity, and gas-flux measurements.
Plant-derived high-C:P amendments enhance legacy phosphorus utilization in phosphorus-enriched greenhouse soil via stoichiometric regulation and microbial functional coupling.CORE3
Authors: Yu-Shu Wang, Hua-Yang Fan, Xiao-Yu Yang, Zhong Wang, Jin-Tao Shang...
Venue: Journal of environmental management
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs
Abstract
Long-term intensive fertilization has accumulated large legacy phosphorus (P) pools in greenhouse vegetable soils, while substituting chemical fertilizers with livestock manure may exacerbate soil P loading. We evaluated pakchoi growth and legacy P utilization in an acidified, P-enriched greenhouse soil, comparing an unfertilized, unamended control (CK) with equal-carbon inputs of low-C:P, high-P chicken manure (OF) and high-C:P, low-P plant-derived amendments: alkali lignin (AL), straw (ST), and alkali-lignin-modified straw (AST). Compared with the OF, shoot dry weight was 47.0% and 12.4% higher under ST and AST, respectively, while shoot P uptake increased by 80.2% and 76.8%. OF produced an apparent P surplus, whereas ST and AST generated negative apparent P balances, indicating current-season amendment P inputs could not offset shoot P removal and supporting enhanced legacy P utilization without further accumulation of available or labile inorganic P. Plant-derived amendments increased soil C:P and pH, generally enhanced C- and N-acquiring enzyme activities, and reduced ecoenzymatic vector angles, alleviating relative microbial P-acquisition limitation. Metagenomic and random forest analyses showed that enhanced P acquisition was not primarily linked to a single P-cycling process, but more closely associated with resource stoichiometry and soluble N status, alongside coordinated restructuring of microbial C degradation, N transformation, and P-cycling functions. Overall, AL most strongly ameliorated acidity but elicited weak plant responses; ST produced the strongest biomass response, while AST maintained comparably high P acquisition and markedly improved soil pH. These high-C:P amendments provide a low-P alternative to high-P manure inputs, offering a strategy for reduced-P management and legacy P utilization.
Spatial Variability of Soil Properties and Implications for Climate-Smart Rice Production in four Agricultural Zones in Anambra State, NigeriaCORE3
Authors: Chidebelu Polycarp Okoyefi, Emeka Iluno
Venue: Scholarly Journal of Science and Technology Research and Development
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Spatial variability in soil properties strongly influences water dynamics, nutrient availability, and crop productivity in tropical agroecosystems, thereby affecting the sustainability of climate-smart rice production systems. This study evaluated the spatial variability of selected soil physical, hydraulic, and chemical properties across four agro-ecological zones (Awka, Anambra, Aguata, and Onitsha) of Anambra State, southeastern Nigeria. Surface soil samples (0–30 cm) were collected from georeferenced locations differing in elevation and landscape characteristics. Laboratory analyses were conducted to determine particle size distribution, bulk density (BD), saturated hydraulic conductivity (Ksat), soil pH, and organic matter (OM). Descriptive statistics, analysis of variance (ANOVA), correlation analysis, and principal component analysis (PCA) were employed to evaluate variability and relationships among soil properties. Results revealed considerable spatial heterogeneity across the study area. Sand content ranged from 332 to 582 g kg⁻¹, while clay content varied between 218 and 354 g kg⁻¹. Bulk density ranged from 1.346 to 1.458 g cm⁻³, whereas Ksat varied from 0.0182 to 0.0468 cm hr⁻¹. Soil pH was moderately acidic (5.3–5.7), while OM content ranged from 2.2 to 3.3%. ANOVA indicated no significant zonal differences in Ksat and BD, suggesting that micro-scale variability exerted a stronger influence than agro-ecological classification. PCA explained 96.5% of total variability, with soil texture and hydraulic properties constituting the dominant controlling factors. The findings highlight the importance of site-specific soil and water management strategies for improving rice productivity, enhancing water-use efficiency, and strengthening climate resilience in southeastern Nigeria.

Abstract
Abstract Dolomite, a calcium magnesium carbonate mineral, can adversely affect soil properties when present in excessive amounts. The India-Bhutan border region of West Bengal has experienced substantial declines in the quality and yield of fresh tea leaves, potentially associated with dolomite influx from the neighbouring lower Himalayas through recurrent flooding and the use of river water for irrigation. This study assessed the soil status of the affected area. The overall moisture content of the soil was low, with the lowest average values recorded in riverbeds, followed by the tea plantations. The average value of organic carbon, total nitrogen, available phosphate and potash across the study area were 1.34 ± 0.98%, 0.10 ± 0.08%, 20.27 ± 38.58 ppm and 199.18 ± 270.37 ppm, respectively. The soil pH of the study region varied from 7.85 to 3.91, with an average of 6.16 ± 1.02. The riverbeds showed average pH 6.94 ± 0.46, followed by the tea plantations (5.61 ± 0.91) and forest area (5.03 ± 0.53). The quantity of soil calcium carbonate of the area varied between 1% to 14%, with an average of 7.09%, in comparison to 0.4± 0.2% in unaffected areas. The tea plantations near the riverbeds were most affected. Elevated soil pH causes ammonia volatilization reducing the availability of nutrients such as P, N, B, Mn, Cu, Fe and Zn for the tea bushes leading to decline in productivity. Soil reclamation strategies should therefore include the potential use of calcite-solubilising microorganisms, measures to reduce further dolomite influx associated with mining activities, and appropriate hydraulic interventions, including check dams and the management of sediment accumulation in streams and riverbeds. Tea plantations should also consider avoiding river water for irrigation where it contributes to further carbonate accumulation and soil alkalinisation.
Long-term salinity is associated with deteriorated soil properties, rhizosphere microbiome restructuring, and elevated wheat crown rot: evidence from a 19-year gradient experiment and a 3-year remediation verificationCORE3
Authors: Ya-Jiao Wang, Sen Han, Xue-Tong Liu, Hong-Kai Dang, Chun-Lian Zheng...
Venue: Frontiers in Plant Science
Published: 2026-10-23
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil salinization threatens global agricultural sustainability and may elevate soil-borne disease risk through coordinated changes in soil conditions and the rhizosphere microbiome, yet long-term field evidence remains limited. Here, a 19-year controlled field experiment with an artificial salinity gradient (non-saline, moderate, and severe) was conducted to characterise salinity-associated responses of soil physicochemical properties, wheat crown rot (WCR), and the rhizosphere microbiome. A 3-year field remediation demonstration was implemented to test whether replacing saline irrigation with non-saline water could reverse soil deterioration and reduce disease severity. The results showed that severe salinity increased soil pH from 7.64 to 8.38 and reduced available phosphorus, total nitrogen, and available potassium by 54.11%, 14.63%, and 37.69%, respectively, while the WCR disease index increased from 10.51 to 32.88. It also reduced microbial richness and diversity, altered rhizosphere community composition, with taxon-specific abundance shifts among halophilic taxa, and redistributed community functional potential, with greater representation of genes associated with compatible-solute metabolism and ion homeostasis alongside changes in carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur metabolism. Remediation reduced soil salinity and pH, improved nutrient availability, and decreased the WCR disease index by 49.17%. Together, our results show that long-term salinization is associated with coordinated deterioration of soil conditions, restructuring of the rhizosphere microbiome, redistribution of microbial functional potential, and increased WCR severity. The remediation experiment showed that reducing salinity improved soil conditions and reduced disease severity. Re-establishing favourable soil abiotic conditions may be a prerequisite for alleviating disease risk in salinized agroecosystems.

Abstract
Wildfires can rapidly alter soil physicochemical and biological properties, but short-term paired field evidence from North African forests remains limited. We assessed changes in 14 soil indicators at ten forest sites in northeastern Algeria and interpreted these field observations within a regional GIS analysis of Mediterranean wildfire exposure. The same locations were sampled in March 2026, before the seasonal fire period, and revisited in August 2026. The measured variables comprised soil pH, electrical conductivity (EC), soil organic carbon (SOC), total nitrogen (TN), available phosphorus (P), C:N ratio, microbial biomass carbon (MBC), basal respiration, dehydrogenase activity (DHA), MBC:SOC ratio, gravimetric soil moisture, bulk density, water-stable aggregates, and surface litter biomass. Within-site changes were evaluated using exact paired Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction and paired-bootstrap confidence intervals. Across the ten sites, pH increased by 0.60 units, EC by 112.9%, and available P by 79.6%. In contrast, SOC, TN, MBC, basal respiration, soil moisture, water-stable aggregates, and litter biomass decreased by 26.7%, 36.8%, 49.4%, 44.2%, 40.5%, 37.1%, and 71.0%, respectively. Bulk density and C:N increased by 11.2% and 11.3%, while DHA increased by 29.8%. All 14 paired comparisons remained significant after Holm adjustment (adjusted p = 0.027). PCA accounted for 96.2% of the total variance and separated the March and August profiles. At the regional scale, the complementary EFFIS analysis identified 800,540 ha of mapped wildfire exposure through 6 August 2026, with substantial concentration in Spain, Algeria, France, Italy, and Portugal. Collectively, the paired observations indicate a post-fire-period shift characterized by an ash-associated increase in soluble ions and available phosphorus alongside losses of organic matter, microbial biomass, moisture, aggregate stability, and surface litter. Because contemporaneous unburned control plots and independent plot-level burn-severity measurements were unavailable, the observed changes cannot be attributed exclusively to wildfire and may also reflect seasonal environmental variation.

Abstract

Abstract
