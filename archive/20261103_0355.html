A Straight Line Is Not a Camera Pose: Auditing Short-Clip Pose BenchmarksCORE10
Authors: Xinyi Shi
Published: 2026-10-31
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The absolute trajectory error (ATE) is the standard measure of camera-motion accuracy, from SLAM to feed-forward 3D reconstruction. On the common Sintel protocol, a constant-velocity straight line using no images has a lower mean ATE than 10 of 11 recent models re-run with official code. Two properties of ATE's alignment explain this. First, it maps any constant-velocity prediction onto the best-fitting line through the true path, so the line scores the path's regression residual. A residual signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) criterion predicts when moving a prediction towards its constant-velocity fit lowers ATE. It gives the outcome for 95% of 1056 model-clip pairs, 98% of 792 held-out pairs and 97% of 1034 ScanNet pairs, and it separates Sintel (median SNR 0.86) from TUM, ScanNet and full-length Bonn and Wild-SLAM (2.7 to 27), where straightening raises every model's mean ATE. Second, position-only alignment ignores where cameras look: rotating a trajectory by 30 degrees against its orientations leaves ATE unchanged, yet worsens the reprojection of static points for every model on Sintel, TUM and ScanNet in the main setting, and the line, second by mean ATE on Sintel, places them worst by geometric mean: a lower ATE need not mean better geometry. Fitting the alignment's rotation to the predicted orientations gives pose-anchored ATE, a one-function change in ATE's units costing 0.17 ms per clip (ATE: 0.11 ms). It ranks every image-free reference below every model on Sintel, tracks the reprojection error under rotation and straightening, where ATE does not (Spearman 0.61 vs. 0.32), largely preserves the models' order, and ties 10-frame translational relative pose error (RPE) and the discernible trajectory error (DTE) on our rotation and reference tests. Code, predictions and results will be released.
GOFish: Full Field-of-View Gaussian Splatting and Mesh ExtractionCORE8
Authors: Allen Tu, Meher Gitika Karumuri, Shi-Fan Zhu, Hai-Ling Wang, Yen-Liang Lin...
Venue: Science Robotics
Published: 2026-10-27
Abs
Abstract
Robots, autonomous vehicles, and consumer 360{\deg} cameras use fisheye lenses for wide fields of view (FoVs). Simulation-ready digital twins require photorealistic rendering across the full FoV and an aligned collision mesh. However, prior fisheye Gaussian Splatting methods only rasterize up to 180{\deg} and do not produce meshes, while Gaussian surface reconstruction methods assume pinhole training images. We present GOFish, the first 3D Gaussian Splatting surface reconstruction method to train directly on fisheye images, rasterize FoVs beyond 180{\deg}, and extract an aligned mesh from the same scene. Calibrated densification recovers the surface across the entire FoV, while Calibrated Mip Filtering and Wide-Angle Appearance Modeling keep pixel blur and lens falloff out of the scene and mesh. Across the 200{\deg} FIORD and 205{\deg} FullCircle benchmarks, GOFish improves PSNR over the best prior method by 2.28 and 1.97 dB, respectively. It also outperforms fisheye rendering baselines within their own FoVs and achieves the highest mesh F-score on FIORD and the main ScanNet++ set. Together, the Gaussian scene and mesh form a LiDAR-free, simulation-ready digital twin that renders in real time, ray traces the full FoV, and provides collision geometry.

Authors: Antar Mazumder, Ryan Hartzell, Luke Boyd, Alexander Robinson, Neha Sharma...

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we present a reconstruction pipeline that empirically quantifies the photorealistic scene reconstruction accuracy from both human-controlled and rover mast camera monocular feeds against physical ground truth as a function of Lunar traversal effects (e.g., motion blur, haze due to lofted regolith, trajectory bias, and sparse views). First, we benchmark six variants of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) across three Lunar analog scenes on held-out test sets using standard photometric metrics. We then establish how under traversal effects, this test-set-specific Novel View Synthesis (NVS) performance does not proportionally scale to the same photometric metrics in the reconstructed DTs. Our results quantify that splat training metrics alone are insufficient predictors of Cross-Reconstruction Photorealistic NVS Fidelity (CRPNVS), and that factors such as rover trajectory bias and geometric scene coverage play a critical role in determining the photorealistic quality of the reconstructed scenes targeting Lunar analog Digital Twins. Additionally, we pilot study Coverage Score ($S_c$), a geometry-based diagnostic derived from COLMAP poses and point cloud structure that provides a meaningful heuristic about the highest achievable CRPNVS structural fidelity, independently of splat training performance in the absence of a human-controlled reference. These findings establish a standardized, human control-referenced evaluation pipeline for photorealistic scene reconstruction for Lunar-analog Digital Twin development and future planetary exploration. 3D Neural Rendering for Automated Ultrasonic Laser ScansCORE7
Authors: Thomas Pierson, Matthew Mitchell, Kira Perna, Anand Iyer, Eric Flynn...
Published: 2026-10-31
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Laser doppler vibrometer (LDV) scans can be used to detect defects and cracks in manufactured parts before they are deployed. This work presents a method for reconstructing the steady-state surface wavefield of a three-dimensional part by combining automated robotic manipulation with neural rendering. A six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) robotic manipulator (UFactory xArm6) with an embedded piezoelectric transducer presented an acoustically excited test article to a stationary LDV at a sequence of known orientations, yielding a set of complex-valued 2D scans with poses derived analytically from the arm kinematics rather than from image-based pose estimation. These scans were used to train a novel complex-nerfacto model, a modified Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) pipeline that replaces the standard three-channel RGB output head with a two-channel output. Complex-nerfacto represents the real and imaginary velocity components of a wavefield, preserving the complex measurement without quantization. Reconstructions of a welded aluminum cylinder and a welded T-bracket were evaluated against measured scans using Z-normalized cross-correlation (ZNCC). The complex-valued pipeline achieved a mean ZNCC of 0.60 at held-out viewing angles, compared with 0.28 for a baseline RGB nerfacto model trained on rendered images of the same data. The resulting surface wavefield reconstructions supported defect detection and characterization on complex components. We identify geometry reconstruction from sparse LDV scans as the remaining obstacle to practical deployment.
MoRE-GS: Motion-Regime Experts for Future Gaussian SplattingCORE7
Authors: Shima Salehi, Joshua Peeples
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Future dynamic novel-view synthesis requires extrapolating multiple motion regimes within the same scene. A single dynamics model may not simultaneously represent stationary regions, coordinated motion, local interactions, and articulated trajectories. We introduce Motion Regime Expert Routing for Gaussian Splatting (MoRE-GS), a causal, render-aware framework for forecasting dynamic 3D Gaussian scenes. MoRE-GS freezes a Gaussian representation reconstructed exclusively from the observed temporal prefix and forecasts its control-keypoint motion using four complementary experts: persistence, a coefficient-space Transformer, a graph convolutional network, and a structured Transformer with rigid and non-rigid motion constraints. A compact routing table assigns expert mixtures across soft motion groups and forecast-horizon bins, while a persistence gate suppresses unreliable extrapolation. Across D-NeRF and HyperNeRF scenes, MoRE-GS achieves improved performance against other methods, and these results demonstrate that renderer-calibrated composition of complementary motion predictors improves causal dynamic Gaussian forecasting across heterogeneous motion regimes. Abstract
Long-sequence panoramic reconstruction requires detailed scene geometry and globally consistent camera poses across distant revisits. Panoramic SLAM and incremental SfM rely on feature correspondences, but repeated, similar structures and small partial overlap make reliable long-range connections difficult to establish, leaving accumulated drift unresolved. Feed-forward models offer detailed local geometry, but their high computational costs limit inference window size, necessitating additional constraints for global consistency. This motivates combining continuous feature-based tracking and sparse mapping with rapid, high-quality local reconstruction from feed-forward models to achieve accurate localization and globally consistent dense mapping over long sequences. We therefore propose a panoramic reconstruction framework that couples an ORB-based sparse panoramic mapping frontend with a fine-tuned feed-forward model. Our key insight is that predicted local geometry enables 3D verification of feature matches across distant revisits, yielding long-range camera constraints even between regions. We calibrate predicted depths with sparse landmarks and use verified connections to optimize the global pose graph, updating dense geometry with the corrected camera poses. We also introduce PanoRecon, a seven-scene benchmark with repetitive architecture, large and small loops, and reference camera poses and LiDAR geometry for evaluating global consistency. GeoGSplat: Geometry-Grounded Non-Pixel-Aligned Gaussian SplattingCORE6
Authors: Jia-Le Wang, Chen Zhao, Hao-Yang Zhang, Yan-Hao Wu, Fei He...
Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-10-27
Abs
Abstract
Feed-forward Gaussian Splatting enables efficient novel view synthesis, but pixel-aligned prediction makes the primitive count grow with image resolution and view count, leading to substantial redundancy and increased computational cost. Non-pixel-aligned methods address this limitation by predicting a fixed number of Gaussians. Without explicit ray and depth constraints, these methods yield misplaced Gaussian centers, causing rendering artifacts under large viewpoint changes. To address this issue, we introduce GeoGSplat, a geometry-grounded feed-forward model that explicitly incorporates geometric priors into the non-pixel-aligned framework. A dual-branch encoder constructs Anchors and Triplane to capture coarse 3D structure and fine-grained scene features, respectively. Subsequently, a hierarchical decoder progressively decodes dense 3D points and Gaussian primitives, enabling 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis. GeoGSplat surpasses both state-of-the-art pixel-aligned and non-pixel-aligned Gaussian Splatting methods on DTU and NRGBD in terms of novel view synthesis and reconstruction. The advantage of our geometry-grounded paradigm further increases as target viewpoints move farther from the input cameras, demonstrating stronger generalization to large viewpoint changes. SHield: Watermarking 4D Gaussian Splatting via Spherical Harmonic Coefficient PerturbationCORE5
Authors: Kunal Bhosikar, Prajas Wadekar, Venkata Sai Pranav Bachina, Ankit Gangwal, Charu Sharma...
Published: 2026-10-31
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) models are computationally expensive to train but distributed as easily copied parameter files. Existing watermarks protect these assets by fine-tuning the scene against a neural decoder to extract bit strings from rendered video, a heavy process vulnerable to the temporal dynamics of dynamic scenes. However, if the threat model targets the redistributed file itself, verification can be vastly simplified. Because the canonical Spherical-Harmonic (SH) coefficients of 4DGS are time- and view-invariant, parameter-space perturbations can be recovered via deterministic arithmetic. We propose **SHield**, the first non-blind, decoder-free watermarking approach that natively embeds full-resolution RGB images into 4DGS files. SHield tiles a zero-mean payload across the SH coefficients of a key-selected Gaussian subset, applying a brief anchored optimization to preserve visual fidelity. To verify ownership, it geometrically re-associates the key using canonical Gaussian centres to survive file tampering and recovers the payload in closed form. Across 30 scenes (D-NeRF, HyperNeRF, DyNeRF), SHield guarantees near-perfect payload recovery (Pearson $r \geq 0.991$) on all 150 tested models with a mean rendering penalty of $\leq 0.13$~dB. Held-out evaluation yields perfect detection on 400 true trials and zero false positives across $1{,}600$ wrong-subset null trials. We also strictly bound the method's robustness: SHield survives INT8 quantization and $30\%$ opacity pruning for the image payload, and on aggressive SH-degree-1 truncation bit payloads are still decodable.
SceneBound: How Much Should a Streaming 3D Reconstruction Model Remember?CORE4
Authors: Ziming Wang, Chuyue Gong, Chutian Wang, Jingmin Zhu, Hongliang Cao...
Published: 2026-10-31
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Streaming 3D reconstruction models keep past video frames in a recurrent state, fast weights from test-time training, or a key-value (KV) cache. Each written frame shifts the coordinate frame of later cameras or occupies GPU memory. Training-free rules decide what to write from a signal of the current frame. How much they write accounts for their mean accuracy: spending the update budget of a gate at uniformly spaced frames, with no signal, matches nine published gates, and the budget bounds the drift of the state wherever the writes fall. We propose SceneBound, a training-free rule that writes a frame only when its predicted camera pose lies at least a fixed radius from every stored keyframe. Its keyframe count stays within a constant factor of the covering number of the visited poses, the fewest views that keep a keyframe near every camera, so the count grows with the places visited and stays flat when the camera returns. In large scenes, SceneBound closes a submap once decoding its first frame again reveals drift. SceneBound uses one radius across four released models and three memory types. In the recurrent state of CUT3R and the KV cache of StreamVGGT, it achieves the lowest mean trajectory error among the published training-free rules we evaluate, in one pass and over nine passes of a scene, where its keyframes grow by 3% to 6%.
DrawSplat: Atlanta World-Guided Implicit Gaussian SLAM for Indoor ReconstructionCORE4
Authors: Fan Zhu, Zhisong Xu, Ziyu Chen, Zhenjun Zhao, Takayuki Okatani
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
RGB-D SLAM requires accurate online tracking, high-quality rendering, and geometrically consistent reconstruction. Existing Gaussian SLAM methods mainly optimize Gaussian primitives with photometric, depth, or local frame-to-model constraints, which are often insufficient in indoor scenes with weak textures, large planar structures, noisy depth, and long trajectories. The resulting maps may suffer from pose drift, surface thickness artifacts, noisy normals, and blurred structural boundaries. We propose DrawSplat, an Atlanta World-guided implicit Gaussian SLAM framework for indoor reconstruction. DrawSplat follows a drawing-like process: explicit point-line geometry first sketches the structural layout, and implicit neural Gaussians then fill in high-fidelity appearance and continuous geometric details. It estimates camera poses, sparse geometry, and gravity direction through point-line joint tracking, infers structural planes from tracked lines using the shared-gravity component of the Atlanta World prior, and converts them into differentiable regularization terms on Gaussian centers and normals. To improve compactness and efficiency, DrawSplat further combines an anchor-based implicit neural Gaussian representation with adaptive structural sampling, which allocates Gaussian capacity to structurally informative regions. Experiments on multiple datasets show that DrawSplat achieves robust tracking, high-fidelity rendering, and strong geometric reconstruction among Gaussian-based SLAM systems, while maintaining a practical balance among quality, efficiency, and model size.

Real-VAS: a Real-World Video Amodal Segmentation DatasetCORE3
Authors: Finlay G. C. Hudson, William A. P. Smith
Published: 2026-10-30
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Amodal video object segmentation is fundamentally limited by the absence of datasets that combine real-world complexity with precise ground-truth annotations. To address this, we present Real Video Amodal Segmentation (Real-VAS), a new large-scale, zero-shot evaluation dataset built from composited real-image video. We introduce a novel data generation pipeline that enables the creation of programmatically consistent amodal ground truth for real-world footage, in which the object mask, occlusion boundary, and composited image are all derived from a single unoccluded source, without relying on human estimation or expensive 3D reconstruction. Our dataset is structured into two challenging scenarios: dynamic Occlusion, created by compositing two clips of moving objects, and a unique Container category featuring complex, physically constrained interactions. These container scenarios--On Surface Containment, Articulated Containment, and Mobile Containment--allow us to generate precise ground truth by simulating an object's motion based on its container's tracked transformation. As a result, Real-VAS provides a diverse and challenging benchmark for evaluating amodal segmentation models on realistic video with the precision of synthetic data. The dataset and our generation code will be made publicly available.

Abstract
Recent years have seen multiple impressive results in visual-inertial odometry (VIO) techniques, by which accurate state estimation can be achieved via the extended Kalman filter (EKF) or nonlinear optimization. However, these approaches are rarely open source, and they tend to fail in real experiments due to the temporary lack of feature points and fast motion. Therefore, in this study, we used encoders to overcome the temporary failure of purely vision-based simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). Here, we propose a generative measurement model for encoders and derive an expression for the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate and necessary Jacobians for optimization. Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Camera--LiDAR perception on mobile robots and intelligent vehicles depends on reliable sensor geometry throughout deployment. Installation variations create uncertainty in the initial extrinsics, while changing observation quality makes frame-wise estimates fluctuate during operation. This paper presents CalibSwitch, an observation-driven framework that separates absolute extrinsic recovery from selective state maintenance. A cross-modal absolute extrinsic generator (CAEG) combines projection-free bidirectional feature interaction with geometry-regularized learning to recover complete SE(3) candidates from image--point observations and coarse view context, without a supplied numerical initial transform. A selective recurrent calibration policy (SRCP) combines observation quality and temporal history to determine when a candidate should replace the working geometry consumed by a frozen multimodal detector. On nuScenes, CAEG achieves mean translation and rotation errors of 0.248 m and 5.884$^\circ$. Under sustained geometric transitions, SRCP reaches a mean composite error of 1.46 with 9.70\% candidate commitments, outperforming quality-based and temporal-consistency gating at comparable commitment rates. Relative to unconditional per-frame updating, it improves frozen-detector mAP and NDS by 1.43 and 0.92 points. Paired geometry--observation uncertainty tests, two real-vehicle platforms, and 13.5-FPS single-camera Jetson execution demonstrate the system value of selective geometric-state maintenance for continuous perception.

Authors: Dulanga Weerakoon, Darshana Rathnayake, Van Minh Do, H. Sabbella, Kai-Chen Zhou...
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-27

Abstract
Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes over time (4D reconstruction) from a single moving camera is central to robotics and AR/VR applications, yet state-of the-art feed-forward methods rely solely on RGB frames and therefore inherit the failure modes of conventional cameras, including motion blur and temporal aliasing under fast or non-uniform camera and object motion. Event cameras report per-pixel brightness changes asynchronously at microsecond resolution, offering a complementary signal that is largely immune to these artifacts, but whether this modality can be incorporated into existing RGB based 4D reconstruction pipelines without a full architectural redesign remains an open question. In this preliminary study, we investigate a minimal extension: we augment Monst3R, an existing RGB-only 4D reconstruction model, with an additional event-camera input branch, leaving the rest of the architecture unchanged. Across 3 benchmark datasets, this simple RGB-event fusion reduces Abs Rel by 3.4% on average relative to the RGB-only Monst3R baseline, and a sensitivity analysis shows that the improvement grows with the magnitude of camera and object motion, with the largest gains observed under high object motion conditions where RGB-only reconstruction degrades most. These early results suggest that even a lightweight, non-invasive fusion of event data can meaningfully improve 4D reconstruction robustness, motivating a more thorough architectural investigation in future work.
