Authors: Uzair Shah, Giovanni Pintore, Alberto Jaspe-Villanueva, Muhammad Tukur, Wala Elsharif...
Venue: Proceedings of the 31st ACM International Conference on 3D Web Technology
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Sparse panoramic capture enables efficient indoor digitization, but transforming a few 360° images into web-based, high-resolution 3D representations remains difficult. Limited viewpoint overlap, equirectangular (ERP) distortions, uncertain depth scale, and the perspective-camera assumptions of most image-to-3D pipelines make direct reconstruction unreliable. This paper introduces PanoGS, a pipeline that uses inferred depth from vision foundation models to guide the generation of high-resolution 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes from sparse panoramic images. Given a small set of RGB panoramas, we first estimate camera poses and low-resolution depth priors using a feed-forward panoramic reconstruction model. We then refine these priors at native ERP resolution through global-local depth refinement and fuse the resulting RGB-D panoramas into a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) geometric substrate, which provides stable surface support, normals, visibility information, and local scale estimates for initializing anisotropic Gaussian primitives. The initialized scene is further refined using an ERP-aware Gaussian optimization strategy with progressive levels of detail, appearance warm-start, conservative geometry updates, neighbor-based multiview refinement, and shardwise processing for large scenes. The resulting assets can be exported to Gaussian splat viewers and browser-based inspection tools. Results on indoor panoramic scenes demonstrate that the proposed guided formulation produces more stable and complete Gaussian scenes than direct depth-based seeding. At the same time, high-resolution depth refinement improves fine geometric and visual detail. PanoGS shows how sparse 360° captures can be transformed into practical web-based Gaussian representations for Web3D applications. 1

Venue: Information Fusion

Abstract
Feed-forward transformer models have driven remarkable progress in 3D vision,yet their quadratic complexity renders them impractical for long video sequences. Streaming 3D reconstruction addresses this bottleneck by processing frames sequen?tially with constant memory. However, existing recurrent architectures inevitably suffer from progressive degradation over long sequences, as they fail to account for the intrinsic reliability of observations and the accumulated confidence of the historical state, leading to severe error accumulation. To address these issues, we introduce BAT3R, a training-free Bayesian adaptation framework comprising two synergistic modules: State Innovation Gating (SIG) and Bayesian-guided Adaptive State Updating (BASU). SIG acts as a distributional regularizer that anchors state evolution to its geometric initialization, effectively arresting representational drift during long-term inference. Building on this, BASU reformulates state update as a recursive Bayesian inference process within a Kalman filtering framework. By jointly modeling the temporal reliability of observations via information-theoretic gain and spatial relevance via cross-attention maps, BASU achieves an asymmetric spatio-temporal state fusion: well-consolidated historical regions are protected from redundant updates, while genuinely novel scene content is aggressively incor?porated. Extensive experiments on standard benchmarks (7-Scenes, TUM, ScanNet, KITTI, etc.) demonstrate that BAT3R consistently outperforms state-of-the-art online 3D reconstruction methods, with the most significant gains observed on long sequences and complex scenes.
3D shape measurement under global illumination using window parallel single-pixel imagingCORE3
Authors: Zhou-Jie Wu, Cheng-Min Liu, Fei-Fei Chen, Zheng-Dong Chen
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs
Abstract
With the growing demand for accurate surface geometry characterization in advanced manufacturing and biomedical applications, reliable 3D imaging under complex illumination conditions has become increasingly important. Nevertheless, strong subsurface scattering and interreflections often corrupt the measured signals, limiting the accuracy of conventional optical sensing techniques. Here, we propose a windowed parallel single-pixel imaging (W-PSI) framework that reconstructs 3D geometry through light transport modeling rather than direct phase decoding. By analyzing the frequency response of global illumination, optimal spectral windows are selected to retain geometrysensitive information while suppressing redundant measurements. Experiments on highly scattering jade samples demonstrate robust and accurate 3D reconstruction. Compared with full-spectrum PSI, the proposed method significantly improves measurement efficiency while maintaining reconstruction accuracy, offering a general framework for 3D imaging in globally illuminated scenes.
From VRML to WebGL: Uses of Interactive 3D in Archaeological OutreachCORE3
Authors: Valentin Cotta
Venue: Proceedings of the 31st ACM International Conference on 3D Web Technology
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This presentation traces the origins and use of the first interactive 3D technologies in archaeological outreach, focusing on the transitional period between the emergence of VRML and the adoption of WebGL and real‑time engines (1995–2005). Using case studies (museum applications, educational CD‑ROMs, 3D reconstructions for experimental excavations, prototypes of virtual tours), it shows how technical and pedagogical practices developed around hardware constraints (bandwidth, GPUs), distribution formats (CD‑ROMs, plugins), and pedagogical models (immersive demonstration vs. guided exploration). The main argument is that VRML acted as a testing ground for interaction paradigms (3D navigation, informational hotspots, linear scripting) that helped formalize exhibition and digital workshop formats. Technical choices (level of detail, interaction types, accessibility) often reflected pedagogical and institutional trade‑offs rather than purely technological limits. These pioneering experiences left concrete legacies (usage guidelines, object libraries, documentation practices) that influenced contemporary 3D outreach formats. The method depends on the analysis of a corpus (CD‑ROMs, VRML files, project reports, technical archives) accompanied by a short demonstration (video captures or emulation, possibly live coding) of archived VRML examples to illustrate interaction formats. The presentation will also address heritage issues: format preservation, required documentation, and recommendations for depositing digital archives related to archaeological outreach. Following this heritage, I developed several WebXR-based mediation solutions to make archaeological data and narratives interactively accessible across desktop, mobile (cardboard), and standalone VR headsets. Céraphin generates textured 3D ceramics from profile drawings and exports GLB models for direct integration in databases, games, or VR scenes. "Raconte‑Moi le Néolithique ancien" is a 360° narrative built with A‑Frame and WebXR: a ring of ten scripted 3D vignettes combines 3D assets and 2D cutout illustrations, interactive hotspots that reveal pedagogical metadata on hover, and adaptive reveal mechanics to control user anticipation. The "DioraMarne" platform reconstructs excavation sites from plans and reports, offers pedagogical and first‑person viewpoints, dynamic lighting, hotspot metadata, a WYSIWYG editor for placing structures and vegetation, and AI‑assisted suggestions for palette and annotation. Finally, the Jablines Archéo VR prototype aggregates photogrammetric artefact scans into a WebXR VR gallery to reuse and disseminate field data. All projects emphasize exportable GLB assets, adjustable rendering/resolution, cross‑device WebXR compatibility, and authoring tools to enable researchers to publish and maintain immersive mediation experiences. These applications are implemented with A‑Frame, Babylon.js or Three.js.

Venue: Applied Physics Letters

Abstract
Three-dimensional imaging through scattering obstructions is challenged by multi-path signal mixing. Although parallel single-pixel imaging (PSI) demonstrates strong capability in decoupling complex illumination, the large number of required basis patterns becomes a bottleneck for imaging efficiency. In this work, we propose a spatiotemporal learning parallel single-pixel imaging method (STL-PSI) that achieves high-efficiency dual-layer reconstruction through scattering obstructions. The proposed STL-PSI redefines the data acquisition paradigm by designing a two-stage learning framework to achieve single-frame basis spectral coefficient recovery, which replaces the conventional multi-step phase-shifting mechanism. Furthermore, by leveraging the joint constraints of temporal difference characteristics and the spatial phase retrieval model, we establish a physics- and data-driven pipeline to improve the accuracy of spectral coefficient prediction. Experiments validated that STL-PSI reduces the required basis patterns from over 130 to as few as 21, which corresponds to a reduction of approximately 84%, while simultaneously achieving comparable measurement accuracy.
FlowVLA: Enabling World Dynamics Understanding through Pluggable 3D Flow LearningCORE3
Authors: Junhyeong Go, Ming-Yu Jung, Woo-Bin Im, Jongbin Ryu
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs
Abstract
Recent advances in 3D reconstruction enable the extraction of temporally consistent 3D motion from RGB observations; however, current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models still predominantly rely on 2D image-text priors. Therefore, they do not accurately represent the fundamental dynamics of the 3D world associated with physical actions. This over-reliance on static 2D visual cues limits the capacity of VLA models in achieving robust robot manipulation. To address these limitations, we introduce FlowVLA, a reconstruction-guided, pluggable 3D flow learning framework aimed at empowering VLA models with an inherent understanding of action-relevant dynamics. Instead of requiring explicit 3D sensor inputs or altering the inference-time architecture, our method is designed to utilize temporally aligned 3D flow only during training. This allows the model to learn the dynamic evolution of the physical scene while preserving the computational efficiency of conventional 2D VLA backbones. To bridge scene-level dynamics with robotic manipulation, we propose a modular 3D flow learning framework with dual-level flow supervision. Specifically, point-level flow guides the model to forecast the future displacements of task-critical 3D points, while TCP-level flow aggregates these motions to align with the robot's TCP trajectories. This dual-level flow supervision encourages the model to focus on semantically meaningful dynamic regions, effectively coupling scene-level motion with precise control signals. Extensive evaluations across simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that FlowVLA consistently improves manipulation performance, zero-shot robustness, and optimizes data efficiency. FlowVLA adds only lightweight auxiliary heads during training and discards them at inference. Therefore, FlowVLA introduces no additional 3D sensor inputs, architectural modifications, or inference-time computation.
CopyQuad: Quadrupedal Skill Transfer from a Single Side-View Video Without a Depth ModelCORE3
Authors: Mohamad H. Danesh, Raina Hu, Meng-Xue Xu, Hsiu-Chin Lin
Venue: Science Robotics
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs
Abstract
Video-to-robot pipelines for quadrupedal imitation insert a learned lifting stage, either a monocular depth model or a per-clip 3D reconstruction, to recover the one axis a side-view camera cannot see. We present CopyQuad, which omits that stage and pins the missing axis to the robot's own stance width instead. The setting is side-view footage of sagittally dominated motion, where limb motion is almost entirely in the image plane. A frozen 2D keypoint detector and a fixed analytic retargeting then turn a single uncalibrated clip into a robot reference whose foot targets are reachable by construction, so no per-clip trajectory optimization is needed. One phase-indexed reward structure, whose only per-behavior freedom is three scalars from a shared sweep, learns a dog walk, a horse walk, a horse trot and two non-periodic gestures, all deployed zero-shot on hardware. We then measure what omitting the axis costs. In a training-free rigidity test, a video depth model varies the distance between the animal's hips by $40$ to $107\%$ of its own mean and a single-image metric model by $22$ to $58\%$, though that distance cannot move. Motion capture puts the true lateral paw travel of a dog walk at $4.5$ cm peak to peak, a mean deviation near $1.4$ cm against the $3.5$ cm of lateral foot error our policies already leave, so the discarded axis sits below the pipeline's resolution. Depth repaired to feasibility never beats the constant, and the unrepaired estimates put the robot on the ground.
Unravel: Recovering an Interactive 3D Scene from a Single ImagePROXY2
Authors: Shan Hui, Ke-Yang Luo, Bin-Jia Zhou, Ming Li, Kai Li...
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs
Abstract
We present Unravel, a training-free framework that turns a single image into an interactive 3D scene, where every object is an independent and movable asset. Existing generators either produce one entangled geometry in which no object can be moved, or predict all objects in one feed-forward pass, where a wrong guess cannot be detected or repaired. Unravel instead takes a different approach: it first takes the scene apart, and then puts it back together. Starting from the input image, an agentic harness that iteratively peels the image layer by layer, and adopts point cloud as major modality for spatial relationship recovery. Specifically, a vision-language model plans which objects are fully visible, an image editor removes them and restores the occluded regions. Every edit is verified before being incorporated into the reconstruction. This process gradually turns occluded parts of the scene into direct observation, allowing objects to be reconstructed when they are best exposed rather than hallucinated from the original image alone. The scene is then rebuilt in reverse removal order, so that the supporting objects are naturally placed before the objects they support. Since a single-view image only reveals part of an object, we treat the observed points as a lower bound on its size: an asset may grow to cover its points, but can not shrink away from them. Finally, we physically settle the reconstructed scene to remove floating objects and interpenetrations, followed by an agentic audit that corrects remaining placement errors. Extensive experiments on diverse indoor scenes show that Unravel recovers more objects, places them more faithfully, and produces scenes that are more physically-plausible than existing methods.
Reliability of spatial resolution metrics as a function of acquisition parameters in industrial x-ray CTECO1

Venue: Pattern Recognition
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Industrial X-ray Computed Tomography (xCT) is widely used for nondestructive inspection and dimensional metrology of complex components, where acquisition parameters such as the number of projections strongly influence achievable spatial resolution. However, statistically supported guidelines that relate these parameters to reliable resolution remain limited. In this study, the influence of reducing the number of projections on the reliability and stability of selected spatial resolution metrics in industrial xCT is investigated using a resolution phantom manufactured by Quality Assurance in Radiology and Medicine GmbH (QRM). For each number of projections, multiple reduced projection subsets are generated from one reference measurement by varying the angular offset. After reconstruction of each subset separately into the corresponding 3D voxel volumes, 2D slice images containing the phantom stripe patterns are extracted. Image quality is then evaluated from these stripe patterns using relative stripe response and approximate Modulation Transfer Function (MTF) indicator based metrics. The results show that increasing the number of projections improves the relative stripe response. The approximate bar pattern MTF indicator shows that the median response level changes only moderately with the number of projections, whereas the Q1–Q3 range decreases as the number of projections increases. Thus, projection number mainly affects the repeatability and stability of the estimated frequency response rather than shifting its central value.
