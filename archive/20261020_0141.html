DriveWorld3D Evidence Constrained 3D World Modeling and Generative Simulation for Real-World Autonomous DrivingCORE11
Authors: Wei Wang, Yiru Veronika Wang, Yihan Wang, Zihao Li, Xiaohui Liang
Published: 2026-10-15
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Three dimensional modeling for autonomous driving must continuously estimate road geometry, obstacle occupancy, traffic participant behavior, and observational uncertainty under limited computational resources. Existing reconstruction methods can synthesize realistic novel views, but visual fidelity does not sufficiently characterize the geometric reliability required for driving. Generative world models can expand scenarios and predict future observations, yet may introduce content unsupported by measurements. We propose DriveWorld3D, a framework combining temporal video learning, measurement constraints from multiple sensors, and offline generative simulation. Its online component maintains a unified world state comprising road surfaces, volumetric occupancy, dynamic entities, and road relations, while separately representing physical occupancy, observability, and evidence provenance. Action conditioned future prediction and risk sensitive planning connect this representation to driving decisions. Its offline component reconstructs recorded scenes using neural radiance fields or 3D Gaussian Splatting, then expands long tail conditions using generative models while restricting the use of generated content as geometric ground truth. We specify data partitioning, sensor ablations, fault injection, paired closed loop testing, and real to simulation consistency assessment. The central hypothesis is that explicit evidence management can reduce dangerous false free space predictions and occlusion risk within a matched computational budget while retaining acceptable driving efficiency. All performance claims remain subject to experimental validation. Keywords: autonomous driving; 3D occupancy; sensor fusion; uncertainty; generative world models; NeRF; 3D Gaussian Splatting; closed loop simulation
Frame-Consistent Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting from Sparse Unposed ImagesCORE10
Authors: Viruthshaan Vinasirajan, Ranga Rodrigo
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Feed-forward Gaussian models have made novel view synthesis almost instantaneous. Feed-forward methods predict Gaussians directly, either in two stages or in a single merged pass. Using two separately trained models is attractive because each can specialize in its own objective: one for accurate pose estimation and the other for high-quality reconstruction, rather than forcing a single jointly trained network to compromise between the two. A natural instantiation pairs a frozen pose estimator, VGGT, with a frozen reconstruction backbone, TokenGS. Prior work, such as VGGT-X and 3R-GS, also adopts a two-stage design, but attributes the resulting quality degradation to noisy pose estimates and introduces learned refinement. We show that this is not the primary source of the problem. Through per-camera analysis across 107 scenes from two datasets, DL3DV and MipNeRF360, using COLMAP poses as the reference, we find that the rotation error after alignment is small (median 1.6 degrees), whereas the translation scale is offset by a large, dataset-consistent factor. Controlled ablations over 140 DL3DV scenes show that the primary obstacles are two integration failures rather than pose error: a coordinate-frame inconsistency and a translation-scale mismatch, both of which can be corrected deterministically. With these fixes, a frozen VGGT driving a frozen TokenGS achieves 22.47 PSNR, within 0.51 dB of the COLMAP reference, with no additional training, when poses are estimated from all scene views. In the sparse setting, where reconstruction uses only four context images under the standard pose-free protocol, the same pipeline matches this all-view quality, yielding a fully COLMAP-free pipeline without any refinement step.
SwitchSplat: Learning Discrete Object States from Casually Captured ScenesCORE8
Authors: Yizheng Xie, Yalda Foroutan, George Shramko, Daniel Rebain, Shrisudhan Govindarajan...
Venue: SIGGRAPH
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting has made high-fidelity 3D reconstruction fast and accessible, yet existing representations assume a static world. This assumption breaks down in casual captures, where a user naturally interacts with the environment -- opening doors, toggling switches, or moving objects -- introducing discrete state changes that no current framework can model. Time-conditioned dynamic methods such as 4DGS conflate when a state was observed with what state the scene is in, limiting them to replaying seen sequences rather than composing arbitrary object configurations. We present SwitchSplat, the first 3DGS representation to address this gap via a base+slot decomposition: a shared static base captures the invariant scene, while each slot independently holds the two binary states (on/off) of one interactable object. Because the assignment of Gaussians to slots is fully differentiable, the model discovers the geometry of each state directly from video, without manual annotation. At inference, the user can freely combine slot states and render the resulting scene from any viewpoint. We validate SwitchSplat on a new casual-capture dataset and show that it outperforms static and dynamic baselines in novel-view synthesis quality, while uniquely supporting rendering of state combinations never seen during capture. Directionally Factorized Illumination with Spherical Harmonic RotationCORE8
Authors: Yimeng Dou, Adam M. Saunders, Andre T. S. Hucke, Michael E. Kim, Adam W. Anderson...
Venue: ICRA
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many real-world capture scenarios involve an active light source rigidly attached to the camera: camera flashes for dark-environment imaging, underwater vehicle lights, and endoscopic/laparoscopic medical procedures. As the camera moves, the co-mounted light moves with it, causing the illumination direction at each surface point to change across views. Neural rendering methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) encode this pose-dependent illumination as surface coloration, producing appearance inconsistencies and making it harder to recover the underlying scene. We present \textbf{DaFISH}, which can be plugged into any novel-view synthesis (NVS) pipeline such as NeRF. It factorizes per-point emitted color into surface albedo and a spherical harmonic (SH)-represented illumination factor. During training, the SH illumination is evaluated directly, enabling stable joint optimization of albedo, lighting, and geometry. \textbf{DaFISH} provides analytical generalization of illumination to arbitrary camera poses without retraining. Building on the closed-form SH-to-irradiance conversion, the training pipeline is fully differentiable from SH evaluation through volume rendering. Averaged over the held-out frames of the five scenes, \textbf{DaFISH} attains the best mean PSNR (38.91\,dB), SSIM (0.9613), and LPIPS (0.0582) among all compared methods. It improves over the strongest splatting baseline as well as the closest NeRF-based baseline, while recovering the scene consistently across views compared to prior methods.
FLoTS-SLAM: Optical Flow-Derived Uncertainty for Monocular Dynamic SLAM with Two-Stage OptimizationCORE8
Authors: Haorui Du, Lipu Zhou
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Monocular simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) remains challenging in dynamic environments. Real-time dynamic object identification in such environments is a critical problem. Recent approaches estimate per-pixel uncertainty from rendering residuals or multiview feature inconsistency to identify dynamic regions. These methods lack direct motion evidence, and their accuracy may degrade when reconstruction is inaccurate or features are ambiguous. In addition, such uncertainty merely downweights pixels in bundle adjustment (BA), so inaccurate uncertainty estimates can still impair camera pose estimation and 3D reconstruction. Accordingly, we present a monocular RGB SLAM system for dynamic scenes. Specifically, a motion uncertainty estimation method is proposed to derive uncertainty from the residual between optical flow and camera motion induced flow, directly reflecting object motion. The estimation varies with frame alignment accuracy, reducing false detections and yielding accurate pixel-level masks of dynamic regions. To make BA more robust, we estimate camera poses from high confidence pixels. These poses are then used to reproject dynamic pixels during joint refinement of poses and depth, reducing the influence of object motion on both pose and depth updates. Moreover, we provide a quantitative evaluation of dynamic masks and analyze how these masks affect the downstream tracking and reconstruction of dynamic objects. Our approach improves the mean dynamic mask IoU by 35\% over a state-of-the-art baseline on DAVIS, and reduces the average ATE by 59\% on Bonn and 19\% on TUM relative to the SLAM backbone used in our system. The source code will be made publicly available.

Abstract
Jointly reconstructing RGB and X-ray provides new insights by connecting external appearance with internal structure, enabling a more complete understanding of real-world objects than single modality. Yet existing methods train separate models per modality and suffer from redundant geometry, alignment artifacts, and loose coupling that limits cross-modal insight discovery. We propose a unified representation in which all Gaussian primitives share a single set of geometric parameters, with only surface-proximal primitives additionally carrying appearance attributes. We perform cross-modal registration through clean multi-view stereo and X-ray geometric proxies instead of aligning two sets of optimized Gaussians, improving alignment stability. We then jointly optimize the unified representation via supervision from both modalities, complemented by a surface-depth consistency loss derived from X-ray geometry to guide RGB reconstruction. Experiments on the InsideOut dataset and our Flower & Insect dataset show that our framework achieves more robust cross-modal alignment, higher RGB rendering quality without compromising X-ray reconstruction, and a more compact representation than maintaining two independent geometries.
RoMo-GS: Efficient Online 3D Gaussian Splatting for Robot Mapping via Exposure-Integrated Motion BlurCORE7
Authors: Sangwoo Jung, Di Deng, Jiahui Fu, Laura Herlant, Ayoung Kim...
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Online 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is emerging as a practical way to build a representation for robotics applications. However, deploying it on real-world platforms exposes multiple challenges. Rapid maneuvers of a robot induce motion-blurred images, which corrupt the reconstructed geometry and cause camera pose drift. Furthermore, the number of Gaussians grows without bound during continuous online mapping. In this study, we present RoMo-GS, an online 3DGS system designed to address these challenges. Instead of averaging N discrete sub-frames as in prior motion blur-aware 3DGS, we analytically integrate each projected Gaussian over the exposure interval, yielding an O(1) complexity per Gaussian. Building on this, we bound the Gaussians' growth with a Hessian-based pruning score, while jointly refining pose, velocity, and Gaussians with Adam and an online LM step over the Gaussians. On multiple public datasets and a self-collected dataset, RoMo-GS matches or exceeds offline deblurring 3DGS methods in rendering quality while running 15--150x faster, and operates online at competitive quality with about 20% fewer Gaussians than the online 3DGS baseline.

Abstract
Echolocating bats navigate dense forests using sonar alone, and in-air 3D imaging sonars now capture the entire frontal hemisphere with every pulse. Sonar SLAM, however, has so far been planar: current systems estimate a 2D trajectory and discard the elevation information in the sonar images, an assumption that does not hold on rough and sloped terrain such as a forest floor. In this paper, we introduce RTISSLAM-3D, a novel 6-DoF SLAM system for a wheeled robot that carries an in-air 3D imaging sonar, an IMU and wheel encoders. O-SCD++: Replay-Free Online Scene Change Detection via Recursive Evidence FusionCORE5
Authors: Chamuditha Jayanga Galappaththige, Jason Lai, Lloyd Windrim, Timothy Patten, Donald G. Dansereau...
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Online scene change detection requires an agent to detect what has changed in a previously mapped environment while it is still on site, observing from unconstrained viewpoints and without access to future observations. This causal setting is strictly harder than its offline counterpart, and online methods have consistently trailed offline accuracy. O-SCD closed that gap by treating change as a persistent property of a 3D Gaussian splatting map of the reference scene, surpassing the strongest offline methods in change detection accuracy while operating at over 10 frames per second. O-SCD optimizes a change field over the Gaussians of the reference map, refitting it to all past cue maps at every frame, a step that consumes more than half of the per-frame budget and forces accuracy to be traded against speed. We present a replay-free formulation that eliminates this trade-off. Because the reference map stays fixed, rendering the change field is a linear operation. Each new observation can therefore be carried onto the map's Gaussians directly, by a single backward pass of the renderer, which reduces multi-view fusion to an exact two-scalar recursion per Gaussian. Past observations are never stored or revisited. The self-supervised objective that O-SCD minimizes by descent also determines the decision threshold exactly, by sorting, yielding a label-free per-frame cutoff whose margin provides a selective-prediction signal. On PASLCD, our method improves mean intersection over union from 0.489 to 0.575 and throughput from 11.8 to 25.6 frames per second, even exceeding O-SCD's offline result while remaining strictly causal.

Abstract
Measurement-level LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry (LIVO) can achieve high accuracy, and its practical resource requirements depend on the adopted association and map-maintenance strategy. Persistently attaching dense photometric patches or appearance attributes to mapped LiDAR points can introduce additional storage, association, and update costs. This paper presents F-LIVO, a filter-based LIVO framework that couples a visual-inertial subsystem and a LiDAR-inertial subsystem through feature-level point-to-plane constraints between visual points and LiDAR planes. Venue: NeurIPS 2026
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
N-dimensional splatting extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with view direction and time to model view-dependent and dynamic effects. Each primitive is lifted into a joint distribution over position and conditioning variables, then conditionally sliced at each rendering query. N-DGS and UBS share this step across Gaussian and Beta kernels: recovering the conditional mean, opacity, and covariance requires per-primitive matrix inversions and regression products in every forward pass. We introduce direct conditional parameterization, replacing this covariance-derived readout with explicit per-effect parameters: a Cholesky precision factor for opacity and a factorized query-to-position regression operator with learnable per-dimension coupling. Unrestricted conditional coordinates recover a valid joint covariance; our factorization adds spatial-scale and coupling priors. Applied to both kernels, the parameterization yields dGS (Gaussian) and dBS (Beta). Across five static and dynamic benchmarks, they match or exceed their covariance-derived counterparts on quality, with gains up to +1.26 dB in the main MCMC evaluation. Production slicing is 6.9-7.7 times faster than on-the-fly covariance-derived conditioning; complete-render profiling gives 1.25-1.91 times speedups on four trained scenes. Direct conditioning provides a structured, kernel-agnostic alternative for N-dimensional splatting. Project page: https://gaozhongpai.github.io/dgs/.

Abstract
We introduce avatar referring segmentation, which selects regions of animatable Gaussian avatars from language queries. A query can also specify a pose or motion to select matching intervals. This lets users interact with avatars for local editing and motion inspection. Abstract
Reconstructing high-quality, renderable 3D scenes from long videos is essential for robotic perception and embodied intelligence. Many feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction methods rapidly predict 3D scenes from a small set of images, but extending them to longer sequences presents two bottlenecks: jointly processing all views increases GPU memory requirements, while accumulating pixel-aligned predictions redundantly represents the same surfaces and increases storage and rendering costs. Abstract
