Facet-SLAM: Real-Time Triangle Splatting SLAMCORE8
Authors: Edward Beng Wai Tan, Siew-Kei Lam
Published: 2026-11-18
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Dense SLAM systems based on 3D Gaussian splatting build maps that render photorealistically, but their surface geometry is poor, and obtaining a mesh requires extraction or fusion stages. Triangle splatting avoids this with surface primitives whose vertices can be triangulated directly into a connected mesh, yet SLAM with this representation has so far relied on photometric tracking, which is too slow for localization at sensor rate. We present Facet-SLAM, the first real-time triangle splatting SLAM system for RGB-D cameras. Fast geometric registration is the natural alternative, but it is not sufficient alone, since the mapper's optimization displaces triangles from the observed surface and makes them unreliable registration targets. We therefore separate the two roles: the tracker sets each triangle's centroid from fused depth and registers frames to these centroids by GICP, while the mapper is restricted to non-translating updates via a change of variables. Our system achieves competitive tracking accuracy at around 100 FPS on a desktop GPU and runs at sensor rate on a Jetson AGX Orin. Compared with the photometric approach, it is over 20 times faster, matches rendering quality, and improves surface accuracy, reducing the chamfer distance of the Delaunay mesh on Replica from 1.55 to 0.86 cm. We will release the implementation at https://github.com/edward62740/Facet-SLAM.
NIRJALA: Draining the Sea out of Gaussian Splatting with Three-Path Optics and Reversible PhysicsCORE7
Authors: Latchan Chhetri, Aman Kumar
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Water does not just tint an underwater photograph; it rewrites it. Each pixel mixes attenuated scene radiance, backscattered light, and scene light scattered forward on its way to the camera, and many combinations of scene and water explain the same pixels. Most Gaussian-splatting methods for underwater scenes model only the first two effects and remove the water by dividing by a transmission that decays exponentially with range, so their inversion gain grows without bound. We present NIRJALA (Sanskrit for "without water"), a 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that recovers water-free appearance and geometry together. TRIVENI, a three-path image formation model, adds a range-saturating forward-scattering term to direct transmission and backscatter; we prove that this keeps the inversion gain bounded at every range and bound how errors in the estimated backscatter and attenuation propagate into the recovered colours. PRATIBIMBA, a reversible physics anchor, ties a flexible neural water model to a ten-parameter physical model in both directions, so scene content cannot hide inside the water. SAMARASA collects the physics-informed objectives, including a range-binned colour prior. To score water removal against ground truth, we built SAJALA ("with water"), a 30-scene suite path-traced in Blender Cycles with water-free images, depth, and normals under controlled water colour and turbidity. On SAJALA, NIRJALA raises PSNR from 24.34 to 24.76 dB and lowers depth AbsRel from 0.109 to 0.091 against the strongest baseline; on real SeaThru-NeRF scenes it lowers LPIPS from 0.194 to 0.175 while rendering at 44 FPS. We also discuss where appearance metrics and geometry disagree underwater, and the limits of our evaluation.
Gaussian Splatoms: Direct Atomic Structure Identification in Electron TomographyCORE6
Authors: Nalini Singh, Tiffany Chien, Cedric Lim, Arthur R. C. McCray, Colin Ophus...
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Atomic electron tomography (AET) computationally reconstructs the 3D atomic structure of materials from a series of 2D projection measurements acquired at different tilt angles. Inspired by Gaussian splatting techniques from computer vision, we introduce an atom-centric parameterization for modeling AET samples. Instead of a conventional volumetric representation with a grid of voxels, we model the sample as a list of atoms, where each atom is a 3D Gaussian characterized by learned location, scale, and amplitude. The Gaussian model is a natural fit for atoms that allows us to easily incorporate prior information grounded in physics; we impose isotropy of atoms, a minimum interatomic distance, and realistic atomic radii. We show in simulation that our method is more robust than volumetric baselines to real-world imaging challenges, including a limited number of measurements and low electron dose. We further show that our method yields a more physically plausible reconstruction of an experimental 23,000 atom iron-platinum nanoparticle than a previously published volumetric reconstruction. Beyond tomography, our representation is a promising tool for any atomic resolution imaging modality (e.g. multislice electron ptychography).

OCLDA-GS: Object-Centric Local Deformation-Aware Gaussian StreamingCORE5
Authors: Ashim Dahal, Nikesh Subedi, Rabab Abdelfattah, Nick Rahimi
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Streaming 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) allows for online reconstruction of dynamic scenes from multi-view videos for Free Viewpoint Video (FVV). Current time and storage efficient methods rely on selecting a subset of anchor Gaussians and neighborhood interpolation for fast streamable 3DGS. However, these methods can overlook object-specific motion and introduce inconsistencies near object boundaries. We propose OCLDA-GS, an object-centric streaming framework that dynamically reallocates object and motion-aware Gaussian anchors to allocate anchor Gaussian support for objects undergoing large motion while at the same time, restricting the gaussian neighborhood within object groups for more precise object boundaries. Furthermore, it estimates object-wise rigid transformations (e.g. a hand moving) and leverages these estimates to learn per-anchor local deformation correction for small deformations (e.g. wrinkles on a shirt). Then, we conduct experiments on three dynamic multi-view video datasets and demonstrate improvements over the previous baselines alongside improved quality between refinement timesteps. Under matched refinement budgets, our method improves PSNR over Instant Gaussian Stream (IGS) by 0.169 dB on N3DV, 0.202 dB on Meeting Room and +0.199 on Google Immersive, while reducing the mean PSNR drop between refinement timesteps by 9.2% and 13.8% in N3DV and Meeting Room, respectively.
TetraCoder: Adaptive Tetrahedral Encoding for Neural FieldsCORE5
Authors: Daniele Della Pietra, Antonella Rech, Nicola Conci, Nicola Garau
Published: 2026-11-13
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Implicit and explicit representations of geometry are central in computer graphics applications and come with their pros and cons. With surface sampling being faster on explicit representations and spatial queries being faster on implicit representations, finding efficient conversions between them is a highly desirable property. We present TetraCoder, a single resolution hash-free adaptive tetrahedral encoding that links an explicit tetrahedral spatial encoder with an underlying implicit signed distance field (SDF). The resulting hybrid implicit-explicit space is continuous piecewise affine (CPWA) by design, allowing to obtain an exact correspondence between the implicit SDF and the explicit analytic mesh. TetraCoder's design comes with additional perks, in particular: (a) it scales better than multi-resolution encoding methods due to its single resolution optimizable grid, (b) unlike hash-based methods, it is not affected by collisions, (c) it enables fast tetrahedral ray-tracing, geometry and appearance editing, directly through its adaptive tetrahedral scaffold. We demonstrate the versatility of TetraCoder by showing an improvement over state-of-the-art methods on multiple tasks: 2D/3D fitting from images and point clouds, 3D novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and geometry/appearance editing.

Authors: Luosong Guo, Xiang Yang, Chaoyang Li, Ao Gao, Jiangming Shi...
Published: 2026-11-18
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Feed-forward 4D reconstruction enables efficient creation of renderable driving scenes for autonomous driving simulation. However, most existing methods rely heavily on dataset-provided cameras, LiDAR depth, or 3D box annotations, limiting their applicability to unannotated driving videos. Pretrained visual models can predict cameras, depth, and object tracks from RGB alone, but their geometry is mutually inconsistent: multi-view predictions come in short windows with independent scales and frames, while monocular depth is metric only per image. Our key insight is that, once reconciled into a single metric frame, these priors can replace dataset annotations for feed-forward 4D reconstruction. We present \textbf{FreeForward4D}, which calibrates overlapping prediction windows against monocular metric depth and registers them into one frame across time. On this geometry, 2D tracks are associated across cameras in 3D, and objects move through hierarchical motion that combines a shared object velocity with local Gaussian corrections, so that both training and inference use only RGB videos. On Waymo, FreeForward4D achieves 27.66 dB PSNR and 0.836 SSIM while recovering metric-scale cameras and depth without scale alignment. It surpasses STORM, SLARM-F, UFO, DGGT, and StreetForward, which rely on dataset annotations, in full-image PSNR and attains the best RGB fidelity on 8-second windows. RGB-only fine-tuning further adapts the framework to nuScenes and Argoverse 2. Code will be released.

Abstract
Lidar-inertial odometry (LIO) has an efficiency problem. High-resolution lidars produce millions of points per second that must be processed online on the resource-constrained hardware of robots, cars, or drones, yet most LIO methods neither bound their memory nor report their compute footprint. We introduce Pelorus, a resource-aware LIO with a fixed-capacity multiresolution map of voxelized normal distributions and an iterated equivariant state estimator. A relevance-based eviction keeps the map memory bounded, and a maturity-weighted map update suppresses inconsistent observations. The pipeline runs in parallel on CPUs and GPUs. On five sequences from three public datasets, Pelorus achieves the lowest relative pose error in seven of ten metrics with a small, bounded memory footprint and, with GPU offloading, the lowest CPU load of all compared methods. On an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX, it processes a 128-beam lidar at 10 Hz. Pelorus is open source with CUDA, Vulkan, and Metal backends.

Abstract
Industrial cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) is essential for non-destructive testing, but accurate 3D reconstruction from noisy projections is hindered by cone-beam artifacts, scatter, high-density metal attenuation, and limited paired training data. We propose a CAD-driven spectrum-simulated CBCT framework coupled with an FDK-guided score-diffusion Model-Based Deep Learning (MoDL) network. First, a realistic virtual acquisition pipeline is built from 350 CAD models using a 260 kVp SpekPy spectrum, material attenuation, scatter, and detector noise. Proprioceptive Simultaneous Base and Terrain Elevation Estimation for Legged Robot LocomotionCORE3
Authors: Michael Maravgakis, Stylianos Piperakis, Panos Trahanias
Published: 2026-11-18
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Legged robots in visually degraded environments must rely exclusively on proprioceptive sensing, under which global position and yaw are unobservable. Vertical drift is particularly detrimental, as it corrupts the height reference required by swing-leg trajectories and can destabilize the robot. We treat the terrain itself as a persistent proprioceptive landmark: every footstep provides a height measurement that anchors the vertical axis. We propose Simultaneous Base and Elevation Estimation (SBEE), a real-time open-source framework in which a Left-Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (LI-EKF) for floating-base estimation and a grid-based elevation map are coupled in a bidirectional loop, mirroring SLAM with terrain elevation in place of landmarks. Stable foot contacts insert uncertainty-aware height samples into the map, and previously mapped cells are fed back as uncertainty-weighted vertical pseudo-measurements that act analogously to loop closures. Unlike contact-aided invariant filters, the LI-EKF consumes a kinematic velocity measurement instead of augmenting the state with a contact model per leg, so its state dimension is independent of the number of legs. We evaluate SBEE in MuJoCo on quadrupedal and bipedal platforms and on the ANYmal-D GrandTour dataset over 296 m of locomotion, reducing the absolute trajectory error by 22.1% relative to the best of three state-of-the-art proprioceptive estimators.

Abstract
LiDAR and cameras provide complementary measurements for 3D vision, yet agreement between their observations does not establish the accuracy of either sensor's internal geometry. We show that systematic LiDAR elevation biases can be absorbed into the estimated extrinsics. Using synchronized LiDAR intensities and camera-estimated checkerboard poses, we jointly estimate a sensor-specific elevation correction shared across recordings and extrinsics for each recording. Ray-plane intersections locate the checker pattern without using reported ranges, preventing range errors from directly moving the lookup. Under matched intensity and correction models, this lookup gives smaller camera-consistency residuals than measured-point lookup. Across six identically designed rigs using the same sensor models, recording-excluded corrections reduce the mean translation-reference distance from 7.57 to 3.04 mm. On separate structures, they reduce inter-plane angle errors against references from independent dense camera scans and improve image-edge alignment. Rolling the rigs shifts the plane pairs exhibiting the largest angular improvements, as predicted for an elevation-only correction. Within the ray-plane model, calibration-target projections and loop-consistency residuals change little despite these geometric improvements. These findings show why agreement after extrinsic calibration need not establish correct internal LiDAR geometry. Synchronized observations of a familiar checkerboard provide a practical route to estimating and testing elevation corrections without a dedicated large calibration field. We plan to release code and data for reproducing the main results.

Abstract
Semantic labels say what a building element is, not what it does: two identical-looking windows on one facade may be operable or fixed, a column may carry load or only decorate. Abstract
Once deployed on vehicles, end-to-end driving models continuously encounter challenging scenarios, generating valuable human takeover data that capture both model outputs and human behaviors. However, human interventions are often imperfect, as drivers may intervene too late or unnecessarily. To address this challenge, we propose an offline post-training framework that transforms imperfect takeover data into proactive supervision. Abstract

Abstract
Arbitrary-scale super-resolution (ASSR) of remote sensing images remains challenging due to the limited high-frequency information in a single low-resolution (LR) observation.
