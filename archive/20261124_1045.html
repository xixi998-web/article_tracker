Estimation and Spatial Distribution of Critical Loads for Acid Deposition in South KoreaCORE15
Authors: Sang-Deok Lee, Myeong-Ju Kim, Joon-Young Ahn, Jae-Yoon Lee, In-Ho Song...
Venue: Atmosphere
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Acid deposition can cause long-term soil acidification and nutrient imbalance in forest ecosystems. This study estimated the critical loads (CLs) of sulfur and nitrogen for forest ecosystems in South Korea using the Simple Mass Balance (SMB) model and recent national datasets. The estimated critical loads were compared with atmospheric deposition to assess sulfur, nitrogen, and combined sulfur–nitrogen exceedances. The maximum sulfur critical load (CLmax(S)) showed substantial spatial variation, mainly ranging from 0 to 2500 eq ha−1 yr−1, reflecting differences in base cation weathering rates. In contrast, the nutrient nitrogen critical load (CLnut(N)) was relatively uniform, ranging mainly from 640 to 700 eq ha−1 yr−1. Nitrogen deposition exceeded CLnut(N) across most forested areas, whereas sulfur deposition remained below its critical load throughout the country. Compared with the previous assessment, the average nitrogen exceedance decreased by approximately 72%. This change may be related to reduced atmospheric deposition and changes in forest growth and nutrient uptake. The results indicate that the risk of sulfur-induced acidification has decreased, while nitrogen deposition remains a concern for forest ecosystems. The estimated exceedances represent potential ecological risks rather than direct evidence of forest damage. Continued monitoring and nitrogen deposition management are therefore needed.

Venue: Frontiers in Soil Science
Published: 2026-11-17

Abstract
Soil fertility constraints in the Northern Guinea Savanna of Nigeria were evaluated in relation to slope position, parent material, and World Reference Base (WRB) Reference Soil Groups (RSGs) using 157 georeferenced legacy soil profile pits. Descriptive statistics, Moran's I, Pearson correlation, principal component analysis (PCA), general linear models (GLMs) with and without interaction terms, and a Fertility Deviation Index (FDI) referenced to high-fertility benchmarks were applied. All 11 soil properties showed significant positive spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I = 0.14 -0.47). Organic carbon (OC) correlated positively with total exchangeable bases (TEB, r = 0.348), cation exchange capacity (CEC, r = 0.272), and base saturation percentage (BSP, r = 0.233), while clay correlated with CEC (r = 0.329). Four principal components explained 61.5% of the total variance. Parent material was associated with exchangeable potassium (F = 5.40, p < 0.001) and pH (F = 5.72, p = 0.002), slope position with particle-size fractions and total nitrogen (TN), and WRB RSGs with CEC (F = 7.35, p = 0.001) and clay (F = 3.49, p = 0.003). Models explained 3 -27% of the variance. Valley-bottom soils had higher OC, TN and available phosphorus (av. P), while Gleysols had the highest OC and BSP. FDI values were negative across all indicators and groups, with the largest deviations for OC (−50.3 to −91.8%), CEC (−52.4 to −75.3%) and av. P (−43.8 to −68.9%). Fertility constraints reflect the combined influence of slope position, parent material and soil class. Low OC and CEC were the most pronounced constraints relative to high-fertility benchmarks. FDI values indicate benchmark deviations, not temporal depletion, and management implications require field validation.
Analysis of Key Factors Influencing Soil Property Changes in Continuously Cropped Greenhouse Strawberry in the Guiyang AreaCORE5
Authors: Rong-Fei Li, Shi-Pin Yang, Hong-Ye Ma, B. Shu, Yu-Rong Li...
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
To identify the key factors driving shifts in soil properties in continuously cropped greenhouse strawberry soils in Guiyang, soil with no prior strawberry cultivation was used as the control (G1). Four additional treatments were established: fallow (G2), rain leaching (G3), solarization combined with calcium cyanamide disinfection (G4), and dazomet fumigation (G5). Soil physicochemical properties, nutrients, salt ions, and enzyme activities were determined before and after one year of cultivation. Short-term cultivation exerted minimal effects on soil water content and bulk density; only G1 exhibited a notable reduction in bulk density. After one year of cultivation, soil pH generally increased and electrical conductivity (EC) decreased, whereas macronutrients remained abundant despite partial declines under prolonged continuous cropping. Available Cu and Fe were sufficient. Available B ranged from extremely to severely deficient in most treatments (moderate only in S1 and S6-F), and available Zn was mostly abundant. F−, SO42−, and Mg2+ increased markedly after cultivation, while Cl−, Na+, and HCO3− decreased. Catalase and sucrase activities generally declined. Accordingly, treatments without effective disinfection (G2 and G3) suffered severe yield losses, whereas disinfection treatments (G4 and G5) effectively alleviated this yield reduction. Continuous cropping and disinfection substantially modified soil chemical characteristics, salt-ion composition, and enzyme activities. Trace element imbalance (predominantly available B deficiency), salt-ion imbalance, and reduced enzyme activities may serve as key risk factors responsible for shifts in soil properties and productivity decline in this greenhouse strawberry continuous-cropping system in Guiyang.
Conversion of coastal wetlands to rice cultivation increases methane and nitrous oxide emissions through altered microbial communities in the Sundarban, India.CORE4
Authors: S. K. Nayak, P. Bhattacharyya, C. Pradhan, A. Moharana
Venue: Environmental research
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs
Abstract
Coastal mangrove wetlands play a crucial role in global carbon sequestration and climate regulation. However, their conversion to rice systems to meet food demand significantly alters greenhouse gases (GHGs) dynamics. While previous studies have primarily focused on methane (CH4) emissions and methanotrophic responses, the role of methylotrophic communities and their interaction with nitrogen-cycling processes in regulating combined CH4 and nitrous oxide (N2O) emissions remains poorly understood. In this study, a space-for-time substitution approach was employed to investigate the effects of mangrove-to-rice conversion on CH4 and N2O fluxes, associated microbial functional groups (methanotrophs, methylotrophs, methanogens, nitrifiers, and denitrifiers), and soil physicochemical properties in the Sundarban, India. Our results showed that conversion to rice cultivation led to an 837.7% increase in CH4 fluxes and a 47.1% increase in N2O fluxes compared with mangrove wetlands. These changes were accompanied by reduced methanotrophic abundance and diversity, increased representation of methanogenic and denitrifying communities, and marked shifts in methylotrophic community structure. These microbial responses were strongly associated with increased ammonium (NH4+) and nitrate (NO3-) concentrations, reduced salinity, and changes in soil pH. Overall, the findings link coastal land-use change with elevated CH4 and N2O fluxes and shifts in microbial communities involved in methane and nitrogen cycling. The results highlight the importance of conserving mangrove wetlands to maintain their climate-regulating functions and of incorporating greenhouse gas mitigation into rice-management strategies in converted coastal landscapes.
Ecological consequences of coastal land formation: soil bacterial succession and assembly mechanisms along a reclamation chronosequenceCORE3
Authors: Qiang Wang, Ji-Zhen Jiao, Feng-Yi Wang, Fan Li, Jie Cheng...
Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Tidal flat land formation is a major land-use practice in coastal regions, profoundly influencing soil ecological functions mediated by microbial communities. However, the mechanisms underlying soil bacterial succession during coastal land reclamation remain poorly understood. Here, we examined soil bacterial community dynamics along a 1- to 14-year land formation chronosequence on Hengsha Island in the Yangtze River Estuary. Significant temporal variation in bacterial communities was observed, with markedly lower diversity but higher relative abundance of Actinobacteriota in the initial stage. Community diversity stabilized after three years, while Nitrospirota gradually increased with land formation time. Beta-diversity analysis revealed clear successional patterns dominated by species turnover. Venn analysis further indicated substantial shifts in the local species pool across different land ages, identifying it as the primary mechanism shaping community variation. Environmental drivers were assessed using random forest and structural equation modeling. Results showed that soil physicochemical properties, particularly pH and electrical conductivity, were key factors regulating bacterial communities. Land formation time was related to soil temperature and pH, which directly affected bacterial composition and indirectly regulated it through changes in phylogenetic diversity. Overall, our findings highlight the critical role of environmental filtering and species pool dynamics in microbial succession during coastal land formation. This study provides important insights into the ecological impacts of reclamation and offers a scientific basis for sustainable land-use management aimed at preserving biodiversity and maintaining ecosystem health.

Venue: Land Degradation &amp; Development
Published: 2026-11-17

Abstract
Microbial inoculants and organic substrates are effective measures for saline‐sodic soil remediation, but their individual and interactive effects across rhizosphere and non‐rhizosphere soils remain unclear. We evaluated three microbial inoculants (M1, M2, and M3), an organic substrate (B), and their combinations (BM1, BM2, and BM3) during two consecutive 45‐day Sesbania cannabina pot experiments. The substrate primarily promoted soil organic carbon accumulation, accounting for 57.31% of its variation, whereas inoculants explained more than 80% of the variation in pH and sodium adsorption ratio. M2 and BM2 increased mean plant biomass across the two growing seasons by 279.39% and 281.14%, respectively, relative to the control. Inoculants were also the dominant drivers of bacterial community structure, explaining 18.05% and 18.78% of the variation in non‐rhizosphere and rhizosphere soils, respectively, compared with 3.71% and 3.40% for the substrate. Treatment effects were compartment dependent: M2 was associated with greater microbial network robustness, whereas M3 and BM3 enriched bacterial taxa and predicted functions related to carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur cycling. Overall, microbial inoculants and organic substrates contributed distinctly but complementarily to saline‐sodic soil amelioration, with inoculants primarily regulating salinity‐related properties and microbial communities and the substrate mainly enhancing soil carbon accumulation.

Assessing the suitability of a new porcine dry bone scoring method for South African burial taphonomy when applied to two posthumous human skeletal samples.PROXY2
Authors: Nicola Jackson, Claudia Landsman, Anja Meyer
Venue: International journal of legal medicine
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Taphonomy, the study of environmental effects on decomposition and skeletonisation across different environments, requires an in-depth understanding of contextual variation. This study aimed to test the suitability of porcine taphonomic scoring criteria when applied to the human skeletal remains of individuals from the historic Koffiefontein and Witwatersrand Deep mines and to assess the effects of different burial environments on bone. The sample comprised 60 individuals and was divided into six skeletal regions and scored on six alterations: bone weathering, adipocere formation, acidic soil corrosion, depositional soil staining, plant activity and animal activity. Soil staining showed the most variation between the Koffiefontein and porcine groups, with Koffiefontein exhibiting unique artefact staining. Significant differences were found between the Koffiefontein and porcine groups for all alterations (p < 0.026), except animal activity. Following these results, amendments were made to the scoring criteria to better fit patterns observed in human bone taphonomy, and taphonomic comparisons were then conducted between the two human groups to assess the effects of differential burial environments. Bone weathering and corrosion showed the most prominent differences between the human groups. Significant differences were found between the human groups in all dry bone alterations (p < 0.035), except for soil staining. Variation in climatic conditions and burial microenvironments appears to underlie these taphonomic differences. Taken together, the findings indicate that the porcine scoring system is inadequate for characterising taphonomy in human remains, underscoring the need to account for the specific burial environment when interpreting taphonomic evidence.
Depth-Dependent Dynamics of Humus Composition and Clay Minerals in Albic Soils: Arbor Woodland and Dryland in Northeast ChinaPROXY2
Authors: Ming Liu, Dong-Hui Dai, Ming-Shuo Wang
Venue: Sustainability
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Northeast China’s albic soil, classified as Stagnic Planosols in the World Reference Base for Soil Resources (WRB), is a critical agricultural soil type that suffers persistent degradation driven by its unique albic horizon (E). The coarse-textured, low-clay albic horizon restricts nutrient retention via low cation-exchange capacity, while its compact underlying illuvial Bt layer impedes vertical water and solute translocation, jointly hindering nutrient migration and release, coupled with long-term tillage-induced soil structural deterioration. This study characterized vertical profile differentiation of dissolved organic matter (DOM) properties, humus (HS) composition, humic acid (HA) molecular structure, and clay mineral dynamics in two representative individual profiles under natural arbor woodland and human-disturbed dryland in the same small watershed. The main results were as follows: (1) The Ah layer of the arbor woodland exhibited higher proxy-indicated microbial contribution and humification degree (derived from DOM optical indices) than the Ap layer of the dryland, with a higher DOM carbon content (CDOM, 0.390 mg kg−1) that decreased by 40.5% with increasing depth. (2) Arbor woodland maintained higher contents of water-soluble substances, HA, humic-extracted acid, and humin (CWSS, CHA, CHE, and CHu) in the Ah layer, with more pronounced depth-dependent declines relative to dryland. (3) With increasing soil depth, the HA H/C ratio decreased and aliphatic C shifted toward aromatic C (enhanced aromatization) in arbor woodland, whereas in dryland the aliphatic-to-aromatic C ratio increased (weaker aromatization) and the H/C ratio followed a non-monotonic sequence. (4) Clay minerals were dominated by 2:1 silicates; arbor woodland had more illite-smectite mixed layers (I/S), whereas dryland had elevated kaolinite content consistent with tillage-enhanced leaching. Overall, the arbor woodland profile showed patterns consistent with HS humification, HA aromatization, and molecular indicators suggestive of potential organic-C stabilization (not direct measurements of long-term C persistence), whereas long-term agricultural practices appeared to impair these key biogeochemical processes in the investigated dryland profile. These findings represent a comparative case study of two individual soil profiles (without landscape-level independent replication) and require validation with replicated landscape-scale sampling; they provide a preliminary theoretical foundation for scientific and sustainable management of albic soils.

Abstract
The content of gross and mobile forms of heavy metals (Cd, Pb, Co, Cu, Ni, Zn, Cr, Mn, Fe) in the soils of the catchment area and bottom sediments of the cooling reservoir of the Zainskaya SDPP was studied. It is noted that the depositing media are comparable in particle size distribution but differ statistically significantly in the content of coarse silt and clay fractions, as well as in the reaction of the medium (pH), while exhibiting a similar organic matter content (averaging about 6.5%). It is established that the average values of the gross content of Pb, Cu, Zn, Cr, and Mn in bottom sediments are 1.5–3.6 times higher, and mobile forms of Cd, Pb, Co, Ni, Cr, Mn, and Fe are 2–9 times higher than in the catchment soils. It was revealed that in the soils, the metal content is generally close to regional background values, whereas in the bottom sediments, multiple exceedances of the background are observed for a number of elements (Cd, Cu, Ni, Zn, Cr, Mn). Priority pollutants (Cd, Cu, Zn, Mn) and environmental risk factors associated with the transformation of metal forms under reducing conditions in bottom sediments have been identified.
Coupled carbon-nitrogen dynamics and soil stabilization under perennial alley cover cropping in raspberry plantingECO1
Authors: A. Messiga, Busayo Kodaolu, N. Ziadi, N. Pandey, Shibli Md Abedin
Venue: Frontiers in Soil Science
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Agriculture faces the dual challenge of sustaining productivity and mitigating climate change through soil organic carbon (SOC) sequestration. The current management practice in perennial raspberry production in the Fraser Valley, British Columbia, Canada, pre-disposes the soil to continual degradation via incessant tillage of the alleys with no C input. Therefore, a regenerative approach is critical for restoring SOC and structural stability. The objective of this study was to determine how specific cover crops and selected mixtures influence the reorganization of soil organic matter fractions and the distribution of C and nitrogen (N) across water-stable aggregates and Silt+Clay size fraction after eight years of production. Five cover crop treatments including Red Clover (RCl, Trifolium pratense ), Ryegrass (RG, Lolium multiflorum ), RCl+RG mixture, Mustard+RCl+RG (Mustard, Mu, Brassica juncea ) mixture, and Radish+RCl+RG (Radish, Ra, Raphanus sativus ) mixture were compared with a tilled Control treatment. Results showed that alley cover cropping significantly improved soil structural stability by increasing the macroaggregates (2000-250 µm) from 24.67% in the tilled Control to 38.61% under RG monoculture and RCl+RG mixture. Conversely, mesoaggregates (250-53 µm) decreased from 51.72% in the tilled Control to 41.56% under RCl+RG, but the effect was not significant. The mean weight diameter (MWD) improved from 360.01 µm in the tilled Control to a peak of 507.14 µm in the RCl+RG mixture. This mixture also achieved the highest concentration of C (24.23 g kg⁻¹) and N (2.09 g kg⁻¹) when compared to other treatments. Soil C stabilization pathways were highly treatment-specific: RCl+RG primarily enhanced water-stable aggregate associated C concentration (2.02%) compared with the tilled Control (1.49%). In contrast, only RG improved C content, exhibiting the highest C and N concentrations associated with Silt+Clay size fraction (3.92% C and 0.41% N). In conclusion, eight years of alley cover cropping effectively changed soil structure and established diverse C stabilization pathways. These findings underscore the importance of functional species complementarity of cover crops for enhancing soil C concentrations, providing a mechanistic framework to climate-resilient perennial agroecosystems in cool-temperate regions.
Assessment of Physical Composition of Municipal Solid Waste in Ikot Ekpene Metropolis, Akwa Ibom State, NigeriaECO1
Authors: E. S. Akpanudo, G. Usoh, E. O. Sam, Ndianabasi I. Sampson
Venue: Asian Journal of Advanced Research and Reports
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study assessed the physical composition of municipal solid waste at Abiakpo Ntak Inyang Municipal Waste Dumpsite in Ikot Ekpene Metropolis to inform effective waste management strategies. Effective municipal solid waste management requires a comprehensive understanding of the types and proportions of waste generated within a given locality. Samples of MSW were collected and characterized using the spot sampling method. The Abiakpo Ntak Inyang waste dumpsite was divided into northern, southern, eastern and western sections for sample collection. MSW samples were collected from four sampling points at the dumpsite and bulked to form a composite sample. Forty kilograms (40 kg) of raw MSW were collected at each of the four spots and bulked to form a composite sample of 160 kg. Sampling was conducted three times per month for a total of three months. A total of twelve (12) composite samples totalling one thousand four hundred and forty kilograms (1440 kg) of MSW samples were collected.  The samples were sorted into eight (8) different components, namely: vegetables and food wastes, plastics, papers, metals, glasses, textiles, nylons and others. The ‘others’ category represents solid wastes that do not fall into the seven categories.  Sorting and weighing of the collected waste were carried out at the dumpsite.  The components of the wastes were weighed using a weighing balance; volumes and percentages were calculated, and the proximate composition of the waste was analysed. The results showed that the solid waste at the dumpsite consisted of vegetable/food waste (40.50%), plastics (9.12%), papers (6.51%), metals (5.23%), glasses (6.18%), textiles (7.15%), nylon (22.74%) and other materials (2.57%). The composition demonstrates the heterogeneous nature of dumpsite waste and suggests that both biodegradable and recyclable materials constitute substantial proportions of the waste stream. Their proportions, however, varied among waste categories in the order vegetable/food wastes > nylon > plastics > papers > textile > metal > glasses. The variability of the solid waste materials at the dumpsite was low, suggesting that the sampled waste contained broadly similar materials. Proximate analysis of the MSW in the study area showed that moisture content had the highest mean value, indicating that food waste contributes significantly to leachate production from the wastes.  It was concluded that dumpsite waste/leachate has a significant effect on the chemical properties of the soil including the heavy metal concentration.  It was recommended, among other measures, that hazardous waste items should be eliminated at source through proper waste sorting and segregation to reduce the level of soil pollution by the dumpsite leachate.
