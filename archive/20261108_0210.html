Authors: Ori Francos, D. Raviv
Venue: IEEE Sensors Journal
Published: 2026-11-01
Abs · PDF
Abstract
We introduce HumetricNeRF, a depth-supervised dynamic neural radiance field (NeRF) for reconstructing humans in motion from sparse views. Our method leverages physical depth sensors, which provide reliable, albeit noisy, metric measurements and serve as supervisory signals for scene geometry reconstruction. When active sensing is degraded or unavailable, monocular depth estimation (MDE) is incorporated as a learned sensing modality, providing dense virtual depth priors. By fusing these complementary sensing modalities, our approach overcomes key challenges in full-body radiance field reconstruction under sparse-view conditions, where NeRF methods are known to suffer significant performance degradation. While previous works leveraged depth priors to improve photorealistic rendering and enforce geometric constraints in static scenes, extending depth supervision to constantly changing dynamic scenes presents substantially greater challenges. Our novel framework builds on the representation of humans as a neural field, which enables free-viewpoint rendering given a monocular video. Using our approach, fused depth priors are incorporated during training to improve the volumetric representation of humans in motion, grounding learning with sensor-based metric supervision when NeRF struggles to infer accurate scene geometry. Additional background-aware color and density penalties further suppress undesired artifacts, yielding cleaner reconstructions. We demonstrate improved visual and quantitative results on numerous few-view dynamic human scenes.

Authors: Gyeong Chan Kim, Youngseok Jang, Jeong-Dae Heo, H. Kim
Venue: IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters
Published: 2026-11-01
Abs
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as an effective scene representation for visual localization, but most existing methods rely on embedding keypoint descriptors into Gaussian primitives, leading to high memory overhead and requiring joint optimization of geometry and features. We propose SparKLoc, a visual localization framework that directly matches 2D image features with 3D Gaussian primitives, eliminating the need for descriptor distillation. Our method operates on a pre-built 3DGS model, fully decoupling scene reconstruction from feature learning. Furthermore, we introduce a sparse selection strategy that extracts a compact set of discriminative key-Gaussians for matching, reducing the memory footprint significantly compared to the keypoint-distillation approaches. We train a cross-modal attention matcher that establishes 2D-3D correspondences between patchwise image features and learned 3D primitive features. Camera pose is first estimated via PnP + RANSAC based on the correspondence set and the estimate is subsequently refined by image-to-image feature matching against a rendered view. Experiments on standard visual localization benchmarks demonstrate that SparKLoc outperforms the prior 3DGS-based feature-distillation approaches with substantially improved memory efficiency.

Abstract
The growing demand for physical AI is expanding the capabilities of autonomous systems; however, these capabilities require accurate trajectory estimation and precise 3D map reconstruction for understanding the surrounding environment. In this context, LiDAR Odometry and Mapping (LOAM) has gained attention as a widely adopted SLAM framework that offers dense geometric map reconstruction. Nevertheless, the large number of keypoints captured per frame makes real-time hardware acceleration of LOAM extremely challenging in mobile robots, and even high-performance CPUs/GPUs fail to meet real-time constraints. This paper presents a real-time and energy-efficient heterogeneous processor with four key architectural features to address the dominant memory and computational bottlenecks of LOAM acceleration: 1) a 3D spherical-bin partitioning with neighboring bin search tailored to LiDAR point distributions, reducing the number of comparisons by 99.6%; 2) a hash-page-based memory management unit that lowers the required on-chip memory capacity by 96.9% through prediction-based dynamic memory allocation exploiting spatio-temporal coherence; 3) a neighboring-bin cache that achieves a $10.5\times $ improvement in kNN energy efficiency by reducing redundant memory accesses and increasing PE utilization; and 4) a reconfigurable non-linear optimization core that shortens NLO latency by 92.3% through multi-level computational parallelization. Abstract
Point cloud–based robot policy learning has emerged as a powerful approach for robotic manipulation by enabling policies to directly exploit 3D geometric structure. In such pipelines, depth image acquisition plays a critical role in point cloud construction. However, raw depth from depth cameras is often noisy, sensitive to transparent, reflective, and repetitive surfaces, and limited by a minimum sensing distance. Recent advances in learned depth estimation offer a promising alternative: modern monocular, stereo, and multi-view approaches have achieved impressive performance in vision benchmarks. However, robotics still lacks a systematic understanding of how these advances translate to end-to-end policy learning and execution. In this work, based on our introduced Depth-Estimated Imitation Learning (DEIL) general framework, we present the first systematic study of how modern depth estimation impacts point cloud–based imitation learning across two paradigms: In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) and Behaviour Cloning (BC). Within each paradigm, we keep the point cloud reconstruction procedure, policy backbone, and evaluation protocol fixed across depth sources. Through 71,800 simulated and 160 real-world rollouts, several depth estimators achieve reasonable downstream performance in simulation, although a consistent gap to GT depth remains. In real-world ICIL, the best-performing multi-view estimator is competitive with the tested RGB-D cameras on normal tasks and achieves higher success when depth sensing is unreliable. Our failure mode analysis further shows that reconstructed geometric quality generally correlates with policy success, with flying points being particularly harmful to BC. These findings highlight the need for improved geometric awareness and stability before learned depth estimation can be broadly deployed in robot manipulation.

Multi-View 3D Reconstruction by Learning Robust Correspondence Structure With Shape PriorCORE4
Authors: Zan Li, Xin Jia, Xu Cheng, Fan Shi, Sheng-Yong Chen
Venue: IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems for Video Technology
Published: 2026-11-01
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Most multi-view 3D reconstruction pipelines reconstruct the 3D shape of a given object by aggregating features from multiple images. We believe that learning the correspondence structure across views conditioned on mining information from self-occluded parts within each view can enhance the effectiveness of feature aggregation. Unfortunately, this insight is yet to be investigated in current methods. This paper presents an approach termed Multi-view 3D Reconstruction by learning robust Correspondence structure with Shape Prior (M3RCS). It reconstructs the high-quality voxel representation from multiple RGB images. Firstly, a shape prior extraction network is devised in this paper. It applies a pre-trained prototype attentive model to capture global semantics for each view. Then, the shape prior of the given object is constructed via combining the global semantics from all views, promoting the mining of information from self-occluded parts. Secondly, a correspondence structure learning network is proposed to model an elementary correspondence structure among different views. Further, the network injects the constructed shape prior into the elementary correspondences, so as to learn a robust correspondence structure across all views despite the presence of self-occlusion in each view. This can realize the feature aggregation from multiple images effectively. Lastly, a fine-grained reconstruction network is designed, which progressively up-samples the aggregated feature and preserves useful information from the up-sampled result to produce a fine-grained 3D shape in voxel representation. Quantitative and qualitative experiments on the ShapeNet and Pix3D datasets verify that our M3RCS outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods.

Abstract
Pose-Free sparse 3D multi-view reconstruction plays a crucial role in computer vision, as it relies only on a limited number of views. Although existing methods have achieved significant progress, they depend solely on the restricted information available in the sparse views and often struggle to fully reconstruct the structure of objects. To address this challenge, we propose a Region-Weighted Semantic Guidance (RWSG) method for pose-free sparse 3D multi-view reconstruction, enabling accurate 3D reconstruction using only sparse views together with semantic information. Specifically, by fully leveraging semantics as contextual cues to weight the image information, our method guides the model to more effectively focus on object-relevant regions within the images—similar to how humans infer 3D structures from limited viewpoints based on real-world prior knowledge. Finally, we introduce the Cross-Modal Dual-Path Aggregator (CMDPA) module to mitigate the inevitable noise introduced during the fusion of semantic and visual features. This module enhances feature representations through learnable tokens and selectively retains features that are more relevant to the object’s structure. Extensive experiments on the synthetic ShapeNet dataset and real-world datasets demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms current state-of-the-art approaches across multiple evaluation metrics.

Abstract
Computational imaging can reduce the acquisition burden when the system is designed around task-relevant information rather than complete image recovery. We investigate this framework for sparse-view planar X-ray tomography (XCT) of layered objects, using measurement of the axial termination gap (ATG) of back-drilled printed-circuit-board (PCB) vias as a representative task. Instead of using 3D reconstruction, the proposed method estimates ATG directly from polychromatic projection data through a physics-based sensitivity model. A gated hierarchical estimator first fits ATG together with radiometric perturbations and selectively introduces global geometry and specimen-warp models only when a reduced chi-squared adequacy test and Bayesian information criterion justify the added degrees of freedom. The same low-dimensional perturbation model is incorporated into marginalized Fisher-information analyses, yielding task-specific conditional and calibration-aware Cram'er-Rao bounds. Numerical experiments show that, under a fixed total photon budget, task-driven metrology changes little over 12-64 views, while reconstruction-driven metrology is strongly view-dependent with much larger errors. The direct estimator also follows the expected photon-limited scaling. These results demonstrate a computational sensing strategy in which XCT acquisition, physical modeling, and inference are evaluated according to the quantitative information required by the task rather than for general-purpose volumetric image fidelity.
