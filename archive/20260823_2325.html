Gaussian-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning for 3D Gaussian SplatsCORE5
Authors: Bin Ren, Qi Ma, Yue Li, Zongyan Han, Yidi Li...
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-08-16
Abs
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents 3D content with anisotropic primitives that jointly encode geometry and appearance. Fixed-budget encoders consume sampled observations of Gaussian assets, so the same object may be observed through different primitive realizations. Existing self-supervised methods mainly reconstruct masked Gaussian attributes, tying supervision to one sampled realization and requiring an input-space decoder. Latent prediction offers an alternative, but its application to Gaussian tokens requires targets that accommodate coupled attributes and heterogeneous spatial support. We introduce Gaussian-JEPA, which predicts representations of held-out Gaussian token blocks from visible context. An online encoder processes the context, while a shared exponential-moving-average encoder supplies stop-gradient features for multi-scale targets. Complementary target projections and feature-space grounding provide latent supervision without reconstructing Gaussian attributes. We evaluate the features under Gaussian resampling, partial observations, and renderable shape completion, together with transfer to part segmentation and object classification. Compared with matched reconstruction pretraining, Gaussian-JEPA is more consistent across resampled inputs, retains more instance information under partial observations, and provides stronger frozen features for Gaussian completion. These results support latent prediction as an effective objective for reusable 3D Gaussian representations. Code is on the project page (https://amazingren.github.io/Gaussian-JEPA/).
An affective computing framework for preschool music education using MoE architecture and neural radiance fieldsCORE5
Authors: Xinxin Shi
Venue: Discover Artificial Intelligence
Published: 2026-08-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
The key content of this paper is the digital construction of preschool music education scenarios and affective computing. It adopts an innovative approach, integrating the MoE architecture and neural radiance field technology together. The Edu-NeRF reconstruction framework is relied on to achieve high-fidelity reconstruction of dynamic scenes, and its PSNR value is 28.5. The SSIM value is 0.92, which is 10.8% higher than that of the traditional method. This paper also constructed an affective computing model. After training this model with 60 h of multimodal data from 120 preschool children, its accuracy rate for emotional state recognition reached 89.7%, with a macroscopic F1 value of 0.881. By combining cross-modal fusion algorithms, we have successfully broken through the technical bottlenecks in dynamic scene reconstruction and affective computing. In terms of real-world applicability, this approach significantly outperforms conventional 2D music education products. It enables real-time, high-fidelity interaction on standard low-power devices, thereby reducing hardware deployment costs and lowering the barrier for widespread school adoption. Furthermore, experimental verification demonstrates that this model increases children’s learning engagement and the efficiency of personalized interaction by 30%. By enhancing both the immersive learning experience and teacher acceptance, this framework provides a highly practical and scalable technical path for the digital transformation of early childhood education.
