Trace-metal responses to enhanced rock weathering with basalt in agricultural and riparian soilsCORE5
Authors: Quinn C. Zacharias, Rob Rioux, Feng-Chao Sun, Wyatt S. Tatge, Joshua L. Warren...
Venue: Nutrient Cycling in Agroecosystems
Published: 2026-11-14
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Enhanced rock weathering (ERW) is a promising carbon dioxide removal strategy, but its field deployment requires careful evaluation of metal-related risks. Crushed, low-Ni basalt (20 t ha−1) was surface-applied in June 2023 to hayfield and pasture soils in a working Vermont dairy agroecosystem, and an adjacent hydrologically connected riparian corridor was monitored for downstream changes in extractable soil metals and Ca:Al ratios. Agricultural surface soils (0–15 cm) were sampled twice before application (fall 2022 and spring 2023) and four times afterward (fall 2023; spring 2024; fall 2024; spring 2025), and central riparian corridor soils were sampled once before application and during four post-treatment campaigns. Forage biomass and tissue metal concentrations (Ni, Cu, Zn, Mn) were measured at first cut in spring 2023 (pre-treatment) and spring 2024 (one-year post-treatment). Extractable soil metals were quantified using the Modified Morgan extractant. In agricultural plots, extractable Ni, Cr, Pb, Al, Mn, Zn, Cu, and Cd showed no sustained or systematic increases relative to controls, and forage biomass and tissue metal concentrations did not differ between treatments. Across plots, extractable Ni, Al, and Pb covaried most strongly (inversely) with soil pH. Downslope riparian soils showed large declines in extractable Ni, Al, and Pb (58–79% in arithmetic campaign means), with mixed-effects models indicating statistically significant departures from baseline. These declines coincided with an increase in extractable Ca:Al ratios (charge-equivalent basis), consistent with pH-driven reductions in metal lability, particularly for metals exhibiting strong hydrolysis and sorption behavior. The soil, forage, riparian, and groundwater observations indicate that agronomic-rate application of this low-Ni basalt did not create detectable metal-mobilization concerns in treated fields and coincided with reduced extractable metal concentrations in hydrologically connected riparian soils. Effective ERW monitoring should therefore consider hydrologic connectivity and track soil pH and extractable Ca:Al ratios as additional useful indicators of Ca–Al balance alongside direct measurements of metals of concern.
Physiological and Biochemical Effects of Polystyrene Micro- and Nanoplastics on Rhizophora stylosa Seedlings: A Preliminary StudyCORE3

Venue: Toxics
Published: 2026-11-14
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Mangroves are important sinks for marine microplastics, yet the effects of micro- and nanoplastics on mangrove seedlings remain insufficiently understood. Here, an outdoor pot experiment simulating mangrove field conditions was conducted to investigate the physiological and biochemical responses of Rhizophora stylosa (R. stylosa) seedlings to polystyrene microplastics (PS MPs; 5 μm) and nanoplastics (PS NPs; 100 nm) at concentrations of 50 and 500 mg/kg dry soil. Leaf net photosynthetic rate, total chlorophyll content, oxidative stress indicators in roots and leaves, and soil physicochemical properties were assessed. All four PS treatments significantly reduced leaf net photosynthetic rates compared with the control. At the end of the exposure period, total chlorophyll content decreased significantly by 52.6–64.8% under all treatments except the low-concentration PS NP treatment. PS NPs increased leaf catalase activity and reduced hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) content in both roots and leaves, whereas PS MPs had limited effects on the measured oxidative stress indicators. No significant changes were observed in soil physicochemical properties, including nitrogen content, C:N ratio, ammonium nitrogen, nitrate nitrogen, pH, and oxidation–reduction potential. Overall, PS exposure primarily affected photosynthetic performance and chlorophyll accumulation in R. stylosa seedlings, while its effects on the measured oxidative stress indicators and soil physicochemical properties were comparatively limited. These findings provide experimental evidence for assessing the potential physiological impacts of micro- and nanoplastic pollution on mangrove seedlings.
Assessment of Heavy Metals Accumulation in Freshwater Snails (Physella acuta) as Potential Bioindicators of Aquatic PollutionECO1

Abstract
The presence of heavy metals in freshwater represents a global health problem. Freshwater gastropods can integrate exposure to trace metals over time and therefore have potential value in aquatic biomonitoring, but interpretation depends critically on sample identity, tissue basis digestion volume, quality-control records and the environmental matrices measured alongside the organisms. This study evaluated the concentrations of lead (Pb), nickel (Ni), zinc (Zn), chromium (Cr), and copper (Cu) in acid-digested soft tissue samples of the freshwater snail Physella acuta collected from the Shah Alam River, Peshawar District, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. The fresh snails were collected from an approximately 100 m2 area of river, in a period from 2-8 February 2023. After removal of shells, soft tissues were separated and six samples were prepared from them each weighing 1g. Two strong acids, nitric and perchloric acids were used for tissue digestion. And the heavy metals concentrations were found out by using a technique called atomic absorption spectrophotometry (AAS). The analytical tables contained six labels, including sample 1, sample 2, sample 3, sample 4, sample 5 and sample 6. For Samples 1–6, reported digest concentrations ranged from 1.53 to 5.73 mg/L for Pb, 0.146 to 0.295 mg/L for Zn, 0.089 to 0.110 mg/L for Cr, 0.044 to 0.090 mg/L for Ni and 0.041 to 0.096 mg/L for Cu. The results represent that, the concentration of 3 heavy metals out of 5 are exceeded from the acceptable level. The results of this study showed high concentration of lead (Pb) followed by Zinc (Zn), Chromium (Cr), Nickel (Ni) and Copper (Cu) in tissues sample, out of which Pb had the highest nominal digest concentrations among the metals reported, Cr and Ni also exceeded from the normal level. However, the concentration of Zn and Cu are within the permissible levels provided by world health organization (WHO) of the United Nation. The findings provide a preliminary analytical record of heavy metals for snail-derived digests from the Shah Alam River and identify specific requirements for a stronger follow-up study.
