Measurable Semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting with Joint Region and Depth Supervision for Interactive Building InspectionCORE13
Authors: Yi Zhang, Tian Tan, Ray Kai Leung Su
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-09
Abs
Abstract
Image-based 3D reconstruction provides a scalable representation for building inspection and maintenance. However, photorealistic reconstruction alone does not fully provide the geometric measurability, semantic structure, and interactive information access required for engineering applications. This study proposes a measurable semantic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework with region and depth supervision for interactive building inspection from multi-view imagery. Mask-based region supervision suppresses irrelevant backgrounds and focuses optimization on target regions. Point-wise depth supervision guides Gaussian centers toward physical surfaces using reference geometry derived from multi-view stereo (MVS) point clouds, providing geometric anchors for measuring physical dimensions and areas. A semantic Gaussian field associates learnable class features with individual Gaussian primitives, enabling semantic rendering and component-level inspection. These capabilities are operationalized through AEC-SplatTwin, an interactive building inspection platform supporting real-time visualization, semantic analysis, quantitative measurement, and multimodal large language model (MLLM)-assisted interaction. On the synthetic UAV-inspection QuakeCity and smartphone-captured HKUPB datasets, the framework reduces Gaussian-center depth MAE by 58.1% and 86.8%, while its semantic field achieves mIoU values of 0.8529 and 0.8642. On HKUPB, the mean relative errors for height, width, and area measurements are 0.33%, 1.07%, and 0.60%. These results show that the proposed framework extends 3DGS beyond visual reconstruction into a measurable, semantically structured, and interactive engineering representation for building inspection.
Feed-Forward Multi-View Feature Coding for 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE8
Authors: Seungmi Choi, Ji-Hyoun Choi, Suhyun Jo, Tae-Gwon Park, Hui Yong Kim
Published: 2026-10-14
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods enable efficient reconstruction of 3D scenes from sparse multi-view images in a single forward pass, without scene-specific optimization. However, existing feed-forward scene-coding pipelines first generate a complete Gaussian representation and then encode its geometry and appearance attributes separately, often involving non-differentiable operations. The resulting 'generate-then-compress' pipeline requires complex attribute-specific codec designs and limits rate-distortion performance by restricting the exploitation of cross-attribute redundancy. We propose multi-view feature coding for Gaussian splatting (MFGS), an end-to-end feed-forward framework for scene coding. Instead of compressing an explicit Gaussian representation, MFGS extracts multi-view features from the input images and encodes them into a compact bitstream. Through end-to-end rate-distortion training, MFGS learns compact multi-view features that jointly represent geometry and appearance, enabling the receiver to predict Gaussian attributes from the decoded features. We further introduce importance-guided Gaussian selection to accommodate different rendering budgets, allowing the receiver to adjust the number of Gaussians used for rendering from the same bitstream. Across RE10K, ACID, and DL3DV, MFGS achieves 99.76-99.86% bitrate savings over the generate-then-compress pipeline, as measured by BD-rate, while reducing total GMACs by 52-55% and providing an approximately 12 times end-to-end speedup.

Abstract
This paper proposes a handheld multi-line laser 3D scanning system capable of real-time point cloud stitching. The hardware consists of dual industrial cameras and a laser projector, which can acquire laser line point clouds and marker point clouds from a single viewpoint. To realize the real-time fusion of multi-view point clouds, a high-precision point cloud stitching scheme is constructed based on simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) architecture. Specifically, an adaptive weighted iterative closest point (ICP) algorithm is adopted to match marker points for real-time handheld pose estimation; local mapping based on viewpoint classification is utilized to manage motion trajectories, and a multi-layer loop closure optimization structure is designed to suppress cumulative drift. Meanwhile, a stable landmark map is established to unify cross-view geometric constraints, which efficiently supports loop closure detection, viewpoint classification and relocalization, and improves the real-time performance of the system. Experimental results verify that the proposed method can achieve robust point cloud stitching without relying on two-dimensional features. Quantitative analysis shows that for locally layered point clouds, compared with the tracking-only scheme, the complete pipeline increases the volumetric density of processed local point clouds by 50% and reduces the surface thickness error by 33.04%. In addition, the scanning accuracy of the system remains stable within the range of 0.014-0.074 mm. The proposed scheme effectively addresses the key challenges of laser scanning, including insufficient stable features, inconsistent reconstruction errors and long-sequence pose drift, and balances the requirements of real-time performance and global high-precision stitching for multi-line laser reconstruction.

Venue: Image and Vision Computing

Abs
Abstract
We present HorizonNav, a video diffusion-based framework for anticipatory robot navigation that transforms a single RGB image and a text prompt into a geometrically aligned representation of plausible surrounding 3D space. Given the initial observation and a textual description of the reconstruction objective, a vision-language model, guided by designed instructions, interprets the scene and constructs structured prompts for a video diffusion model. The resulting video sequence provides plausible future observations of the environment, which are subsequently processed using monocular ORB-SLAM3 for camera pose estimation and Video Depth Anything for dense depth prediction. Our central contribution is a geometry-guided depth alignment stage that combines the estimated camera poses with triangulated visual landmarks to constrain dense depth predictions across multiple views. This alignment improves the geometric consistency of the reconstructed 3D environment, addressing inconsistencies that temporal coherence of the generated video alone cannot resolve. The experimental evaluation of navigation in imagined 3D environment shows that across a 19-scene horizon study, planning-feasible scenes increase from 13/19 at P20 to 15/19 at P81, while successful RRT trials increase from 62/90 to 75/90. In a separate corridor study, 6 of 25 evaluable generated-camera trajectories violate the reconstructed collision constraints; RRT planner recovers feasible alternatives in 5 of those 6 cases. A larger archive contains 58 usable reconstructions from 67 attempted cases, of which 36 support a downstream plan and 22 do not, exposing reconstruction and free-space failure modes rather than hiding them without lidar. Across 15 corridor reconstructions, the mean reconstruction error is approximately 0.30 m at matched structural landmarks.

Abstract
Panoramic 3D reconstruction enables the holistic recovery of complete 360 $^\circ$ scene geometry, offering significant benefits for robotics and AR/VR applications. However, directly estimating geometry from ERP images is inherently challenged by spherical distortions. To reduce spherical projection distortions, a common strategy is to decompose panoramas into distortion-free perspective views, but independently processing these views often introduces cross-view geometric inconsistencies. To address these challenges, we introduce GeoPano, a feed-forward framework for single-view panoramic 3D reconstruction that recovers a geometrically accurate and consistent point cloud from a single panorama. Abstract
Anatomical labels help 3D Gaussian splatting preserve vessels in cinematic renderings of CT, but the representation is still found by optimising each patient's scene, where densification decides where Gaussians go, while real-time displays cap their number. We address one part of this process: deciding where a fixed budget of B Gaussians should be placed. We propose a reusable, labelaware allocation front end that maps a patient's CT, labels and transfer function to a density over 2mm cells and draws exactly B positions from it without per-patient optimisation; the other Gaussian attributes are still fitted per patient. Under altered rendering presets, VACF transfers to new colours and lighting but not to a label-free transfer function, whereas the neural prior shows the opposite pattern.

Abstract
Real-time mobile volumetric video enables immersive free-viewpoint experiences, but delivering high-quality 3D content under constrained bandwidth and computation remains challenging. Recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) systems, such as V$^3$, enable efficient mobile streaming by encoding time-varying Gaussian attributes with commodity video codecs and hardware decoding. However, existing pipelines treat all Gaussians as frame-varying signals, which is poorly suited to full-scene content where large portions remain temporally stable. Our empirical study shows that frame-wise adaptation and video coding of all Gaussians cause quality degradation in complex full-scene content, particularly in temporally stable regions. To address this issue, we propose ResStreamGS, a residual-sensitive dual-stream framework that processes Gaussians according to their temporal behavior. Abstract
Autonomous mobile robots in warehouses and production facilities operate in precisely the environments that defeat point-feature visual odometry: featureless aisles, uniform walls, repetitive racking and specular floors. Such environments are, however, rich in straight-edge structure: rack uprights, door frames, wall junctions and cabinet outlines present high-contrast linear edges stable across wide viewpoint changes. A line-feature extension to cuVSLAM, a CUDA-accelerated visual-SLAM system, is presented that recovers this signal. Efficient color representation for 3D Gaussian splatting via structured spherical harmonic optimizationCORE5
Authors: Qing-Yang Zhou
Venue: Applications of Digital Image Processing XLIX
Published: 2026-10-09
Abs
Abstract
High-order spherical harmonics (SH) provide 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with view-dependent appearance, but also introduce a heavily over-parameterized color representation: each Gaussian is assigned the same full SH basis regardless of its position, shape, and visibility across the training views. In this work, we revisit SH optimization from an observability perspective. With the current Gaussian geometry fixed, fitting the AC coefficients forms a linear least-squares problem whose local Gram structure reveals the effective SH rank supported by the observations. When this rank is lower than the nominal SH dimensionality, the remaining directions are weakly constrained and need not be introduced as independent optimization variables. We therefore estimate the observable SH subspace after the scene geometry has largely stabilized and restrict the subsequent AC representation to the supported degrees of freedom. This rank-adaptive optimization suppresses poorly constrained null-space directions while preserving the expressive components required by the training observations. Experiments across multiple datasets show that the proposed method improves PSNR by more than 0.05 dB over conventional full-SH optimization while significantly reducing the effective number of optimized AC degrees of freedom. These results suggest that SH redundancy in 3DGS should be addressed first as an over-parameterization problem for the optimization, rather than solely as a downstream compression problem: the appearance dimensionality can be matched to the information actually provided by the training views, reducing the learnable parameter space while preserving reconstruction quality.

Authors: Ya-Nan Guo, Jia-Jun An, Ben-Kui Zhang, Ying Chang, Kang-Jian He...
Venue: Remote Sensing
Published: 2026-10-09
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Accurate reconstruction of digital surface models (DSMs) from multi-date high-resolution urban satellite imagery supports large-scale Earth observation and three-dimensional city modeling, with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) emerging as an efficient solution. Existing shadow-aware methods provide shadow-related spatial evidence, whereas geometric priors are generally applied across the image or scene without using this evidence to allocate supervision. Consequently, regions where shadows overlap abrupt elevation transitions may remain geometrically under-constrained, producing spatially non-uniform elevation errors whose severity varies with the geometry covered by shadow. To address this issue, we propose Shadow-Adaptive Geometric Supervision for Earth Observation Gaussian Splatting (SAGE). SAGE is primarily designed for urban scenes dominated by regular human-made structures and retains image-wide altitude guidance while allocating additional geometric supervision at two levels. At the spatial level, patch-level shadow weights determine where a shadow-targeted local altitude prior is applied to constrain relative altitude agreement. At the scene level, SAGE computes the reconstruction-derived Shadow Geometric Complexity (SGC) from multi-view shadow evidence and gradient statistics of the predicted DSM. SGC is used as a scene-level descriptor of shadow–gradient contrast and determines how strongly the local altitude prior and the complementary Manhattan orientation regularization are applied. Experiments on seven predominantly urban scenes from the DFC2019 and IARPA 2016 benchmarks show that SAGE achieves the best mean DSM accuracy on both benchmarks while retaining minute-level processing.

Abstract
Nearest-neighbor (NN) search underlies countless robotics algorithms, but many state spaces are not Euclidean: orientations live on $SO(2)$ or $SO(3)$ and poses on $SE(2)$ or $SE(3)$, and a Euclidean KD-tree on their raw coordinates returns wrong neighbors. Abstract
Traditional keypoint descriptors, such as SIFT (Scale-Invariant Feature Transform) and ORB (Oriented FAST and Rotated BRIEF), are computed from local image patches. They may be unreliable in scenes with repetitive patterns or weak textures, where patches at different locations can look similar. We propose the Contextual Line-Assisted Descriptor (CLAD), a keypoint matching method that addresses this problem by using global geometric context from line correspondences. CLAD represents each keypoint with a vector whose elements indicate its proximity to matched lines. It can therefore use the keypoint's spatial relationships to the lines in the scene. We use HOME (Hough-space One-dimensional Matching of Extrema) to detect both clear and weak linear structures and to establish enough line-to-keypoint associations to represent this context. Evaluations on the TUM RGB-D SLAM dataset and outdoor video sequences show that CLAD provides geometrically consistent matches that complement those obtained with traditional local descriptors. In repetitive scenes, where local descriptors may not distinguish similar patches, CLAD uses global structure to improve matching reliability. The method is computationally lightweight and practical to implement.

Real-time volumetric mapping for a planning robot is dominated by the cost of integrating and storing information at the finest resolution, yet only a small fraction of any map ever lies on or near a path the planner actually considers. Abstract
Tiny robots have limited computation and memory, yet visual navigation systems often maintain more state than a task requires. We ask whether persistent memory can instead be shaped by the task. We present \textbf{RingNav}, a \(10.6\)k--parameter visual heading estimator that constrains its memory to the circular manifold \(S^1\). RingNav learns only the turn between consecutive images, while heading is composed directly on the circle. On the TartanAir Visual SLAM Challenge, RingNav reduces mean absolute turn error by \(27\%\) and accumulated heading error over $4$ sec by \(15\%\) relative to a similarly sized learned-memory model. The structured update also gives exactly zero turn for identical frames and exact cancellation under sequence reversal. Without fine-tuning, RingNav transfers to real-world drone experiments and continues estimating heading under severe visual degradation. RingNav runs at $27.3$Hz on Raspberry Pi Zero 2W and can be deployed on an ultra-low power GAP8 processor at $33.4$Hz.

Bronchoscopy inserts a camera through the airways to diagnose endoluminal disease. Abstract
World models capture environment dynamics and support counterfactual reasoning for planning. In image-goal navigation, where the target often lies beyond the current field of view, the agent must not only model environmental dynamics but also maintain a persistent spatial memory of the scene. Existing approaches either introduce spatial context by fine-tuning pretrained models on the target task, which underexploits their spatial priors, or rely on external 3D reconstruction, which is computationally expensive for real-world deployment. In this paper, we propose the Geometry Grounded World Action Model (G$^2$WAM) for image-goal navigation.
