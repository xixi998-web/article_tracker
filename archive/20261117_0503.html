SemLite-GS: Semantic-Selective Parallel Gaussian Splatting for Lightweight Building Reconstruction from UAV Oblique ImageryCORE7
Authors: Tengfei Wang, Xin Wang, Zihan Li, Quanjian Ji, Yifei Yu...
Published: 2026-11-15
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Lightweight 3D building models support urban spatial analysis, but their production often requires dense scene reconstruction, building extraction, and mesh simplification. Although 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is more efficient than conventional multi-view stereo, existing large-scale methods lack semantic selection and parallel instance-level training. We present SemLite-GS, a building-centric framework that reconstructs lightweight building meshes from large-scale UAV oblique imagery. First, an orthographic reference map establishes globally consistent building identities, which are propagated to oblique views to form camera-instance pairs and regions of interest. Second, semantic-selective parallel PGSR training jointly processes multiple pairs within relevant image regions while updating only target Gaussians; contextual Gaussians remain fixed to preserve visibility. Third, architectural planes extracted from rendered depth, opacity, and normal maps guide signed distance field optimization, producing meshes with adaptive polygon allocation and well-defined structural boundaries. Experiments on multiple large-scale UAV datasets demonstrate that our SemLite-GS substantially reduces building reconstruction time compared with representative full-scene 3DGS methods. With comparable polygon budgets, SemLite-GS achieves superior preservation of dominant planar structures and sharp architectural transitions compared with conventional dense reconstruction followed by mesh decimation. The source code is available at https://github.com/IRIS-LAB-whu/SemLite-GS.

Abstract
Water--land cross-domain robots require continuous localization across land, transition and underwater phases, where sensor characteristics and availability change abruptly. Existing datasets and conventional metrics are not designed to capture transition-induced degradation and failures. We present \textbf{M-CROSS}, to the best of our knowledge, the first multisensor simulation dataset and benchmark for SLAM across continuous water--land transitions. It provides synchronized LiDAR, sonar, stereo camera, IMU, DVL, and depth measurements; 6DoF pose ground truth; sensor-wise medium-state annotations; and medium-dependent sensor degradation models. We establish a transition-aware protocol comprising the Trajectory Completion Rate (TCR), Piecewise Absolute Pose Error (PW-APE), Transition End Error (TEE), and Transition Survival Period (TSP), which measure trajectory completion, single-domain accuracy, transition-induced error, and localization survival under prolonged partial immersion, respectively. Experiments with 6 representative SLAM systems show that existing methods fail to maintain both localization accuracy and trajectory continuity across the full set of water--land transitions, exhibiting transition-induced drift, trajectory discontinuities, and tracking failures. Abstract
Modern autonomous driving and driver assistance systems rely predominantly on cameras, radars and LiDARs for environmental perception. However, these modalities face different environmental limitations, including poor illumination for cameras and adverse weather for LiDAR. Acoustic sensing, particularly using microphone arrays, could provide complementary perception capabilities for autonomous driving, but has remained largely underexplored. In this work, we design a horizontally arranged microphone array that functions as the "ears" of a vehicle, enabling localization of nearby moving vehicles in a bird's-eye view (BEV) representation that captures both their relative direction and distance. Using this array, we collect a real-world driving dataset, AcousticScenes, which contains 2,117 static and 2,863 dynamic samples with synchronized spatial acoustic signals, LiDAR point clouds and 3D bounding box annotations. Building on this dataset, we propose AcousticBEV, a learning-based method for acoustic BEV object localization, which estimates the center locations of moving traffic participants in BEV from the microphone-array signals, conditioned on the ego vehicle's own speed. Experimental results demonstrate that AcousticBEV outperforms both classical signal-processing baselines and other learning-based methods, achieving F1 scores of $47.7 \pm 1.2$% on "Easy" scenarios and $45.1 \pm 1.4$% on "Difficult" cases. Our findings demonstrate that acoustic signals from a low-cost, vehicle-mounted microphone array can localize nearby moving vehicles in both azimuth and distance.
EcoVGGT: Pinned-Key Inference for Efficient and Streamable Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction from Long VideosCORE6
Authors: Hongsen Cao, Shanxin Yuan, Mona Jaber, Ahmed Sayed
Published: 2026-11-13
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Feed-forward multi-view transformers such as VGGT recover the cameras and geometry of a video in one pass, but the cost of their cross-frame attention grows quadratically with the frame count, and their accuracy drops on long videos. Most remedies approximate attention over all frames within a budget or select a few keys by their content. We hypothesize that the keys of a long video can be chosen without looking at its content, and our experiments on VGGT and $π^3$ support it: a few dozen evenly spread frames can serve as the keys of every frame. We present EcoVGGT, a training-free inference framework whose keys form a Pinned Key Set of 31 such frames. A pin pass stores their tokens in a Scene Cache, and the other frames read it in independent batches that reproduce joint inference with these keys exactly, at a constant cost per frame. An Online Stream updates the pinned frames during recording. Six middle global blocks align the frames and form a reusable VGGT module: the Alignment Score reads their attention to flag failures, and Alignment Refocusing reduces them by fine-tuning only these blocks (3.6 GPU hours). EcoVGGT ($π^3$) matches the success rate of $π^3$ on 1,000-frame ScanNet videos at 13.8 times its speed. With refocusing, EcoVGGT (VGGT) raises the success rate of VGGT from 70% to 82% at 8.5 times its speed on 500 frames. EcoVGGT reconstructs 4,000 frames on one GPU, and EcoVGGT ($π^3$) streams 1,000 frames at 18.6 frames per second with the lowest online absolute trajectory error.

Abstract
This report presents a complete technical solution for the UrbanTwin V2X-Real Sim2Real LiDAR Challenge, covering both synthetic point cloud generation and 3D object detection. For data generation, we adopt per-axis quantile calibration. Public V2X-Real training data are used to construct the real reference distribution, and independent one-dimensional quantile mappings are estimated for the x, y, and z axes of the base synthetic point clouds. Abstract
Recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have revolutionized novel view synthesis and inspired extensions toward 3D scene understanding. Authors: Daniele Della Pietra, Antonella Rech, Nicola Conci, Nicola Garau
Published: 2026-11-13
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Implicit and explicit representations of geometry are central in computer graphics applications and come with their pros and cons. With surface sampling being faster on explicit representations and spatial queries being faster on implicit representations, finding efficient conversions between them is a highly desirable property. We present TetraCoder, a single resolution hash-free adaptive tetrahedral encoding that links an explicit tetrahedral spatial encoder with an underlying implicit signed distance field (SDF). The resulting hybrid implicit-explicit space is continuous piecewise affine (CPWA) by design, allowing to obtain an exact correspondence between the implicit SDF and the explicit analytic mesh. TetraCoder's design comes with additional perks, in particular: (a) it scales better than multi-resolution encoding methods due to its single resolution optimizable grid, (b) unlike hash-based methods, it is not affected by collisions, (c) it enables fast tetrahedral ray-tracing, geometry and appearance editing, directly through its adaptive tetrahedral scaffold. We demonstrate the versatility of TetraCoder by showing an improvement over state-of-the-art methods on multiple tasks: 2D/3D fitting from images and point clouds, 3D novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and geometry/appearance editing.

Abstract
Learning dynamic object representations from monocular videos is a crucial challenge in computer vision and graphics, demanding accurate reconstruction and realistic rendering. The recent 3D Gaussian Splatting method offers a powerful representation for dynamic rendering. However, the discrete and unstructured nature of Gaussians makes accurate surface reconstruction difficult. Some methods use squashed 3D Gaussians to improve geometric accuracy, sacrificing rendering fidelity, while others adopt dual-model architectures with high computational cost. To address these limitations, we propose LDSG, which learns Decoupled Structured Gaussians to jointly improve rendering and reconstruction. Galaxies evolve over hundreds of millions of years, and no telescope has ever watched one move. Abstract
Often the output of a learned mapping must satisfy prescribed constraints. In the control of autonomous systems, for example, the chosen action must keep the system within a collision-free region. We consider the case in which a proposed output is corrected by projection onto a safety ellipsoid. Computing this projection amounts to solving a quadratically constrained quadratic program (QCQP). We construct a neural-network safety layer by unrolling a feasibility-preserving polynomial penalty iteration. The unrolled structure allows the loss gradient to propagate from the feasible output through the safety layer to the network parameters during training. Abstract
Arbitrary-scale light field spatial super-resolution reconstructs high-resolution views at any requested scale from low-resolution multi-view observations. Gaussian primitives enable continuous reconstruction, but generating separate primitive sets for each view redundantly encodes shared scene structure across views and causes the primitive count grow linearly with the number of views. To avoid such redundancy, we propose MacroGS, a feed-forward decoder that jointly predicts a single shared Gaussian set per macro pixel rather than generating separate sets for each view. Disparity-dependent replacement and angle-conditioned appearance allow each set to be reused across all output views, keeping the predicted primitive count independent of the number of views at each requested scale. The decoder iteratively predicts per-primitive disparities, retrieves the corresponding multi-view features, and updates the primitive states. Gaussian splatting and residual refinement then reconstruct the output light field. Abstract
Plan-conditioned driving world models are used to compare candidate plans, so their forecast should change with the plan as the real scene would, yet widely used control metrics check only whether the camera follows the command. Recorded drives already contain real outcomes of plans that were never driven: retiming a log gives pose-matched images and LiDAR for other speeds, and a stereo rig records a laterally shifted view.
