PixGR: Pixel-Space Generative Decoding for Fast Feed-Forward 3D ReconstructionCORE10
Authors: Ranran Huang, Wenjing Bian, Weixun Luo, Ye Mao, Krystian Mikolajczyk
Published: 2026-10-22
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present PixGR, an efficient framework that integrates lightweight pixel-space generative modeling into feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction to model scene uncertainty. Context observations are encoded once into a scene latent representation, which conditions generative decoding from noisy target views to produce a shared 3D Gaussian representation. We formulate this process using pixel-space flow matching, enabling end-to-end optimization of 3D reconstruction with direct pixel-space generative supervision through differentiable rendering. This avoids VAE-based latent compression and error propagation inherent in previous two-stage pipelines. To preserve feed-forward efficiency, PixGR caches the scene representation and performs iterative inference using a lightweight DiT decoder. Experiments demonstrate that PixGR outperforms regression-based approaches in challenging sparse-view settings, particularly with widely spaced observations and view extrapolation. Meanwhile, it maintains sub-second inference and remains competitive with substantially heavier pretrained generative models. Our project page is available at https://ranrhuang.github.io/pixgr/.
RLSplat: Resource-Linear Budget Control for Feed-forward 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE8
Authors: Injae Kim, Minseok Joo, Jihwan Park, Sehyung Kim, Minseong Bae...
Published: 2026-10-20
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (FF3DGS) reconstructs 3D scenes from images in a single forward pass, making it well-suited to applications that must operate within limited resource budgets. To further improve its practicality, recent budget-controllable FF3DGS methods allow the number of Gaussians to be adjusted at inference, so that the output meets a given storage limit or rendering-latency constraint. However, these methods overlook the cost of inference itself: to produce a representation with the target number of Gaussians, they first predict a dense set of Gaussians and then prune or select from it, requiring nearly constant latency and peak memory regardless of the budget. We therefore propose RLSplat, a resource-linear budget-controllable FF3DGS framework whose representation size, inference latency, and peak memory all decrease linearly as the Gaussian budget is reduced. Instead of pruning or selection, RLSplat learns a Gaussian allocation policy that distributes the budget across local scene regions, so that exactly the requested number of Gaussians is generated. These Gaussians are then refined through resource-linear Gaussian decoding, in which each Gaussian attends only to nearby Gaussians found via an efficient hierarchical neighbor search, so that the decoding cost decreases linearly with fewer Gaussians. In zero-shot evaluation, RLSplat outperforms prior budget-controllable methods at every budget, surpassing the strongest baseline even with 4$\times$ fewer Gaussians, while running up to 4.1$\times$ faster with 9.6$\times$ less peak memory at low budgets.

Abstract
The real-time reconstruction of landscape exhibition scenes puts forward high requirements for image continuity, viewpoint switching and interactive response. Aiming at multi-view data redundancy, complex spatial structure and rendering load fluctuation, this paper proposes a real-time reconstruction frame rate optimization method based on 3D Gaussian Splatting. The method takes multi-view images, camera pose, depth information and interactive trajectory as inputs, and controls Gaussian density and level of detail through keyframe screening, feature encoding, Gaussian point cloud initialization and lightweight representation. On this basis, parameter update, visibility culling, tiled rendering and cache reuse are combined to adjust computational overhead and maintain stable rendering during continuous interaction. Four representative scenes, including outdoor garden exhibition, indoor landscape sand table, night lighting exhibition and mixed installation exhibition, are used to verify the reconstruction performance. The experimental results show that the PSNR of the optimized method reaches 32.7, 33.4, 32.0 and 32.6 dB respectively, and the SSIM remains above 0.931. The LPIPS is controlled within 0.082, and the reconstruction error remains within 0.92 - 1.03 cm, indicating that the proposed method does not weaken key visual details while optimizing frame rate. The FPS, response delay and frame rate stability scores reach 91.8, 90.7 and 89.5, respectively. This study can provide a method reference for real-time reconstruction optimization in digital landscape exhibition and virtual interactive scenes.
MEP-3DGS: Multi-Scale Pyramid Optimization and Event-Aware Masking for Efficient Motion-Deblurred 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE7

Venue: Applied Sciences
Published: 2026-10-19
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Motion blur weakens multi-view geometric consistency, causing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to produce inaccurate geometry, floating artifacts, and blurred structures due to missing high-frequency information. Event cameras provide microsecond-level temporal cues, motivating an investigation of how event supervision can be incorporated into coarse-to-fine Gaussian optimization while accounting for sensor noise. We propose MEP-3DGS, a multi-scale event-aware progressive framework for motion-deblurred 3D Gaussian reconstruction. A coarse-to-fine pyramid optimization strategy first establishes stable global geometry before recovering high-frequency details. A piecewise-linear event warm-up mechanism gradually introduces event constraints during Gaussian densification, while an event-activity mask guides spatially selective edge refinement to enhance motion boundaries and reduce unnecessary supervision in low-activity regions. On the E2NeRF benchmark, MEP-3DGS achieves peak signal-to-noise ratios (PSNRs) of 30.31 dB for deblurred-view reconstruction and 27.89 dB for novel-view synthesis, together with competitive structural and perceptual quality. Real-world results are evaluated using a no-reference image-quality metric and qualitative comparisons. Training takes approximately 30 min per scene, with rendering speeds above 250 frames per second and peak memory usage below 5 GB on an RTX 3090 GPU.
SplatXR: A Toolkit for Multi-Layered Extended Reality via On-Headset 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE7

Venue: Machine Vision and Applications
Published: 2026-10-19

Abstract
Recent advances in Gaussian splatting, 3D foundation models, and video-passthrough mixed reality offer unprecedented fidelity for capturing and blending physical and virtual worlds. However, established XR methods rely on polygon-based pipelines that treat physical and digital environments as separate domains, making seamless integration across recent novel representations difficult. Because splats lack explicit surface geometry, they impede standard graphics techniques necessary for cross-reality interactions, such as shadow or collision handling. Furthermore, while 3D foundation models can reconstruct physical objects on-the-fly into digital splats, current XR systems fall short of leveraging this capability for user experiences that transition seamlessly between physicality and virtuality. To address this, we organize XR as a multi-layered reality of interacting primitives, letting pixels and objects move across physical and digital boundaries. We contribute SplatXR, a Unity toolkit that operationalizes this structure as building blocks for interactive, immersive apps leveraging 3D Gaussian splatting: a standalone renderer, splat-to-mesh reconstruction, splat physics, splat blending, and offloaded splat object reconstruction via on-headset capture. SplatXR unites vision and graphics techniques to make splat-based XR development broadly available, and we validate it through demonstrative applications and a small developer study.

Abstract
Recent advances in 3D semantic segmentation have significantly improved scene understanding in autonomous driving. However, existing methods are typically developed under a closed-set assumption and evaluated under standard driving conditions, where all categories are known in advance. In real-world driving, autonomous vehicles inevitably encounter previously unseen objects, making closed-set perception insufficient for safe deployment. To address this challenge, we introduce DriveOSS, which, to the best of our knowledge, is the first real-world multimodal dataset and benchmark specifically designed for 3D anomaly segmentation and open-set semantic segmentation. It contains 12,661 synchronized and annotated frames captured using six surround-view RGB cameras, a 128-beam LiDAR, and a GPS/IMU unit. The dataset comprises 52 real-world driving scenes collected under clear, nighttime, cloudy, rainy, and snowy conditions and is organized into ID (in-distribution) and OOD (out-of-distribution) subsets. The ID scenes contain only known classes and are used for training, whereas the OOD scenes additionally contain unknown objects annotated as the outlier class and are used for validation and testing. Extensive experiments show that unseen objects remain a major challenge for current 3D perception systems. The dataset, benchmark protocols, and code are available at https://github.com/chrisyan/DriveOSS.
NeRF-Inspired Neural Fields for 3D Super-resolution of Reacting-FlowsCORE5
Authors: D. Amarathunga, Shang-Ru Li, T. Echekki
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-19
Abs
Abstract
The multiscale nature of turbulent reacting flows and the importance of fine-scale structures pose challenges for super-resolution. We investigate a 3D super-resolution framework inspired by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), called Neural Field Super-Resolution (NFSR), to reconstruct fine-resolution fields from coarsened reacting-flow fields. The proposed formulation adapts the continuous coordinate-conditioned representation of NeRF to physical flow-field reconstruction by conditioning spatial coordinates with local coarse-grid thermochemical information, while eliminating radiance prediction and volume rendering. Principal component analysis is used to provide a compact and low-dimensional representation of the thermochemical state, and a hierarchical extension, H-NFSR, progressively reconstructs the solution through intermediate resolution levels. The framework is evaluated using three-dimensional direct numerical simulation data for a turbulent premixed hydrogen-air flame, with an overall spatial coarsening factor of eight in each coordinate direction. The results demonstrate consistently better prediction measures and a higher ability to reconstruct fine-scale information than tricubic interpolation on the unseen test realization. The hierarchical formulation reduces visible coarse-cell imprinting. In contrast, biased sampling mainly benefits the most localized structures. Three-dimensional isosurface comparisons further show that H-NFSR recovers much of the small and intermediate features of the flow, including surface wrinkling and local flame curvature effects. These results highlight the potential of coordinate-conditioned neural fields as a flexible framework for three-dimensional coarse-to-fine reconstruction of reacting-flow simulations.

Abstract
Fine industrial robotic manipulation requires 6D pose, scale, and an operation-ready 3D model. Existing industrial pose estimation relies on CAD models, object-specific training data, or stable workcells, limiting use with unseen objects; zero-shot methods suffer from single-view occlusion, generative bias, and scale uncertainty. We present OV-PEAR, an observation-verified framework for 3D reconstruction and pose-scale estimation of unknown industrial objects. Its three-stage object-centric pipeline: (1) segments RGB-D instances and generates object-level 3D hypotheses; (2) anchors them to the robot frame via appearance-aware view retrieval, explicit scale search, and visibility-sensitive registration; (3) back-projects multi-view RGB-D evidence into the object-canonical frame to verify voxel occupancy, suppress hallucinations, and produce reusable trusted object models. On BOP-Industrial, OV-PEAR achieves 0.657 average AR and improves pose and voxel-level geometric consistency in real scenes; real-robot connector assembly reaches 99.67% grasp and 84.00% assembly success. The model library and pose-scale log enable short-term object-level state maintenance at 0.02 ms/frame.

Abstract
Background/Objectives: The golden hours, the critical initial phase of each criminal investigation, are crucial for securing evidence, engaging witnesses, and directing investigative efforts, yet this phase often relies heavily on individual expertise and intuition. This paper explores how artificial intelligence (AI) can enhance evidence-based and data-driven policing during these early hours by improving decision-making and investigative quality. Methods: Drawing on a source pool of 1589 open-source case records, covering solved and unsolved homicides, suspected homicides, suspicious disappearances, and deaths initially of unclear cause in the Netherlands, compiled from public repositories that aggregate material originating from police files and published court decisions, the study develops a conceptual framework that integrates AI techniques such as automated narrative generation, 3D reconstruction, and digital evidence analysis into early-stage investigations, and illustrates it retrospectively with a purposively selected, coded twenty-case sample; scenario generation in the proposed system follows the twelve elementary scenario components (ESC12). Results: By linking empirical data from previous cases with new operational information, that is, information that emerges or is newly discovered during the ongoing investigation, AI is designed to support the narrative approach, accelerate scenario reconstruction, and generate candidate hypotheses and leads for investigator assessment. An author-assigned preliminary evidence-and-readiness assessment rates four AI application domains against five golden-hour criteria, and the retrospective case analysis maps each framework component onto documented investigative problems. Conclusions: AI may improve investigative efficiency and consistency and is hypothesised to mitigate the impact of cognitive bias and time pressure; these effects remain to be demonstrated in prospective evaluations. Meanwhile, challenges remain regarding explainability, reliability, and ethical compliance.

Abstract
Egocentric video supports diverse applications, including robot learning, augmented reality, and human activity understanding. Recovering metric 3D hand trajectories from such video requires estimating both hand motion and camera egomotion to express the hands in a consistent reference frame. Many existing pipelines recover hand and camera motion in separate stages, combining hand detection, hand reconstruction, camera tracking, and trajectory refinement. We present V-JEPA Hand, a simple feed-forward video model that jointly reconstructs bilateral hand motion and camera motion from egocentric clips. It uses a self-supervised V-JEPA 2.1 encoder to extract spatio-temporal features from full-frame video and a query-based predictor to decode these features into per-frame hand poses, hand presence, and camera motion. We use the predicted camera motion to express the hand trajectories in a common world frame. The model accepts known focal lengths or predicts them when unavailable. It reconstructs each clip in a single forward pass. We train both components end to end in a single stage on a mixture of diverse pseudo-labelled videos and high-precision 3D annotations. A single model achieves the lowest coverage-aware mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) among comparable baselines on ARCTIC, HOT3D, and EgoStandard. It also yields lower world-frame trajectory errors than the compared SLAM-based pipelines. Ablations support the benefits of video pretraining, temporal context, and mixed supervision. Abstract
Structural time-lapse microscopy experiments aim to follow cells or subcellular structures over time, typically by detecting objects in each frame and linking them into trajectories. In practice, this process yields sequences of spatial coordinates derived from segmentation, while identity information across time is often absent or unreliable. In addition, the observed coordinates may be affected by systematic geometric drift. As a result, two coupled inference problems arise: geometric drift must be corrected, and object identities must be reconstructed from unlabeled point clouds. Here, we formulate both drift correction and identity reconstruction within a unified point-cloud framework and evaluate them jointly using controlled synthetic benchmarks and biological datasets. For drift correction, we compare rigid, local, and nonparametric models across spatially varying and non-rigid drift regimes. For identity reconstruction, we analyze a family of Hungarian assignment methods with constant-velocity refinement and different gating strategies, including innovation-based normalization. Across benchmarks, we find that increased model flexibility does not necessarily improve drift correction. A simple rigid stepwise model provides the most robust performance across a wide range of conditions, with a local community model as a competitive alternative under structured spatial heterogeneity. On the association side, innovation-based gating emerges as the key determinant of performance. Hungarian constant-velocity assignment with normalized innovation gating consistently outperforms coordinate-space variants, particularly under heterogeneous motion scales, birth and death processes, and density-induced ambiguity. Application to biological datasets confirms that the combined pipeline improves trajectory consistency and restores interpretable motion statistics, while highlighting the role of proxy references when ground truth is unavailable. Together, these results establish a practical and robust workflow for post hoc trajectory analysis in point-cloud-based time-lapse data, and clarify the methodological trade-offs between model expressivity and robustness in both drift correction and identity reconstruction. Author Summary Time-lapse microscopy is widely used to study how cells and subcellular structures move and change over time. A common analysis goal is to track these objects by detecting them in each frame and linking them into trajectories. In practice, however, this process often produces only their spatial coordinates, while consistent identity information across time is missing or unreliable. In addition, the entire field of view may shift or rotate during imaging, a problem known as drift. We developed and systematically evaluated a computational framework that addresses both challenges using coordinate-based data alone. By combining controlled synthetic benchmarks with real biological datasets, we show that simple and robust approaches often outperform more complex ones. In particular, a rigid drift correction model remains effective even when drift varies across space, and trajectory reconstruction improves substantially when candidate matches are evaluated relative to their uncertainty rather than by fixed distance thresholds. Our results provide practical guidance for analyzing time-lapse data when only coordinates are available. More broadly, they show that accounting for uncertainty and prioritizing robustness over model complexity leads to more reliable trajectory reconstruction in noisy and heterogeneous biological systems.
Robust Cross-Modal UAV Detection via Bidirectional Collaborative Energy Field under Weak CalibrationPROXY2
Authors: Chao Peng, Peng Shao, Qiao Ding, Xiao-Qing Chen, Ao-Jia Han...
Venue: SAE Technical Paper Series

Abstract
The precise detection and localization of 'low-slow-small' (LSS) unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) pose significant challenges in terminal defense scenarios. These non-cooperative targets have extremely sparse representations in radar point clouds because of their minimal radar cross-sections and appear as textureless, blurred patches in visual images. Furthermore, traditional multi-modal fusion paradigms rely heavily on rigid geometric projection matrices, which frequently fail under the mechanical vibrations, thermal deformations, and extrinsic perturbations inherent in rapid deployment environments, which often results in severe feature misalignment and contamination. To overcome these critical bottlenecks, we present an end-to-end cross-modal detection framework built on Bidirectional Collaborative Energy Fields (Co-Field). By redefining the 3D object detection task as an energy field reconstruction process within a latent multimodal feature space, we entirely remove the need for absolutely precise extrinsic parameters. Our framework uses a novel bidirectional mutual-guided interaction mechanism. Specifically, a vision-guided radar branch employs deformable cross-attention to adaptively aggregate highly discrete radar geometric features around visual semantic centers, achieving a feature-level soft alignment that implicitly compensates for calibration drift. Concurrently, a radar-corrected vision branch utilizes radar-derived geometric occupancy probabilities as depth anchors to gate and calibrate visual features, effectively suppressing the inherent divergence of monocular depth estimation. Ultimately, the framework regresses the target's precise 3D states and fine-grained categories directly from the extrema of the reconstructed collaborative energy field, circumventing complex heuristic post-processing. Experiments on the curated MMAUD dataset show that the proposed Co-Field method achieves better robustness. It remains highly effective even under severe calibration errors up to 10 degrees and achieves a 74.2% recall rate at extreme sparse distances ranging from 70 to 100 meters, and consistently outperforms state-of-the-art rigid and soft fusion baselines.
