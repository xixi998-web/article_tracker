Authors: Giovanni Vonsowski Guarido, Lorrayne Zampar Alves, G. P. Notarantonio, A. Calandrelli, João Manuel Hernandes Dorce Domingues...
Venue: Journal of Soil Science and Plant Nutrition
Published: 2026-10-02
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Purpose. Liming, a common practice in soil fertility management, can affect plant-parasitic nematodes, particularly the lesion nematode Pratylenchus brachyurus . This study investigated the effects of different rates of calcium carbonate (CaCO 3 ) on the development of P. brachyurus in soybean grown under greenhouse conditions. Methods. Two trials were conducted in a completely randomized design with five CaCO 3 rates: 0, 0.34, 0.81, 1.29, and 1.76 g kg − 1 . Lime was incubated in the soil for 15 days before soybean sowing. After plant emergence, 500 specimens of P. brachyurus were inoculated per experimental unit. At 75 days after inoculation, total number of P. brachyurus in roots, population density, chlorophyll index, soil chemical properties, and shoot nutrient contents were evaluated. Results. CaCO 3 rates of 1.0–1.25 g kg − 1 were estimated to reduce P. brachyurus number by 59.0–74.5% and population density by 60.0–67.0%. Vegetative variables were not affected by CaCO 3 rate, although chlorophyll index decreased linearly with increasing rate. Liming increased soil pH, calcium content, and base saturation and reduced potential acidity, aluminum, and magnesium. Reductions in foliar sulfur, phosphorus, potassium, boron, and zinc were also observed. Multivariate analysis indicated differences among treatments across the liming gradient, associating higher soil pH and calcium levels with lower P. brachyurus populations. Conclusion. CaCO 3 application interferes with P. brachyurus populations and influences soil nutrient dynamics, altering physiological responses in soybean.

Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-10-02

Abstract
The mobile, not the total, content of trace elements governs micronutrient supply and heavy-metal risk in arable soils. We related the mobile forms of 18 elements to soil pH, organic matter (OM) and available N, P, K and S in four districts of the Kostanay Region, Northern Kazakhstan. Samples (n = 1050) from 67 fields were extracted with ammonium acetate buffer (pH 4.8), measured by ICP-OES and analysed at the field level by non-parametric and multivariate statistics. Two opposing groups emerged: mobile Co, Ni, Mn, Cr and Fe rose with OM and fell with pH, whereas Ca, Mg, K, Na, S, Cd, Pb and Zn behaved oppositely. pH and OM dominated (mobile Co R2 ≈ 0.75); N–P–K–S added little for Co, and more for Zn and Cd, which OM most suppressed. Mobile Co exceeded its hygienic threshold (5.0 mg kg−1) in 41% of samples, almost exclusively in low-pH, high-OM northern districts (all p < 0.001). The labile pool is organised by a single pH–OM axis; soil organic matter and reaction are therefore the principal candidate targets for managing trace-element mobility, with the qualification that mobile cobalt calls for site-specific rather than unconditional reduction, pending soil–plant validation.

Abstract
The effects of cow and poultry manures on nematode populations were evaluated in experimental plots at the Zoology Department, University of Kashmir, on 3-year-old Sahibi grapevines. Manures were applied at four dosages (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 kg/m2), and soil sampling was done for three months post-application. After 1-, 2-, and 3-months post-treatment, the reduction percentage in phytonematode populations in poultry manure treatment (at 2.0 kg/m2) was as follows: Pratylenchus spp. (46.22 ± 0.12, 48.14 ± 0.15, and 48.97 ± 0.10), Tylenchorhynchus spp. (40.63 ± 0.11, 47.16 ± 0.18, and 48.07 ± 0.16), Helicotylenchus spp. (38.43 ± 0.10, 40.24 ± 0.14, and 46.79 ± 0.12), Hemicyclophora spp. (46.49 ± 0.11, 53.09 ± 0.20, and 54.24 ± 0.10), Psilenchus spp. (43.24 ± 0.11, 49.65 ± 0.10, and 49.10 ± 0.13), Quinsulcius spp. (41.21 ± 0.10, 44.80 ± 0.11, and 48.96 ± 0.10), and Xiphinema spp. (38.39 ± 0.15, 43.58 ± 0.11, and 50.00 ± 0.10). Gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis revealed presence of major bioactive compounds in applied manures, including fatty acids, phenols, and nitrogenous substances, which influenced soil nematode dynamics as well as soil physico-chemical properties like soil pH, macro and micro-nutrient concentrations, etc. By the third month post-treatment, poultry manure (at 2.0 kg/m2) increased free-living nematodes to 109 ± 5.03 and predatory nematodes to 64 ± 4.35 per 250 g of soil, compared to 77 ± 6.02 and 53 ± 6.50 in the cow dung treatment, respectively, demonstrating its superior efficacy in promoting beneficial nematodes. These findings underscore the potential of organic amendments as sustainable strategies for integrated nematode management.

Abstract
Hydrothermal Sb 2 (S,Se) 3 deposition proceeds through the time‐dependent release of Sb species, chalcogenide precipitation, and changes in proton balance, making solution pH a dynamic variable rather than a fixed initial condition. Here, a carboxylate buffering strategy that operates throughout the hydrothermal reaction is introduced to reduce voltage loss. The central design rule is that the buffer must operate within the actual pH trajectory of the solution during growth. Poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) satisfies this requirement through its COOH/COO − equilibrium and suppresses reaction‐induced pH fluctuations. Control experiments based on initial pH adjustment and alternative carboxylic acids show that the open‐circuit voltage ( V OC ) gain originates from effective buffering in the solution. PAA‐mediated pH stabilization reduces the Sb‐O related surface disorder and improves grain‐boundary electronic homogeneity, reducing localized recombination that can limit quasi‐Fermi‐level splitting and contribute to V OC loss. However, PAA‐induced acidification also raises the availability of reactive Sb species. A higher ethanol content moderates this excess Sb supply and the deposition rate, coupling voltage‐loss reduction with efficient charge collection. This combined strategy of pH buffering and growth control improves morphology, local photoresponse, and device‐level trap characteristics, driving a simultaneous improvement across all photovoltaic parameters and raising the power conversion efficiency from 8.95% to 10.25%.

Authors: Mohammadreza Rahimi, B. C. N. M. de Castilho, M. Pearson, R. Chromik, S. Alidokht
Venue: Surface Topography: Metrology and Properties
Published: 2026-10-02

Abstract
Cold spray additive repair (CSAR) is a solid-state additive manufacturing approach used to restore the thickness and extend the service life of titanium (Ti) components operating in aggressive hydrometallurgical environments. In this study, high-pressure cold spray deposition was used to restore commercially pure titanium (CP Ti) components by building up a 5 mm thick CP Ti repair layer, and its microstructure and corrosion performance were compared with those of as-received wrought CP Ti in 60 g/L H2SO4, 20 g/L NaCl, and a mixed H2SO4 and NaCl electrolyte, selected to represent aggressive chemical conditions relevant to nickel laterite high-pressure acid leaching (HPAL) hydrometallurgical processing. Microstructural characterization revealed a dense splat-based architecture with ~1% porosity. Electrochemical testing demonstrated a strongly electrolyte-dependent corrosion response. In sulfuric acid, the CSAR CP Ti exhibited lower corrosion current density and higher polarization resistance compared with the wrought material. In contrast, in chloride-containing environments, the CSAR material showed increased corrosion current density and reduced impedance response, attributed to inter-splat boundaries and microstructural heterogeneity that facilitate aggressive ion transport and localized film disruption. No crevice corrosion was observed for either material under ASTM G48-11 Method B conditions. The results demonstrate that CSAR is a viable restoration approach for high-value Ti components in acid-dominated hydrometallurgical environments, while highlighting the importance of electrolyte chemistry in governing long-term corrosion performance.
Enhanced Hydrogen Evolution Activity of MoS2 Through Solid Solution Modification of Its Edge Sites With Ruthenium.PROXY2
Authors: Jin-Song Zhou, Tsz-Kei Leung, Denis A. Kuznetsov, C. Müller, Lei Ran...
Venue: Angewandte Chemie
Published: 2026-10-02

Abstract
In hydrogen production, electrocatalysts featuring edge sites as active centers, modulation of its edge site coordination environment could greatly improve the reaction kinetics by effectively reducing the energy barrier. Here, we report a novel catalyst formulation obtained by the electrochemical deposition of ruthenium onto the edge sites and near-edge basal plane of MoS2 to yield Ru-coordinated solid solution species. The Ru atom acts as a site for water dissociation, increasing the proton supply rate and, in turn, the catalytic activity of MoS2. Based on the Tafel slope, in situ Raman, and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis, the edge solid solution creates a locally acidic chemical environment, enhancing the kinetics of the hydrogen evolution reaction by altering the reaction pathway from Volmer-Heyrovsky observed for pristine MoS2 to Volmer-Tafel. Edge-coordinated Ru solid solution catalyst features a very high current density in anion exchange membrane water electrolysis (AEMWE) and demonstrates excellent long-term stability when compared with other reported MoS2- and Ru-based catalysts. Overall, our studies demonstrate that modification of MoS2 by the incorporation of an edge solid solution can markedly improve its catalytic activity and, therefore, represents a promising strategy for materials design in the field of renewable fuel generation.
Towards Sustainable Mine Restoration: Environmental Safety Assessment of Phosphorus Migration Following Sewage Sludge Compost Application to Abandoned Phosphate Mines in Southwestern ChinaPROXY2

Abstract
Repurposing waste-derived organic amendments for degraded land restoration supports the transition toward a circular economy, yet the associated environmental risks must be rigorously evaluated to ensure long-term sustainability. Sewage sludge compost (SSC) is a promising soil amendment for the ecological restoration of abandoned phosphate-mine sites (APMSs). However, SSC application may alter phosphorus (P) migration and release, thereby posing a risk of secondary pollution that could undermine the sustainability of mine reclamation efforts. Here, dynamic leaching-column experiments simulating three years of natural rainfall infiltration were conducted to investigate P-release characteristics under different fertilization scenarios, including application rate, application method, and compost maturity. Ecological risk was evaluated using a modified phosphorus pollution index (PPI), and the likely mechanisms governing P leaching and release in APMS soils were explored. Dissolved reactive P was the predominant released form in all treatments, accounting for 67.9–85.6% of total released P. Increasing the SSC application rate reduced the cumulative total phosphorus (TP) release ratio (on a soil TP content basis) from 0.0348% in the unfertilized control to 0.0264–0.0299%. Deep full-profile incorporation within the upper 20 cm of APMS soil and subsurface application reduced TP release by 36.8% and 34.1%, respectively, relative to shallow full-profile incorporation within the upper 10 cm of APMS soil. Primary sewage sludge compost (SSC*) controlled P release only when cumulative rainfall infiltration was ≤669 mm; by the third year, the ecological risk increased from slight to the severe-pollution level. SSC applied at 120 t ha−1 under the shallow full-profile fertilization mode reduced the PPI of APMS1 and APMS2 by 30.8–44.8% and 5.6–12.3%, respectively. P dissolution was positively associated with Ca release. Humic substances likely formed larger, less mobile, non-rigid colloidal complexes through Ca2+ bridging with carboxyl and phenolic groups, thereby immobilizing phosphate. These findings under the experimental conditions provide a scientifically grounded technical basis for the subsequent environmentally safe and sustainable use of SSC as a waste-to-resource strategy in APMS reclamation, contributing to the sustainable management of both municipal solid waste streams and degraded mining landscapes.

Abstract
- Crude oil contamination of soils in the Niger Delta has led to severe loss of soil fertility, reduced crop productivity and groundwater pollution. This study evaluated the bioremediation potential of compost (CP) and biochar (BC) on total hydrocarbon and heavy metals concentration in crude oil contaminated soil. Hydrocarbons can easily find their way to the soil’s ecosystem through a lot of sources. People living in oil-rich areas experience lots of oil pollution in their environment and this has been a major concern. The experiment was laid out in a Completely Randomized Design (CRD) with 9 treatments. The remediation materials compost and biochar were prepared on and offsite respectively. The soil was collected from Igbo-Etche following standard procedure. It was artificially contaminated with 200ml of Bonny light crude oil and allowed to stand for 10days. The amendments were applied in two (2) rates namely: 40tonnes/hectare and 100tonnes/hectares for biochar while compost was 60tonnes/hectare and 100tonnes/hectare. The moisture content of soil was maintained at field capacity for 60 days. All samples were transferred into appropriately labeled polyethylene bags and taken to the laboratory for analysis. The soil samples were spread out individually to air dry. This was followed by crushing which was done using a cylindrical crusher and sieving was done using a 2mm mesh size sieve. The result showed Zinc content in the soil increased significantly from 0.10mgkg-1 to 2.66mgkg-1 upon contamination. After amendment, BC1+CP0 had the lowest as 1.45mgkg-1. Copper content in the soil increased from 1.27mgkg-1 to 2.12mgkg-1. Treatment BC1+CP1 had 1.45mgkg-1 as the least. There was a significant difference (P<0.05) among treatment means. Iron content had significant difference (P<0.05) among treatment means. The study concluded that organic amendments significantly reduced THC and the studied heavy metals (Zn, Cu and Fe). However, THC reduction was highest in BC2+CP0 as 87.66mgkg-1. For heavy metals, biochar alone gave the least available Zn, while biochar + compost gave the least available Cu (BC1+CP1 – 1.58mgkg-1) and Fe (BC1+CP2 – 1.39mgkg-1) due to adsorption, complexation and pH increase. This confirms that organic amendments are effective for remediation of crude oil contaminated soils.
