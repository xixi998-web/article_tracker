Robust structure from motion for aerial-ground images via detector-free feature matching and multi-view track refinementCORE6
Authors: San Jiang, Hui Wang, Xing Zhang, Zhongwen Hu, Zhijun Wang...
Venue: Image and Vision Computing
Published: 2026-08-15
Abs
Abstract
Integrated 3D reconstruction from aerial-ground images is essential for generating high-precision urban 3D models, yet severe variations in viewpoint, scale, and rotation make robust feature matching highly challenging. To address these limitations, this study introduces a rotation-robust detector-free matching network coupled with multi-view track refinement for incremental Structure from Motion (ISfM). The proposed workflow features four key modules. First, rotation-aware feature extraction replaces traditional convolutions with an Omnidirectional State Space Block (OSS Block) that selectively scans across eight symmetrical directions to model long-range spatial dependencies and synthesize rotation-invariant feature maps. Second, multi-scale attention transformation utilizes quadtree attention to build a hierarchical token pyramid that isolates high-association token regions and discards irrelevant areas, capturing long-range context with linear computational complexity. Third, bi-directional feature matching executes a symmetric coarse-to-fine matching scheme where coarse alignment computes dual-direction Softmax confidence matrices under mutual nearest neighbor constraints, and fine alignment uses a multi-layer perceptron to regress sub-pixel coordinate offsets. Finally, multi-view track refinement employs an integrated indexing structure to evaluate localized spatial proximity and link disjoint sub-tracks to the highest-confidence anchor point, ensuring stable feature repeatability across the ISfM pipeline. By using real aerial-ground datasets, experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method improves AUC at 5{\deg} pose error by 93.9% compared with LoFTR and achieves the highest precision in ISfM reconstruction, with the improved accuracy ranging from 27.6% to 32.7%. The proposed method provides a reliable solution for integrated 3D reconstruction of aerial-ground images.
Aqua-Verse: Augmented Quasi-Physical Aquatic Universe for Realistic Underwater Robot SimulationECO1
Authors: Jibo Bai, Daqi Zhu, Mingzhi Chen
Venue: IEEE Internet of Things Journal
Published: 2026-08-15
Abs · Code1
Abstract
As a crucial tool for the development and testing of underwater robots, underwater simulators play an essential role in the Internet of Underwater Things (IoUT) research by providing a controlled environment for validating robot motion stability, communication behaviors, and cooperative control strategies. However, most existing studies primarily focus on simulating mathematical equations derived from the dynamics models of underwater robots while lacking realistic simulation of physical collisions and hydrodynamic interactions between the robot and the surrounding fluid. To address this challenge, we propose Aqua-Verse, a novel underwater robot simulation platform built on Unreal Engine 5 (UE5). Aqua-Verse integrates an advanced rigid body physics system to enable real-time detection of contact between the robot and the dynamic water environment, thereby achieving high-fidelity physical interaction that significantly enhances the realism and responsiveness of the simulation. In terms of wave simulation, the platform adopts a Gerstner wave-based model, which simulates the rotational trajectories of surface water particles to naturally integrate visual and physical interactions, effectively reproducing the complex dynamic characteristics of real ocean waves. Furthermore, we present a physically based underwater image rendering scheme that incorporates multimask subgraph techniques, greatly improving the realism and detail representation of underwater visual imaging. To meet the demands of efficient underwater acoustic simulation, Aqua-Verse leverages GPU-accelerated compute shaders in conjunction with an octree-based spatial partitioning structure to implement an optimized multiray sonar collision detection algorithm, substantially enhancing the efficiency and accuracy of sonar image generation. We conduct both qualitative and quantitative evaluations of Aqua-Verse. The source code and detailed documentation are available at: https://github.com/StarTeardrop/AquaVerse.git, providing valuable support for research and engineering applications in marine science, underwater robotics, and IoUT.
