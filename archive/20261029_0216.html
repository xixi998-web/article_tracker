RaSFNet: Ray-aligned and Scan-aware Fusion Network for LiDAR Semantic SegmentationCORE7
Authors: Cheng-Hsun Chuang
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-22
Abs
Abstract
LiDAR point cloud semantic segmentation is a fundamental component of 3D perception for autonomous driving and robotics. Projection-based methods map 3D point clouds into 2D representations for feature extraction using 2D networks. However, aggregating multiple points during projection can obscure geometric differences within each frustum, while local feature extraction limits the modeling of spatial relationships across scanning regions. Moreover, proximity in the projected representation does not necessarily imply proximity in 3D space, and interactions between point and projected features may mix features from different ranges, reducing semantic discriminability. To address these challenges, we propose the Ray-aligned and Scan-aware Fusion Network (RaSFNet), which incorporates point cloud geometry and LiDAR scanning characteristics into projection-based semantic segmentation. Specifically, the Ray-Aligned Geometric Distribution Encoding (RAGDE) module characterizes point distributions within each frustum using a ray-aligned coordinate system. To model spatial context, a Scan-Topology-Aware Mamba (STAM) block organizes frustum features according to the LiDAR azimuth sampling pattern and captures long-range dependencies across scanning regions. In addition, a Range-Constrained Point-Frustum Fusion (RCPFF) mechanism uses shared range information to guide feature exchange between point and frustum representations while maintaining spatial correspondence. RaSFNet achieves mIoU scores of 70.2% and 77.7% on the SemanticKITTI and nuScenes validation sets, respectively, indicating competitive segmentation performance compared with the evaluated methods.
OmniRay: Camera-Agnostic Monocular 3D Reconstruction with Composite Ray FieldsCORE5
Authors: Namitha Guruprasad, A. Yadav, Cheng Peng, Ramalingam Chellappa, Anand Bhattad
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-22
Abs
Abstract
Monocular models recover 3D geometry from a single image in one pass, but most assume a pinhole camera and produce distorted geometry on the wide-angle imagery produced by fisheye and panoramic lenses. Camera-aware models remove this assumption by predicting a per-pixel ray field. However, they represent different camera geometries using a single fixed basis to capture both the overall camera projection and its finer variations. We argue that camera geometry can be viewed as having a hierarchy of complexity for representing rays. We present OmniRay, a feed-forward model whose Ray Module is composed of three components: a monotone radial base that captures radially symmetric structure, a spherical-harmonic residual for smooth deviations from it, and a free-form residual for remaining local variation. These components are composed as successive rotations on the sphere, allowing the representation to accommodate diverse camera geometries. Given a single image, OmniRay predicts a ray direction and an up-to-scale radial distance for every pixel, from which a 3D point cloud is recovered without needing camera information at inference time. Extensive experiments across different camera configurations demonstrate that OmniRay establishes a new state-of-the-art in camera-agnostic monocular 3D reconstruction.
VPR-Space: A Benchmark for Visual Place Recognition in Space Analog DomainsCORE4
Authors: Richard Fechner, Peer Neubert
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Visual Place Recognition (VPR) is an important means for loop closure detection in visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). While state-of-the-art VPR and SLAM approaches excel in urban and feature-rich natural environments, the vast and feature-poor environments of extraterrestrial surfaces like on the Moon or Mars pose severe challenges for existing approaches. This paper presents a novel benchmark, VPR-Space, to evaluate VPR capabilities on extraterrestrial surfaces. There are three contributions: (1) We collected sixteen VPR database-query datasets based on existing data from Space Analog Domains (SAD), terrestrial environments that resemble properties of the Moon or Mars. The collection includes a range of challenges regarding environment, viewpoint change, and illumination conditions. (2) To address the ill-posed nature of VPR, we propose a novel approach to generate the ground-truth information in VPR benchmarks. Instead of defining that a query and datbase image show the same place if and only if their camera poses are within fixed position and viewing direction thresholds, we propose to make the decision dependent on whether the match would be valuable in the downstream SLAM task. To avoid the overhead and restrictions of evaluating VPR in-the-loop within SLAM systems, we provide VPR ground-truth (GT) matrices based on the performance of an ensemble of state-of-the-art Relative Pose Estimators (RPE). Under this GT we require a VPR method to identify exactly those image pairs for which a subsequently applied RPE is expected to provide a valuable pose estimate in a SLAM context. (3) We evaluated nine state-of-the-art VPR approaches. The results show considerable room for improvement and indicate directions for future research. We provide a pre-release version of the dataset on github: $\texttt{https://github.com/rfechner/VPR-Space}$

Abstract
Evaluating deformable 3D reconstruction requires ground truth linking camera motion, surface deformation, material correspondence and image appearance. We introduce the Synthetic Deformable Tubular Benchmark, generated with LumenMorph, an open-source toolkit for creating, annotating and evaluating tubular scenes. The release contains 5,580 RGB images. Its baseline crosses three surface-motion conditions with moving and stationary cameras in six scene families; matched appearance variants cover only moving-camera finite-element actions in four families. Documented public and evaluation interfaces expose camera poses, geometry, depth, deformation fields, persistent material correspondences and discrete finite-element records. Persistent triangle identities and material weights let users track the same surface location across frames and query its three-dimensional trajectory. Separately implemented geometric and mechanical checks assess annotation consistency with the generating models. Structure-from-motion, optical-flow and supplied-geometry displacement-interpolation studies demonstrate how the resource distinguishes registration coverage from geometric accuracy, exposes camera- and surface-motion effects, and tests the benefits and mismatch of mechanical priors. Public readers, evaluation contracts and the configurable generator support reuse and extension. Data integrity, fixed-environment repeatability and retained-prediction score replay have distinct verification scopes. The benchmark provides a controlled synthetic reference without claiming anatomical or clinical realism.

Open-vocabulary 3D occupancy prediction is important for embodied agents operating in complex environments whose semantic categories extend beyond fixed taxonomies. Abstract
Novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction have advanced rapidly with radiance fields and splatting, yet their memory and computational demands limit their use in resource-constrained settings. We introduce Hexels: novel planar hexagonal surface elements with a first-order spatial color field for efficient 3D scene and 2D image representation. Published: 2026-10-27

Abstract
We present RESPLE2, a recursive multi-sensor state estimation framework using non-uniform B-splines for 6-DoF motion representation. By exploiting the knot-clamping boundary property, we propose a theoretically grounded mechanism for exact control-point prediction directly informed by inertial kinematics along the trajectory, with knot placement adapted to motion and measurement availability. Within an iterated extended Kalman filter, we perform this prediction using a subset of inertial measurements. A subsequent reset step preserves trajectory consistency as the knots extend. The update step further incorporates heterogeneous sensor measurements at their native timestamps through continuous-time observation models, supporting flexible combinations of LiDARs, cameras, inertial measurement units, and leg kinematics. We further develop a lightweight global optimization module that directly refines non-uniform B-spline control points by incorporating loop-closure constraints derived from LiDAR-visual cues. We conduct extensive evaluations on public and experimental datasets across handheld, tracked, legged, and hybrid aerial-legged platforms, covering highly dynamic maneuvers with extreme accelerations. Compared with state-of-the-art multi-sensor odometry systems, RESPLE2 achieves competitive or superior estimation accuracy and robustness in the corresponding sensor configurations, particularly under aggressive motion, while maintaining real-time operation. We publicly release our code and experimental datasets at https://github.com/ASIG-X/RESPLE2.

Abstract
In smart factory environments, passive entities such as humans and movable obstacles may interfere with autonomous robot operations, posing significant safety risks. Detecting potential conflicts between robots and entities outside their field-of-view (FoV) is therefore of paramount importance. However, conventional passive object detection systems rely on sensors such as LiDAR, radar, or cameras, which operate under direct line-of-sight (LoS) and are limited by obstacles and occlusions. To overcome these limitations, integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) enables environmental perception via the communication infrastructure thanks to cooperation among distributed wireless devices. Abstract
Active simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) addresses the problem of planning robot motion to build complete and accurate maps of unknown environments through efficient exploration while maintaining high localization accuracy. This raises a dilemma between exploring unknown spaces to expand coverage and exploiting visited regions to perform active loop closures. Despite numerous prior works on ground robots, aerial robots with high agility and constrained sensor suites face more significant localization drift and thereby demand dedicated active SLAM planning. Moreover, a unified strategy that adapts performance based on task-specific priorities remains an open question. In this article, we introduce RAVEN, a unified active SLAM framework for Rapid Autonomous Visual ExploratioN using quadrotors, which systematically accounts for localization uncertainty. The framework starts with identifying frontiers and active loop closure candidates, from which global tour candidates are generated. These candidates are evaluated using a utility vector that separately quantifies exploration efficiency and localization accuracy, and the final tour is selected according to a prioritization parameter ranging from $[0,1]$. Finally, a perception-aware navigation planner generates smooth execution trajectories that minimize visual state estimation error based on the online Fisher information field that provides visual localization information in real-time. Benchmark evaluations demonstrate that RAVEN outperforms state-of-the-art active SLAM methods, highlighting its advantages in producing a spectrum of performance across prioritizations. Fully onboard field experiments with a customized stereo-inertial quadrotor further validate its practical applicability in challenging environments. GaussianSpline: Adaptive Bézier Splines for Highly Dynamic 4D Gaussian SplattingCORE3
Authors: Tom Yachil, Andrei Sharf
Published: 2026-10-26
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present GaussianSpline, a dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting method that models per-Gaussian motion using adaptive, non-uniform piecewise cubic Bézier splines. Unlike approaches based on global deformations, fixed-order polynomials, or uniformly parameterized splines, GaussianSpline jointly learns the Bézier control points and the temporal partition of each Gaussian's trajectory. This adaptive representation allocates greater temporal capacity to highly dynamic regions while maintaining compact trajectories for near-static regions, enabling high-fidelity reconstruction of complex and rapidly changing motion. To jointly achieve globally smooth motion and local adaptivity, we enforce $G^1$ continuity between consecutive Bézier segments using a reflection-based constraint on their control points. This produces smooth, fully differentiable trajectories while preserving the flexibility of non-uniform temporal parameterization. Experiments on the Neural 3D Video benchmark, the Google Immersive dataset, as well as on synthetic scenes demonstrate that GaussianSpline substantially improves reconstruction quality in scenes with highly non-uniform and challenging dynamics, while maintaining competitive performance on general dynamic scenes compared with state-of-the-art 4D Gaussian Splatting methods.

Abstract
Recent studies on generalizable human Gaussian splatting have achieved the notable progress in synthesizing novel views of unseen human subjects from multi-view images. Despite this progress, existing methods still struggle to represent fine-grained details of human subjects, which inherently involve high geometric and textural complexity. This is because previous methods do not impose a constraint on how densely Gaussians are placed, which often leads to blurry rendering results in complex regions. To address this problem, we propose a complexity-guided regularization scheme. The key idea is to leverage a complexity prior computed via the local entropy in geometric and textural characteristics of a clothed human. Furthermore, we sample local patches predominantly from complex regions according to this complexity prior, which allows the model to be sufficiently supervised by local details. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method delivers state-of-the-art performance while maintaining the real-time inference speed in generalizable human Gaussian splatting. HiP3R: Hierarchical Pose Graphs for Long-Horizon 3D ReconstructionCORE3
Authors: Zunnan Xu, Shang-Chen Zhou, Yi-Hang Luo, C. Loy
Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-10-22
Abs
Abstract
Feed-forward models accurately estimate camera motion and 3D geometry from short video windows. However, computational and memory costs prevent direct inference on sequences containing thousands of frames. Long videos must therefore be processed as overlapping chunks. This division produces independent local reconstructions with inconsistent coordinate frames and duplicate estimates of shared cameras. Achieving global consistency requires propagating constraints from adjacent overlaps and distant revisits across the sequence. This requirement naturally motivates pose graph optimization. However, the choice of graph level creates a trade-off. Chunk-level graphs efficiently resolve scale drift and close large loops. Yet, their rigid transformations cannot correct internal errors or conflicting frame predictions. Conversely, frame-level optimization offers local flexibility, but scaling it globally is computationally expensive and can break metric scale. HiP3R resolves this trade-off using a hierarchical pose graph. HiP3R first optimizes a robust chunk-level $\mathrm{Sim}(3)$ graph to establish the global layout and scale. It then holds this layout fixed and estimates one shared $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ camera per frame, turning overlapping window predictions into a single trajectory. Experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of HiP3R in improving camera trajectories and 3D reconstruction. Our method applies to both chunk-based and streaming backends without additional training or fine-tuning. The codes are available at https://kkakkkka.github.io/HiP3R.
