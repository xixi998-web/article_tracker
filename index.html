Availability and Shoot Cadmium Content in Wheat Differentially Respond to Liming: The Impact of Exchangeable Calcium and pH in Bulk and Rhizosphere SoilCORE7
Authors: Mahboobe Safari Sinegani, Maria Manzoor, Karl H. Mühling
Venue: Water, Air, & Soil Pollution
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Cadmium (Cd) contamination in soil poses significant risks to environmental and crop safety. One of the most important parameters controlling Cd uptake by plants from soil is pH. The role of soil pH in regulating Cd availability and its uptake by plants has been studied; however, no study has yet compared how pH changes in the rhizosphere and bulk soil differentially influence these processes. This study examined how nitrification inhibitor (3,4-Dimethylpyrazole phosphate (DMPP)) and lime affect Cd availability and concentration in wheat via modulating pH and calcium (Ca) availability in rhizosphere and bulk soil. Wheat was grown in acidic sandy soil under three lime application rates and two DMPP levels to investigate Cd accumulation. Our results indicated that DMPP application was associated with decreased rhizosphere pH, exchangeable Ca and Cd accumulation in wheat shoots. The impact of lime on shoot Cd varied depending on the presence of DMPP. Liming generally increased soil pH and exchangeable Ca and was associated with decreased plant-available Cd (CaCl₂-extractable Cd) in both bulk soil and the rhizosphere. In the absence of DMPP, liming was associated with a greater increase in exchangeable Ca in the rhizosphere and reduction in plant-available Cd (CaCl₂-extractable Cd) in the rhizosphere and bulk soil. Despite the decline in soil Cd availability, these changes were associated with higher Cd concentration and content in wheat shoots. In contrast, under DMPP application, liming decreased shoot Cd concentration but did not affect shoot Cd content, suggesting that DMPP may influence the response of shoot Cd concentration to liming. The results provide useful insights into Cd management in agricultural systems and may contribute to strategies for reducing Cd-related risks in crop production.
An Explainable Uncertainty-Weighted Multi-Task Learning Framework for Joint Crop Recommendation and NPK Prediction with Probabilistic Crop ConditioningCORE3
Authors: Aakash Verma and Vineet Kumar Avasthi
Venue: International Journal of Advanced Research in Science Communication and Technology
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Crop selection and fertilizer planning are of generally based on farmer experience, even though suitable crops and nutrient needs depend on soil and environmental conditions that vary between fields. In many existing systems, crop recommendation and nutrient prediction are handled by separate models, and few explain how reliable a given prediction is. This study proposes a multi-task learning model that predicts the recommended crop and the nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium (NPK) requirement from seven pre crop inputs: soil type, season, soil pH, temperature, water requirement, relative humidity, and water source. A shared network feeds a crop classification head and an NPK regression head. The NPK head receives the full predicted crop probability vector instead of the true crop label, and the two task losses are balanced using learned uncertainty parameters. Monte Carlo Dropout estimates the uncertainty of each prediction, and SHAP shows which inputs drove it. On a dataset of 57,000 observations covering 57 crops, the model reached an accuracy of 0.9496 and a macro F1-score of 0.9463 for crop classification, below Random Forest (0.9627) and XGBoost (0.9618). The mean NPK R² was 0.8537, compared with 0.8518 for XGBoost and 0.8501 for a standalone DNN. The calibration error was 0.0072, and results were stable across five random seeds. SHAP showed that soil type, season, and relative humidity contributed most to both tasks. The trained model was built into a recommendation system that returns a crop, NPK values with uncertainty intervals, and an explanation.
Effectiveness of Compost Produced from Spent Substrate of Deep Root Mushroom (Xerula radicata) on the Growth and Yield of Maize (Zea mays L.)CORE3
Authors: Tin Nguyen Chanh, L. Dang, Thanh Nguyen Ngoc, Phong Huynh Xuan, Giang Tran Thi
Venue: Agriculture
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Spent mushroom substrate from Xerula radicata cultivation is an underutilized organic resource that requires appropriate management. This study evaluated three initial C:N ratios (25:1, 30:1, and 35:1) and three Trichoderma-CTU inoculation rates (0.1%, 0.5%, and 1.0%) in terms of composting performance, followed by an evaluation of maize seed germination, soil properties, plant growth, and yield. The initial C:N ratio influenced several composting characteristics, with treatments at a C:N ratio of 35:1 reaching the thermophilic phase earlier and showing higher particle uniformity than the lower C:N ratio treatments. The resulting composts had final C:N ratios within the range of 13.89–20.57, low heavy-metal concentrations, and favorable microbiological characteristics. Compost application significantly affected several germination and seedling-growth parameters, increased soil pH, and enhanced soil microbial populations following maize cultivation. Although several compost treatments showed favorable numerical trends in maize growth and yield, these responses were not consistently significantly different among treatments. Overall, the findings support the potential of Xerula radicata spent substrate as a raw material for compost production and provide an initial evaluation of its agronomic performance under pot-scale maize cultivation.
Role of Smart Fertilization in Modern AgriculturePROXY2
Authors: S. T. Panusha, T. Soumya
Venue: International Journal of Plant &amp; Soil Science
Published: 2026-10-17
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Global food demand is increasing rapidly owing to population growth, placing considerable pressure on agricultural systems to enhance productivity under constraints on natural resources. Although conventional fertiliser use has contributed significantly to improved yields, its excessive and imbalanced application has resulted in soil degradation, nutrient leaching, groundwater contamination, and increased emissions of nitrous oxide (N₂O), thereby reducing nutrient use efficiency (NUE). In this context, smart fertilisation has emerged as a science-driven nutrient management strategy that emphasises the application of the right nutrient source at the right rate, time and place (4R principle), supported by advanced tools such as soil and plant sensors, remote sensing, geographic information systems, and decision-support platforms. Key components include controlled-release fertilisers, nano-fertilisers, bio-formulations, fertigation systems, variable rate technology (VRT), and unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)-based crop monitoring. Evidence from multi-location field studies across diverse agro-climatic regions of India and other countries indicates that smart fertilisation can enhance nutrient use efficiency by 50–80%, reduce fertiliser input by 19–30%, and improve crop yields by 22–30% in major crops such as rice, wheat, maize and pulses. With the global fertiliser market exceeding USD 200 billion, the adoption of precision nutrient management technologies is gaining momentum. Overall, smart fertilisation offers a sustainable intensification pathway by improving productivity while simultaneously minimising environmental externalities. However, challenges related to technology accessibility, cost, and farmer adoption remain critical constraints that require policy and institutional support.
