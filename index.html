MOMASPLAT: Dynamic Novel-View Demonstration Augmentation for Mobile ManipulationCORE8
Authors: Sateesh Kumar, Arnav Balaji, Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Georgios Pavlakos...
Published: 2026-11-05
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning are highly sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint. This is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa), where robots rarely execute manipulation from the exact same base pose used during demonstration collection due to navigation uncertainty, localization drift, and environmental constraints. Recent approaches use novel-view synthesis (NVS) to augment demonstrations with unseen viewpoints, but they assume static scenes and therefore fail to re-render the manipulated object during the post-contact interaction phase, leaving the most critical parts of the demonstration unaugmented. We present MOMASPLAT, a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses, including dynamic object interactions. Our key insight is that standard MoMa data collection naturally provides the multiview observations needed to reconstruct both the static scene and the manipulated object in 3D. MOMASPLAT combines 3D Gaussian Splatting, feed-forward 3D foundation models, and kinematics-based action retargeting to separately reconstruct the scene and manipulated object, render them from novel viewpoints, and generate geometrically consistent synthetic demonstrations paired with transformed actions. We evaluate MOMASPLAT on five real-world mobile manipulation tasks involving both rigid and articulated objects across three scenes, and validate the fidelity of its synthesized demonstrations against ground truth in simulation. MOMASPLAT substantially improves robustness to unseen base poses, more than doubling the average success rate of the strongest baseline and maintaining non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail, while reducing the need for largescale real-world data collection.
InstantHand: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting for Animatable Hand Avatar Reconstruction from a Single ImageCORE8
Authors: Chanho Kim, Muhammad Salman Ali, Kyeonghwan Gwak, Muhammad Shaheryar, Incheol Park...
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
Published: 2026-11-05
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hand avatar reconstruction typically relies on multi-view capture or per-subject optimization, limiting its scalability. We introduce InstantHand, a feed-forward framework that constructs an animatable 3D Gaussian hand avatar from a single image using off-the-shelf MANO, camera, and mask estimates, without test-time optimization. Image-averaged losses provide insufficient supervision for alpha leakage, which thickens thin finger contours. To address this issue, we propose a Soft Alpha Band regularizer that concentrates supervision on silhouette boundaries. Furthermore, colors regressed from image features can drift toward a learned prior even in observed regions. Our Reliability-gated Appearance Transfer preserves observed appearance by transferring source colors according to each Gaussian's rendering contribution, while completing unobserved regions using the learned prior conditioned on global source identity. InstantHand constructs an avatar in 2.84 seconds and renders at 161 FPS, matching per-subject-optimized baselines on InterHand2.6M and remaining competitive in zero-shot evaluation on HanCo.
Hierarchical Incremental Beta Splatting for Online ReconstructionCORE7
Authors: Linus Franke
Venue: WACV 2027
Published: 2026-11-05
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Online 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs a scene during capture: each keyframe spawns primitives once and refines them for only a few dozen iterations, leaving little chance to fix poor initialization or missing detail. Gaussians are also inherently soft, needing many overlaps for solid surfaces. We present Hierarchical Incremental Beta Splatting (HIBS), which brings beta kernels to incremental reconstruction. Their shape parameter interpolates between a soft, Gaussian-like profile and a flat-topped, near-opaque surfel, acting as a confidence dial for geometry. Our confidence-aware spawning uses geometric observability and multi-view depth agreement to initialize both opacity and shape, producing solid primitives where geometry is verifiable and soft, correctable primitives where it is not. As online densification, error-driven relocation recycles stale primitives into photometric-error hotspots. Bounded beta kernel support enables exact tile culling and faster rasterization, and a closed-form, shape-preserving merging formulation provides a level-of-detail (LOD) hierarchy for fast optimization and real-time rendering. On Mip-NeRF 360 in the on-the-fly protocol, HIBS improves PSNR by up to 0.8 dB while reducing optimization time by up to 40%. Project page: https://linus-franke.com/hibs/
3D Neural Rendering for Automated Ultrasonic Laser ScansCORE7
Authors: Thomas Pierson, Matthew Mitchell, Kira Perna, Anand Iyer, Eric B. Flynn...
Venue: Science Robotics
Published: 2026-10-31
Abs
Abstract
Laser doppler vibrometer (LDV) scans can be used to detect defects and cracks in manufactured parts before they are deployed. This work presents a method for reconstructing the steady-state surface wavefield of a three-dimensional part by combining automated robotic manipulation with neural rendering. A six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) robotic manipulator (UFactory xArm6) with an embedded piezoelectric transducer presented an acoustically excited test article to a stationary LDV at a sequence of known orientations, yielding a set of complex-valued 2D scans with poses derived analytically from the arm kinematics rather than from image-based pose estimation. These scans were used to train a novel complex-nerfacto model, a modified Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) pipeline that replaces the standard three-channel RGB output head with a two-channel output. Complex-nerfacto represents the real and imaginary velocity components of a wavefield, preserving the complex measurement without quantization. Reconstructions of a welded aluminum cylinder and a welded T-bracket were evaluated against measured scans using Z-normalized cross-correlation (ZNCC). The complex-valued pipeline achieved a mean ZNCC of 0.60 at held-out viewing angles, compared with 0.28 for a baseline RGB nerfacto model trained on rendered images of the same data. The resulting surface wavefield reconstructions supported defect detection and characterization on complex components. We identify geometry reconstruction from sparse LDV scans as the remaining obstacle to practical deployment.
Trainspotting: Learning Object-Centric 3D Reconstruction from Found PhotosCORE6
Authors: Hanyu Chen, Noah Snavely
Published: 2026-11-04
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
State-of-the-art feedforward reconstruction models perform poorly on in-the-wild object-centric photo collections. They are trained largely on single-session object captures whose images vary in viewpoint while the object and its environment remain nearly fixed. Public photo archives contain a different form of multi-view data. They hold found photos of the same object or design: photos taken independently by many people across years, environments, cameras, and sometimes physical units. In this work, we introduce Trainspotting, a dataset of 12,068 objects and 311,886 photos from Wikimedia Commons. We reconstruct each object to recover camera poses, multi-view stereo depth, and object masks. We categorize the objects into three regimes: objects fixed at one site, objects that move between sites, and designs shared by many physical units. Evaluating recent feedforward reconstruction models on Trainspotting shows that they fail most severely in the last two regimes. Fine-tuning VGGT-$Ω$ on Trainspotting significantly improves camera pose, depth, and 3D geometry on held-out objects. We observe similar improvements on the independent NAVI in-the-wild benchmark, which contains photos of each object in different environments. Performance on conventional benchmarks remains largely unchanged.
Educational robotics beyond ROS: a custom library for direct transfer of Webots controllers in the Turtlebot3CORE4
Authors: Jaime P. Pérez-Moro, Alejandro Romero, Hector Quintian, Francisco Bellas
Published: 2026-11-05
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robot Operating System (ROS) is becoming a standard in robotics courses at higher education, but its installation, configuration and distributed programming model can introduce a significant entry barrier in the new landscape of AI-based master and degree studies, where many students do not have a strong informatics background. This paper presents an educational experience that enables students to program a TurtleBot3 Burger directly in Python, while preserving a direct transfer path from simulation to the physical robot. The approach is based on a custom library that mirrors the Webots API, allowing the same controller code to be executed in the simulator and on the real TurtleBot3 with minimal modification. The library provides access to the robot motors, wheel encoders, inertial sensors and LiDAR, and is complemented by scripts that automate deployment and execution on the physical platform. It was used in a master-level course on introductory autonomous vehicles, and initial student feedback suggests that the approach reduces the perceived complexity of ROS-related infrastructure and helps learners focus on algorithmic logic and real-world behaviour.
VisionSIM: Beyond Idealized Sensors - A Multi-Modal Simulator for Robot PerceptionCORE3
Authors: Sacha Jungerman, Kaustubh Sadekar, Avery Gump, Sriram Narayanan, Shantanu Gupta...
Published: 2026-11-05
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robotic perception increasingly relies on heterogeneous sensor suites combining cameras with emerging modalities (event, SPAD, iToF/dToF, and thermal). Yet existing simulators idealize these sensors, hiding critical modality-specific failure modes that limit real-world deployment. We present VisionSIM, a unified, physically grounded simulation platform that elevates sensor realism to a first-class concern by modeling the raw physical measurement process for diverse conventional and emerging modalities. We expose fine-grained control over physical sensor parameters, reproducing failure modes overlooked by general-purpose simulators: pileup and glare for LiDAR; phase wrapping, saturation, and motion artifacts for iToF; refractory drops for event cameras; and history-dependent emission for thermal cameras. This enables principled cross-modality comparison on identical scenes and trajectories. We release the platform and a large indoor dataset with synchronized raw measurements and rich ground-truth annotations.
An Integrated Open-Source Stack for Vision-Based Autonomous Navigation of Wheeled RoversCORE3
Authors: Iaroslav Kolomiets, Iaroslav Sheipak, Mikhail Reppo, Vsevolod Egorov, Viktor Chertopolokhov...
Published: 2026-11-05
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-based rover autonomy requires integrating perception, localization, planning and platform-specific control under limited sensing and computing resources. We present an open-source ROS 2 stack for following directional arrows, reaching a terminal cone and returning autonomously to the start. It connects YOLOv8n detection and geometric direction, range and bearing estimation with RGB-D SLAM, wheel-inertial state estimation, kinematics-aware planning, behavior-tree mission management and STM32 actuation. Standard interfaces separate reusable autonomy from the hardware layer; a proportional path-following plugin supports low-speed rover motion. Public resources include software, configuration, a labeled dataset, trained weights and evaluation logs. On 478 held-out images, the detector achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.720 under the Ultralytics evaluator and outperforms a classical OpenCV baseline under a shared evaluation protocol. The reported perception latency is 25 ms on a Jetson Orin NX. Three missions, one indoor and two outdoor, demonstrate marker-guided navigation and autonomous return without operator motion commands or reported collisions. Field results document integration failures and corrective choices; repeated instrumented trials remain necessary to quantify physical navigation errors and reliability.
