ThermalSplat: Generalizable Pose-Free Gaussian Splatting for Sparse-View Thermal 3D ReconstructionCORE9
Authors: S. Prasanna, Gopi Raju Matta, Divya Madhuri Vemunuri, Kaushik Mitra
Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs
Abstract
Thermal infrared imaging is vital for applications like search-and-rescue and autonomous navigation, yet trans- lating it into 3D scene representations remains a critical bottleneck. The low-texture nature and view-dependent emissivity of thermal images cause standard pose estima- tors to fail catastrophically, collapsing standard 3D Gaus- sian Splatting pipelines under sparse-view constraints. Fur- thermore, existing thermal reconstruction methods rely heavily on dense capture and computationally expensive per-scene optimization. Critically, many of these ap- proaches depend on aligned RGB-thermal datasets to ob- tain reliable camera poses and initial point clouds, ren- dering them unusable in purely thermal, unposed envi- ronments. To break this bottleneck, we present Thermal- Splat, the first generalizable, feed-forward Gaussian splat- ting framework that reconstructs 3D scenes directly from sparse thermal images without requiring known camera poses, RGB guidance, or per-scene training. We over- come thermal-specific challenges using a soft census loss for brightness-invariant structural matching and a bound- ary structure loss to explicitly supervise temperature discon- tinuities. Trained on an aggregated multi-source dataset, ThermalSplat synthesizes sharp reconstructions of unseen scenes in a single feed-forward pass. Extensive experi- ments demonstrate that ThermalSplat holds a strict advan- tage over scene-specific thermal splatting methods in the sparse regime. Furthermore, even compared against these baselines operating under their ideal dense capture set- tings, ThermalSplat achieves superior perceptual fidelity. By eliminating the need for computationally expensive per scene optimization, ThermalSplat offers an efficient and generalizable approach to thermal 3D reconstruction.
Refin3R: Geometry-Guided Refinement for Feed-Forward 3D ReconstructionCORE7
Authors: Jongmin Lee, Jaeyeong Kim, Seungryong Kim
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models predict dense point maps from multiple images in a single pass. Without explicit geometric constraints, however, their predictions are often inconsistent across views and lack fine-grained precision. Recent methods incorporate geometric priors such as camera pose or depth, but use them merely as auxiliary inputs, process each view independently, or refine the geometry only where the priors are available. We present Refin3R, which refines feed-forward point maps with Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points, propagating SfM geometry over the image grid. In its dual-branch architecture, a 2D branch extends SfM geometry beyond its coverage using geometry-aware backbone features, while a 3D branch reasons over the SfM points of all views, capturing multi-view structure that the image grid alone cannot represent. The two branches exchange information bidirectionally to jointly refine each view, yielding dense and accurate point maps. Across ScanNet++, ETH3D, Tanks\&Temples, and out-of-domain human and surgical scenes, Refin3R consistently outperforms feed-forward and geometry-guided methods in point map accuracy while running faster than the strongest geometry-guided baseline.
Déjà-Events: Multi-view 3D Geometry with Event Cameras via Sparsity-Aware Adaptive GatingCORE7
Authors: Arda Cinar Demirtas, Pablo Varela Bernal, Marc Pollefeys, Sunghwan Hong
Published: 2026-11-16
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Event cameras keep capturing structure where conventional cameras blur or saturate, which makes them attractive for 3D perception in fast or poorly lit scenes. Event datasets with dense 3D ground truth, however, are far smaller and less diverse than the image collections behind feed-forward 3D reconstruction models. We present Déjà-Events, an event-only model that predicts depth, camera pose and pointmaps from multiple views in a single forward pass. Rather than relying on scarce 3D labels, we distil it from Déjà View, a feed-forward RGB model that refines its tokens by repeatedly applying one shared transformer block. On paired RGB and event recordings, the event student learns to reproduce the frozen teacher's final tokens, which requires no depth or pose ground truth and trains only about 1% of its parameters. Because events are dense at edges and moving texture but nearly absent in textureless regions, we further introduce sparsity-aware adaptive gating, which adapts the shared block's gates to events and adjusts them per patch by event density, so that accuracy holds as refinement steps are added. Trained only on simulated data, Déjà-Events achieves the lowest pointmap error among the event-based methods we evaluate, on EventScape and zero-shot on the real MVSEC and DSEC datasets. On DSEC it halves the error of the strongest baseline (0.15 vs. 0.30 AbsRel) with about a tenth of its parameters. It also has the lowest depth error on EventScape and DSEC and the lowest trajectory error on EventScape.
QuantileGS: Quantile-Based Conformal Bounds on RGB Rendering Errors for Novel View Synthesis with 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE7
Authors: Haiyang Xu, Ziye Zhang, Bin Pan, Xia Xu, Zhenwei Shi
Published: 2026-11-16
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Uncertainty quantification for novel view synthesis with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assesses rendering reliability at unseen viewpoints. This reliability depends on the discrepancy between rendered and true colors, which cannot be measured without ground-truth images. We therefore quantify uncertainty in this unknown discrepancy through upper bounds on per-pixel RGB mean absolute error. However, learned bounds may underestimate these errors and overstate rendering reliability, motivating calibration to a prescribed coverage level. In this paper, we propose QuantileGS, a post-hoc framework that combines learned rendering-error quantiles with scale-normalized conformal calibration to produce spatially adaptive error bounds. The framework comprises three phases. Phase 1, Rendering-Error Computation, measures RGB mean absolute error between observed images and predictions from a frozen 3DGS model. Phase 2, Ordered Gaussian Quantile Fields, learns two scalar attributes per Gaussian to predict lower and upper rendering-error quantiles. Phase 3, Scale-Normalized Conformal Calibration, calibrates the upper prediction on held-out images, using the quantile gap to scale the correction. Under the stated assumptions, the resulting bounds provide finite-sample marginal coverage. At a nominal coverage level of 90%, calibration raises mean empirical test coverage from 69.6% to 89.6% on LLFF and from 80.1% to 92.1% on Mip-NeRF 360. On LF, coverage remains conservative at 96.3% before and 96.4% after calibration. Applications to active view selection, Gaussian pruning, and RGB-D simultaneous localization and mapping illustrate how these bounds inform downstream decisions.

Abstract
Three-dimensional reconstruction can extend field phenotyping to individual maize organs, but thin, flexible, occluding structures and plant movement complicate reconstruction. We evaluated whether consumer-grade UAV RGB video processed with Structure from Motion and multi-view stereo could recover recognizable field-grown maize structures, and examined the effects of acquisition trajectories and frame sampling. COLMAP reconstructions preserved recognizable leaves, stems, tassel branches, ears, silks, and brace roots. For an equal-frame comparison, we reconstructed one plant from two sets of 295 frames from the same video: a single orbit sampled at 5 frames/second and three orbits at different altitudes (ribbon skirt) sampled at 2 frames/second. After ground removal, the reconstructions contained 520,601 and 375,318 points, respectively. The single orbit yielded greater point concentration within plant structures and fewer visible gaps, while the ribbon skirt yielded fewer scattered points and slightly lower proportions of isolated points. In densely planted nursery rows, visible voids occupied 17.57% of the rendered scene with one orbit at 1 frame/second, decreasing to 5.07% with additional serpentine passes and 2.68% when the original orbit was sampled at 3 frames/second. Additional observations also increased point dispersion associated with plant movement. A curlicue trajectory tested in a different field in 2024 produced distinguishable individual plants without visible duplicated structures, although substantial voids remained, mainly on the soil surface. These results demonstrate the feasibility of recovering recognizable maize organs from consumer UAV imagery and reveal trade-offs between acquisition strategies, reconstruction completeness, and point dispersion. Independent geometric validation is still needed before quantitative phenotyping.

Abstract
Despite impressive advances in large language models (LLM), vision-language models (VLM) still exhibit limited spatial reasoning capabilities. Recent progress in feed-forward 3D reconstruction has enabled precise, real-time geometric understanding from visual inputs. In this work, we propose SpaceMCP, a framework that leverages these advances to introduce an MCP-style paradigm for spatial reasoning. Our approach augments a VLM with structured function calls, allowing it to query a scene graph for spatial information such as distances, object positioning, and camera properties. The scene graph is constructed in real time using feed-forward 3D reconstruction to recover metric geometry, combined with open-vocabulary segmentation to identify and localize relevant objects within the scene. Paired with the VLM's strong reasoning capabilities, the VLM is able to combine this primitive information to solve more complex spatial tasks. Without any training, SpaceMCP achieves state-of-the-art results on VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench and VSTI-Bench, outperforming recent spatially post-trained VLMs and improving its base VLM by 15-22 points per benchmark. We argue that, given the contrast between the relatively slow progress in native VLM spatial reasoning and the rapid advances in feed-forward 3D computer vision, this paradigm offers a promising path forward for enabling robust spatial reasoning.

Abstract
Dense collaborative mapping is essential for search and rescue, building inspection, and warehouse automation, where panoramic cameras capture the entire surroundings in a single frame. Unlike pinhole agents that share views only at similar headings, panoramic agents observe overlapping scenes at any 3D orientation, yet searching full rotations also produces mutually consistent false loop closures across distinct symmetric rooms. Moreover, continuous monocular scale drift splits rigidly merged maps at revisited surfaces, while depth noise at short baselines distorts loop translation directions. To address these challenges, we propose CoSphere-SLAM, the first multi-agent Gaussian splatting SLAM framework for panoramic cameras, whose spherical loop closure aligns spherical features over $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ via fast Fourier correlation across arbitrary orientations and rejects symmetric false closures through global $\mathrm{Sim}(3)$ consensus. Experiments on indoor and cross-session multi-agent panoramic benchmarks demonstrate that CoSphere-SLAM achieves state-of-the-art trajectory accuracy and rendering quality among single-agent and multi-agent methods. OrthoMark: Place Recognition with Point-Anchored Orthographic Height MarkCORE6
Authors: Haichuan Li
Published: 2026-11-16
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LiDAR place recognition provides the loop closures of Simultaneous Localization and Mapping(SLAM). Perspective from Z-axis, a gravity-aligned scan is an orthographic map of the scene, and a revisit from another position and heading moves this map rigidly in the plane, a motion that a polar Fourier spectrum ignores and phase correlation recovers. Along the vertical, the heights of the returns describe the structures, but which heights are observed depends on the range, because the sensor's vertical field of view cuts nearby structures low; height thus misleads a global key, yet is decisive once two scans are aligned and compared cell by cell where they overlap. We present \method, a training-free method built on one principle: two LiDAR scans can only be compared through features computed from the same geometric surfaces in both scans. \method retrieves candidates with a translation- and rotation-invariant polar spectrum of the bird's-eye view (BEV), 1.9,KB per keyframe, and verifies each candidate by estimating its planar pose through phase correlation and scoring it with \emph{point-anchored height columns}: every BEV cell keeps a 16-bit mask of the heights of its own returns; within a session, the score is weighted by the expected overlap of the two scans' sensing ranges, computed from their distance. We show that this vertical link pays off in verification but hurts in retrieval, where what a height slice contains depends on the range to the structure. For single robot, \method has the highest AP of the training-free methods without 3D registration on the KITTI development sequences; across robots, on all multi-robot sequences, it matches or exceeds RING++ at 1/350 of its descriptor size and closes more inter-robot loops than the learned BEVPlace++ on all tests.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Feed-forward multi-view transformers such as VGGT recover the cameras and geometry of a video in one pass, but the cost of their cross-frame self-attention grows quadratically with the frame count, and their accuracy drops on long videos. Most remedies approximate attention over all frames under a fixed computational budget or select a few keys by their content. We hypothesize that the keys of a long video can be selected independently of video content, and our experiments on VGGT and $π^3$ support it: a small number of uniformly sampled frames can serve as the keys of every frame. We present Eco3R, a training-free inference framework whose keys form a Pinned Key Set of 31 such frames. A pin pass stores their tokens in a Scene Cache, and the other frames read it in independent batches that reproduce joint inference with these keys exactly, at a constant cost per frame. An Online Stream updates the pinned frames during recording. Six middle global blocks align the frames and form a reusable VGGT module: the Alignment Score reads their attention to flag failures, and Alignment Refocusing reduces them by fine-tuning only these blocks (3.6 GPU hours). Eco3R ($π^3$) matches $π^3$'s success rate on 1,000-frame ScanNet videos while running 13.8 times faster. With refocusing, Eco3R (VGGT) raises VGGT's success rate from 70% to 82% while running 8.5 times faster on 500 frames. Eco3R can reconstruct sequences of up to 4,000 frames on a single A100 GPU, while Eco3R ($π^3$) streams 1,000 frames at 18.6 fps, achieving the lowest online absolute trajectory error.

Abstract
Reliable localization in long underground infrastructures remains a major challenge for autonomous inspection robots. In hydropower spillway tunnels and drainage galleries, repetitive geometric structures provide insufficient constraints along the tunnel axis, while weak texture and illumination variations further degrade visual estimation. Under this coupled degradation, existing LiDAR-inertial and visual-inertial odometry systems may lose the ability to recover continuous longitudinal motion. Abstract
Scenes captured with handheld and wearable cameras are still shipped to a CUDA workstation for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training. Training on the device that captured them, or on its companion phone or laptop, requires more than translating kernels: GPUs and drivers differ in parallel features, memory limits, and command submission, and these differences decide whether a training schedule executes and returns the correct update. We present Cross3DGS, a cross-vendor, cross-platform Vulkan trainer that keeps one optimizer and chooses its execution schedule per device in two steps: rules on device limits, workload size, and observed driver failures exclude the schedules known not to run correctly, and a fixed preference order selects one of the remaining feasible schedules. Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting enables object-level operations on Gaussian primitives, yet existing segmentation methods are largely trained and evaluated through rendered views. A Gaussian Primitive Data Management Framework for Efficient 3DGS Trajectory RenderingCORE5
Authors: Haolun Ma, Ziyi Liu, Zizhuo Xu, Chuhan Hu, Lei Li
Published: 2026-11-16
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Trajectory rendering, which renders novel views of a reconstructed 3D scene along a camera path, is a core operation in digital twins and autonomous inspection. Structured 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) renders a single view in real time by decoding sparse anchors into Gaussians on demand, but it renders a trajectory view by view: every view projects all anchors to select them, re-decodes anchors already requested by neighboring views, and runs all steps on the GPU while the CPU idles. We treat this as a spatial data management problem, where anchors are spatial objects, views are range queries, and decoded Gaussians are shared derived data, and propose GDM-GS, which removes redundancy at three levels. Within a view, an anchor index and an occluder index answer each view as a holed-frustum range query, so out-of-view and provably hidden anchors are never decoded. Across views, shared materialization decodes each requested anchor once per group of consecutive views while preserving each view's selection. Across processors, a hybrid pipeline balances selections between the CPU and the GPU before grouped decoding under bounded memory. Experiments on large-scale public scenes show that GDM-GS substantially reduces trajectory rendering latency while preserving image quality.

Abstract
Hyperspectral image (HSI) and LiDAR data provide complementary spectral and elevation information for land-cover classification. However, the spatially varying class-discriminative reliability of modality features is rarely modeled explicitly, allowing weak or ambiguous features to adversely affect cross-modal interaction and fusion. To address this issue, we propose ReDF, a reliability-driven framework that quantifies the reliability of each modality's features at each spatial location through the strength of class-related evidence they provide, and uses it to guide feature correction and fusion. Specifically, a Reliability-Guided Feature Correction module introduces a class prototype bank for each modality and converts feature-prototype similarities into class evidence for reliability estimation. The estimated reliabilities are used to construct a reliability-weighted shared representation and to guide bidirectional cross-modal feature correction based on neighborhood agreement, refining modality features with weak class evidence through information from the other modality. Building on these outputs, a Reliability-Guided Feature Reweighting module conditions a low-rank modulation on the estimated reliabilities and the cross-modal disagreement, and applies it to the corrected modality-specific descriptors to adaptively rescale each modality's contribution to the fused representation used for final classification.
