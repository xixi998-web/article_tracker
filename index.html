Abstract
RÉSUMÉIntroduction. L’acidité des eaux de consommation constitue un enjeu émergent de santé publique en Guinée. Dans la préfecture de Kindia, les eaux issues des forages et des unités de conditionnement sont exposées aux interactions avec des sols naturellement acides, susceptibles d’influencer leurs caractéristiques physicochimiques et leur qualité sanitaire. L’Objectif restant d’Évaluer les caractéristiques physicochimiques des eaux de consommation et des sols associés, ainsi que les interactions sol–eau susceptibles de contribuer à l’acidité des ressources hydriques dans la préfecture de Kindia. Méthodes. L’acidité des eaux de consommation dans la préfecture de Kindia est associée aux caractéristiques pédologiques locales. Drinking water acidity driven by naturally acidic soils is an emerging public health concern in Kindia, Guinea. This study evaluated the physicochemical properties of drinking water and associated soils to determine their interactions and contribution to water acidity. Methods. A cross-sectional descriptive and analytical study was conducted on 605 samples—including 325 drinking water samples and 280 soil samples associated with boreholes—collected between 2024 and 2025. Analyses focused on water physicochemical parameters (pH, electrical conductivity, TDS, alkalinity, major ions, and metals) and soil pedological properties (KCl pH, exchangeable acidity, cation exchange capacity, organic matter, and exchangeable mineral elements). Waters exhibited persistent acidity with a mean pH ranging from 5.77 to 5.89 across periods, showing significant variations according to seasons (p = 0.032) and source types (p = 0.041). Drinking water acidity in the prefecture of Kindia is associated with local pedological characteristics. These findings highlight the importance of integrated soil–water monitoring to improve sustainable water resource management and prevent health risks related to metal mobilization.
Integrated Soil-Analysis-Based Fertilization, Organically Modified Biochar, and Reduced Tillage Mitigate Nitrous Oxide Emissions and Sustain Maize ProductivityCORE7
Authors: P. Sriphirom, Natnaree Ngamnate, Kanokpol Kaewnoi
Venue: Environments
Published: 2026-10-29

Abstract
Mitigating nitrous oxide (N2O) emissions while maintaining maize productivity and soil quality is an important challenge for sustainable crop production. A three-year field experiment comprising six cropping seasons was conducted in tropical Thailand to evaluate the effects of soil-analysis-based fertilization (SF), two organically modified biochars—organic acid-soaked biochar (BI-OS) and co-composted biochar (BI-CP)—and selected combinations of these treatments with reduced tillage (RT) on N2O emissions, grain yield, and soil properties. Compared with traditional farmer practice (TP), SF reduced annual direct N2O emissions by 28.1% without a statistically significant reduction in grain yield. The addition of BI-OS to SF further reduced N2O emissions, whereas BI-CP resulted in emissions comparable to those under SF alone but provided greater improvements in soil fertility indicators. Among the tested treatments, SF+BI-OS+RT achieved the greatest N2O mitigation, reducing annual emissions by 46.6%, from 3.52 kg N2O ha−1 under TP to 1.88 kg N2O ha−1, while maintaining maize productivity and reducing yield-scaled emissions. In contrast, SF+BI-CP+RT produced the greatest increases in soil pH, cation exchange capacity, and soil organic carbon sequestration. Overall, integrating SF, organically modified biochar, and RT improved the environmental performance of tropical maize production, with BI-OS providing greater N2O mitigation and BI-CP offering greater benefits for soil fertility.

Abstract
Cadmium (Cd) pollution of paddy soils poses a serious threat to food safety and human health. Calcium- and magnesium-aluminum layered double hydroxides (CaAl-/MgAl-LDHs) have recently been deployed to remediate Cd-contaminated soils; nevertheless, the responses of soil properties and microbial communities to CaAl-/MgAl-LDHs are not well elucidated. Herein, a field trial was implemented to evaluate the impacts of CaAl-/MgAl-LDHs and conventional amendments (lime (LM) and biochar (BC)) on soil Cd availability and bacterial community structure, together with the consequent impacts on rice grain Cd enrichment. Compared with CK without amendments, CaAl-LDHs, MgAl-LDHs, LM, and BC amendments significantly raised rice yield-related agronomic traits, with the greatest yield increment observed in the CaAl-LDHs treatment. The four amendments enhanced soil pH, cation exchange capacity (CEC) and available phosphorus (AP), while reducing DTPA-extractable Cd concentration. Notably, CaAl-LDHs and MgAl-LDHs treatments outperformed LM and BC in suppressing DTPA-extractable Cd at rice-grain filling and maturity stages. The four amendments substantially decreased rice Cd concentration, with the lowest values observed in CaAl-LDHs and MgAl-LDHs treatments. Furthermore, the four amendments boosted bacterial α-diversity and reconstructed bacterial community structure by elevating the phyla Thermodesulfobacteriota, Bacteroidota, and Firmicutes. Based on redundancy and Pearson correlation analyses, soil pH, CEC, available Cd, ExCa, and AP were identified as critical factors governing bacterial community structure. Partial least squares path model suggested that soil amendments modified pH and CEC, which influenced bacterial communities and available soil Cd, thereby suppressing rice Cd accumulation. These findings suggest that bacterial communities and soil chemical properties play critical roles in Cd immobilization upon amendment addition, and support the application potential of CaAl-/MgAl-LDHs to achieve safe rice production in Cd-polluted soils.
Burn intensity regulates post-fire effects on soil N2O emissions: A grassland soil pyrocosm studyCORE5

Venue: bioRxiv
Published: 2026-10-29

Abstract
Post-fire soil emissions of nitric oxide (NO) and nitrous oxide (N2O) are important ecosystem nitrogen (N) loss pathways, but their flux dynamics vary widely across studies, making generalizations challenging. Natural variation in burn intensity—or heat energy released during combustion—may help resolve this variability by mediating the soil physicochemical and biological drivers of these emissions. Here, we used an experimental "pyrocosm" system to evaluate how varying burn intensity influences post-fire NO and N2O emissions using soil from a Southern California grassland across 18 weeks. Over several months, we measured soil NO and N2O fluxes alongside soil pH, extractable nitrate (NO3-) and ammonium (NH4+), net NH4+ production, net nitrification, soil carbon (C) pools, microbial biomass C, and the potential activities of five extracellular hydrolytic enzymes under Control, Low-(100.1 ± 20.0 °C peak soil temperature at 5cm), and High-intensity (243.8 ± 7.4 °C peak soil temperature at 5cm) burns. Soil NO emissions were not significantly affected by burn intensity. In contrast, N2O emissions tripled under low burn intensity relative to unburned controls, but did not differ from controls at high burn intensity. This stimulation of N2O emission under low burn intensity was driven by increased denitrification, as supported by elevated soil pH and labile C availability, and an 88% reduction in net nitrification that signaled active NO3-consumption. Under high burn intensity, however, severe loss of microbial biomass, soil C, and NO3-constrained denitrification, with rising hydrolytic enzyme activities over time suggesting C limitation. Together, these findings demonstrate that post-fire N2O emissions reflect a fire intensity-dependent balance between microbial mortality and resource limitation, helping to explain divergent N emissions across ecosystems.
Biodegradable polylactic acid microplastics pose greater field nitrous oxide emission risks than conventional polyethylene by reshaping microbial production-reduction pathways in a wheat-maize rotation system.CORE5

Abstract
Microplastic contamination in agroecosystems is increasing, yet the field-scale effects of biodegradable microplastics on soil nitrous oxide (N2O) emissions remain unclear. We conducted a wheat-maize rotation field experiment to compare conventional polyethylene microplastics (PE-MPs) and biodegradable polylactic acid microplastics (PLA-MPs) at three application levels. Measurements included N2O fluxes, soil physicochemical properties, N-transformation enzyme activities, potential nitrification and denitrification rates, functional gene abundances, N2O/(N2O+N2) product ratios, 15N-18O-based N2O production pathways. Two-way ANOVA showed that microplastic type was the most consistent determinant of key responses, whereas concentration and interaction effects varied among indicators and seasons. PE-MPs caused weak and variable changes in soil N cycling and cumulative N2O emissions. In contrast, PLA-MPs increased cumulative N2O emissions by 38.9-61.1%, increased dissolved organic carbon, lowered pH, suppressed ammonia monooxygenase activity and nitrification, and stimulated nitrite reductase activity, potential denitrification, and nirK/nirS abundances. Increased N2O/(N2O+N2) and (nirK+nirS)/nosZ ratios indicated a stronger imbalance between N2O production and nosZ-mediated reduction. Dual-isotope tracing further showed that microplastics reduced nitrifier nitrification and shifted N2O production toward nitrifier denitrification, coupled nitrification-denitrification, and heterotrophic denitrification, with the strongest shift under PLA-MPs. These results demonstrate PLA-MPs pose greater N2O emission risks than PE-MPs, biodegradability does not necessarily imply environmental safety.
Development of an Adaptive Grid-Based Soil pH Mapping and Lime Recommendation SystemCORE3
Authors: Abdul Rasheed, Thangavel Krishnasamy, Rajesh Moozhiyil Razhikulath
Venue: ECSA-13
Published: 2026-10-29

Abstract
Maintaining optimum soil pH for specific crops is essential for improved nutrient intake and maximum yield. Conventional soil testing methods use composite samples tested in laboratories, which fail to capture the spatial variability within the field. This paper presents an adaptive grid-based soil pH mapping and rule-based, zone-specific lime recommendation system. The system incorporates an ESP32-based pH sensing node and a smart phone for fetching GPS coordinates. Bluetooth communication is established between the microcontroller and smart phone. The pH sensor is calibrated using laboratory results for improved measurement accuracy. Median filtering with averaging is used to eliminate sensor noise. Adaptive grid sampling is implemented, so that a greater number of samples is taken from regions with high pH variability and a reduced number of samples from relatively homogenous areas. pH measurements are then used to generate a spatial map with an interpolation technique. Based on the soil pH and agricultural recommendations, the system estimates zone-specific lime requirements for the field. This approach suggests variable-rate lime application for different zones of the field and reduces unnecessary lime usage. The proposed system aims to provide a cost-effective alternative to the conventional technique, suitable for precision agricultural application.

This study aims to examine the effect of sago husk waste biochar dosage on the chemical properties of Kambisol soil. Abstract
Char is a carbon (C)-rich pyrogenic material generated either during natural fire events (natural charcoal) or through the thermochemical conversion of plant biomass (engineered biochar). However, whether these two types of char have comparable effects on soil CO 2 emissions remains unclear. In this study, we conducted a 30-day incubation experiment to compare the effects of charcoal produced from Pinus massoniana bark during broadcast burning and engineered biochars produced from the same feedstock at 300, 500, and 900 °C (B300, B500, and B900) on soil CO 2 emissions, physicochemical properties, and bacterial diversity and composition. The addition of B300 significantly increased soil CO 2 emissions ( p  < 0.05), whereas B500, B900, and charcoal had no significant effects. B300 addition markedly increased the relative abundances of Proteobacteria and Cyanobacteria while decreasing those of Acidobacteriota, Chloroflexi, and Verrucomicrobiota ( p  < 0.001). Mixed-effects models accounting for treatment variation showed that soil pH was negatively associated with cumulative CO 2 emissions, whereas Proteobacteria exhibited a positive association. Random forest analysis identified Proteobacteria and Acidobacteriota as potential predictors of cumulative CO 2 emissions, although these relationships require further validation. In contrast, natural fire-derived charcoal caused minimal changes in bacterial community composition. Overall, our findings demonstrate that high-temperature biochars (>500 °C) exhibit similar effects on soil CO 2 emissions and bacterial community to natural charcoal in subtropical plantation soils, highlighting their potential as functional analogues of fire-derived charcoal.

Abstract This study is the first of two companion papers (Part II addresses rhizosphere microbial communities) and aims to investigate the influence of soil properties on GLSD foliar symptom expression by comparing high recurrence of symptomatic vines areas (HRSVAs) and low recurrence of symptomatic vines areas (LRSVAs). Abstract
Water pollution by toxic metals remains a persistent challenge due to their persistence and substantial risk to the environment. The development of water‐soluble ligands for metal‐ion detection is key for enabling effective sensing in aqueous environments. In this work, two water‐soluble receptors were synthesized by coupling 2‐aminobenzothiazole (ABT) with polyaminopolycarboxylic acid backbones derived from ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA, R1) and diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid (DTPA, R2). The resulting ligands integrate strong metal‐binding sites with chromophoric ABT units, enabling simultaneous coordination and optical transduction. Protonation studies in aqueous media revealed three acid–base equilibria for R1 and four for R2, reflecting the increased denticity and basicity of the DTPA scaffold. Distinct spectral responses and binding affinities were observed, with log K values of 4.8–4.9 for divalent metals and enhanced Pb 2+ stabilization by R2 (log K  ≈ 6.3). These findings demonstrate that incorporating ABT into polyaminocarboxylate frameworks yields water‐soluble receptors with tunable optical behavior. The dual chelating and chromogenic character of these ligands highlight their potential as optical recognition motifs for environmentally relevant metal ions.

Abstract
Monitoring and mathematical modeling of the spatial distribution of heavy metal soil pollution is a critical environmental task, particularly under theconditions of increasing anthropogenic pressure, industrial emissions, and the consequences of warfare. The object of this study is a threedimensional concentration field of heavy metals within a soil mass. The complexity of its modeling lies in the fact that traditional core sampling is destructive anddiscrete, whereas modern non-contact methods provide information in the form of continuous linear structures – namely, vertical measurement profiles.This paper addresses the problem of reconstructing a scalar function of three variables that describes the volumetric concentration of a pollutantbased on the known traces (profile functions) of this function on a system of regular vertical observation lines. To solve this problem, the framework offunction approximation theory is applied, specifically the methods of polynomial interlination of functions on a system of lines. Classical Lagrangebasis polynomials are utilized as approximation operators for individual spatial coordinates. A theorem on the structure of the polynomial interlination operator of a spatial field on a system of regular vertical profiles is formulated and mathematically proven. It is demonstrated that the constructed operator accurately reproduces experimental data at the nodes of the monitoring grid, thereby satisfying the interlination conditions. Analytical expressions for the interlination error at each fixed depth value are obtained, enabling the evaluation of the quality of the field approximation. A numerical experiment was conducted using a test function that simulates the behavior of pollutants in soil, modified by a piecewise-smooth barrier to mimic a geochemical barrier within the soil column. The results show that the proposed method ensures high accuracy in reconstructing the spatial field. Thisapproach allows for the reconstruction of linear surface scanning data from remote sensing alongside vertical profiles from subsurface geophysicalscreening, significantly reducing the need for costly and destructive core drilling.

Abstract
Degraded soil undergoes long-term physicochemical changes. This study evaluated the relationship among microbial diversity (metagenomic analysis), the possible reactivation of nutrient cycles, and physicochemical parameters in soil subjected to different tillage (zero, minimum, and conventional) and management treatments. The results showed no significant differences (p > 0.05) in any of the physical and chemical characteristics and properties of the soil that were associated with the characteristics inherent to soils in semi-arid regions, such as low natural fertility and limited water availability.
