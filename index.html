Abstract
Cactus species is increasingly recognized as a nature-based solution for restoring degraded drylands, yet quantitative evidence of its influence on soil quality across contrasting arid environments remains limited. This study evaluated the effects of three cactus canopy covers (bare, scattered, and dense) on soil physicochemical properties in two zones (EST=Eastern zone and SE=Southeastern zone) of Tigray, Ethiopia. Soil samples were analyzed for key indicators of soil quality, including moisture content (MC), bulk density (BD), pH, electrical conductivity (EC), soil organic carbon (SOC), total nitrogen (TN), available phosphorus (P), exchangeable bases potassium (K), calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), sodium (Na), and cation exchange capacity (CEC). Results showed that canopy cover significantly (p < 0.05) influenced SOC, TN, P, and exchangeable cations with dense canopy exhibiting the highest values. Soil moisture content was significantly (p < 0.05) higher in SE than in EST, whereas soil pH also varied significantly between the two sites. In contrast, bare soils consistently showed the lowest nutrient status, indicating severe soil degradation. The findings demonstrate that cactus vegetation acts as an ecosystem engineer by creating fertility islands that promote nutrient accumulation and improve soil functioning in dryland ecosystems. Integrating cactus-based vegetation management into restoration programs may therefore be an effective strategy for enhancing soil quality and supporting sustainable land management in semi-arid and arid regions.

Abstract
Capsicum, often known as sweet pepper (Capsicum annuum var. grossum L.), is becoming more and more popular in Bangladesh as a vegetable. However, a number of obstacles must be overcome for its production to occur, such as limited cation exchange capacity, poor soil fertility, fast mineralization of soil organic matter, and acidity of the soil. These problems are addressed by applying biochar, a carbon-rich biomass made by pyrolysis under low or no oxygen conditions, as a soil amendment. The purpose of this research was to evaluate how applying biochar affected the qualities of the soil and the production of capsicum in the experimental fields of the Soil Science Department at Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman Agricultural University (BSMRAU), Salna, Gazipur 1706. The treatments included T0 (control, no biochar), T1 (2.5 t/ha biochar), T2 (5.0 t/ha biochar), T3 (7.5 t/ha biochar), and T4 (10.0 t/ha biochar). The experiment followed a Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) with three replications. Two capsicum genotypes, BU capsicum 1 (G-1) and a capsicum line (G-2), were used as test crops. Biochar application improved soil properties, such as increasing pH, organic carbon (OC), cation exchange capacity (CEC), and the content of nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur. Soils amended with rice straw biochar showed significantly higher plant height, fruit number, fruit length, fruit weight, and capsicum yield compared to untreated soils. The most notable improvement in plant growth due to biochar was reflected in the biomass yield. Fruit yield varied significantly, ranging from 54.17 t/ha (T0) to 88.36 t/ha (T2) for G-1 and 92.44 t/ha (T2) for G-2. These findings suggest that converting rice residues into biochar and applying it to soil has significant potential to enhance capsicum production and improve soil health. Farmers are encouraged to apply 5.0 t/ha of rice residue-derived biochar to sustainably increase capsicum yields and improve soil quality.

Abstract
Compost amendment can improve soil health, but rate‐dependent responses in sandy orchard soils remain insufficiently characterized. This study evaluated the short‐term effects of compost application on soil physical, chemical, and biological properties during the first 2 years of peach ( Prunus persica (L.) Batsch) orchard establishment on a sandy Ultisol at Clemson University's Musser Fruit Research Center in South Carolina. Compost was incorporated into soils before planting at rates of 25.6 (1x treatment) and 51.2 Mg ha −1 (2x treatment) in 2019, followed by annual spring surface applications at rates of 8.5 and 17.0 Mg ha −1 for the 1x and 2x treatments, respectively. A no‐compost control (CT) was included for comparison. Surface soil (0–15 cm) was sampled in spring and fall of 2020 and 2021. Compost increased soil moisture, pH, permanganate oxidizable carbon (C), potentially mineralizable nitrogen (N), extracellular enzyme activities associated with C, N, and phosphorus (P) cycling, and total C and N by the end of the study. In contrast, compost did not increase extractable inorganic N and P. Across most short‐term biological indicators, the increase from CT to 1x was more consistent than the increase from 1x to 2x, indicating limited additional functional gains at the higher compost rate. Overall, compost enhanced surface soil moisture, organic matter accumulation, and biologically mediated nutrient cycling more than short‐term inorganic nutrient pools under this fertilized orchard establishment system. These results suggest that moderate compost input may capture much of the near‐term surface soil functional benefit during peach orchard establishment on sandy Ultisols, although long‐term studies with deeper sampling, tree performance measurements, and economic analysis are needed to identify optimal compost rates for sustainable orchard production.

Abstract
Optimizing manure recycling and mitigating antibiotic resistance gene (ARG) dissemination are critical for sustainable agriculture. This field study evaluated nutrient-matched half-dose fertilization regimes (T1: organic-chemical mix; T2: organic alone; T3: chemical alone) against conventional full-dose fertilization (TF) and a no-current-season-fertilizer control (CK) along the soil-root-crop continuum. Topsoil under T2 maintained a total nitrogen concentration (1.29 g·kg-1), showed a comparatively narrow nitrate concentration range (4.59-7.39 mg·kg-1), and decreased antibiotic residues by approximately 45% (20.41 μg·kg-1) relative to TF. Hierarchical partitioning showed that soil pH (27.20%) and available nitrogen-related variables had the largest relative explanatory contributions to bulk-soil ARG variation. The rhizosphere-root compartment showed strong microbiome filtering, with the highest relative explanatory contributions from Chloroflexi (96.60%) and WD2101_soil_group (98.10%), whereas bacterial profiles in washed edible tissue samples were most strongly associated with Pantoea and Sphingomonas (combined contribution 23.40%) among the measured genera. Under TF, higher nutrient and contaminant loading was associated with greater enrichment of key resistance genes (fexA, ISCR1, strA, tetQ) in roots and washed edible tissues (BCFRoot > 1.0, TFTotal > 1.0). Under equal macronutrient inputs, T1 and T2 reduced total bulk-soil ARG abundances by 60%-90% compared with TF (p < 0.05), with no significant difference between them. T1 reduced the proportion of manure applied through partial synthetic substitution, whereas T2 retained organic substrate inputs at the reduced rate. Both regimes showed low root and tissue enrichment indices (BCFRoot ≤ 0.03, TFTotal ≤ 0.04), indicating markedly lower relative enrichment than TF. These findings support reduced-input fertilization as a promising strategy for mitigating resistome pressure; regulatory compliance and food-safety implications require separate evaluation.

Abstract
Phosphorus (P) availability in highly weathered tropical soils is limited by strong adsorption onto iron and aluminum oxides, reducing fertilizer efficiency. Silicon (Si) may modulate P dynamics by competing for adsorption sites. This study evaluated whether silicon-coated triple superphosphate (TSP) improves phosphorus use efficiency, physiological performance, and radish (Raphanus sativus L.) biomass in a greenhouse pot experiment. Six fertilizer treatments (0:0, 1:0, 1:1.5, 1:3, 1:4.5, and 1:6) were assessed using Mehlich-1 P, remaining P, apparent P recovery (PRR) based on whole-plant P uptake, chlorophyll a fluorescence (OJIP), gas exchange, and morpho-agronomic traits. Intermediate P:Si ratios improved extractable P, PRR, photosynthetic responses, and biomass accumulation. The 1:3 treatment gave the highest observed tuber fresh mass (29.38 g plant⁻¹), 66.7% above uncoated TSP (1:0). These associations support further investigation of calcium-silicate-coated TSP; the relative contributions of Si, Ca and possible soil-pH changes could not be separated in this experiment.

Abstract
A combined elemental (CHNS) and physico-chemical analytical approach was employed to assess the impact of scrap debris on soil quality in Attingal Municipality, Kerala. During pre-monsoon and post-monsoon periods, 66 soil samples were collected at three depth intervals (0–15 cm, 15–30 cm and 30–45 cm) from ten scrap collection sites and five control locations. CHNS elemental analysis revealed that scrap-affected soils exhibited elevated sulfur (S), reduced organic matter and extreme C/N ratios, indicating poor decomposition and pollution. In contrast, control sites contained significantly higher levels of carbon (C), nitrogen (N) and hydrogen (H). Physico-chemical measurements showed that scrap sites had acidic pH, increased electrical conductivity (EC) and higher bulk density whereas control sites showed neutral pH, low EC and greater organic carbon content. Post-monsoon samples showed slight improvements in water holding capacity (WHC), moisture content, C and N, indicating seasonal recovery. These findings demonstrate that CHNS profiling, combined with physico-chemical assessment, is effective for identifying pollution stress and guiding soil remediation in urban areas affected by scrap debris.

Abstract
This study evaluated the influence of surface-applied wet algal biomass on key physicochemical properties of saline soil collected from the Abu Ghraib region of central Iraq. The algal mixture, a naturally occurring consortium obtained from a freshwater canal located within the University of Baghdad, was taxonomically identified as belonging to four major algal classes: Bacillariophyceae, Chlorophyceae, Cyanophyceae, and Zygnemataceae. These indigenous algae were applied to the soil surface to assess their capacity to modify salinity-related parameters through biological and physicochemical mechanisms. Temporal measurements of electrical conductivity (EC), total dissolved solids (TDS), NaCl percentage, and pH over 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours revealed distinct differences between the Control and algae-amended soils. The untreated Control showed a steady increase in EC, TDS, and NaCl%, indicating progressive dissolution and mobilization of inherent salts within the saline soil matrix. In contrast, soils treated with the algal mixture showed reductions in EC and TDS at most time points, reflecting decreased ionic strength and lower dissolved salt concentrations. NaCl% also declined at later intervals in algae-amended treatments, suggesting effective ion immobilization through biosorption, bioaccumulation, and organic matter interactions associated with the algal biomass. Soil pH remained relatively stable but exhibited slight alkalinization in some treatments, consistent with the release of basic cations and the decomposition of algal organic matter. Collectively, these results demonstrate that indigenous freshwater algae possess significant potential for reducing salt mobility and modifying the chemical behavior of saline soils when applied to the soil surface. The findings underscore the value of native algal communities as sustainable, low-cost bioremediation agents for managing salinity in agricultural systems such as those in Abu Ghraib. Furthermore, the study highlights the necessity of employing multiple salinity indicators (EC, TDS, NaCl%, pH) to capture the complex suite of interactions governing soil algae salt dynamics.

Abstract
Soybean sudden death syndrome (SDS), caused by Fusarium virguliforme, is a major constraint to soybean (Glycine max) production in North America, yet disease expression and yield loss remain highly variable across environments. From 2017 to 2024, field trials were conducted across 66 site-years in Michigan to evaluate the relative contributions of pathogen pressure, edaphic factors, and management inputs to SDS development and soybean yield. Fusarium virguliforme DNA abundance and soybean cyst nematode (SCN; Heterodera glycines) density were quantified, alongside soil properties, root disease index (RDX), foliar disease index (FDX), and yield, using linear mixed models and multivariate analyses. Across site-years, F. virguliforme DNA abundance and SCN density were consistent predictors of both FDX and yield, while soil pH was significantly associated with pathogen abundance, disease expression, and yield. However, FDX itself was not consistently aligned with yield loss, and substantial yield reductions were observed in the absence of visible foliar symptoms. Significant seed treatment effects were not detected across all site-years but were detected among site-years under high disease pressure, with yield and revenue benefits confined to site-years characterized by elevated pathogen pressure. Similarly, cultivar SDS tolerance reduced disease and protected yield under specific conditions but was not consistently effective across environments. These results demonstrate that SDS is best understood as a context-dependent disease complex structured by interacting soilborne pathogens and environmental gradients, with management inputs exerting secondary, conditional effects. Collectively, these findings support diagnostic-informed, risk-stratified strategies for integrated SDS management.

Abstract
Background: Teff (Eragrostis tef (Zucc.) Trotter) is an underutilised cereal crop with potential for diversification of dryland farming systems due to its adaptability and nutritional value. However, standardised agronomic recommendations for teff cultivation in India remain limited, particularly regarding optimal planting geometry and fertiliser management for improving crop productivity and nutrient-use efficiency. Aims: To evaluate the effects of planting geometry and nutrient management on teff yield, nutrient-use efficiency, and post-harvest soil chemical properties to develop suitable agronomic recommendations. Study Design: Factorial randomised block design with ten treatment combinations and three replications. Place and Duration of Study: Zonal Agricultural Research Station, Gandhi Krishi Vignana Kendra, University of Agricultural Sciences, Bengaluru, Karnataka, India, during the Kharif season of 2021. Methodology: The experiment comprised two planting geometries (30 × 10 cm and 45 × 10 cm) and five nutrient treatments: 50%, 75%, 100%, and 125% of the recommended dose of fertilisers (RDF; 20:20:20 kg ha-1 of N: P2O5:K2O) and an absolute control. Farmyard manure (FYM) was applied at 6 t ha-1 to all treatments except the absolute control. Brown-seeded teff was harvested at 110 days after sowing. Yield parameters, nutrient-use efficiencies, and post-harvest soil pH, electrical conductivity, and organic carbon were assessed. Data were analysed using analysis of variance at the 5% significance level. Results: Planting geometry significantly influenced grain and straw yields. Narrow spacing (30 × 10 cm) recorded higher grain (2.37 q ha-1) and straw (3.65 q ha-1) yields than wider spacing (2.04 and 3.34 q ha-1, respectively). Among nutrient treatments, 100% RDF produced the highest grain yield (2.73 q ha-1), whereas 125% RDF recorded the highest straw yield (4.36 q ha-1). Test weight and harvest index were not significantly affected. Agronomic efficiency and phosphorus physiological efficiency were highest under 100% RDF, reaching 5.00 and 4.81 kg kg-1, respectively, at 30 × 10 cm spacing and 4.95 and 4.87 kg kg-1, respectively, at 45 × 10 cm spacing. Nitrogen recovery efficiency peaked under 125% RDF at 12.31% and 14.73% for the two respective spacings. Treatment interactions did not significantly affect grain or straw yield. Soil pH, electrical conductivity, and organic carbon were not significantly affected. Conclusion: Planting teff at 30 × 10 cm with 100% RDF and FYM at 6 t ha-1 may offer a suitable strategy for improving grain yield and nutrient-use efficiency in the Eastern Dry Zone of Karnataka (Zone-V).

The cultivation of chili plants faces challenges in early disease detection and accurate soil condition monitoring, which can potentially reduce crop yields and increase the risk of plant damage. This research aims to develop a disease detection and soil condition monitoring system based on Raspberry Pi, utilizing the You Only Look Once (YOLO) version 5 algorithm to detect diseases in chili plants from images captured by a camera. Abstract
Nanotechnology is showing promise as a revolutionary strategy to improve crop productivity, environmental sustainability and resource efficiency. Nano clay polymer composites (NCPCs) have drawn considerable interest among advanced nanomaterials because of their distinct physicochemical characteristics and broad agricultural applications. Montmorillonite, Bentonite and Hectorite are examples of nano clays, which are layered silicate minerals with large surface area, nanoscale dimensions and potent adsorption capabilities. They form hybrid materials with enhanced mechanical strength, thermal stability and barrier properties when integrated into polymer matrices. Water, nutrients and agrochemicals flow through a convoluted diffusion pathway created by the layered structure of nano clays, allowing for regulated and prolonged release. The NCPCs can have phase-separated, intercalated, or exfoliated structures depending on how they are dispersed within the polymer matrix; exfoliated forms function better. In agricultural applications, NCPCs function as soil conditioners, smart fertiliser carriers and plant protection delivery systems. They improve soil aggregation, enhance water retention and increase nutrient availability, particularly in coarse-textured soils. Nano-enabled fertilisers facilitate synchronised nutrient release, improving nutrient use efficiency while minimising leaching and volatilisation losses. Emerging applications include delivery of nucleic acids for plant disease management, antimicrobial crop protection and micronutrient nanocomplexes that enhance bioavailability and soil biological activity. Overall, NCPCs offer a promising pathway toward sustainable and precision agriculture, Future research should focus on developing biodegradable polymer systems, optimising scalable synthesis methods, conducting long term field evaluations and estabilishing regulatory frameworks to enable their safe and agricultural application.
