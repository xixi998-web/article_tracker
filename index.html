VeilGS: Solving Darkness as a Veil of 2D Basis Maps over Retinex 3DGSCORE8
Authors: Mohab Kishawy, Chengzhou Tang, Shahram Shirani, Jun Chen
Published: 2026-11-20
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In a dark capture, the darkness is not uniform. How much light reaches a surface depends on where it lies relative to the few light sources, and the camera compresses what remains with a tone curve. Most low-light 3D reconstruction methods either apply one curve to every pixel of a view, so a dark object beside a lamp and a bright one in an unlit corner receive the same correction, or store the darkness in the 3D representation, where it can absorb scene detail. We present VeilGS, a 3D Gaussian Splatting method for low-light novel view synthesis that treats the degradation as a veil drawn over a clear scene. Each Gaussian carries an albedo and a shading latent instead of spherical harmonics, separating surface colour from illumination. Each view is darkened by a spatially varying gain followed by a global tone curve, and rendering without this veil gives the restored scene. A small network predicts a coarse basis for the gain from the observation, and its coefficients are solved for each view rather than learned. Training through this solve discourages the Gaussians from absorbing what the veil can explain. VeilGS needs no paired normal-light supervision and stores no per-view parameters. On Gaussian-DK, its restored renders score best on no-reference NIQE and CLIP-IQA and are rated highest in a blind user study with 55 participants. On LOM, it achieves results competitive with the state of the art.
SparseHPS: A Unified Model for Human Pose and Shape across Depth Cameras and LiDARsCORE7
Authors: Ilya Zaychuk
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recovering 3D human pose and shape from point clouds is central to motion capture, robotics and self-driving vehicles, because depth cameras and LiDARs sense a person directly in metric 3D and work under any lighting. However, current approaches are each tailored to one sensor type: point density differs by more than ten times between devices, public datasets are labeled with incompatible body models, and LiDAR approaches need long input sequences to offset sparse scans. In this work, we present SparseHPS, a novel sensor-agnostic network that recovers SMPL-X pose, shape and global translation from a human point cloud with one set of weights, for single frames and streams alike. Specifically, SparseHPS encodes the cloud with a LitePT backbone over sparse voxels, represents every joint by a learnable query decoded with one common head, localizes the body by voting over voxels, and draws temporal context from a causal memory of tokens from the seven preceding frames. A single model is trained on ten datasets that mix rendered clouds with real depth-camera and LiDAR captures, all converted to one body model. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art results on the SLOPER4D and FreeMotion LiDAR benchmarks, where SparseHPS lowers the root-relative joint error by 22% and 11%, respectively, compared with the strongest prior method, which needs 32 frames. On real Azure Kinect captures of held-out HuMMan subjects, SparseHPS attains a joint error of 34.7 mm. Moreover, the current frame alone already yields the bulk of this accuracy.

Published: 2026-11-20

Abstract
The structural integrity of wind turbine blades is critical for safe, efficient energy production. Accurate 3D reconstruction allows precise localization and quantification of wear and early damage detection. While drone-based inspections are standard, their high-resolution imagery poses challenges such as weak texture, lighting variability, and high computational cost. We present BladeR, a scalable pipeline for high-quality 3D reconstruction from inspection imagery. A hierarchical sparse reconstruction strategy leverages GPS/IMU data and blade shape priors to guide advanced keypoint matchers on full-resolution images. For dense reconstruction, a tiled Gaussian Splatting framework processes 45 MP images at native resolution, isolates the blade from background, and adaptively enhances detail recovery along the leading edge. Validation against commercial and open-source baselines shows significant improvements in model completeness and detail.

Abstract
LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry (LIVO) is a fundamental technology for autonomous systems; however, deployment on resource-constrained platforms remains challenging due to physical limitations. This paper presents Instant-LIVO, a tightly coupled, high-speed, real-time LIVO system. The system introduces a compact geometric map based on spatial-hashed OctVox and accelerates local-neighborhood retrieval and plane fitting through cache-assisted heuristic staged K-nearest-neighbor searches. It also employs a bounded single-reference-frame visual map that adaptively updates the reference frame according to the tracking status, while using Schmidt-type uncertainty-aware photometric fusion to maintain the correlation between the reference pose and the current state. The system performs multisensor fusion through a sequential process consisting of IMU prediction, LiDAR geometric correction, and visual photometric correction. It was evaluated on 32 public benchmarks and custom datasets. While maintaining competitive accuracy on both X86 and ARM platforms, Instant-LIVO achieves average processing-speed improvements of approximately 2.3x and 2.1x, respectively, and reduces peak memory usage to approximately 1/20 and 1/13, respectively. A Lightweight Method for Validating Simulated LiDAR Configurations Based on Point Density over DistanceCORE5
Authors: Benedikt Degenstein, Michael Spengler, Julian Stähler, Carsten Markgraf
Published: 2026-11-20
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Simulated LiDAR sensors play a crucial role in validating autonomous systems, yet achieving signal-level realism remains challenging due to simplified sensor models and limited configurability in common simulation environments. This paper presents a lightweight, platform-agnostic method to assess one observable aspect of LiDAR simulation fidelity: the range-dependent point density on annotated objects. The method uses only 3D bounding boxes and point clouds and does not require semantic labels, detailed maps, simulator internals, or perception models. To quantify agreement between real and simulated range-dependent point distributions, we formulate the Density Similarity Score (DSS), a bounded similarity representation derived from the Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) over discretized distance bins. The method is evaluated against a real-world LiDAR using three CARLA configurations of varying complexity. Results show that naive parameter matching based on sensor datasheets leads to systematic point-density deviations, particularly in the near field, while a custom configuration using the sensor's non-uniform vertical beam angles achieves the highest similarity in the evaluated scenario (DSS: 0.559--0.709). The proposed approach is intended as an early-stage, signal-level consistency check for identifying gross mismatches in simulated LiDAR configurations. It should be interpreted as a complementary validation step rather than a complete measure of simulation quality or downstream perception performance.

Abstract
Individual tree segmentation (ITS) from LiDAR point clouds is one of the most widely studied segmentation tasks in forestry. Although deep learning methods define the state of the art on the majority of ITS benchmarks, their high computational complexity and long runtime currently prevent their adoption in large-scale operational workflows. In this work, we present SCATR-ITS, a scalable transformer model specifically designed for efficient ITS over large geographical areas. Based on the ForestFormer3D framework, SCATR-ITS transitions to superpoint-level processing to accelerate inference, with the superpoints constructed from learned embeddings. The architecture incorporates size-aware superpoint-level postprocessing, a dynamic query count module to handle high variation in forest structure, and a maximal input stride designed to minimize prediction redundancy. SCATR-ITS achieves accuracy on par with the state of the art on the FGI-EMIT and FOR-InstanceV2 datasets with an up to 8.5$\times$ higher inference speed. In addition, SCATR-ITS has a significantly lower GPU VRAM footprint during both training and inference, and exhibits excellent zero-shot generalization across multiple datasets. To demonstrate large-area ITS capabilities, SCATR-ITS is applied to a 142 km$^2$ region of Finnish national laser scanning data where it achieves an average throughput of 13.8 min/km$^2$, $\sim$18.6$\times$ faster than ForestFormer3D and approaching the speed of the unsupervised watershed algorithm, which it vastly outperforms in accuracy and segment quality. Overall, SCATR-ITS delivers state-of-the-art accuracy at speeds where operational-scale adoption becomes viable, paving the way toward countrywide deployment. The source code for SCATR-ITS and pretrained weights are available at https://github.com/ruoppa/scatr_its.

Abstract
Hyperspectral LiDAR (HSL) has emerged as a cutting-edge technique for underwater 3D imaging. Nevertheless, wavelength-selective absorption and scattering of light by water induce severe distortion in the spectral information of submerged targets and further result in color casts. In this paper, the underwater target spectral radiometric correction (UWSRC) method is adopted to correct the spectral reflectance of underwater targets by combining pre-measured water spectral transmittance and range data acquired by LiDAR. This approach obviates the complicated in situ measurement of water inherent optical parameters, which reduces the operational complexity for field deployments of underwater hyperspectral LiDAR remote sensing. Its spectral correction capability is experimentally validated under varying detection distances and diverse aquatic environments, including clear tap water and eutrophic turbid water. Furthermore, a dedicated HSL prototype for underwater scanning and a semi-submerged pod are developed. With this prototype, the 3D scanning imaging distance can be extended to 5.3 m in an outdoor swimming pool. Experimental results demonstrate that the UWSRC method can effectively compensate for the spectral reflectance of underwater targets under different water qualities and detection ranges. Meanwhile, true-color 3D point clouds of a color-calibration board are successfully obtained during long-distance scanning. Error analysis of reflectance inversion is carried out to explore the sensitivity to water transmittance bias and ranging uncertainty. The experimental investigations in this paper verify that the UWSRC method achieves favorable performance in underwater target spectral restoration and true-color reconstruction across different water qualities and detection distances.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Existing legged-locomotion systems commonly rely on depth images or online elevation maps. However, depth-based policies suffer from perceptual sim-to-real discrepancies due to imperfect depth sensor modeling, while map-based approaches are vulnerable to drift and misalignment. LiDAR offers direct measurements of the surrounding geometry, but raw LiDAR scans lack a compact, structured representation for high-frequency control. We propose a LiDAR perception pipeline that maintains perceptual consistency across simulation and reality through sensing- and representation-level alignment. At the sensor level, we recover frame-wise angular sampling patterns from the physical LiDAR and replay them in simulation with GPU ray casting. At the representation level, simulated and real returns undergo the same spherical range image encoding, yielding a fixed-size policy observation for end-to-end training and direct deployment without teacher--student distillation, online elevation mapping, or adaptation. Experiments show that our range-image policies achieve stronger sim-to-real transfer than depth- and elevation-map-based baselines, while ablations confirm the importance of the proposed sensing components. Egocentric video is the most scalable source of manipulation data for robot learning, but its difficulty lies in producing high-quality supervision. Abstract
The demand for three-dimensional reconstruction of membrane proteins in native lipid environments has grown substantially in recent years. Both single-particle analysis and tomographic techniques have enabled structural studies across diverse vesicular samples; however, these methods remain limited in either efficiency or resolution. Here, we present an AI-enabled, vesicle geometry-guided 3D reconstruction strategy based on untilted data. Given that some vesicles exhibit anisotropic shapes in solution and adopt near-spherical or ellipsoidal geometries, we trained an AI model to estimate membrane geometric parameters and compute normal vectors based on the fitted ellipsoidal surface. This information facilitates post hoc filtering of particles picked using the isSPA framework based on the angular difference between their orientations and the membrane normals, and also enables the exclusion of particles outside the vesicles. Our method provides a simple and efficient tool for achieving such reconstructions in physiological lipid bilayers.

Abstract
Generating consistent multi-view RGB images and normal maps from a single input image provides complementary appearance and geometric cues for 3D reconstruction. However, maintaining cross-view consistency requires information exchange across viewpoints, and dense cross-view attention becomes computationally expensive as the number of views and spatial resolution increase. In this paper, we propose Global-Local Proxy Attention (GLPA) to reduce cross-view communication costs while preserving fine-grained within-view processing. Specifically, GLPA retains full-resolution interactions within each view and uses compact global proxies for cross-view communication. To further leverage the exchanged information, GLPA incorporates iterative proxy refinement, which rebuilds the proxies from updated features and reuses the same attention parameters for subsequent interactions within a single denoising step. We evaluate GLPA on two structurally different diffusion backbones, FLUX and Stable Diffusion. Experiments on FLUX show that GLPA achieves a $2.65\times$ speedup in attention computation per denoising step over dense attention, while improving reconstruction IoU from 0.6849 to 0.7019 and reducing NVS LPIPS from 0.086 to 0.076. Results on Stable Diffusion further demonstrate the effectiveness of GLPA, with a $1.80\times$ attention speedup and improvements across all five quality metrics.

Published: 2026-11-20
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Event-supervised Gaussian reconstruction combines the high temporal resolution and wide dynamic range of event cameras with efficient explicit rendering. However, recovering a continuous surface remains difficult: changes in rendered appearance can compensate for depth errors while preserving the predicted brightness changes. We introduce Event-Anchored Surface Transport (EAST), which couples an event-constrained signed distance field to Gaussian rendering. Triangulation-supported candidates anchor the zero set, while normal flow measured from event timing constrains ray-surface intersection depths. Gaussian centers receive bounded corrections from the descriptor difference between the live field and a frozen initial reference. The correction is zero at initialization, yet transmits image gradients to the live field. Experiments on PAEv3D and NeRF Synthetic show improved surface accuracy and reference-view rendering over Gaussian baselines, together with efficient mesh extraction from the learned field.

Abstract
Within the golden 72 h after an earthquake, the rapid generation of emergency thematic maps is critical for disaster assessment and rescue decision-making, yet it faces core challenges such as the difficulty of reusing experience from historical earthquakes and the weak association among multi-source heterogeneous data. The spatial hallucination inherent in conventional spatial RAG systems can further mislead situational awareness of the affected region and increase decision risk. To address these issues, this paper proposes an adaptive dynamic thematic map generation method that fuses LLMs with Graph RAG for earthquake emergency scenarios. At the knowledge reconstruction level, a dual-channel association constraint mechanism over spatial and semantic channels is designed to link base geographic data, emergency resources, and historical cases across modalities, thereby constructing a directed heterogeneous event-region-resource spatial knowledge graph and achieving spatially grounded semantic consolidation of heterogeneous spatiotemporal multi-source data in emergency scenarios. At the retrieval and reasoning level, asymmetric query rewriting and multi-dimensional parallel recall over graph topology are introduced together with causal constraints of earthquake-triggered spatiotemporal propagation and proactive circuit breaking, which upgrades the text matching of conventional RAG to multi-hop spatiotemporal causal reasoning over the entire cross-domain chain of earthquake emergency response, suppresses spatial hallucination at the source of spatial decision making, and ensures the rigor of historical case reuse. At the collaborative mapping level, a multi-dimensional text fusion model of evolutionary evidence chains is constructed, which converts the discrete reasoning results of the LLM into standardized structured semantics and realizes mechanism-driven mapping of disaster semantics such as seismic intensity and lifeline engineering damage onto emergency thematic map layers. In addition, in-memory and distributed multi-level caching strategies are introduced to reduce the computational response latency when massive data floods in after strong earthquakes. Experiments on real earthquakes such as the Lushan and Luding events show that the proposed method raises the layer match rate to 0.791 (versus 0.295 for the strongest baseline), achieves the best layer-set F1 (0.252), reduces the in-window viewport-center displacement to 21.2 km, and attains a blind-judged semantic accuracy of 0.429 over the full 200-query set (0.881 on in-window queries) against 0.00 for all baselines, while a spatially filtered hybrid baseline reaches only 0.068. Driven by a lightweight 7B model, the cold-start generation of a single thematic map takes only 0.83 min, and the dynamic viewport refresh is kept within 12 s when the cache is activated, which meets the time window requirement for rapid post-earthquake map production. Given the moderate overall semantic accuracy and the disclosed out-of-graph coverage boundary, the method is positioned as an efficient and verifiable decision-support solution whose outputs still require human verification, rather than as a fully autonomous cartographic product.

Abstract
Embodied capabilities such as visual grounding, spatial reasoning, navigation, and driving understanding are often developed in separate vision-language specialists fine-tuned from a common pretrained model.Deploying the specialists separately multiplies storage, whereas merging them into one dense model forces conflicting task updates to share parameters and loses part of each specialist's expertise.We introduce UNITE, a framework that consolidates such specialists into a compact shared-backbone mixture of experts. UNITE keeps the feed-forward block of each specialist as a routable expert and represents the expert bank through a shared-anchor one-bit factorization: a shared floating-point anchor plus expert-specific, row-scaled binary deviations.The anchor is optimized jointly with the scales and signs through closed-form alternating updates, using only source weights and no calibration data.Router adaptation then adjusts token-level expert selection with the backbone and experts frozen, updating fewer than $0.6$M parameters ($<0.01\%$ of the $8.8$B active parameters). Built from four Qwen3-VL-8B specialists, the complete UNITE model occupies $20.3$ GB, $1.16\times$ the storage of one dense specialist and $59.6\%$ below the uncompressed MoE. Across $22$ benchmarks and R2R Val-Unseen, UNITE matches six static merging methods in mean retention of best-specialist performance, leads all of them on Scan2Cap, ScanQA, and all four navigation metrics, and surpasses every source specialist on all five 3D scene understanding benchmarks (up to $+19.1$ on Scan2Cap) and on R2R navigation. Relative to the uncompressed MoE, factorization is nearly lossless on $21$ of $22$ benchmarks; the exception is VSI-Bench, where frame-count experiments suggest impaired use of cross-frame evidence.UNITE thus consolidates complementary embodied capabilities near single-model storage.
