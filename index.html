Nitrogen Reduction Combined with Calcium-Based Amendments Regulates Soil Acidification Buffering and Maize Productivity in Acidified Croplands of Northeast ChinaCORE10
Authors: Yi-Ting Liu, L. Jin, Na-Hong Yuan, Meng-Meng Bao, Feng-Zhuo Geng...
Venue: Plants
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Excessive nitrogen fertilization accelerates soil acidification and threatens soil fertility and crop productivity, highlighting the need for management strategies that reduce nitrogen inputs while maintaining soil function and crop production. This study investigated the effects of nitrogen reduction combined with calcium-based amendments on soil acidity, nutrient availability, and maize productivity in an acidified aquic brown soil. A field experiment was conducted with three nitrogen-reduction levels (10%, 20%, and 30%) and four amendment treatments (no amendment, dolomite, quicklime, and fused calcium–magnesium phosphate fertilizer). The results showed that, under non-amended conditions, greater nitrogen reduction resulted in higher soil pH and measured total exchangeable base cations (TEBC), whereas nitrogen-reduction level had no significant effect on soil pH buffering capacity (pHBC). Among the calcium-based amendments, dolomite and quicklime increased pHBC by 25.3% and 21.4%, respectively, relative to the non-amended treatment, while dolomite generally showed higher measured concentrations of exchangeable Ca2+ and Mg2+ and higher TEBC. Amendment effects on nutrient availability differed among treatments: fused calcium–magnesium phosphate fertilizer produced the highest available phosphorus, whereas dolomite increased alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen and generally resulted in higher NH4+-N and available potassium. Maize grain yield was maintained when nitrogen reduction increased from 10% to 20% but declined significantly under 30% nitrogen reduction. Across nitrogen-reduction levels, dolomite was associated with the highest grain yield, exceeding the non-amended and quicklime treatments by 4.1% and 5.4%, respectively. Overall, moderate nitrogen reduction maintained maize productivity, while dolomite showed favorable responses across multiple indicators of soil acidity, exchangeable base-cation status, nutrient availability, and grain yield. These findings highlight the importance of coordinating nitrogen-reduction intensity with amendment-specific effects to balance soil amelioration and maize productivity in acidified croplands.
Soil Nutrients Rather than Heavy Metals Dominate the Succession of Microbial Communities in Subtropical Coal Gangue Piles: A Case Study from a Successional Gradient in Southern ChinaCORE7
Authors: Han-Chang Zhou, Tian Wang, Zi-Ying Zhang, Yi Li, Quan-Quan Jia...
Venue: Microorganisms
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
The natural revegetation of coal gangue piles relies on microbial community succession, yet limiting factors in subtropical regions remain unclear, hindering the acceleration of natural revegetation. This study examined coal gangue tailings of varying successional ages in southern China to identify the factors that limits on microbial succession. Soil properties, microbial diversity, community structure, network complexity, and heavy metal fractions were analyzed across successional stages. Results show a progressive soil acidification anda shift from carbon/nitrogen limitation to phosphorus limitation as succession progresses. Bacterial richness peaked at middle stage and then declined, while fungal richness shows no significant difference among different stages. Bacterial community structure changed sharply in early successional stage while fungal structure in the mid-stages. The complexity of microbial cooccurance network initially decreased then increased. Heavy metals mostly accumulated in stable residual fractions during succession, which has only limited toxic effects. The nutrient limitation that shift from C/N to P was the dominant factor shaping core functional modules and community composition, though dissolved organic carbon and few heavy metal fractions were also the main driving factors of microbial network complexity. These findings indicate that microbial succession is constrained more by nutrient availability than by heavy metal toxicity, with early stages limited by C/N sources and later stages limited by phosphorus. This provides a basis for targeted management to accelerate natural recovery of coal gangue piles.

Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-10-12

Abstract
Rubber plantations serve as significant carbon reservoirs within China’s terrestrial ecosystems. To provide a scientific foundation for improving soil fertility management, this study investigated the response of soil organic carbon and its components in rubber plantations to different soil amendments. Soil samples were collected from the 0–20 cm horizon of acidic soils in second-generation rubber plantations in Jinghong City, Yunnan Province. A laboratory incubation experiment was then conducted with four treatments: an unamended control (CK), organic fertilizer alone (T1), limestone powder alone (T2), and a combined organic fertilizer and limestone powder treatment (T3). The effects of these amendments on soil organic carbon and its fractions, iron and aluminum oxides, and microbial necromass carbon were systematically evaluated. The results demonstrated that under T3, Organic carbon (SOC) increased significantly by 12.80%, relative to CK ( p < 0.05). Treatments T2 and T3 significantly enhanced microbial necromass carbon (by 6.92–10.90%), fungal necromass carbon (by 6.60–11.32%), and bacterial necromass carbon (by 7.79–9.74%) compared to CK ( p < 0.05). T3 also significantly increased amorphous (Fe o , Al o ) and complexed (Fe p , Al p ) iron and aluminum oxides (p < 0.05). Two-way ANOVA revealed a significant interaction between organic fertilizer and limestone powder on POC, MAOC, MNC, and oxide forms, indicating an interactive regulatory effect on the distribution of carbon fractions and the transformation of mineral forms. Regression and correlation analyses showed microbial necromass carbon and Fe/Al oxides significantly positively correlated with SOC and its fractions, suggesting close associations among necromass accumulation, oxide protection, and SOC fraction changes. VPA showed that the shared explanatory power of microbial and mineral variables for SOC stabilization exceeded their respective pure effects. In summary, microbial necromass carbon, iron and aluminum oxides, and pH are closely related to soil organic carbon and its components. The combined application of organic fertilizer and limestone powder is highly effective in mitigating soil acidification and increasing SOC, microbial necromass carbon, and iron and aluminum oxides, and can serve as a reference for soil fertility management and carbon pool regulation in acidified soils of tropical rubber plantations.
AquaSteady seaweed-derived alginate hydrogel alters soil chemistry and biological indicators in a rate- and soil-dependent mannerCORE6

Venue: Scientific Reports

Abstract
AquaSteady (AQST) is a biodegradable, alginate-based, seaweed-derived hydrogel used in agriculture to enhance soil water retention, but its effects on soil physicochemical and biological indicators across contrasting soils remain poorly understood. We evaluated four AQST rates (0, 2, 5, and 10 mg/g soil) in six contrasting soils over a 56-day incubation, and measured soil chemistry and phospholipid fatty acid (PLFA)-based biological indicators. Soil pH rose from 7.38 (control) to 8.23 (high rate; p  < 0.001), shifting several soils toward alkaline, while cation exchange capacity declined from 25.32 to 23.83 cmolc kg − 1 ( p < 0.001 ). Exchangeable K, Ca, and Mg fell by ~ 13%, 8%, and 12%, and sulfate and nitrate-N decreased by ~ 29% and 62%, at high AQST rates versus control, while Na roughly doubled (271 to 548 mg kg − 1 ; p  < 0.001), indicating salinization risk in Na-sensitive soils. Total living microbial, fungal, protozoa, and undifferentiated microbial biomass increased mainly at medium and high rates; bacterial biomass and functional diversity showed no dose response. Mantel analysis found no statistically robust physicochemical-biological association after correction for multiple comparisons in any soil. Biological indicators were generally highest at the high rate, though medium (5 mg g⁻¹) did not differ statistically from high for most indicators, while sodium rose less and pH rose comparably at both rates. The medium rate may offer a better trade-off between biological benefit and salinity risk for sodicity-prone soils, pending calibration. These findings show soil-dependent trade-offs between biological stimulation and chemical shifts, indicating field validation is needed before broader AQST recommendations.
Short-Term Simulated NH4+/NO3− Inputs Reorganize Soil Microbial Communities and Modify N-Cycling Functional Potential in Temperate ForestsCORE6
Authors: Yun-Zhe Hu, Shu-lan Cheng, Hua-Jun Fang, Ming-Zhu Lu
Venue: Microorganisms
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Forest soil nitrogen (N) turnover and nitrous oxide (N2O) release are strongly influenced by atmospheric N deposition. Although the effects of individual inorganic N species on soil biogeochemistry are well documented, the consequences of changing their relative supply for N2O formation and the underlying microbial pathways remain less clear. We therefore examined soil N2O emissions, microbial communities, and N-transformation potential across a gradient of ammonium-to-nitrate (NH4+/NO3−) inputs in a temperate forest soil from Northeast China. Seven treatments covering NH4+/NO3− ratios from 1:5 to 5:1 were established. Soil physicochemical properties and N2O emissions were measured, while microbial taxonomic profiles and N-cycling genes were characterized by metagenomic sequencing. Altering the composition of inorganic N reorganized the microbial community, including the relative representation of archaeal, bacterial, and eukaryotic groups. N2O release was greatest when NH4+ and NO3− were supplied in equal proportions, a pattern that may reflect a stronger contribution of nitrification-associated processes. Across the treatment gradient, the response of N2O emissions to inorganic N composition was nonlinear. By contrast, the 1:2.5 NH4+/NO3− treatment produced comparatively low N2O emissions and contained a greater representation of genes associated with N2O reduction, consistent with enhanced microbial potential for N2O consumption. Under the more nitrate-dominated 1:5 treatment, genes involved in dissimilatory nitrate reduction to ammonium (DNRA) were enriched, indicating a shift in the potential routes of nitrate transformation. Metagenomic data further revealed marked variation in genes linked to nitrification, denitrification, and biological N fixation. In comparison with these compositional and functional responses, microbial diversity changed relatively little, possibly because overlapping functions were distributed among different taxa under contrasting N inputs. Overall, the findings show that the balance between ammonium and nitrate can affect forest-soil N2O emissions by restructuring microbial assemblages and their potential to transform N. These responses emphasize the need to consider the composition, rather than only the amount, of N deposition when evaluating microbial regulation of ecosystem N cycling and greenhouse-gas release. Field studies are still needed to test the generality of these patterns and their relevance to N management.
Viral regulation of N2O emissions depends on soil acidity.CORE5
Authors: Hai-Lin Zhang, Rui-Yuan Yan, Jiao Wang, Lin-Fa Fang, Xin-Rong Huang...
Venue: Journal of hazardous materials
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs
Abstract
Acidification generally enhances N2O emissions from soils. Yet, the adaptive responses of denitrifiers and the regulatory roles of soil viruses in denitrifier-mediated N2O emissions under persistent acidity remain poorly understood. Here, we combined D2O-labeled single-cell Raman spectroscopy, 15N tracing, metagenomics, machine learning, and virus-addition experiments to examine virus-denitrifier interactions across soils with contrasting long-term acidity conditions maintained for > 17 years. Acidic soils showed the highest N2O emissions despite the lowest potential denitrification rate and highest microbial anabolic activity. Denitrifiers and viral populations in acidic soils exhibited reduced microdiversity, stronger purifying selection, and distinct functional specialization, while viruses showed a lower virulent-to-temperate ratio and greater genetic potential for host interaction and persistence. Host antiviral defense capacity was the strongest genomic predictor of abundance responses to active virus. In acidic soils, active viruses increased N2O reductase level by 33.2% without altering nosZ abundance and reduced N2O accumulation by up to 62.5%, indicating an efficiency-optimized response. Neutral soils showed a quantity-driven response, whereas alkaline soils showed no significant reduction in N2O accumulation. These findings reveal acidity-dependent viral regulation of N2O emissions, strongest in long-term acidic soils.
A Carboxymethyl Cellulose Hydrogel Switch Enabling pH-Responsive Fertilizer.PROXY4
Authors: Yang Wang, Yang Lyu, Dongfang Zheng, Miao-Miao Zhang, Juan Li...
Venue: ACS applied materials & interfaces
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs
Abstract
The Green Revolution boosted crop yields with synthetic fertilizers, but their high solubility causes environmental pollution and poor synchronization with crop nutrient demands. To address this problem, we developed green intelligent fertilizers (GIF) using a pH-responsive material to enable root-fertilizer feedback. An ultra-sensitive and biodegradable pH-responsive material (pH-B) was synthesized by coordinating carboxymethyl cellulose with Ca2+ and cross-linking, with H+ sensitivity at 10-4 mol L-1. We then constructed a smart release system by embedding this pH-B switch into a 5% polyurethane coating to encapsulate diammonium phosphate (DAP) granules. In this design, the polyurethane matrix impedes the contact between DAP granules and soil water and thus minimizes phosphorus solubilization, leaching, and fixation, while the pH-B switch acts as an acidity-activated gatekeeper whose shrinking enables water penetration through the coating material. In acidic soil, the shoot P content of faba bean increased significantly (by 37-61%) in the treatment with pH-responsive fertilizer compared with DAP. Consequently, in calcareous soils, these fertilizers release nutrients specifically in response to rhizosphere acidification caused by proton exudation from phosphorus-deficient plant roots. This mechanism precisely synchronizes nutrient supply with crop demand. Our work provides a novel strategy for enhancing nutrient-use efficiency and promoting sustainable agriculture.

Abstract
Chemical, physical and biological indicators of soil condition are seldom assessed together across a crop cycle on tropical Ultisols. We examined how selected soil quality and arbuscular mycorrhizal (AMF) indicators differ among sugarcane crop stages. Twenty plots at three commercial plantations in South Sumatra, Indonesia, were sampled at four crop stages (3, 5, 7 and 11 months; n = 6, 6, 4 and 4). Soil pH, organic carbon (SOC), Bray-1 available phosphorus (P), aggregate stability (mean weight diameter, MWD), bulk density, AMF spore density, root colonization, Shannon diversity and stalk height were measured, with yield and Pol at harvest. Analyses used one- and two-way ANOVA, correlation, principal component analysis, a soil quality index (SQI) and resampling procedures suited to the modest sample size. Stage mean available P was 66.1, 11.7, 18.3 and 15.3 mg kg⁻¹ at 3, 5, 7 and 11 months. AMF spore density and root colonization were highest at 5 months, and colonization was negatively correlated with available P, although that association is a contrast between the 3-month plots and all others. Plots at later stages had higher MWD, lower bulk density and higher SOC concentration, although SOC stocks differed little. Stage differences persisted after adjustment for site and no stage × site interaction was detected, but two of three sites contributed one block per stage. The SQI ranked the earliest stage lowest, a result determined by construction. These observational findings motivate experimental testing of phosphorus timing.
Soil C:N:P Stoichiometric Differences Between Farmland and Shelterbelts and Their Associations with Soil Physicochemical Properties in the Yellow River FloodplainCORE4
Authors: Fu-Cheng Lu, Yan-Yan Li, Kong-Sen Wu
Venue: Forests
Published: 2026-10-12

Abstract
Shelterbelts are an important component of ecological restoration in the Yellow River Floodplain, but their associations with soil carbon (C), nitrogen (N), and phosphorus (P) stoichiometry remain incompletely understood. We compared farmland and shelterbelt soils at two depths (0–20 and 20–40 cm) and examined how nutrient indicators were associated with soil physicochemical properties. In the 0–20 cm layer, C/N, C/P, and N/P ratios were significantly higher in shelterbelt than in farmland, whereas total P and inorganic N contents were higher in farmland. Soil organic carbon and total N generally decreased with depth under both land uses. Factorial analysis identified significant land use × soil depth interactions for total P and all three stoichiometric ratios, indicating that their depth-related changes differed between farmland and shelterbelt. Correlation and random forest analyses showed that soil water content, bulk density, particle-size characteristics, and pH were associated with nutrient indicators, with their relative importance varying by land use and depth. These findings reveal land-use- and depth-dependent differences in soil C:N:P stoichiometry and identify soil properties relevant to monitoring soil quality during shelterbelt management in the Yellow River Floodplain.
Combined effects of brewer’s spent grain biostimulation and Bacillus bioaugmentation on crude oil biodegradation in contaminated soilCORE4

Abstract
Petroleum hydrocarbon contamination is a major environmental challenge because of its persistence and adverse effects on soil quality and ecosystem functioning. This study evaluated the combined effects of brewer’s spent grain (BSG) biostimulation and Bacillus bioaugmentation on crude oil biodegradation in contaminated soil. The study was performed using standard microbiological and physicochemical procedures to assess the composition of BSG, soil properties, microbial populations, and petroleum hydrocarbon degradation during remediation. Soil microcosms were amended with 1%, 10%, and 20% BSG and bioaugmented with Bacillus subtilis. Heterotrophic bacterial abundance (HBA), oil-degrading bacteria (ODB), Nitrosomonas, and Nitrobacter populations were monitored during the remediation period, while residual petroleum hydrocarbons were determined at the end of the experiment. The experimental data were subjected to analysis of variance (ANOVA) to determine differences among treatments. The composition of BSG included organic carbon of 76.0 ± 0.84%, protein of 27.5 ± 0.36%, nitrogen of 2.33 ± 0.05%, and a calorific value of 399.4 ± 0.35 kcal, indicating its suitability as a nutrient-rich amendment for stimulating microbial activity. The soil was acidic, with a pH of 5.05 ± 0.18, 7.15 ± 0.25 mg/kg total petroleum hydrocarbons, and 0.57 ± 0.21 mg/kg polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. The heterotrophic bacterial abundance of 7.30 ± 0.32 log₁₀ CFU/g and hydrocarbonoclastic bacterial abundance of 2.60 ± 0.61 log₁₀ CFU/g included genera such as Bacillus, Pseudomonas, Alcaligenes, and Micrococcus sp., indicating organisms with intrinsic biodegrading potential. HBA and ODB populations in Controls 1 and 2 differed significantly from those in the amended and treated soils (ANOVA, p < 0.05), indicating enhanced microbial proliferation following nutrient supplementation and bioaugmentation. Biostimulation with 1%, 10%, and 20% BSG amendments improved soil physicochemical conditions, with pH values ranging from 7.26 ± 0.03 to 8.21 ± 0.03, redox potential from 54.0 ± 0.2 to 90.0 ± 0.1 mV, and moisture content from 14.97 ± 0.06 to 16.52 ± 0.43% compared with the untreated controls. Combined treatment stimulated microbial proliferation, with the 10% BSG treatment recording the highest HBA of 8.82 ± 0.88 log₁₀ CFU/g, while oil-degrading bacteria were enriched during remediation. Bioaugmentation with Bacillus subtilis alone resulted in 49.93% hydrocarbon degradation, whereas combined bioaugmentation and BSG biostimulation improved degradation efficiencies to 88.58%, 76.85%, and 68.61% in the 1%, 10%, and 20% BSG treatments, respectively. Preferential degradation of low- and mid-molecular-weight hydrocarbons (C10–C22) and substantial reduction of biodegradation indicator compounds such as pristane and phytane indicated enhanced microbial activity. Although 10% BSG supported heterotrophic bacterial abundance, the 1% BSG treatment achieved the highest hydrocarbon degradation efficiency, suggesting that moderate nutrient supplementation through BSG amendment, rather than excessive organic loading, favored effective biodegradation. These findings indicate that synergy between nutrient-driven microbial stimulation, functional hydrocarbon degraders, and optimized nutrient amendment strategies enhanced bioremediation efficiency and support the sustainable use of agro-industrial wastes as effective biostimulants for environmental remediation.

Abstract
Annotation The mechanisms by which coarse woody debris (CWD) affects soil microbial diversity across different spatial scales and forest disturbance regimes remain poorly understood. This study investigated the impact of CWD from multiple tree species on forest floor bacterial and fungal communities under varying landscape-scale conditions, specifically comparing old-growth forests situated on Phaozems and Retisols with mass-windthrow areas located on loamy Retisols and sandy Podzols. We analyzed both chemical compound and metagenomic profiles of 63 CWD-associated soil samples collected from the Kaluzhskie Zaseki Nature Reserve (European part of Russia). High-throughput Illumina sequencing of the 16S rRNA gene and the ITS region was used to profile both bacterial and fungal communities, respectively. Scale-dependent diversity patterns and taxonomic accumulation were assessed using GLM, NMDS, dbRDA, and iNEXT frameworks. Our findings revealed a profound spatial decoupling in forest floor microbial communities, where a localized reduction in alpha-diversity under CWD drove a sharp increase in landscape-scale gamma-diversity. While standard soil physicochemical properties rigidly dictated baseline matrix richness, CWD operated as a powerful micro-environmental hotspot overriding these constraints. Deadwood inputs filtered out dominant matrix-associated taxa, creating highly specialized micro-refugia characterized by high compositional dispersion. This niche selection was particularly pronounced beneath Quercus logs on Phaozems and Retisols. Furthermore, post-windthrow microbial responses were heavily filtered by wood traits and baseline soil textures. On loamy Retisols within the windthrow mosaic, this breakaway dynamic was highly taxon-specific: while Acer and Fraxinus CWD triggered partitioned, site-specific hotspot dynamics yielding an exceptionally large pool of unique ASVs for bacteria, the distinct enrichment for fungal communities was primarily driven by Ulmus CWD and log-free windthrow areas. Ultimately, iNEXT accumulation profiles confirmed that deadwood does not merely multiply common soil taxa, but enriches the forest landscape by injecting critical beta-level heterogeneity into the soil matrix. These insights emphasize the role of diverse CWD retention in buffering forest microbiomes against large-scale environmental disturbances across heterogeneous soil landscapes. Highlights Sandy soils suppress bacterial abundance but trigger massive fungal proliferation. Bacterial richness is determined by soil pH; fungal diversity responds to nitrogen. Soil pH and nitrogen shift fungal composition along decoupled orthogonal axes. Coarse woody debris acts as hotspots of micro-environmental diversity. Post-windthrow microbial hotspots are strongly shaped by tree species.

Abstract
Himalayan soils are highly susceptible to the impacts of climate change and land-use shifts. Soil organic carbon (SOC) and the carbon management index (CMI) are key measures of soil health in response to land use changes. This study assessed total organic carbon (TOC), carbon fractions, carbon pool index (CPI), CMI, and physicochemical properties in forest and agricultural land-use systems in the temperate western Himalayas. Soil samples were collected across 32 distinct sites representing forest (FS), wheat (WS), rice (RS), and maize (MS) systems (n = 32). FS exhibited the highest average TOC (25.6 g kg-1), followed by MS (19.3 g kg-1), WS (15.2 g kg-1), and RS (14 g kg-1). TOC levels differed significantly between land-use categories (ANOVA; F = 13.4, P< 0.001), with all agricultural systems showing significantly lower TOC levels than forests. The maximum concentration of labile carbon (LC) was identified in FS (4.3%), while the lowest was observed in WS (2.8%). MS had the highest CPI (0.75), whereas RS had the highest average CMI (85.5), indicating distinct carbon management dynamics despite lower baseline TOC. TOC was positively correlated with altitude and electrical conductivity but negatively correlated with soil pH. Multiple linear regression (R2 = 0.70) confirmed land-use type as the primary predictor of TOC variation, showing significant reductions for RS (11.6 g kg-1), WS (10.7 g kg-1), and MS (4.9 g kg-1) compared to the forest baseline (P< 0.05). Principal component analysis successfully differentiated agricultural and forest systems based on topography, soil properties, and carbon pools. The primary cause of SOC depletion is the initial conversion of forests to farmland, underscoring the need for climate smart land management to maintain ecosystem services and replenish regional carbon stocks.

Venue: Water, Air, & Soil Pollution
Published: 2026-10-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Heavy metal pollution, mainly from industrial wastewater, is an ongoing worldwide concern that threatens water quality. The present study investigated the potential of Cannabis sativa L. buds, both raw and chemically modified with glycine and sodium bicarbonate, to decontaminate Lead (Pb2+) and Copper (Cu2+) ions from wastewater. Fourier transform Infrared spectroscopy results showed that the chemical modification process altered the surface functional groups, as evidenced by shifts in the functional group peak intensities of the modified bio-adsorbents compared to those of the unmodified bio-adsorbent before adsorption. After the adsorption process, a reduction in peak intensities was observed due to adsorption interactions with metal ions. Scanning electron microscopy revealed an irregular and multi-layered surface with interconnected pore network structures of all assessed bio-adsorbents. However, sodium bicarbonate modified cannabis appeared to be more porous than both bio-adsorbents. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy identified carbon and oxygen as the major elemental components of the bio-adsorbents. X-ray diffraction spectroscopy revealed that the sodium bicarbonate modified bio-adsorbent exhibited a more crystalline form, while thermogravimetric analysis showed that it was more stable than glycine and raw cannabis biomaterial. These findings indicate that bio-adsorbents exhibit an enhanced adsorption capacity, whilst sodium bicarbonate modified cannabis is expected to perform better than the other bio-adsorbents. A batch adsorption method was employed and assessed by varying solution pH, temperature, mass dosage, initial metal concentration, and contact time in a binary system. The optimal adsorption conditions were determined to be a solution pH of 7, reaction time of 10 min, mass dosage of 10 mg, an initial adsorbate concentration of 10 mg/L, and an adsorption temperature of 25 ºC. According to the analysis of variance results, the effects of temperature and adsorbate concentration on the adsorption of metal ions showed the highest (> 61%) and lowest (> 3%) percentage contributions. The adsorption rates of Cu2+ and Pb2+ ions fitted a non-linear pseudo-first-order model, and the sorption mechanism was best described by a multilayered surface (the Freundlich isotherm). The metal ion adsorption process was found to be exothermic, spontaneous, and feasible. Sodium bicarbonate modified cannabis showed a larger maximum adsorption capacity (qmax) (31.222–56.875 mg/g) than glycine modified cannabis (28.983–53.888 mg/g) and raw cannabis (26.151–30.527 mg/g) for Cu2+ and Pb2+ ions, respectively. The percentage removal efficiency in real wastewater samples was 74–88% for the raw bio-adsorbent, 80–100% for glycine, and 96–100% for the sodium bicarbonate-modified bio-adsorbent for Cu2+ and Pb2+ ions, respectively. Though all bio-adsorbents showed good performance in removing both metal ions, sodium bicarbonate-modified cannabis was shown to be superior. This study showed that Cannabis sativa L. buds can be efficiently applied and regenerated at least three times as bio-adsorbents for the removal of metal ions from wastewater samples. Cannabis sativa L. buds offer an efficient, affordable, and eco-friendly alternative for water monitoring compared with conventional adsorbents. Additionally, the potential applicability of Cannabis sativa buds could be further assessed on a large industrial scale analysis.

Abstract
Petroleum exploration and production can alter the geochemical quality of surrounding soils and water through the introduction and redistribution of potentially toxic metals. This study assessed the degree of heavy-metal pollution and associated ecological risk in soils and water from the Apkai and Umusedege oil-field communities of Ndokwa East and Ndokwa West Local Government Areas, Delta State, Nigeria. Ten heavy metals, namely Ni, Cr, Cu, Co, Pb, Cd, Fe, Mn, As and Zn, were investigated in soil, while the detected subset was assessed similarly in water. Abstract
Litter decomposition regulates carbon and nutrient cycling in forest ecosystems, yet its response to interacting effects of seasonality, elevation, and microclimate remains poorly understood, especially in mountain forests experiencing climate change (Panayotov and Tsvetanov (2023), Ma et al. (2024)). Slope aspect can generate strong microclimatic contrasts over short distances and may therefore outweigh elevation as a driver of decomposition. We tested this in the Rila Mountains, Bulgaria, across an altitudinal gradient of 1500–1800 m a.s.l. on north- and south-facing slopes during spring, summer, and autumn. Eight permanent plots were established, with four plots per slope aspect. North-facing plots supported mixed montane forest dominated by Norway spruce ( Picea abies Karst.) and European beech ( Fagus sylvatica L.), whereas south-facing plots were characterized by Norway spruce ( Picea abies Karst.) and Scots pine ( Pinus sylvestris L.) stands. Litter decomposition was measured using the Tea Bag Index 3.0 protocol (Middelanis et al. 2023), with green tea and rooibos buried at 8 cm depth and excavated at days 20, 40 and 90 per season. Decomposition rate ( k , d⁻¹) and labile fraction ( a , g g⁻¹) were estimated by nonlinear least squares fitting. Soil moisture, pH, and β-glucosidase activity were measured at each retrieval, while static soil properties, stand mensuration, and species composition were recorded for each plot. Meteorological variables, including air temperature and precipitation, were used to characterize regional conditions during the study period. Environmental controls on decomposition were assessed using Wilcoxon tests, Pearson correlations, season-specific Principal Component Analysis with TBI parameters as supplementary variables and Spearman correlations. North- and south-facing slopes differed significantly in CN ratio, soil texture, moisture, β-glucosidase activity, species composition, and stand basal area (Wilcoxon tests, p < 0.05). Multivariate analyses revealed consistent seasonal patterns in the environmental controls of tea decomposition. PCA showed that the main ordination axis (Dim1, ~60% variance) reflected a soil-microclimate gradient driven by moisture, temperature, silt content, basal area, and enzyme activity. North‑ and south‑facing slopes separated clearly in spring and autumn, when microclimatic contrasts were strongest, but converged in summer. The supplementary TBI variables aligned with distinct parts of this gradient: decomposition rate ( k ) consistently associated with warmer, drier, more exposed conditions, whereas the stabilization factor ( a ) aligned with moister, finer‑textured soils and higher enzymatic activity. Spearman correlations supported these patterns. Moisture was negatively related to k but positively to a , while basal area and enzyme efficiency were strong predictors of stabilization. Silt and temperature-precipitation interactions further modulated decomposition responses. Together, the results indicate that short‑term microclimate controls early mass loss, whereas soil properties and microbial functioning govern stabilization, with exposure acting as a seasonal modifier of these relationships. Long-term microclimate and soil monitoring is essential for understanding how future climate change may affect decomposition processes and forest carbon balance. Because these findings are based on a single field experiment, broader observations across seasons, sites, and forest types are needed to confirm the patterns observed here and strengthen their general applicability.

Abstract
Cadmium (Cd) contamination poses a severe ecological risk to ecosystems. Trehalose (TR) is a non-reducing disaccharide which can mitigate the adverse effects of heavy metals. The physiological responses and transcriptomic characteristics underlying TR-mediated heavy metal tolerance remain unclear in maize. In this study, maize seedlings were exposed to 200 mg L−1 CdCl2·2.5H2O with or without 23 mM exogenous TR for 5 days.
