Effects of reductive soil disinfestation and fertilizer reduction on soil health, tobacco performance and microbial communities under continuous croppingCORE11

Venue: Bangladesh Journal of Botany
Published: 2026-11-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
A field experiment was conducted in a long-term tobacco monoculture field to evaluate the effects of reductive soil disinfestation (RSD) using graded corn straw doses combined with reduced fertilizer application on soil quality and microbial environment of continuously cropped tobacco soil in the karst region of Guizhou. Five treatments were set including blank control (CK), flooding plus mulching without straw (T0), and RSD with 400 kg/mu (T1), 800 kg/mu (T2), 1200 kg/mu (T3) corn straw. The responses of soil physicochemical properties, tobacco agronomic traits, disease incidence, economic characteristics, leaf chemical quality, and soil bacterial and fungal community structure to different RSD treatments were systematically analyzed. The results showed that RSD treatments significantly increased soil pH and alleviated soil acidification and notably elevated the contents of soil alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen (AN), available phosphorus (AP), available potassium (AK) and organic matter (SOM), among which T2 treatment presented the optimal improvement effect. RSD treatments significantly optimized tobacco agronomic traits at vigorous growth, budding and topping stages, effectively reduced the incidence and disease index of tobacco black root rot, and greatly improved tobacco yield, output value and chemical quality coordination, with the best performance in T2 treatment. RSD treatments dramatically reshaped soil microbial community structure. At the phylum level, RSD enriched beneficial microbial phyla and inhibited potential pathogenic phyla; at the genus level, the relative abundance of beneficial genera such as Trichoderma and Penicillium increased, while the pathogenic genus Fusarium decreased significantly. Correlation analysis revealed that soil physicochemical properties were closely correlated with microbial community composition, and RSD treatments enhanced soil nutrient cycling-related microbial functions. In conclusion, RSD with 800 kg/mu corn straw combined with fertilizer reduction can effectively ameliorate soil physicochemical properties, suppress soil-borne pathogens, optimize microbial community structure and improve tobacco yield and quality. This approach offers a feasible green remediation strategy for managing soil degradation under tobacco monoculture in karst regions. Bangladesh J. Bot. 55(3): 727-739, 2026 (September) Special
Effects of Ferric Nitrate-Modified Biochar on N2O Emissions from an Acidic Moso Bamboo Forest SoilCORE5
Authors: Bang-Liang Deng, Aamer Muhammad
Venue: Forests
Published: 2026-11-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Nitrous oxide (N2O) is a potent greenhouse gas, and acidic forest soils are major sources. Biochar can mitigate N2O emissions in some soils but its effectiveness is pH-dependent, and can vary with biochar modification. It is unclear whether modified biochar reduces or increases N2O emissions in acidic forest soils. This study was designed primarily to evaluate ferric nitrate-modified biochar as an amendment for improving soil pH in acidic Moso bamboo (Phyllostachys edulis) forest soils, and to determine whether this pH improvement can be achieved without increasing N2O emissions. We investigated effects of modified biochar on N2O emissions and nitrogen transformation genes in acidic Moso bamboo forest soils. An incubation study (121 days; 5% w/w dose; four replicates per treatment) included seven treatments: control, pristine biochar (B), and ferric nitrate-modified biochars (MB1–MB5) prepared at different pH values. Cumulative N2O emissions were lowest in the control and 4.8–7.7-fold higher in all biochar variants than in the control, peaking at MB3 and declining at MB5. Soil pH was the main factor: the N2O emission rate peaked at pH ≈ 5.18, and the (nirK + nirS)/nosZ ratio showed a fitted maximum at pH 5.19, although this quadratic fit was not significantly better than a linear model. Bacterial amoA and nosZ abundances increased with pH. Under MB treatments, N2O emission rate was negatively correlated with pH and nosZ and positively correlated with ammonium nitrogen, nitrate nitrogen, and the (nirK + nirS)/nosZ ratio (all p < 0.05). Soil pH and the amount of added nitrate varied in tandem across treatments, so their effects could not be fully disentangled.

Abstract
To investigate the effect of spent substrate incorporation following Stropharia rugosoannulata cultivation on soil fertility and soil microecological environment, a field experiment was conducted, with uncultivated soil without S. rugosoannulata as the control (CK). The responses of soil nutrient contents, bacterial and fungal community structures, diversity patterns, and community assembly mechanisms to the spent mushroom substrate incorporation treatment (W) were analyzed. The results showed that: soil alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen (AN) and available phosphorus (AP) contents in treatment (W) were significantly increased by 23.82% and 38.86%, respectively (P <0.05). Soil organic matter (SOM) and available potassium (AK) showed non-significant increasing trends, while soil pH decreased slightly. Alpha diversity analysis indicated that treatment W reduced species richness (sobs, Chao1 index) and community diversity (Shannon index) of both bacterial and fungal communities; the reductions were non-significant for bacteria. Community composition analysis revealed that treatment W markedly altered soil microbial community structure. At the phylum level, the relative abundance of Actinobacteriota was significantly enriched among the bacteria, whereas Mortierellomycota abundance was greatly elevated in case of fungi. At the genus level, beneficial microorganisms including Mortierella and Penicillium increased in relative abundance, while potential phytopathogens such as Fusarium declined. Community assembly analysis based on βNTI indicated that deterministic processes, particularly homogeneous selection, dominated microbial community assembly under treatment W. In conclusion, incorporation of spent substrate from S. rugosoannulata cultivation can effectively elevate soil available nutrient levels, optimize soil microbial community structure, enrich beneficial microbes, suppress pathogens, and improve soil ecological functions. This practice represents a promising green soil amendment strategy for agricultural soil improvement. Bangladesh J. Bot. 55(3): 695-710, 2026 (September) Special
Development of an Integrated Physicochemical and Biological Soil Quality Assessment Method for Evaluating Soil Health and Agricultural SuitabilityCORE4
Authors: Devjit Datta
Venue: International Journal of Technology and Applied Science
Published: 2026-11-15

Abstract
Soil quality is an essential determinant of agricultural productivity, ecosystem stability and environmental sustainability. Conventional soil testing primarily focuses on physicochemical characteristics, including pH, electrical conductivity, organic carbon and nutrient concentrations. However, these parameters alone may not adequately represent the biological condition and functional capacity of soil. The present research project was designed to develop an integrated soil quality assessment method combining physicochemical, biological and physical indicators. Soil samples representing three different land-use conditions were considered: agricultural soil, garden soil and roadside soil. Parameters including soil pH, electrical conductivity, moisture content, organic carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, microbial activity and water infiltration capacity were evaluated. An integrated Soil Quality Index (SQI) was developed by combining normalised measurements of the selected indicators. The illustrative experimental findings demonstrated that garden soil exhibited the highest overall soil quality, followed by agricultural soil, whereas roadside soil demonstrated comparatively lower biological activity and organic carbon content. The proposed assessment method provides a framework for evaluating soil health through multiple indicators rather than relying exclusively on conventional nutrient measurements. It may contribute to sustainable agricultural management, environmental monitoring and soil restoration programmes.

Venue: Bangladesh Journal of Botany

Abstract
This study investigates the prediction of soil organic matter (SOM) based on visible-near-infrared spectroscopy and machine learning techniques in farmland soils of Hengshan district, northern Shaanxi. The statistical distribution characteristics of soil pH and SOM were first analyzed. Hyperspectral data were then collected, and spectral information was improved through mathematical transformations including first derivative (FD), second derivative (SD), and logarithmic transformations. Feature wavelengths were extracted using the successive projections algorithm combined with correlation analysis. Finally, PLSR (Partial Least Squares Regression) and SVM (Support Vector Machine) were constructed and compared. The results show that the coefficient of variation of SOM has exceeded 50%, indicating an extremely high variability, and SOM showed a significant negative correlation with spectral reflectance. The first derivative transformation increases the correlation coefficient from -0.55 to -0.29. Among the models, the CA+Log′(1/R)+PLSR combined model performed the best, achieving a validation coefficient of determination (Rv²) of 0.8973 and a residual predictive deviation (RPD) of 2.4664, demonstrating a superior accuracy and stability. This study confirms that, with appropriate derivative or logarithmic transformations, the linear PLSR model can achieve high-precision inversion of soil organic matter, providing technical support for regional precision agriculture and sustainable land use. Microorganism Content in Soils of Organic Apple Production in the Elmalı District, Antalya Province, TürkiyeCORE4
Authors: E. Basım
Venue: Black Sea Journal of Agriculture
Published: 2026-11-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil microorganisms play important roles in nutrient cycling, organic matter transformation, plant growth, and the biological regulation of soil-borne organisms. However, information on the culturable microbial populations associated with organically managed apple orchards in the Elmalı district of Antalya, Türkiye, remains limited. This study aimed to characterize the culturable bacterial and fungal populations present in soils from organically managed apple orchards and to identify selected microbial isolates using matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS). Soil samples were collected from ten organically managed apple orchards in the Elmalı district during the apple harvest period. Culturable bacteria and fungi were isolated using Nutrient Agar and Potato Dextrose Agar, respectively. Microbial population densities were determined by the colony-forming unit (CFU) method. Bacterial isolates were preliminarily characterized according to their Gram reaction using the KOH test, and selected bacterial and fungal isolates were identified by MALDI-TOF MS. The mean culturable bacterial population density across the sampled orchards was (1.21 x 106) CFU/mL, whereas the mean fungal population density was (4.96 x 104) CFU/mL, indicating that the bacterial population was approximately 24-fold higher than the fungal population. Among 93 bacterial isolates, 54 (58.1%) were classified as presumptive Gram-positive and 39 (41.9%) as presumptive Gram-negative. MALDI-TOF MS identified bacterial isolates belonging to Brevundimonas diminuta, Bacillus thuringiensis subsp. israelensis, Bacillus subtilis, Bacillus atrophaeus, Pseudomonas chlororaphis, Priestia megaterium and Bacillus mojavensis. The identified fungal taxa included Penicillium citrinum, the Aspergillus flavus/oryzae group, Fusarium proliferatum, Aspergillus ochraceus, and Aspergillus niger. The results provide a preliminary characterization of the culturable microbial fraction of organically managed apple orchard soils in Elmalı and indicate the presence of bacterial and fungal taxa of potential interest for future agricultural studies. In particular, the recovery of several Bacillus, Pseudomonas and Priestia isolates provides a basis for subsequent investigations of plant-growth-promoting and biocontrol-related traits. However, the culture-dependent approach and the database-dependent nature of MALDI-TOF MS identification should be considered when interpreting the results. Further studies combining molecular identification, soil physicochemical analyses, and functional assays are required to determine the ecological and agronomic significance of the recovered microorganisms.

Abstract
Understanding the ecological stoichiometry of soil extracellular enzymes and its driving factors across tea plantations of varying ages is crucial for elucidating ecosystem dynamics, maintaining tea quality, and promoting the sustainable development of the tea industry. In this study, tea plantations were selected of two different stand ages (5 and 16 years) in Kaiyang County, Guizhou Province, along with adjacent natural vegetation land without tea planting as a control. Systematic analysis of soil pH, organic carbon concentration, total nitrogen (TN) and total phosphorus (TP) contents, plus the concentrations of accessible nitrogen (AN), phosphorus (AP), and potassium (AK), together with the enzymatic activities of four extracellular enzymes were conducted. The results indicated that with increasing tea plantation age, soil pH decreased significantly. Soil organic carbon, TN, and TP exhibited a trend of first increasing and then decreasing, while AK content showed a continuous increase. Enzyme activity analysis revealed that LAP activities increased with plantation age, whereas ALP activity declined markedly. Enzyme vector analysis showed that vector length ranged from 0.28 to 0.47, following a pattern of initially decreasing and then increasing, while the vector angle ranged from 29.14 to 37.90°, showing an initial increase followed by a decrease. All vector angles were below 45°, indicating an overall nitrogen limitation, with a slight alleviation during the early growth stage. Random forest and correlation analyses identified pH, AP, TP, and AN as key drivers regulating enzyme activities and nutrient limitations. Collectively, these findings reveal the critical factors controlling nutrient limitation as tea plantation age increases, providing a scientific basis for the sustainable management of tea garden soils. Venue: Bulletin of Science and Practice

Abstract
Presents an agroecological assessment of the soil condition of an agricultural site located in the Samukh District of Azerbaijan. The aim of the study was to investigate the physicochemical and agrochemical properties of the soil, as well as to determine residual pesticide concentrations and assess their environmental significance. The study was conducted over a 14-month period using field observations, systematic soil sampling, and laboratory physicochemical and toxicological methods. Agrochemical parameters were determined in five soil samples collected from the 0–30 cm soil layer, while pesticide residues were analyzed in samples collected from the 0–20 cm layer three times during the study period. The results showed that soil pH (7.1), humus content (2.75%), and total nitrogen (0.14%) were within the specified reference ranges. Phosphorus (43.4 ppm) and manganese (3.4 ppm) concentrations were below the reference values, whereas potassium, calcium, magnesium, iron, and copper concentrations exceeded the corresponding reference levels. Among the detected pesticides, the highest concentration was recorded for p,p'-DDE (164 μg/kg), followed by p,p'-DDT (36 μg/kg), p,p'-DDD (2.6 μg/kg), and endosulfan I (1.7 μg/kg). The concentrations of the other investigated compounds were below the detection limits. The obtained results indicate the need for continued systematic agroecological monitoring of the soil.

Abstract
<p>Honey bees (<em>Apis mellifera</em>) are reliable bioindicators of environmental quality due to their role as pollinators, their wide foraging range, and their continuous interaction with various components of the ecosystem. The aim of this paper is to examine the importance of honey bees as bioindicators by analysing the mechanisms of pollutant accumulation and their application in ecological risk assessment. During the collection of nectar, pollen, water, and plant resins, honey bees come into contact with pollutants originating from air, water, and soil, including heavy metals, pesticides, and microplastic particles, which can subsequently be detected in their tissues and bee products. In addition to chemical analyses, changes in the behaviour, physiological condition, and mortality of bee colonies represent important indicators of environmental contamination. Particular attention is given to pesticides, especially neonicotinoids, owing to their pronounced toxic and sublethal effects. Although research on the presence of microplastics in bee products is still evolving, current findings indicate their potential value in biomonitoring. Overall, honey bees represent an effective and cost-efficient tool for environmental biomonitoring, with considerable importance for ecological risk assessment and biodiversity conservation.</p>

Abstract
The non-biodegradable nature and contribution to pollution and greenhouse gas emissions of conventional plastics produced from fossil fuel is becoming a major environmental concern. Bioplastics on the other hand offer a sustainable solution to this problem by being derived from renewable resources, such as agricultural waste, and exhibiting biodegradability, reducing the environmental burden associated with their production and disposal. This study aims to synthesize and characterize bioplastics from agricultural wastes, by specifically investigating the use of Potato peels and watermelon rinds as feedstock for bioplastic production. Bioplastic films were efficiently synthesized from different ratio of potato peels starch to watermelon rinds starch with combination percentage ratios of 100:0, 90:10, 80:20, 70:30 and 60:40. Glycerol was used as plasticizer together with acetic acid. The synthesized bioplastics were characterized based on their biodegradability, mechanical and thermal properties. The bioplastics exhibited good biodegradability with samples 100:0, 90:10 and 80:20 degrading completely in 15 days while samples 70:30 and 60:40 degraded in 18 days. The bioplastics also possessed promising thermal properties with melting points ranging from 163oC to 179.9oC. The 60:40 sample showed the highest tensile strength of 50.20 MPa while sample with a ratio of 70:30 possessed high tensile strength of 48.77 MPa and the highest elongation of 7.5 mm (15 % elongation). The functional groups of the bioplastics were investigated using FTIR. Bioplastics produced from combination of potato peels starch and watermelon rinds have highest level of crystallinity of 24.1o by using X-ray diffraction analysis. The surface morphology of the bioplastics was also investigated using SEM analysis. Hence, bioplastics synthesized from agricultural wastes (potato peels and watermelon rinds) can be a sustainable substitute for conventional plastics.

Venue: Bangladesh Journal of Botany

Abstract
The major maize-producing region of Northeast China faces dual pressures from an aging rural population and the implementation of an extended land contract policy, potentially leading to significant divergence in production sustainability across farmer generations. To investigate this, a study was conducted from 2024 to 2025 in typical agricultural areas of Heilongjiang and Liaoning provinces. 72 maize-growing households were categorized into three groups based on the household head's age: Young Adults (YA), Middle-aged Adults (MA), and Old Adults (OA). Using the Sustainable Intensification Assessment Framework (SIAF), the sustainability of their production systems was systematically evaluated across five dimensions: productivity, economy, environment, human, and social. The results indicated that the YA group significantly outperformed others in maize grain yield, net income, and labor productivity. They also exhibited lower soil bulk density (BD), higher soil water content, and significantly better partial factor productivity of fertilizer (PFP). While the OA group maintained higher soil organic matter (SOM), they scored the lowest in technology adoption index and willingness of youth to engage in farming, showed poor system coordination, and had the lowest comprehensive sustainability score (CS). This study found that the YA group has developed a more resilient and coordinated sustainable intensification model by integrating scale management, mechanization, precision fertilization (e.g., soil testing-based formula fertilization), and conservation tillage. The study suggests that implementing the extended land contract policy should account for generational differences. It recommends promoting a youth-oriented agronomic system centered on the synergy of "scale-technology-market" and fostering the integration of traditional soil fertility management experience with modern precision agriculture to enhance the systemic sustainability of regional maize production.
