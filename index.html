Effects of Liquid Microbial Fertilizer on Tomato Yield, Fruit Chemical Quality, and Rhizosphere Soil Quality in Acidified Phosphorus-Enriched Greenhouse SoilCORE7
Authors: Pu-Sheng Geng, Chuan-Xing Liu, Yi-Qiao Wang, Si-Tong Liu, Wen Guo...
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Continuous intensive fertilization in greenhouse vegetable production can cause soil acidification, salinity, and phosphorus (P) enrichment, yet direct comparisons between microbial and chemical fertilizers under equivalent nutrient inputs remain limited. We hypothesized that liquid microbial fertilizer (LM) would improve soil functions more effectively than chemical fertilizer (CF) and that reduced LM input could maintain tomato productivity. A fixed-site greenhouse experiment was conducted in 2025 at the Acheng Experimental Base of Northeast Agricultural University, Harbin, China, as the third year of a trial established in 2023. A randomized complete block design with four blocks was used, comprising seven treatments with four independent replicate plots per treatment: an unfertilized control (CK) and LM and CF applied at 50%, 75%, and 100% of the conventional N–P2O5–K2O rate. Soil chemical properties, enzyme activities, aggregate stability, soil quality, tomato yield, and fruit quality were evaluated. Compared with CF at matched nutrient inputs, LM alleviated soil acidity and salinity, enhanced C and N retention, enzyme activities, and aggregate stability. The soil quality index (SQI) was 27.80%, 56.62%, and 99.30% higher under LM1, LM2, and LM3 than under the corresponding CF1, CF2, and CF3 treatments, respectively. LM1 and LM2 increased tomato yield by 46.20% and 39.40% relative to CK, whereas LM3 favored fruit and soil quality. Overall, reduced LM application effectively balanced tomato productivity, fertilizer-input reduction, and soil improvement in acidified, P-enriched greenhouse soil.

Abstract
This study examined land characteristics and the actual land suitability classifications reported in the source assessment for two land mapping units (LMUs) intended for avocado (Persea americana Mill.) cultivation in Naman Teran District, Karo Regency, North Sumatra, Indonesia. According to the availablestudy records, 13 land mapping units (LMUs) were delineated using GIS. Two units, LMU 1 in Sigarang-garang and LMU 2 in Sukanalu, were purposively selected for detailed assessment based on recommendations from the sub-district government. Laboratory resultsreported by SOCFINDO for the two traceable compositesoil samples revealedcontrasting soil properties. LMU 1 had a pH of 4.37, organic carbon content of 1.90%, cation exchange capacity(CEC) of 10.33 cmolc kg−1, base saturation of 14.81%, and a loamy sand texture. LMU 2 had a pH of 5.31, organic carboncontent of 0.47%,CEC of 11.04 cmolc kg−1, base saturation of 25.67%, and a sandy loam texture. The source assessment classified LMU 1 as not suitable (N) and LMU 2 as marginally suitable (S3). The findings should be interpreted as a preliminary assessment of two selected sites rather than as a definitive district-wide land suitability evaluation. These findings also identify priorities for further field verification and provide a cautious evidence base for subsequent land-use planning and site-specific management decisions concerning avocado cultivation in Naman Teran District.
Assessment of heavy metal contamination in soil and native vegetation at municipal solid waste dumpsites in Akure North, NigeriaCORE4
Authors: O. Aluko, O. O. Obaseki, Stephen Oluwaseun Ibikunle
Venue: Discover Chemistry
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study evaluated heavy metal (Zn, Fe, Cu, Cd, Pb) concentrations and physicochemical composition across four dumpsites in Akure North Local Government and compared them to those of the WHO and the National Environmental Standard and Regulation Enforcement Agency (NESREA) standards. Two soil samples (0-15 cm and 15-30 cm) and two plant samples were analysed alongside controls to assess pollution indices and metal bioaccumulation. Control samples were taken 1 km away. Heavy metal levels in dumpsites significantly exceeded control sites and regulatory limits, reaching up to 55,850 mg/kg in soil and 49,050 mg/kg in plants. While multiple pollution indices confirmed severe environmental contamination, bioaccumulation patterns varied: iron exhibited the highest soil concentration but the lowest plant transfer, whereas cadmium showed the highest transfer coefficient. Furthermore, dumpsite soils displayed higher electrical conductivity, organic matter, and acidity (pH 5.50–6.47) than controls. These findings indicate that waste disposal activities are progressively degrading soil physicochemical properties and driving heavy metal bioaccumulation in surrounding vegetation, highlighting both phytoremediation potential and associated human health risks.
Soil reclamation induced salinity decline is associated with shifts in bacterial, fungal and protistan communities and higher wheat yield in saline-alkali fieldsCORE4
Authors: Chun-Hua Jia, C. Geilfus, Jiao-Rong Du, Ying-Ying Liu, Zhao-Hui Liu...
Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil reclamation is widely practiced in saline–alkali land areas. However, limited research has focused on how reclamation duration regulates soil microbial communities and crop yield via modifying soil environmental conditions. In this study, high-throughput sequencing, ecological network analysis, and soil physicochemical characterization were integrated to investigate variations in soil properties, microbial community composition, and crop yield across saline–alkali fields with different reclamation durations (1, 5, and 10 years). The results showed that prolonged reclamation significantly reduced soil salinity and increased soil nutrient contents as well as wheat yield. The richness of fungi and protists increased with the extension of reclamation durations, whereas bacterial richness exhibited an opposite trend. Network-associated key taxa of bacteria, fungi and protists were significantly correlated with wheat yield, soil salinity, soil organic matter (SOM), total nitrogen (TN), as well as the activities of leucine aminopeptidase (LAP) and β-glucosidase (Glu). Structural equation modeling (SEM) further revealed that soil salinity was associated with soil physicochemical traits, network-associated key taxa and enzyme activities, altered the soil microecological environment, and ultimately affected wheat yield. In conclusion, reclamation durations mitigated soil salinity, which drove the screening of microbial taxa by soil microhabitats and reconstructed the overall microbial community structure, thereby influencing wheat productivity. These findings provide theoretical and technical support for the sustainable reclamation of saline–alkali land, health management of farmland soil, and stable improvement of grain productivity.
Biological nitrogen enriched biochar enhances maize productivity, soil fertility and pest management in the humid tropical zone of Buea and the sub humid western highlands of Mbouda, CameroonCORE3
Authors: C. Tanyi, D. Agbor, C. Dzoyem, N. J. Okolle
Venue: Discover Soil
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Integrated sustainable crop productivity and pest management remain a pervasive modern-day agricultural challenge. The deployment of conventional approaches has jeopardised environmental sustainability with adverse effects including loss of long-term soil and plant productivity necessitating sustainable alternatives. Biological Nitrogen Enriched Biochar (BioNBiochar), a unique constituent of biochar and black soldier fly frass (BSF), offers an eco-friendly alternative to enhance maize yield and mitigate pest infestations across agroecological zones (AEZ) in Cameroon. The randomized complete block design field trials conducted across humid tropical (Buea) and sub-humid (Mbouda) AEZs investigated the effect of treatments: BioNBiochar, BSF frass, NPK, Poultry manure and control on soil fertility, maize yield and pest dynamics. Across the two AEZs, BioNBiochar consistently outperformed BSF frass, poultry manure, NPK fertiliser and the control, exhibiting significantly higher organic carbon (OC) (4.3%), soil pH, total nitrogen, exchangeable potassium and available phosphorus than all other treatments. OC also varied with agroecological zone, being significantly higher in Buea (3.5%) than in Mbouda (3.1%), and the AEZ × treatment interaction was significant, with the highest OC observed for the Buea × BioNBiochar combination. Maize yield was significantly higher under BioNBiochar (7.3 t/ha) than under all other treatments across both AEZs, with the lowest yield observed under the control (4.5 t/ha); yield also differed modestly by AEZ, being higher in Mbouda (5.8 t/ha) than in Buea (5.6 t/ha). Fall armyworm and stem borer infestation and their combined damage severity were lowest on plots amended with BioNBiochar and highest in the control across both AEZs, while pest pressure was somewhat greater in Mbouda than in Buea (fall armyworm: 2/plant; stem borer: 2/plant; damage severity: 3.6). The consistent performance of BioNBiochar across contrasting agroecological environments and better than other treatments demonstrates its potential as a sustainable soil amendment for promoting integrated pest management (IPM) and resilience in agroecosystems.
Trace Element Transfer and Distribution in the Rock-Soil-Plant SystemCORE3
Authors: Raquel Caggiano, Leticia Chiglino, C. Gaucher
Venue: Agrociencia Uruguay
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
The study of systems associated with mining activities is essential to understand the transfer of trace elements from the lithosphere to the biota. This research evaluated the origin and availability of metals in the Mina La Oriental area, in Uruguay. Rock and soil samples were collected from waste rock dumps and control sites, alongside specimens of Plantago brasiliensis, Nierembergia ericoides, and Blepharocalyx salicifolius. Results suggest that the high concentration of trace elements in soils derives from materials accumulated due to base metal mining (Cu, Pb, Zn), showing a heterogeneous distribution where Pb exhibits a lithogenic dominance associated with these waste structures. Extreme enrichment factors (EF > 40) were recorded throughout the area, exceeding the natural geochemical baseline even in locations distant from the dumps. Regarding vegetation, metal transfer was regulated by species identity: P. brasiliensis acted as an Al and Pb accumulator, whereas B. salicifolius exhibited an excluder behavior driven by effective root barriers. Meanwhile, N. ericoides showed intermediate transfer levels dependent on soil availability. Finally, cation exchange capacity, pH, and organic matter content were determined to be the primary geochemical controls of metal mobility. These findings allow for the characterization of retention mechanisms in a mining-impacted system, providing key data for the management of environmental liabilities.
A Subsoiling–Aluminum Sulfate Spraying System for Whole–Plow–Layer Improvement of Soda Saline–Alkali LandCORE3

Abstract
Soil degradation caused by soil salinization is an important factor affecting global food security. The soda saline–alkali soils in the Songnen Plain suffer from poor soil structure, hard topsoil and alkalized horizons, and high pH and Na+ concentrations, which pose a major threat to sustainable agricultural development. However, the development of demand–adapted agricultural machinery and agronomic technologies integrating physical and chemical amelioration remains a challenge for soda saline–alkali soil improvement under rain–fed dryland conditions. Based on the water solubility of aluminum sulfate, this study integrated novel equipment and technologies for efficient, engineering–oriented amelioration of soda saline–alkali soils. Laboratory incubation experiments with aluminum sulfate showed that an application rate of 0.6% soil dry weight was a suitable addition level for ameliorating soil pH, SAR, and ESP. Verification tests demonstrated that the soil disturbance coefficient, soil loosening degree, and coefficient of variation for liquid application uniformity among rows were 53.75%, 13.50%, and 4.83%, respectively; these operational performance indicators met the requirements of relevant industry standards. Comparisons of soil physicochemical properties under different amelioration treatments revealed that subsoiling combined with ameliorant spraying (SAS) outperformed subsoiling alone (S), surface ameliorant application (A), and the control (CK). Relative to CK, the SAS treatment increased soil water infiltration rate (WIR) by an average of 63.11%, reduced bulk density (BD) by 0.14 g cm−3, decreased pH by 0.86 units, lowered soil salt content (SC) by 0.71 g kg−1, decreased the exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP) by 7.61%, and decreased the sodium adsorption ratio (SAR) by 4.01 (mmolc L−1)1/2. Field trials on alfalfa cultivation demonstrated that SAS improved the alfalfa seedling emergence rate, dry matter yield and overwintering rate, with increases of 20.59%, 26.06%, and 20.29%, respectively, relative to CK. This study provides equipment and technical references for the comprehensive improvement and utilization of soda saline–alkali land.

Abstract
Background: Artisanal petroleum refining contaminates soils and may constrain their ecological recovery. Aim: This study assessed the effectiveness of composted agricultural waste and NPK fertiliser in reducing hydrocarbon contamination in soils affected by artisanal refining activities in Obi-Ayagha community, Delta State, Nigeria. Study Design: An experimental study was conducted to evaluate the effects of organic and inorganic amendments on the remediation of hydrocarbon-contaminated soils, using untreated soil as the control. Place and Duration of Study: The study was conducted in Obi Ayagha community, Ughelli South Local Government Area, Delta State, Nigeria, over a period of nine weeks (63 days). Methodology: Contaminated soil samples were treated with composted agricultural waste and NPK fertiliser at different amendment levels, while untreated soil served as the control. Soil pH, moisture content, electrical conductivity (EC), nitrate concentration, nutrient composition, and microbial populations were monitored at three-week intervals. Total petroleum hydrocarbon (TPH) concentrations were also measured throughout the study to determine the effectiveness of the treatments. Results: The amended soils showed greater reductions in TPH concentrations than the untreated control. The 25% contaminated soil plus compost treatment recorded the lowest final TPH concentration of 128.10 ± 10.09 mg/kg, while the 25% contaminated soil plus NPK treatment recorded 182.00 ± 25.06 mg/kg at Week 9. Other amended treatments also showed considerable reductions, whereas TPH concentrations remained relatively high in the control. Soil pH gradually increased towards neutral or slightly alkaline conditions, while electrical conductivity rose sharply by Week 3. Nitrate concentrations generally increased across treatments, suggesting changes in nutrient availability that may have supported microbial activity. Conclusion: The findings show that composted agricultural waste and NPK fertiliser can improve the remediation of hydrocarbon-contaminated soils, with compost alone producing the best result among the reported treatments. This approach offers a potentially affordable and environmentally friendly means of restoring contaminated land. Further research is needed to assess its long-term effectiveness under field conditions.

Abstract
Conversion of Amazonian forests to pastures and crops threatens soil fertility, but whether tree-based land uses conserve it remains poorly quantified in the Ecuadorian Amazon. We compared a secondary forest (SF, reference) with a cacao agroforestry system (AFC), two silvopastoral systems (Axonopus scoparius and Paspalum dilatatum pastures with trees; GGWT, DGWT) and the same pastures without trees (GGWIT, DGWIT) in Napo and Pastaza provinces. Each land use comprised three plots (5 – 7 sampling points each) sampled at 0 – 10 and 10 – 30 cm (184 samples). Land-use effects were tested with linear mixed models with plot as a random effect. In the topsoil, land use affected aeration porosity (AP; 11.4 % in AFC to 19.2 % in SF), soil organic matter (SOM; 8.5 % in GGWIT to 17.9 % in GGWT) and exchangeable K+ (0.09 cmolc/kg in AFC vs. 0.22 – 0.28 cmolc/kg in GGWT, DGWT and DGWIT), whereas bulk density, pH, total N, available P, Ca2+ and Mg2+ did not differ significantly. Relative to SF, AP decreased by 15 % – 41 % in all land uses, SOM by 47 % in GGWIT and K+ by 43 % in AFC. Most nutrients were concentrated in the topsoil. Stocks in the 0 – 30 cm layer of exchangeable K+ (53 – 102 kg/ha) and, except in DGWT, of available P (6.5 – 22.2 kg/ha) were below forest-maintenance thresholds, whereas NH4+ stocks were highest in treeless pastures and lowest in AFC. Multivariate analyses isolated AFC and grouped the silvopastoral systems with SF. Retaining trees in pastures and replenishing K and P in cacao agroforestry systems are recommended.
Growing Media Effects on Low-Altitude Adaptation of Geranium psilostemon Ledeb.: Growth, Leaf Chlorophyll Content, and ANN ModelingECO1
Authors: Türker Oğuztürk, Cem Alparslan, E. Ekren, Emircan Ersayin, Gulcay Ercan-Oguzturk
Venue: Life
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Geranium psilostemon Ledeb. is a rhizomatous perennial whose cultivation performance under low-altitude conditions requires further investigation. This study evaluated survival, vegetative growth and meter-estimated total leaf chlorophyll content across five growing media in a single experiment conducted in an open-top section of a greenhouse facility. Rhizomes collected from a natural clump at 1375 m elevation in Maçka, Trabzon, Türkiye, were cultivated at 53 m elevation in peat, cocopeat, river silt, forest soil and an equal-volume mixture of these materials, with 30 individually potted rhizomes per treatment. Following approximately one year of cultivation, seven measurements were conducted between May and August 2024. Plant height, crown diameter, leaf number and chlorophyll content were recorded alongside substrate temperature, moisture, pH and electrical conductivity. Data were examined using exploratory ANOVA, post hoc comparisons, correlation analysis and artificial neural network (ANN) modeling. River silt and peat supported the highest survival percentages (83.3% and 80.0%, respectively), whereas survival in cocopeat was 10.0%. Peat produced the highest mean plant height and crown diameter, while river silt and peat had the highest mean leaf chlorophyll content. Chlorophyll content showed weak negative associations with substrate temperature (20.8–45.9 °C) and pH (5.30–10.20), and a weak positive association with electrical conductivity (0.00–0.31 mS cm−1). ANN modeling showed limited-to-moderate discrimination for survival status (ROC-AUC = 0.6653) and limited predictive performance for continuous traits, with plant height yielding the highest R2 (0.3192). These findings provide an initial basis for growing-media selection for low-altitude cultivation of G. psilostemon, with further independent trials needed to evaluate their consistency.
Soil Erosion and Carbon Cycling in Farmland Under Climate Change: A Bibliometric Analysis and Research ProgressECO1
Authors: Yan-Ran Tian, Jia-Rong Hou, Tong-De Chen, Bo-Xin Zeng, Bo Yang
Venue: Land
Published: 2026-11-12
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Climate change may alter the coupling between farmland soil erosion and soil carbon cycling, yet the evolution of research themes and the net carbon consequences of erosion remain unclear. We combined bibliometric analysis with a critical literature synthesis of 653 publications indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection (1993–2025), using CiteSpace and VOSviewer, with keyword analyses focusing on 2016–2025. Publication output increased after 2010, particularly since 2020. Eight keyword clusters were identified, encompassing land-use change, soil organic carbon, soil erosion, nitrous oxide, remote sensing, climate change mitigation, soil hydrology, and carbon emissions. Keyword bursts for “stabilization” and “productivity” persisted through 2025. The literature synthesis suggests that extreme rainfall, drought–rainfall sequences, and freeze–thaw processes can modify erosion thresholds and sediment connectivity, influencing the selective transport and redistribution of soil organic carbon. Eroded carbon may be buried and stabilized or re-exposed and mineralized; thus, local carbon losses or depositional gains do not necessarily indicate net carbon emissions or sequestration. Divergent findings partly reflect differences in accounting boundaries, observation periods, carbon fractions, and methodological approaches. We propose a conceptual framework linking climate-driven erosion, carbon redistribution, stabilization–mineralization processes, and agricultural management feedbacks. Future research should integrate event-scale observations, source-to-sink carbon budgets, greenhouse gas assessments, and crop productivity to validate these proposed linkages.
