Anchor3DGS: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting with Compact Anchor-Based RepresentationCORE11
Authors: Sheng Ye, Jiang-Ke Lin, Fu-Dong Wang, Yi Yuan, Zhen-Hui Dong...
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-10-15
Abs
Abstract
Recent feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods have enabled effective multi-view 3D reconstruction by predicting Gaussian primitives in a single forward pass. However, existing approaches predominantly adopt a pixel-aligned paradigm that predicts one Gaussian per input pixel, coupling the Gaussian count to the input resolution and number of views. This also leads to redundant primitives in textureless regions and insufficient coverage in geometrically complex areas. We present Anchor3DGS, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that replaces pixel-aligned prediction with a compact anchor-based representation. Guided by information entropy, our method places a set of 3D anchors in the scene, concentrating them in regions of higher visual complexity while maintaining broad spatial coverage. Each anchor performs occlusion-aware aggregation of multi-view image features, exchanges information with other anchors, and is decoded into a set of local Gaussians. A lightweight feature enhancement module further refines the coarse RGB output from Gaussian splatting and improves rendering fidelity. Extensive experiments on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that our Anchor3DGS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis quality while only using an order of magnitude fewer Gaussian primitives.

Venue: Information Fusion

Abs
Abstract
Three dimensional modeling for autonomous driving must continuously estimate road geometry, obstacle occupancy, traffic participant behavior, and observational uncertainty under limited computational resources. Existing reconstruction methods can synthesize realistic novel views, but visual fidelity does not sufficiently characterize the geometric reliability required for driving. Generative world models can expand scenarios and predict future observations, yet may introduce content unsupported by measurements. We propose DriveWorld3D, a framework combining temporal video learning, measurement constraints from multiple sensors, and offline generative simulation. Its online component maintains a unified world state comprising road surfaces, volumetric occupancy, dynamic entities, and road relations, while separately representing physical occupancy, observability, and evidence provenance. Action conditioned future prediction and risk sensitive planning connect this representation to driving decisions. Its offline component reconstructs recorded scenes using neural radiance fields or 3D Gaussian Splatting, then expands long tail conditions using generative models while restricting the use of generated content as geometric ground truth. We specify data partitioning, sensor ablations, fault injection, paired closed loop testing, and real to simulation consistency assessment. The central hypothesis is that explicit evidence management can reduce dangerous false free space predictions and occlusion risk within a matched computational budget while retaining acceptable driving efficiency. All performance claims remain subject to experimental validation. Abstract
High-fidelity 4D physical simulation requires accurate physical parameter assignments that align with diverse material properties. Previous methods struggle with critical bottlenecks: they either rely on computationally intensive per-scene optimization or predict parameters from purely visual inputs. The latter suffers from inherent ambiguity, e.g., the inability to distinguish the stiffness of rubber from its appearance alone. To bridge this gap, we propose AutoPhyX, a text-conditioned framework for predicting spatially-varying physical properties. Since training robust feed-forward models requires fine-grained supervision, we first introduce a novel part-controllable data generation pipeline that decomposes complex 3D assets into semantically distinct components and pairs them with physically plausible, diverse parameters and corresponding text descriptions. By formulating property prediction within a voxel field, AutoPhyX ensures compatibility with diverse 3D representations, including meshes, point clouds, Gaussian Splatting, and NeRF. Experiments demonstrate that our method enables physically plausible, text-driven parameter assignment in a single forward pass. It achieves high accuracy and robust generalization to in-the-wild objects, paving the way for future physics-intensive embodied and robotic applications.

Abstract
Video generation and feed-forward 3D reconstruction typically use separate representations, requiring RGB decoding and visual re-encoding to recover geometry from generated latents. We propose Latent Bridge, which connects video generative latent spaces with geometric feature spaces to directly predict 3D geometry from video latents. We develop representation-specific bridges for Wan2.1 and V-RAE, enabling feed-forward prediction of cameras, depth, and 3D point clouds without the intermediate RGB pathway. Each bridge is trained on RGB videos through feature distillation, while the video encoder and geometry model remain frozen. Abstract
Feed-forward transformer models have recently achieved strong performance in multi-view 3D reconstruction, but they still rely primarily on appearance-based attention and often struggle in dynamic scenes with weak geometric cues. In this work, we introduce a motion- and gravity-aware framework under the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm. CrossFit-GS: Out-of-Fold Pose Estimation for COLMAP-Free 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE6

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Pose-free 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) aims to bypass cumbersome Structure-from-Motion (SfM) by directly reconstructing scenes from unposed image sequences. In this work, we present CrossFit-GS, a deferred-commitment progressive framework that mitigates cumulative trajectory drift and breaks the self-reinforcing loop of distorted-map error propagation. Specifically, we partition the input sequence into interleaved subsets and maintain two complementary submaps. Each candidate pose is cross-refined against a frozen opposite submap that has never been optimized using the target frame, thereby providing an independent geometric reference for pose estimation. Checkerboard validation and spatial coverage safeguards further reject unreliable updates, and only verified poses are committed to their corresponding owner submap. Once the trajectory has been refined, the intermediate submaps are discarded and the final global representation is trained from scratch using the fixed refined poses. Extensive evaluations across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that CrossFit-GS substantially reduces cumulative trajectory drift and consistently improves both camera pose estimation and novel-view synthesis over existing methods.

Abstract
Panoramic 3D reconstruction enables the holistic recovery of complete 360 $^\circ$ scene geometry, offering significant benefits for robotics and AR/VR applications. However, directly estimating geometry from ERP images is inherently challenged by spherical distortions. To reduce spherical projection distortions, a common strategy is to decompose panoramas into distortion-free perspective views, but independently processing these views often introduces cross-view geometric inconsistencies. To address these challenges, we introduce GeoPano, a feed-forward framework for single-view panoramic 3D reconstruction that recovers a geometrically accurate and consistent point cloud from a single panorama. Progressive Permutation Alignment with Temperature-Controlled Spectral SharpeningCORE5
Authors: Yuta Horioka, Naoya Chiba, Yuki Uranishi
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In implicit neural representations (INRs), 3D signals are encoded directly in network weights, making stable weight-space operations essential. However, permutation symmetry causes independently trained yet functionally equivalent models to appear distant in weight space, often inducing large loss barriers under linear interpolation. Existing neuron alignment methods either commit early to discrete matches and become trapped in inconsistent local optima or rely on computationally expensive gradient optimization over continuous relaxations, both of which are unreliable in challenging cases such as NeRF. We adopt progressive alignment, a layer-wise strategy that follows inter-layer dependencies from input to output. At each layer, a correlation matrix is estimated and aligned with a discrete anchor obtained by the Hungarian algorithm, after which temperature-controlled spectral annealing gradually sharpens the correlation structure toward a permutation matrix. This update is deterministic and lightweight, avoiding the inner-loop gradient optimization required by continuous relaxation methods. A final refinement stage further optimizes the network layer-by-layer via weight matching, starting from the aligned solution. Experiments on MLPs, CNNs, and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) show that the proposed method reliably recovers neuron correspondences and achieves stable alignment across architectures. In practical experiments aligning independently trained NeRF models, our method reduces the loss barriers under weight interpolation compared with existing approaches.

DriftNeRF: Cross-Ray Density Drifting for Sparse-View Neural Radiance FieldsCORE5
Authors: Juntang Wang, Yihan Wang, Sheng'en Li, Hao Wu, Dongmian Zou...
Published: 2026-10-21
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) synthesize photorealistic novel views from dense image collections, but degrade sharply when few views are available. We attribute a specific, measurable defect to the objective itself: the rendering loss is a sum over independent rays and contains no term comparing densities that different rays assign to the same region of space, so nearby 3D points can settle on contradictory densities while every training pixel is reproduced correctly. We make this quantity explicit by measuring cross-ray density disagreement directly during training, and show that it stays high in vanilla NeRF, falls only incidentally under frequency-based regularization, and moves together with held-out quality in the sparse regime. We then close the gap with DriftNeRF, a drop-in regularizer that instantiates the drifting-field template on density samples: a masked exponential kernel aggregates densities from nearby points on other rays into a stop-gradient consensus target, and the resulting drifting field penalizes unsupported disagreement while leaving agreed-upon boundaries intact. The regularizer reads only sampled positions, ray identifiers, and predicted densities, so it requires no pretrained model, external geometry, or additional images, and transfers across field parameterizations. On NeRF-Synthetic it reduces residual disagreement by an order of magnitude, and across NeRF-Synthetic, DTU, and LLFF it improves 3-view PSNR over the strongest MLP-NeRF baseline by 0.42-0.65 dB and remains competitive with sparse-view Gaussian pipelines under a matched protocol without their depth, flow, or covisibility preprocessing.

Abstract
Deploying a trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene under shrinking compute budgets requires one primitive set to remain effective when rendered at smaller prefixes. In this continuous level of detail (LoD) setting, the challenge is to decide which Gaussians should retain capacity as the budget decreases. A pilot study on 3DGS-MCMC reconstructions shows that image complexity, computed from the input views before optimization, predicts where reconstruction error concentrates in image and 3D space. It also reveals that simple regions contain too many Gaussians and degrade as much as complex ones at lower budgets, despite being inherently cheaper to represent. Online scene change detection requires integrating incoming observations to determine which regions have changed relative to a reference scene. We update change beliefs as new images arrive using generalised Bayesian evidence fusion. Abstract
Existing feature lifting approaches for open-vocabulary segmentation rely on simple backprojection to assign features to primitives. In the case of 3D Gaussian splatting reconstruction, the primitives overlap heavily, leading to suboptimal reconstructions, governed by many primitives per ray. Backprojection, however, reduces to a least-squares solvable problem, exactly when no two primitives share a ray. Motivated by this insight, we propose OV-Foam, which uses PowerFoam as the underlying representation. This representation uses a power-diagram to partition the scene, resulting in zero overlap between primitives and a substantial reduction in primitives per ray, which ultimately simplifies the lifting problem and allows for closer-to-optimal feature assignment. We show that the space partitioning primitives of OV-Foam reach near-optimal assignment, and our experiments demonstrate improved open-vocabulary segmentation over Gaussian baselines.

Near the lunar poles, low sun elevations produce long, high-contrast shadows that challenge stereo vision, terrain segmentation, and localization. Developing perception and navigation algorithms for these conditions requires large amounts of sensor data with accurate ground truth, which field campaigns and analog facilities can only provide at high cost and with limited variety. This paper presents MARE (Modular Autonomy and Rendering Environment), an open-source simulation suite for lunar rover perception and autonomy built on Unreal Engine 5 and ROS 2. MARE consists of three integrated components. Terrain Studio generates cratered and rocky lunar Digital Elevation Maps (DEMs) to build simulation scenes. A dedicated Unreal Engine project hosts two physics-based rovers with plugins for stereo, lidar, event, and Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD) cameras, streaming time-stamped sensor data and pixel-accurate ground truth. Lastly, a Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC) stack provides web-based mission control. Validation shows a mean Intersection over Union (IoU) of 0.79 between predicted and rendered shadows at low sun elevations, and stereo depth analysis verifies decreased pixel coverage in shadowed regions relative to sunlit terrain.

Abstract
Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes from monocular video requires inferring motion from noisy and intermittently visible observations. Long-range tracks provide valuable geometric constraints, but integrating their fragmented temporal coverage into shared, continuous motion remains challenging. We introduce RayCo4D, a ray-conditioned continuous motion model for monocular 4D reconstruction that explicitly separates visual measurements from latent 3D motion states. Each track is associated with a reference-time 3D query constrained to its camera ray, and its image and depth observations constrain shared latent motion nodes through local many-to-many associations. Each node maintains an $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ pose and body-frame velocity at sparse support times. A white-noise-on-acceleration process prior couples neighboring states and defines continuous-time interpolation, allowing observations across visibility intervals to jointly constrain the same latent state sequence. The inferred shared motion is transferred to Gaussian primitives through dual-quaternion blending, while reference-anchored local $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ residuals capture fine-scale non-rigid deformation beyond the shared scaffold. Experiments on diverse monocular dynamic scenes show that RayCo4D achieves competitive novel-view synthesis quality, improves structural and temporal consistency under rapid motion and incomplete tracks, and supports continuous-time rendering.

Abstract
Accurate ground plane normal estimation is essential for odometry in spherical mobile mapping systems (SMMS), which face challenges from rapid rotations and limited ground coverage. We propose two RANSAC-based approaches: single-scan estimation with normal vector scoring and multi-step validation, and LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO)-based estimation using a global 3D map. Evaluated across diverse real-world datasets against terrestrial laser scanner ground truth, results show that LIO-based estimation significantly outperforms single-scan methods in accuracy and robustness. Integrating ground normal estimates into the motion model improves pose estimation, supporting autonomous navigation in challenging and hazardous environments.

Abstract
Artificial radio emissions have historically been a major focus of technosignature searches. Here, we apply an anthropological lens to expand this scope to surface technosignatures: non-random, intentional patterning on planetary terrain, similar to long-lasting human-made landscapes and monuments on Earth. On geologically inactive bodies such as the Moon and Mars, artificial traces and patterns could potentially remain visible for hundreds of millions of years. We discuss two recent developments that make this search timely: high-resolution imaging datasets, such as those produced by the HiRISE camera on the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO), and ML-based anomaly-detection methods. Finally, we list two possible approaches to developing a model capable of searching for these surface technosignatures: a supervised and an unsupervised one. Complemented by LiDAR, ground-penetrating radar, and reflectance spectroscopy, these methods would enable the first quantitative upper limits on surface technosignatures while yielding ancillary benefits for planetary science.

Abstract
Robust visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (vSLAM) in dynamic environments faces challenges due to the presence of moving objects that may introduce erroneous observations, corrupt the map reconstruction and overall degrade localization accuracy. Existing vSLAM approaches often rely on predefined semantic classes or costly pixel-wise classification, limiting their robustness or applicability in real-world scenarios. This paper presents CAOS-SLAM, a dynamic vSLAM framework that extends ORB-SLAM3 with a hybrid perception pipeline to detect, track, and filter both semantically known and previously unseen moving objects in real-time. On-demand panoptic segmentation is performed for object discovery, with candidate unknown objects instantiated through depth-based clustering of unclassified regions. Between panoptic updates, persistent object tracks are updated using lightweight detection when available or temporal mask propagation based on world-frame Kalman prediction, depth consistency, and appearance similarity. The estimated object motion is then used to reject dynamic features from the SLAM pipeline. Experimental results on multiple dynamic RGB-D datasets and real-world experiments demonstrate improved localization accuracy over state-of-the-art dynamic vSLAM approaches, achieving up to 59% lower localization error in scenes characterized by unknown dynamic objects and up to 88% lower error in heavily occluded scenes, while achieving a median processing rate of 30 FPS on a laptop. Furthermore, the tracking module provides estimates of object trajectories and velocities, supporting potential future applications in perception, navigation, and motion-planning tasks in dynamic environments. Code and dataset will be released upon acceptance.

Abstract
Reconstructing human activity from egocentric video requires more than recovering individual 3D components: the observer's motion, surrounding geometry, and hand-object interactions must fit together in a consistent world. Achieving this coherence requires both joint reconstruction and aligned supervision, yet existing methods and datasets offer limited support for either. We present EgoXperience, a full-stack paradigm that addresses these needs through data collection, automatic annotation, unified modeling, and benchmarking. Central to EgoXperience is an automatic annotation engine, EgoX-Engine, that couples geometrically verified object tracking with SLAM-based world alignment, yielding consistent supervision for the camera, scene, hands, and objects. This yields EgoX-Data-6M, a 50-hour dataset of task-driven manipulation and locomotion within and across indoor and outdoor environments, with second-level task captions. We carefully select one hour across these activity categories as EgoX-Bench to evaluate joint reconstruction. To translate this supervision into reconstruction, we introduce EgoX, trained with EgoX-Data-6M and complementary annotations from additional public datasets. Given monocular egocentric video and first-frame object masks, our model achieves joint metric reconstruction of the camera, scene, hands, and objects in a shared world coordinate frame with a single forward pass. Extensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across scene estimation and world-space hand-object pose recovery. Against the strongest evaluated baselines, EgoX reduces camera, hand, and depth errors by 68.9%, 65.6%, and 42.9%, and improves object pose scores by 28.0%.

Abstract
Wheel kinematics can support odometry for quadrupedal wheel-legged robots when LiDAR geometry provides weak motion constraints. However, slip and contact loss make rolling predictions unreliable, and contact state alone cannot determine their reliability across motion directions. We propose a tightly coupled LiDAR-inertial-kinematic odometry framework that retains analytical rolling predictions and learns per-wheel, direction-dependent process covariances from proprioceptive history. The estimator maintains moving wheel-center states, allowing the learned uncertainty to regulate the influence of rolling predictions through forward-kinematic corrections. Degeneracy-aware projection (DAP) complements this mechanism by suppressing LiDAR constraints along geometrically weak directions. The learned covariance network (CovNet) is trained through a differentiable filter using simulated velocity supervision and deployed on the real robot without fine-tuning. On the six room sequences with the highest degeneracy ratios in our indoor dataset, the method reduces mean per-sequence position root mean square error (RMSE) by 66.2% relative to FAST-LIO2 and by 31.0% relative to a fixed-covariance variant. In a severely degenerate corridor run, it achieves an endpoint position error of 0.198 m, compared with 7.531 m for FAST-LIO2. These results demonstrate robust odometry under challenging geometric and wheel-motion conditions. We plan to publicly release our code and multimodal dataset.
