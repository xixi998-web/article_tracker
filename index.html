GEMOR-GS: Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAMCORE11
Authors: Yuchen Yang, Ming Ding, Xinyu Wu, Hesheng Wang
Published: 2026-11-10
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Photorealistic and metrically consistent world representations are a primary requirement for LiDAR--inertial--visual SLAM systems that serve robots over continuous operation. 3D Gaussian splatting makes such representations attainable by supplying an explicit, renderable, and queryable scene model. We present GEMOR-GS (Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAM) to make the Gaussian map geometrically accurate, globally refined, and re-enterable, providing a better appearance for robot perception and decision-making. To improve the 3DGS map, the LiDAR--camera extrinsic is first estimated offline by direct-method alignment over dedicated calibration segments and reused by all following online and offline processes. Under the calibrated geometry, keyframe poses and Gaussian parameters are refined alternately under a gated differentiable-rendering and LiDAR--projection objective. The final global reconstruction is then rebuilt from the refined keyframe schema with all online constraints lifted. For the robot SLAM lifecycle, the method recovers the pose from the 3DGS map after tracking loss through an XFeat bypass map, RANSAC-PnP coarse localization, and Gaussian RGB-D warp refinement. Experiments on one self-collected and five public sequences quantify each element: the offline reconstruction wins all paired keyframes against the online map by 14.3--19.9 dB, improves PSNR by approximately 9 dB over Gaussian-LIC2, and achieves 86--100% held-out relocalization success tied to extrinsic quality. The results show that GEMOR-GS improves the geometric accuracy, appearance fidelity, and re-enterability of the 3DGS map, serving as a dependable robot world model for SLAM and downstream tasks.
DepthGS: Depth-Guided Gaussian Splatting with Adaptive Regularization for High-Quality Surface ReconstructionCORE9
Authors: Xiao Ren, Yu Liu, Ning An, Jian Cheng, Xin Qiao...
Published: 2026-11-10
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as an efficient representation for novel view synthesis, offering fast differentiable rendering and high visual fidelity, and has increasingly been extended to surface reconstruction. However, the relationship between depth information and reconstruction error remains insufficiently explored, limiting the principled design of geometric optimization strategies. To fill the above gap, we propose DepthGS, a depth-guided Gaussian splatting framework. We derive theoretical relations between depth discrepancies and reconstruction error under single-view and multi-view settings, and rigorously establish corresponding upper bounds on reconstruction error. The above analysis informs the design of adaptive regularization strategies in both single-view and multi-view optimization. More specifically, for single-view optimization, we propose a depth-difference-guided adaptive normal regularization strategy that modulates the normal constraint strength using a geometric reliability weight computed from local depth differences. For multi-view optimization, we introduce a cross-view depth consistency regularization strategy that promotes consistency between neighboring-view depth maps and the corresponding depth predictions obtained by transforming the current view's depth predictions into those neighboring views. Extensive experiments on DTU, Mip-NeRF360, and Tanks and Temples demonstrate that DepthGS achieves competitive performance in both surface reconstruction and novel view synthesis.

Abstract
This study presents a computational intelligence-based method for robust detection and reconstruction of staircases in complex environments. The proposed framework combines point cloud processing with deep learning-based object detection to address challenges related to lighting variability, occlusion, and computational cost. A central component is the generation of Orthogonal Grayscale (OGS) images — 2.5D elevation maps encoded as grayscale images — which preserve spatial structure while enabling the application of efficient 2D convolutional neural networks. A customized YOLOv5-OBB model is trained on a newly collected dataset to localize and classify staircases under various conditions, including complete darkness. The detected regions are then analyzed using grayscale adaptive histograms to estimate stair height and count, followed by 3D reconstruction through bounding volume methods. Experiments demonstrate detection accuracy of 98.8% for upstairs and 94.5% for downstairs scenarios, with real-time inference performance. The integration of neural vision, elevation encoding, and spatial reconstruction exemplifies a hybrid computational intelligence system applicable to robotics, assistive navigation, and autonomous inspection tasks.

Abstract
Internet photos of a scene differ in exposure, illumination and transient objects such as passers-by, so one set of Gaussian colours cannot explain all photos (the in-the-wild setting). Recent in-the-wild Gaussian Splatting methods mostly report hours of per-scene optimization. We present FastWild-GS, which describes each photo by a 128-dimensional Photo Appearance Token and a per-channel transform, a scale and an offset applied to the rendered image (six parameters); a small MLP predicts the colour of each Gaussian from this token and a per-Gaussian feature, replacing the spherical-harmonics colour. The same transform is applied to the renders that score Gaussians for densification and pruning, so appearance differences between photos are not counted as reconstruction error. On PhotoTourism, FastWild-GS trains in 8.8 minutes per scene with the lowest LPIPS among the compared methods, 23x faster than AsymGS, the next best in LPIPS. On NeRF-OSR, it has the best average PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS among the compared methods in 9.7 minutes per scene.
RF3DGS: Reflection-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting with Self-PriorsCORE5
Authors: Qiang Wen, Guotao Meng, Junhong Ruan, Hongyu Liu, Chenyang Lei...
Published: 2026-11-10
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D reflection removal is crucial in applications demanding high-fidelity visualization, such as museum exhibitions. In this work, we propose Reflection-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting (RF3DGS), a novel method for superior reflection removal. RF3DGS exclusively leverages a widely-adopted photometric prior for reflection removal: that the minimum pixel intensity across multi-view images corresponding to the same 3D point strongly correlates with reflection-free information. However, generalizing this principle to a 3D Gaussian Splatting within a multi-view context is non-trivial. To address this, RF3DGS first optimizes a vanilla 3DGS model using a geometric prior to estimate a coarse, reflection-free depth map for each view. Leveraging these depth maps and photometric prior, a 3D warping process aggregates reflection-free information from auxiliary views to construct a clean transmission pseudo-label for a given reference view. This pseudo-label provides a robust self-supervised signal for optimization. We further introduce an Adaptive Twin Rendering (ATR) loss to synergistically utilize this pseudo-label while preserving high-frequency details from the original, reflection-degraded images. By adeptly fusing these internal 3D self-priors with the ATR loss, RF3DGS demonstrates state-of-the-art reflection removal performance across both synthetic and real-world scenarios. Furthermore, we establish the first 3D reflection removal benchmark, based on the Unity rendering engine, for rigorous quantitative evaluation.

Abstract
We introduce two fundamental advances to the NanoVDB data structure: dynamic GPU-native topological updates, and a voxel-sequential access paradigm that dramatically accelerates point-wise and stencil-based operators on sparse grids. NanoVDB's linearized memory layout has delivered strong GPU performance, and the Index-Grid concept introduced in the fVDB framework [Williams et al. 2024] provided topology/data separation and optimal compactness of storage in sparse machine learning workloads. However, all these prior iterations of NanoVDB assume a static topology that has precluded use of this data structure in simulation and dynamic geometric processes. We eliminate this limitation by introducing a suite of dynamic topology operators - morphological dilation, grid merging, pruning, and cross-resolution coarsening and refinement. These operators use bit-parallel algorithms to reconstruct grids containing hundreds of millions of voxels in just a few milliseconds. We also introduce a voxel-sequential access paradigm and data structure for GPU kernels operating on sparse data. We stream active voxels as a flat linear sequence, minimizing or eliminating the need for tree traversal in the process. This achieves compute density and regularity that allow even complex stencil-based operators to approach peak memory bandwidth, with minimal storage overhead. Together, these contributions remove the read-only constraint from NanoVDB, establishing it as a high-performance primary representation for dynamic GPU simulation and geometry manipulation. We evaluate the efficacy of our newly introduced operators in benchmarks sourced from applications such as 3D reconstruction, fluid simulation, and large-scale mesh voxelization.

Abstract
In minimally invasive vascular interventions, intraoperative 3D vascular reconstruction and accurate spatial characterization are crucial for safe and precise robotic surgical navigation. However, unlike conventional supervised learning tasks that rely on a fixed training/testing split, projection-based neural reconstruction aims to establish a patient-specific mapping between sparse X-ray observations and the corresponding 3D spatial representation. Abstract
Feed-forward 3D foundation models recover geometry at high fidelity, yet inference memory constraints keep them from processing long sequences natively. Current streaming systems work around this limit with sliding-window chunking, which introduces severe monocular scale ambiguity. Resolving this ambiguity requires Sim(3) registration that stays accurate and efficient under outlier contamination. We introduce Sim(3)-TurboReg, which reformulates the compatibility graph construction of TurboReg by replacing absolute distance constraints with a strict Tri-Scale Consistency (TSC) criterion. Abstract
Real-time robotic perception requires accurate LiDAR semantics under limited resources. Bird's-eye-view (BEV) aggregation is efficient but can mix differently labeled surfaces in columns. We present HiPFuse, a point-BEV fusion framework that exchanges point and height-partitioned BEV features across horizontal scales, combining point-wise detail with 2D spatial context from one LiDAR scan. Fixed height assignments preserve separate vertical aggregation groups, with auxiliary supervision of initial BEV features using targets from the same 3D cells. A dual-scale BEV context module adds broader context from a coarser grid. Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
2D Gaussian representations provide an explicit and efficient way to represent images, but their rate-distortion performance under limited budgets depends critically on how Gaussian capacity and parameter precision are allocated. Existing methods typically optimize these two resources separately, overlooking content-dependent capacity demands and heterogeneous quantization sensitivity. We present CP-GS, a capacity-precision coupled Gaussian splatting framework for budget-aware 2D Gaussian image representation that jointly optimizes capacity allocation and attribute precision under a shared rate constraint. CP-GS first constructs a content-adaptive Gaussian representation by dynamically reallocating a fixed Gaussian budget from redundant regions to areas with higher representation demand. It then introduces hierarchical distortion-aware representation quantization, using efficient rendering-based calibration to allocate precision across Gaussian attributes and further refine it within heterogeneous attribute groups. Finally, it jointly optimizes Gaussian capacity and attribute precision by constructing candidate representations with different Gaussian counts and precision configurations and selecting operating points according to decoded distortion and actual serialized size. Experiments on DIV2K and Kodak show that CP-GS achieves improved rate-distortion performance over representative 2D Gaussian and implicit neural representations across a range of budgets, while maintaining a fixed overall representation budget and low optimization cost. These results demonstrate the benefit of jointly optimizing spatial capacity and parameter precision for compact Gaussian image representation.

Abstract
Learning representations from multimodal observations is difficult in reinforcement learning with limited interaction data. Multimodal Joint Predictive Representation combines multimodal latent prediction with contrastive learning across modalities, but its recursive rollout depends on earlier predictions and its pairwise alignment focuses on correspondence between modalities. We propose Improved Predictive Representation, which combines prediction from action prefixes with alignment at the observed anchor based on Contrastive Multimodal learning. At each horizon, it predicts future representations directly from the observed anchor and the corresponding sequence of actions. We evaluate two crop configurations: IPR-S with a minimum retained crop area of 10%, and IPR-H with 50%. In Webots, both IPR configurations achieve higher mean final returns than the baseline without LiDAR, but not when LiDAR is available.

Abstract
Laser scanning has been utilized for decades to measure tree structure and geometry. This study investigates the main sources of these errors in mobile laser scanning (MLS) and quantifies their effects on stem curve (stem diameter) estimation. Abstract
Electrical Impedance Tomography (EIT) is a non-invasive and low-cost imaging modality with increasing potential for nondestructive evaluation (NDE) of structural discontinuities in conductive media. However, due to the inherently diffusive nature of the inverse problem, realistic assessment of defect detectability requires physics-consistent forward modeling and quantitative validation. In this study, a finite element method (FEM)-based framework under the Complete Electrode Model (CEM) is employed to investigate the detectability of narrow structural discontinuities and hollow inclusion using both two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) EIT reconstructions. A 16-electrode configuration was implemented, yielding 120 independent voltage measurements per frame in a conductive saline phantom. Conductivity distributions were reconstructed using Gauss-Newton optimization with Tikhonov Regularization. Performance was quantitatively evaluated using contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR), peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR). structural similarity index (SSIM), area deviation, and computational time. Results indicate that defect detectability strongly depends on conductivity contrast and geometric size. Three-dimensional reconstructions demonstrate improved volumetric localization and reduced boundary artifacts compared to 2D models, while 2D reconstruction offers significantly lower computational time & cost suitable for rapid screening applications. The results also indicate that three-dimensional EIT reconstructions significantly improve the localization of narrow structural gaps and hollow inclusions compared to two-dimensional reconstructions, particularly in conductive media such as saline considering both error margins and the visibility quality. Hollow inclusion was successfully identified in both 2D and 3D models, with 3D reconstructions providing improved internal cavity delineation. These findings provide a quantitative assessment of the trade-offs between reconstruction fidelity and computational efficiency in EIT-based structural inspection, supporting its potential application in structural health monitoring, material characterization, and conductive-environment NDE systems.

Abstract
Aiming at the instability of 3D object detection caused by sparse radar point clouds and illumination-susceptible image features in complex traffic scenarios, this paper constructs an intelligent perception algorithm system based on radar-vision collaboration. Taking hybrid radar point generation, distributed point feature encoding and dual-synchronized BEV fusion as its core computational pipeline, the system operates as follows. First, image semantics are utilized to constrain radar foreground regions, and enhanced point clouds are generated via Gaussian-uniform hybrid sampling.
