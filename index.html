Synergistic drivers of microbial carbon, nitrogen fixation and phosphorus mobilization in mining soils by integrating machine learning with causal analysis.CORE5

Abstract
Microbial carbon (C), nitrogen (N) fixation, and phosphorus (P) mobilization are key processes in ecosystem nutrient cycling. However, the key environmental factors driving these microbial functions in heavy metal-contaminated soils of mining areas remain poorly understood. In this study, we employed machine learning and causal analysis models to systematically identify the primary environmental drivers governing the relative abundances of key functional genes associated with C, N fixation and P mobilization in 48 metal mining areas across China. The results revealed that Fe, C/N ratio, and soil pH exert dominant controls on microbial functional genes. Among them, Fe was identified as a shared environmental driver across all functional groups, while C/N balance and soil pH exhibited consistent direct or indirect regulatory effects on microbial functional potential. Furthermore, machine learning-based threshold analysis revealed distinct ecological regulation windows in which microbial functional potential remained at relatively high levels, including Fe contents of 9-30%, C/N ratios of 20.47-50.62, and soil pH values of 5.8-6.30. This study elucidates the key environmental factors shaping microbial functions in mining-affected ecosystems and provides quantitative insights to support precision regulation in ecological restoration of contaminated soils.

Abstract
Gold mining removes vegetation and topsoil, often leaving mercury (Hg) in the soil. However, the recovery of soil insects on mined land is poorly documented because soil macrofauna are usually analyzed as a single group. This study examined soil-dwelling insects along a time-since-mining gradient in an artisanal gold mining landscape in Dharmasraya Regency, West Sumatra, Indonesia, and related them to soil chemical properties. Four land-use conditions on Ultisols were compared from January to March 2026: active mining, 5-year and 10-year ex-mining land, and secondary forest. Insects were hand-sorted from three 30 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm monoliths per condition and identified to family level, and soil pH, organic carbon, total nitrogen, C/N ratio, and Hg were analyzed. In total, 51 insects from 11 families and five orders were found, none of them on active mining land. The number of families rose from 4 to 8 and 11, and the Shannon-Wiener index from 1.27 to 1.81 and 2.24, at 5 years, 10 years, and in the forest. Ants dominated recovering sites, but their share fell from 44.4% to 25.9%. Diversity correlated positively with organic carbon (r = 0.98) and negatively with Hg (r = −0.97), although these exploratory results rest on four land uses. Family-level insect indicators may support monitoring of rehabilitation on gold-mined land.

Authors: Jiang-Xia Luo, Shi-Jie Dong, M. Yuan, Wei-Luan Wen, Yu-Ang Li...
Venue: Journal of hazardous materials

Abstract
Soils are important reservoirs of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs), yet the ecological differentiation and host associations of grassland resistomes remain poorly understood. We investigated topsoil resistomes across desert steppe, typical steppe, and meadow steppe of the Mongolian Plateau using short- and long-read metagenomics. The resistome comprised 28 ARG types and 819 subtypes, dominated by rifamycin, vancomycin, and multidrug resistance genes. ARG abundance increased from desert steppe to meadow steppe. Soil pH, total organic carbon, total nitrogen, and climatic variables were strongly associated with resistome turnover, whereas grazing showed weaker associations. Mobile genetic element (MGE) abundance correlated positively with ARG abundance, but only 3.37% of ARG-carrying contigs contained detectable MGEs. An operational multi-indicator framework distinguished putative intrinsic and putative acquired ARGs with contrasting host and environmental associations. Both fractions were dominated by Actinomycetota, although Pseudomonadota contributed relatively more to putative acquired ARGs. Putative intrinsic ARGs were associated mainly with nutrient-related soil variables, whereas putative acquired ARGs showed stronger climatic and edaphic associations. Long-read assemblies markedly reduced the unclassified fraction. Among 195 ARG-carrying metagenome-assembled genomes, 65.6% represented reference-poor or previously uncultured lineages. These findings reveal spatially structured and heterogeneous grassland resistomes linked to ARG composition, host affiliation, and genomic context.

Abstract
Agricultural intensification in peri-urban belts of central India has raised concerns about the concurrent presence of physicochemical soil degradation and low-level pesticide residues in leafy vegetables that are consumed with minimal processing. The present study characterises a composite agricultural soil (0–15 cm) and seven soil-cultivated leafy vegetable species -- Amaranthus viridis, Trigonella foenum-graecum L., Corchorus olitorius L., Coriandrum sativum, Mentha arvensis, Cicer arietinum and Spinacia oleracea L. -- collected from a single agricultural field, and screens both matrices for baseline residues of 3,5,6-trichloro-2-pyridinol (TCP), the principal soil metabolite of the herbicide triclopyr, and 4-chlorophenoxyacetic acid (4-CPA), a chlorophenoxy-class plant growth regulator with herbicidal application at elevated dose. Soil texture (45.8% sand, 30.7% silt, 23.5% clay) placed the composite sample in the loam class, with a near-neutral pH (6.5), moderate cation exchange capacity (15.7 cmol(+)/kg) and organic carbon content (0.71%) consistent with regional literature ranges. Proximate biochemical profiling showed protein content ranging from 3.6–5.1 mg/g fresh weight and carbohydrate content ranging from 4.0–5.9 mg/g fresh weight across species, with Corchorus olitorius recording the highest values for both parameters and Spinacia oleracea the highest tissue moisture (92.4%). TCP and 4-CPA were detected in the composite soil at 0.167 ± 0.019 and 0.113 ± 0.015 mg/kg, respectively, and in vegetable tissue at concentrations one to two orders of magnitude lower (0.008–0.021 mg/kg TCP; below detection limit (BDL)–0.015 mg/kg 4-CPA), with detection frequencies ranging from 33.3% to 100% across species. Apparent soil-to-plant transfer ratios were consistently below unity (0.05–0.13), indicating limited but measurable translocation. A positive association was observed between tissue moisture content and residue concentration for both analytes. These findings establish a quantitative baseline against which post-remediation or seasonal monitoring data can be compared, and they are discussed in relation to reported soil-plant transfer behaviour of structurally related organic pollutants.

Abstract
Continuous cropping is a major constraint that limits the sustainable cultivation of medicinal plants. However, the effects of continuous cropping on yield, medicinal quality, and the rhizosphere soil microecological environment of Atractylodes chinensis , particularly in the Horqin Sandy Land, remain unclear. In this study, we conducted a field experiment with continuous cropping and rotation systems to compare the yield, bioactive compound content, soil chemical properties, and rhizosphere microbial communities of A. chinensis and to further elucidate their potential relationships. Plant and soil samples were collected to assess plant yield and bioactive compounds, soil chemical properties, and soil microbial communities using high-throughput sequencing, followed by bioinformatic analysis of the sequencing data. Compared with crop rotation, continuous cropping significantly reduced yield and the contents of major bioactive compounds, including atractylodin, atractylon and β-eudesmol. Continuous cropping significantly increased soil pH, electrical conductivity (EC), and available potassium, and decreased alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen and organic matter (OM). Additionally, continuous cropping decreased fungal richness (Chao1 index) and increased bacterial diversity (Shannon index) and evenness (Pielou’s evenness). Fusarium and Volutella were significantly enriched, whereas Nocardioides and Arthrobacter were depleted. The predicted microbial functional potential associated with carbon and nitrogen cycling significantly decreased under continuous cropping. The correlation analysis revealed that the yield of A. chinensis was negatively correlated with soil EC and positively correlated with OM content, and the relative abundances of Heydenia and Enterocarpus . Atractylon content was positively correlated with Aleuria and Acremonium and negatively correlated with Volutella . These findings demonstrate that continuous cropping reduces the yield and medicinal quality of A. chinensis in the Horqin Sandy Land and is associated with the deterioration of several soil chemical properties and dysbiosis of the rhizosphere microbial community. This study provides novel insights into the continuous cropping constraint associated with A. chinensis and its ecological consequences in the Horqin Sandy Land and establishes a theoretical foundation for optimizing cropping systems, maintaining soil ecological health, and improving the yield and quality of this medicinal herb.

Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology

Abstract
Long-term continuous sorghum cropping degrades soil environments and causes continuous cropping obstacles. This study investigated whether diversified crop rotation can maintain soil health and improve the soil microenvironment for sorghum growth. Using continuous sorghum cropping as the control (SSS), three rotation systems were established: sorghum-potato-sorghum (SPS), sorghum-common vetch-sorghum (SVS), and sorghum-green pea shoots-sorghum (SGS). Soil physicochemical properties, microbial community structure and diversity were analyzed using Illumina NovaSeq high-throughput sequencing, and functional traits were predicted using FAPROTAX and FUNGuild. Mantel tests and co-occurrence network analysis were also performed. All three rotation treatments improved soil physicochemical properties to varying degrees, with SPS showing the greatest nutrient enhancement. SVS markedly increased bacterial richness and diversity, while SPS significantly enhanced fungal richness and diversity. β-diversity analysis revealed that rotation treatments differed significantly from SSS, with SVS and SGS showing similar bacterial structures and SPS and SVS showing similar fungal structures. Functionally important microbial groups increased in rotation soils, whereas the pathogenic genus Fusarium remained relatively high in SPS. Mantel tests indicated that soil pH, TN, TP, TK, and AK were key factors influencing microbial community structure, with AK showing the strongest correlation. Nitrogen fixation and cellulose degradation were the dominant bacterial functions, while saprotrophic and ectomycorrhizal fungi were enriched in rotation soils. Co-occurrence network analysis showed that the rotation network had higher average degree, network density, modularity, and a larger connected component than the continuous cropping network. These findings indicate that diversified crop rotation effectively elevates soil nutrient levels, reshapes microbial community structure and ecological functions, and supports high-quality and stable sorghum production.

Abstract
Heavy metal contamination in agricultural soils and edible crops raises concerns about environmental quality and food safety in Bangladesh. This study investigated arsenic (As), lead (Pb), chromium (Cr), and cadmium (Cd) in 15 paired soil and edible crop samples from Nagarkanda Upazila, Faridpur. Metal concentrations were measured using atomic absorption spectrometry and evaluated through soil contamination indices, ecological risk assessment, soil-to-plant transfer factors, multivariate statistical analysis, dietary health risk assessment and exploratory spatial mapping. Mean concentrations of As, Pb, Cr, and Cd were 36.756, 11.850, 64.933 and 0.373 mg kg⁻¹ in soil, respectively, compared with 0.140, 0.524, 3.675, and 0.0646 mg kg⁻¹ in edible crops. Soil As concentrations exceeded the adopted reference limit while the remaining metals were below their respective limits. The Pollution Load Index (PLI) ranged from 0.857 to 2.020 (mean 1.353 ± 0.291), indicating moderate pollution at 13 sites, heavy pollution at one site and no pollution at one site. The Potential Ecological Risk Index (PERI) ranged from 101.60 to 168.77 (mean 133.05), with 12 sites showing low ecological risk and three showing moderate risk. Cd contributed most to ecological risk and exhibited the highest mean soil-to-plant transfer factor (0.179), followed by Pb (0.061), Cr (0.060), and As (0.004). Principal component analysis showed that the first two components explained 96.8% of the total variance, reflecting differences in metal concentrations across the sampled locations. Dietary risk screening suggested potential health concerns associated with As exposure. Chromium-related estimates, however, represent conservative Cr(VI)-equivalent scenarios because the analysis measured total Cr without distinguishing its chemical forms. Differences between the dry-weight measurements and fresh-weight food standards also limit direct interpretation of dietary exposure. The findings highlight As enrichment and Cd-related ecological risk as important concerns in the sampled agricultural soils. The study provides baseline evidence to guide targeted monitoring and further assessment of soil quality and food safety in Nagarkanda, although broader conclusions require more extensive sampling.

Abstract
This study aims to quantify the activity concentrations of 226Ra and 228Ra in Technologically Enhanced Naturally Occurring Radioactive Material (TENORM) contaminated soils from selected sites within the South Rumaila oilfield, Basra Governorate, southern Iraq, and to evaluate physical and chemical remediation options to reduce contamination below IAEA limits. Using High-Purity Germanium (HPGe) gamma-ray spectrometry, the mean activity concentrations were 7.67× 104; ± 1.5% and 6.26× 103 ± 1.43% Bq/kg for 226Ra and 228Ra, respectively (n = 25). Physical separation by particle size and sequential chemical leaching were applied as remediation approaches. Abstract
Marine oil spills and microplastic (MP) pollution frequently coexist, but the effects of microplastic aging on microplastic–oil agglomerates’ (MOAs) formation and transport remain poorly resolved. In this study, polybutylene terephthalate and polyvinyl chloride microplastics were subjected to acid, UV, and sequential UV→Acid aging and then interacted with three crude oils under controlled mixing conditions. Surface properties were related to oil dispersion efficiency, MOA size, composition, and settling behavior. Aging generated pathway-specific surface states rather than a single monotonic weathering trend. Acid aging increased negative surface charge and wettability, coinciding with reduced oil capture and smaller MP-enriched aggregates. UV aging caused surface roughening and was associated with larger, more MP-rich aggregates, consistent with a greater contribution of MP-MP contacts despite reduced hydrophobic affinity. Sequential UV→Acid aging combined more negative charge, increased roughness and elevated surface free energy. These MPs formed large, MP-rich MOAs with relatively high settling velocities despite reduced oil affinity. Aggregate size became decoupled from captured oil mass after aging, while relatively high oil dispersion could coexist with rapid MOA settling. These findings indicate the potential for partial separation of petroleum-hydrocarbon and MP transport and highlight the importance of aging pathway, polymer chemistry, and aggregate composition.

Restoration as a Process, Not a Product: Why Monitoring Defines SuccessECO1
Authors: M. Zhiyanski
Venue: ARPHA Conference Abstracts
Published: 2026-10-27
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Ecosystem restoration has become a central priority of environmental policy, reflecting the urgent need to reverse biodiversity loss, address land degradation, strengthen climate resilience, and secure the long-term provision of ecosystem services. However, despite increasing political commitments and investments in restoration, a fundamental challenge remains: how can restoration success be reliably defined, measured, and demonstrated over time? Restoration initiatives are frequently evaluated through implementation-oriented indicators, such as the area restored, number of trees planted, or completion of planned interventions, rather than through evidence of ecological recovery. Such approaches risk treating restoration as a final product instead of recognising it as a dynamic, long-term ecological process (Gann et al. 2019). This keynote argues that monitoring is not merely a supporting activity of ecosystem restoration but an essential mechanism for defining its objectives, evaluating its effectiveness, and guiding adaptive management. Restoration success cannot be determined solely by the implementation of management measures (Suppl. material 1). It must be demonstrated through measurable changes in ecosystem condition, structure, functions, biodiversity, resilience, and capacity to sustain ecosystem services under changing environmental conditions (Gann et al. 2019, Lindenmayer and Likens 2009). From an ecological perspective, restoration involves complex interactions among vegetation, soils, hydrological processes, biological communities, and environmental drivers. These interactions operate at different spatial and temporal scales, often producing nonlinear responses, delayed recovery, and trajectories that do not necessarily converge towards historical reference conditions. Climate change further complicates restoration planning by modifying disturbance regimes, species distributions, soil processes, and ecosystem functioning. Consequently, restoration objectives should be based on a realistic understanding of ecosystem dynamics, ecological thresholds, environmental pressures, and the potential for recovery under both current and future conditions (Holl and Aide 2011, Gann et al. 2019). Particular attention is given to forest ecosystems, where restoration outcomes depend on processes extending far beyond vegetation establishment. Soil organic carbon dynamics, nutrient cycling, soil biodiversity, water regulation, natural regeneration, structural complexity, and habitat connectivity are fundamental dimensions of ecosystem recovery. Soil properties and processes are especially important because they influence ecosystem productivity, resistance to disturbance, and long-term resilience, while frequently responding more slowly than aboveground vegetation. Monitoring frameworks that neglect belowground processes may therefore provide an incomplete assessment of restoration effectiveness. A process-oriented restoration framework requires the integration of baseline assessments, reference conditions, measurable ecological indicators, repeated observations, and clearly defined criteria for evaluating recovery trajectories. Long-term ecological monitoring provides the temporal evidence needed to distinguish short-term responses to restoration interventions from persistent improvements in ecosystem condition. It also allows the identification of unexpected changes, emerging pressures, and potential trade-offs among biodiversity conservation, carbon sequestration, ecosystem productivity, and other ecosystem services (Lindenmayer and Likens 2009). The lecture highlights the importance of established long-term observation systems, particularly the Long-Term Ecosystem Research network (LTER) and the International Co-operative Programme on Assessment and Monitoring of Air Pollution Effects on Forests (ICP Forests). These initiatives provide valuable experience in harmonised methodologies, repeated field measurements, environmental data integration, and assessing ecosystem responses to multiple pressures. Their contribution extends beyond documenting environmental change: they provide scientific evidence for interpreting restoration trajectories, identifying drivers of ecosystem degradation, and evaluating whether management interventions generate sustained ecological improvements. An additional challenge concerns the translation of ecological monitoring results into operational indicators and policy-relevant assessments. European initiatives such as Mapping and Assessment of Ecosystems and their Services (MAES) and the System of Environmental-Economic Accounting - Ecosystem Accounting (SEEA-EA) provide conceptual and methodological opportunities to connect ecosystem condition, ecosystem services, and the societal benefits of restoration. Integrating these frameworks with field-based ecological observations, spatial information, and long-term monitoring can strengthen the scientific basis for restoration prioritisation, evaluation, and reporting. Within the evolving European policy framework, particularly Regulation (EU) 2024/1991 on nature restoration, monitoring assumes an increasingly strategic role (European Parliament and Council, 2024). Effective implementation requires moving beyond assessments of restoration activities towards transparent, scientifically robust evaluations of ecological outcomes. This implies developing harmonised but ecosystem-specific indicators, appropriate monitoring frequencies, spatially representative sampling designs, and mechanisms to translate monitoring results into management decisions. The keynote proposes a conceptual transition from activity-based restoration reporting to evidence-based evaluation of ecological recovery, structured around five interconnected elements: understanding ecosystem condition and degradation drivers before intervention; defining realistic and measurable restoration objectives; selecting indicators that reflect ecosystem structure, functions, and resilience; establishing integrated and sufficiently long-term monitoring; and using monitoring evidence to adapt restoration strategies in response to observed ecosystem trajectories and changing environmental conditions (Gann et al. 2019, Lindenmayer and Likens 2009). understanding ecosystem condition and degradation drivers before intervention; defining realistic and measurable restoration objectives; selecting indicators that reflect ecosystem structure, functions, and resilience; establishing integrated and sufficiently long-term monitoring; and using monitoring evidence to adapt restoration strategies in response to observed ecosystem trajectories and changing environmental conditions (Gann et al. 2019, Lindenmayer and Likens 2009). Ultimately, ecosystem restoration should not be understood as a discrete intervention with a predetermined endpoint, but as a sustained commitment to facilitating ecological recovery and maintaining ecosystem functionality. Monitoring transforms restoration from an assumption of improvement into a testable, evidence-based process. Its value lies not only in demonstrating whether restoration measures have been implemented successfully, but also in revealing whether ecosystems are genuinely recovering, why certain interventions succeed or fail, and how management can respond to emerging challenges. Restoration is what we do. Еcological recovery is what we must demonstrate. Monitoring provides the evidence that connects the two.

Abstract
The significant spatial stratified heterogeneity of soil heavy metal concentrations is a critical characteristic, and effectively capturing this feature can improve the accuracy of spatial interpolation and the reliability of regional mean estimations. This study used 412 soil sampling points in Shunyi District, Beijing, to predict the spatial distribution and regional means of soil heavy metals using a supervised learning-based stratified random forest ordinary Kriging (SRFOK) model. Spatial stratification information of soil heavy metals was extracted via supervised learning and incorporated into the spatial interpolation process. The findings indicate that incorporating supervised learning-based stratification information into spatial interpolation enhances both the accuracy of spatial interpolation and the stability of regional mean estimation for soil heavy metals, so it is an effective approach for high-precision mapping and regional pollution risk assessment.
Opposite Environmental Responses of Arsenic and Cadmium Mediated by Redox Conditions, Sulfur Cycling, and MicroorganismsECO1
Authors: Zheng-Qi Wang
Venue: Theoretical and Natural Science
Published: 2026-10-27
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Arsenic (As) and cadmium (Cd) often coexist in paddy soils and sediments, yet they respond differently to changes in oxygen availability. Under moderately reducing conditions, the dissolution of iron(III) oxides releases sorbed As, whereas increasing pH and stronger surface sorption commonly decrease Cd mobility. Under strongly reducing conditions, microbial sulfate reduction generates sulfide and promotes the precipitation of sparingly soluble CdS. Arsenic may instead be immobilized in sulfide minerals or remain dissolved as thioarsenic species, depending on iron availability, sulfide concentration, pH, and reaction time. Reoxidation can reverse these trends by rebuilding iron oxides that retain As while oxidizing CdS and potentially remobilizing Cd. Iron-reducing bacteria, sulfate-reducing bacteria, sulfur-oxidizing bacteria, cable bacteria, and As-metabolizing microorganisms regulate these linked transformations. The apparent opposite response is therefore conditional rather than universal. Its direction and magnitude depend on Fe-S balance, organic matter, phosphate competition, and wet-dry frequency. Knowing these controls is important for managing As-Cd co-contaminated soils without decreasing the risk of one contaminant while increasing the other.
