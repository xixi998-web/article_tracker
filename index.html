The impact of compound soil conditioner on the physicochemical properties of acidic farmland soil in Sichuan Province, ChinaCORE13

Venue: Journal of Ecological Engineering

Abstract
Soil acidification is a critical factor leading to low soil fertility and continuous nutrient loss, severely affecting ag - ricultural production. In this study, a typical acidic soil area in Sichuan, China, was selected as the research region. Based on a systematic analysis of the physicochemical properties of the acidic soils, a compound soil amendment consisting of Ca(OH) 2 -modified biochar (CBC) and fermented chicken manure was prepared to improve soil fertility and reduce nutrient leaching. Four application rates of CBC (0.5%, 1%, 2%, and 4.5%, based on dry soil weight) were investigated through a soil column leaching experiment. The results showed that the majority of soils were rather poor in fertility under acidic conditions, with several nutrients, including available phosphorus, being insufficient. In addition, the combined application of CBC and fermented chicken manure effectively improved soil fertility and reduced nutrient losses. Compared with the control, all amended treatments increased soil pH and enhanced the contents of ammonium nitrogen, nitrate nitrogen, available phosphorus, available potassium, and organic matter. Among them, the CBC(2%) treatment exhibited the highest available phosphorus content, while the CBC(4.5%) treatment showed superior performance in improving available potassium and organic matter con - tents. The lowest total nitrogen loss (139.42 mg/L) was observed in the CBC(2%) treatment, whereas the lowest total phosphorus loss (0.90 mg/L) and organic matter loss (224.62 mg/L) were recorded in the CBC(4.5%) treat - ment. These findings indicate that the combination of Ca(OH) 2 -modified biochar and fermented chicken manure can effectively enhance nutrient retention and improve the fertility of acidic farmland soils. The proposed amend - ment provides a simple, low-cost, and sustainable approach for acidic soil remediation and nutrient management in agricultural ecosystems.

Abstract
Nitrogen application rate and topping are two key agronomic factors affecting tobacco leaf quality, but the synergistic regulatory mechanisms remain unclear. This study aimed to elucidate the physiological and molecular mechanisms by which nitrogen application rate and topping synergistically regulate tobacco leaf quality, and to identify key candidate genes involved in nicotine biosynthesis. Using the tobacco cultivar K326, three nitrogen application levels were established: low (0 kg/ha), medium (120 kg/ha), and high (210 kg/ha). Agronomic traits and rhizosphere soil physicochemical properties were monitored, carbon and nitrogen metabolism enzyme activities were measured before and after topping, and mature leaf chemical composition was determined, integrated with transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses. The results showed that medium nitrogen achieved high yield while enhancing organic matter turnover efficiency, reducing rhizosphere water-soluble chloride content, and alleviating soil acidification and ammonium–potassium ion uptake competition caused by excessive fertilization. Combined with topping, it significantly enhanced carbon and nitrogen metabolism, promoted starch-to-soluble-sugar conversion, and increased nicotine accumulation, thereby optimizing leaf chemical composition. Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses revealed that medium nitrogen combined with topping exerted the most pronounced effects on gene expression and metabolite accumulation. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNAs) identified a module highly correlated with nicotine biosynthesis, with hub genes mainly annotated as cytochrome P450 and terpene synthase family members. In conclusion, this study elucidated the molecular mechanisms by which the combination of medium nitrogen application and topping, as an integrated agronomic practice, improves tobacco leaf quality through coordinating carbon-nitrogen metabolism and optimizing chemical composition, and identified key candidate genes potentially regulating nicotine biosynthesis, providing a theoretical basis for nitrogen fertilizer management and high-quality cultivation of tobacco.

Abstract
Soil acidification constrains rice yield and grain quality in southern China, yet how acidity amelioration affects the filling of inferior grains (IG), and the associated hormonal and starch-quality traits, remains unclear. A two-year field experiment on an acidified Red-yellow soil (pH 5.19) compared farmer conventional fertilization (FCF), quicklime (QL), and quicklime plus a humic acid soil conditioner (HA). Grain filling of superior grains (SG) and IG was fitted with the Richards equation, nine endogenous hormones were quantified during filling, and starch composition and pasting properties were determined. QL and HA corrected soil acidity equally, whereas only HA increased soil organic matter and available K. Averaged over two years, grain yield increased by 18.1% under QL and 26.4% under HA, with HA numerically higher than QL. IG responded more strongly than SG: HA extended IG filling duration from 34.8 to 38.8 d and increased final IG weight by 15.4%, whereas QL mainly increased the IG filling rate. Amelioration, particularly HA, raised indole-3-acetic acid, abscisic acid (ABA) and most cytokinins and lowered gibberellins without shifting their timing; at 24 days after anthesis, IG grain weight was positively correlated with trans -zeatin and ABA and negatively with gibberellin A 1 . HA increased amylose content yet reduced setback viscosity by 26.6% in SG and 39.5% in IG. Combining quicklime with humic acid is therefore a promising practice for acidified paddies, improving rice yield and starch pasting properties in association with prolonged IG filling and coordinated hormone changes.

Authors: Jing-Ya Yang, Ruo-Xin Wang, Na Hu, Bo-Qun Li, Yan-Shuang Li...
Venue: Environmental pollution
Published: 2026-11-01
Abs
Abstract
Soil acidification and microplastic pollution increasingly threaten agricultural systems worldwide, yet their combined effects on crops remain largely unexplored. In this study, we subjected broad bean (Vicia faba L.) to polyethylene (PE) microplastics, aluminum (Al) stress, or their combination, and integrated physiological assays, root metabolomics, and rhizosphere metagenomics to dissect their interactive effects. PE microplastics unexpectedly lowered root Al accumulation by 41.7% compared to Al alone, which was associated with reduced exchangeable Al in the rhizosphere. Despite this reduction, the combined PE and Al treatment still significantly suppressed root (by 50.1%) and shoot (by 42.0%) biomasses and severely compromised nodulation and nitrogen (N) acquisition, with effects similar to or even stronger than those of the mono Al treatment. Metabolomic profiling revealed that combined stress extensively downregulated ten flavonoids and fundamentally rewired flavonoid-rhizobia correlation networks, shifting potential symbiotic signaling away from compatible interactions. Metagenomic analysis further showed that while combined stress increased microbial co-occurrence network connectivity by 54.8%, it simultaneously suppressed the abundance of functional genes related to metal resistance and N cycling, pointing to a systemic metabolic burden rather than a targeted stress response. These results demonstrate that PE microplastics modulate Al bioavailability but do not mitigate phytotoxicity; instead, they exacerbate symbiotic disruption through flavonoid signaling interference and microbiome functional decline. These findings highlight the vulnerability of legume-rhizobia symbiosis to multi-stressor environments and call for a reassessment of soil management practices in regions where acidification and microplastic contamination co-occur.

Authors: Adewole T Adetunji, M. J. Ventura, Michael Kurtzhals, Leslie J. Johnson, A. Schmidt...
Venue: Soil Science Society of America Journal
Published: 2026-11-01
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Improving soil health under variable water regimes remains a challenge in coarse‐textured soils. We evaluated short‐term soil health responses to fertilizer source (beef manure vs. inorganic N) with or without surface wood‐chip mulch during two contrasting growing seasons (2022–2023) in adjacent rainfed and irrigated no‐till sandy loam corn ( Zea mays L.) fields in Nebraska. Soil physical (bulk density and mean weight diameter) and chemical (pH, cation exchange capacity [CEC], NO 3 − ‐N, K, P, Ca, Mg, Na, and SOM/SOC) indicators were measured postharvest, while biological indicators (β‐glucosidase, microbial respiration, and community‐level physiological profiles) were assessed at multiple within‐season sampling dates. By the second year, manure reduced bulk density by 10%–23% under both water regimes and consistently increased soil pH and concentrations of P, K, Ca, Mg, and Na. In contrast, inorganic fertilizer resulted in approximately 17% greater CEC and stimulated β‐glucosidase activity and microbial respiration at selected sampling times. Manure also increased microbial functional diversity at selected rainfed sampling dates. Wood‐chip mulch produced few consistent effects during the 2 years, and aggregate stability and SOC showed limited sustained responses. Overall, manure produced the broadest combination of short‐term physical and chemical responses, whereas inorganic fertilizer generated distinct chemical and transient biological responses. The contrasting response patterns indicate that short‐term soil health assessment in sandy loam systems should integrate responsive chemical and biological indicators with structural measurements rather than rely on a single indicator. Soil organic carbon and aggregate stability may be more informative for longer term assessment because measurable changes in these properties were limited during the study period.

Venue: Ecological Engineering &amp; Environmental Technology

Abstract
Shifting cultivation remains widely practiced across tropical regions and has been implicated in soil degradation processes and broader environmental change. This research examines and analyzes the main soil properties of upland rice at harvest in shifting cultivation locations. Soil samples were collected from nine land mapping units (LMUs) representing shifting cultivation systems of upland rice in the harvest phase. The analyzed soil properties included texture, pH, soil organic carbon (C-organic), total nitrogen (N), available phosphorus (P), exchangeable cations (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, and K⁺), cation exchange capacity (CEC), and base saturation (BS). Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, boxplot visualization, and the Kruskal-Wallis test. The results indicate that soils at the study site are generally acidic, with low to moderate levels of soil organic carbon, total nitrogen, available phosphorus, exchangeable base cations, and cation exchange capacity, while base saturation is very low to low. Soil texture ranged from sandy to clayey, which influenced nutrient retention capacity and cation exchange characteristics. Boxplot analysis revealed substantial variability within LMUs, reflecting fine-scale spatial heterogeneity. How - ever, the Kruskal-Wallis test indicated that most soil properties did not differ significantly among LMUs (p>0.05), suggesting relatively homogeneous soil conditions at the landscape scale. Therefore, sustainable land management in shifting cultivation systems needs to integrate a conservation approach at the landscape scale with site-specific management to maintain soil fertility and the sustainability of land ecological functions.

Venue: Journal of Engineering

Abstract
The contamination of agricultural sandy soils by zinc (Zn) presents not only a risk for the quality of crops but also a problem for the quality of groundwater. Common enhancing agents used in electrokinetic remediation (EKR) of heavy metals, such as EDTA, present persistence in the environment. This work evaluates an EKR technique that involves a biodegradable chelating agent, tartaric acid, and a barrier of untreated rice husk installed in front of the cathode. The experimental design includes a sandy soil (93.4% sand) artificially contaminated with 300 mg/kg Zn and treated for 5 days under the voltage gradient of 1 V/cm. Besides three experiments without enhancement, where the purging solutions were tap water adjusted to pH 3, 7 and 11, there was one experiment with tartaric acid at pH 3 as the anolyte. All experiments were done once. The removal of Zn decreased from 77.8% at pH 3 to 69.7% at pH 7 and 53.2% at pH 11 due to the higher desorption and electromigration of Zn in acidic conditions and the precipitation of Zn as Zn(OH)₂ in neutral and alkaline conditions. In all experiments, the residual amount of Zn decreased along the direction from the anode to the cathode. The removal of Zn in the tartaric acid-enhanced experiment was the highest (90.8%), which corresponds to 13 percentage points more than in the experiment without enhancement at pH 3, with the residual amount of Zn being 15–43 mg/kg in the soil column. This fact is due to the formation of soluble Zn-tartrate complexes and buffering action of tartaric acid that maintained soil pH within 4.2–5.7. The amount of Zn retained in the rice husk barrier was up to 38 mg/kg. Therefore, the EKR enhanced by biodegradable tartaric acid can be considered a potentially useful technique for the remediation of Zn-contaminated sandy soils. Replicate tests, the cost evaluation, the effect of the technique on soil quality and quantification of the barrier contribution are needed.

Abstract
To address the challenge of synergistically mitigating multiple constraints—structure, nutrients, and microorganisms—in newly reclaimed farmland, and to comprehensively improve soil quality and crop productivity, a field experiment was conducted. Treatments with different combinations and application rates of microbial agents, organic fertilizer, modified biochar, and minerals were established. The effects of these treatments on soil physicochemical and biological properties, as well as wheat growth, were analyzed to identify key synergistic measures and their mechanisms for rapidly enhancing soil quality and crop productivity. Results showed that the integrated treatment (MCN), which consisted of organic fertilizer at 6 t ha−1, modified biochar at 1.5 t ha−1, minerals at 0.75 t ha−1, and an appropriate amount of Bacillus amyloliquefaciens agent, was the optimal scheme balancing improvement efficacy and economic benefits. MCN significantly improved soil physical structure and chemical nutrient status. Compared to other treatments, it reduced bulk density by 0.14–0.18 g cm−3, increased large aggregate content by the greatest margin (12 g kg−1), raised field water-holding capacity by 13.0–22.7 g kg−1, elevated pH by 0.41–0.87, and increased soil organic matter by 0.55–4.43 g kg−1, while effectively enhancing phosphorus availability and calcium content. MCN also effectively reshaped the soil microbial community toward a healthier ecological state. Compared to other treatments, it markedly increased the absolute abundance of beneficial bacterial classes, with Fibrobacteria being 2.0–9.4 times more abundant. It also increased β-glucosidase activity by 14.4–73.9 nmol g−1 h−1 and markedly enhanced both α- and β-diversity. Meanwhile, MCN significantly promoted wheat growth indicators such as plant height and spike length, and the yield was more than 3.5 times that of the NPK treatment. This study constructed a cascading efficiency path for improving soil quality and crop productivity in newly reclaimed farmland using PLS-SEM: The integrated treatment improved soil physical structure and provided balanced chemical nutrients and energy, thereby promoting microbial community restructuring and enhanced activity. Subsequently, changes in the microbial community may have further improved soil physicochemical properties and strengthened positive soil–crop interactions, ultimately leading to simultaneous improvements in crop yield and plant growth. The model had a good fit (GOF = 0.70, R2 = 0.90), and through RDA, PCoA, and Pearson correlation, the synergistic efficiency mechanism of four materials (fertilizer, organic fertilizer, biochar, and minerals) in improving soil quality and crop productivity was analyzed. Based on this, this study established a mechanism model for improving soil quality and crop growth in newly reclaimed farmland through integrated treatment, providing a theoretical basis and practical paradigms for improving soil quality and achieving crop yield increase in newly reclaimed farmland.

Abstract
Monitoring the soil mineral nitrogen (SMN) status of cropland is challenging due to large spatiotemporal variability. Here, we present a novel approach that correlates NH 3 , NO, and HONO emissions with SMN content, pH, and soil moisture, eliminating the need for destructive soil sampling. We conducted incubation experiments with five soils with different pH, texture, and organic matter content, using SMN and soil water gradients. In all soils, NH 4 + soil content was positively correlated with NH 3 emission as a function of soil pH and water content. For soil with pH 6.2 and 50% maximum water holding capacity (mWHC), the limit of detection (LOD) was comparable to established methods for total soil NH 4 + analysis (LOD = 2.17 mg NH 4 + ‐N kg −1 ) and was as low as 0.16 mg NH 4 + ‐N kg −1 at pH 7.3 and 50% mWHC. In contrast, a significant correlation was found in only one of the soils between NO/NO 2 − ( R 2  = 0.73, p  < 0.001) and HONO/NO 2 − ( R 2  = 0.57, p  < 0.001). Furthermore, NO (LOD = 37.65 µg NO 2 − ‐N kg −1 ) analysis was preferable to HONO (LOD = 56.14 µg NO 2 − ‐N kg −1 ) due to its lower LOD and 14‐fold higher accuracy. However, the LOD was slightly higher than that of established photometric methods for NO 2 − analysis.

Abstract
Global industrial pollution and agricultural expansion pose severe challenges, such as heavy metal contamination, necessitating sustainable remediation. Dark septate endophytes (DSE) are a group of root-colonizing fungi, some members of which have been reported to tolerate environmental stress and influence host plant performance. This study focused on reeds (Phragmites australis) and their rhizosphere soil in Baiyang Lake, China. Across four sampling sites-sewage discharge areas, farmland areas, abandoned parking areas, and urban residential areas-with varying degrees of heavy metal pollution, soil physicochemical properties were measured, rhizosphere microbial community structure was characterized using amplicon sequencing, and DSE root colonization was quantified through root staining and microscopic examination. The resulting data were used to explore the relationships of DSE colonization rates with soil physicochemical properties and rhizosphere microbial communities. Microscopic colonization analysis of stained root segments revealed widespread DSE colonization in reed roots, with the highest total colonization rate (66.7%) recorded at the sewage discharge site (SA), which exhibited the most severe heavy metal contamination. After BH-FDR correction, hyphae colonization and total DSE colonization rates were positively associated with soil moisture and Pb concentration, but negatively associated with soil pH, total nitrogen, Cr concentration, and the relative abundance of Proteobacteria (q < 0.05). Microscopic analysis further showed that hyphal colonization varied more widely among sites (21.2-63.3%) than microsclerotial colonization (22.2-35.6%), indicating site-specific variation in hyphal and microsclerotial colonization patterns. This study provides insights into DSE colonization behavior under contrasting heavy metal stress conditions and supports further investigation into whether plants recruit or maintain DSE in metal-contaminated environments.

Abstract
Identifying land capable of sustaining oilseed crops under increasing aridity is central to strengthening agricultural resilience in North African drylands. We developed a statistically validated, spatially explicit land‑suitability framework that integrates geographic information systems (GIS) with the technique for order preference by similarity to the ideal solution (TOPSIS) across 34 geo‑referenced sites within the 15.8 km 2 Maader perimeter (Ain Sefra Basin, southwestern Algeria). Eight pedo‑hydrological indicators such as cation exchange capacity (CEC), organic matter (OM), exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP), texture, gypsum, slope, pH, and the Irrigation Water Quality Index were normalized and weighted using the analytic hierarchy process (consistency ratio = 0.07). TOPSIS closeness coefficients ( Ci ; relative proximity to the ideal solution in TOPSIS) ranged from 0.37 to 0.76, classifying the area into one highly suitable site (2.9%), 29 moderately suitable sites (85.3%), and four marginal sites (11.8%); no location was classified as unsuitable. Statistical validation using Kruskal–Wallis tests (CEC: p  = 0.00056; OM: p  = 0.00313; ESP: p  = 0.0272), Pearson correlations (CEC: r  = +0.65; OM: r  = +0.62; ESP: r  = −0.70), principal component analysis, and variance inflation factors (VIF < 2.5) shows that CEC, OM, and ESP are the dominant, independent drivers of suitability. Spatially, a central calcimagnesic belt with high CEC, natural gypsum content, and gentle slopes emerges as a “ready‑to‑scale” zone for sunflower ( Helianthus annuus L.) intensification (observed yields ≈ 2.8 t ha − 1 ), whereas sodic depressions adjacent to the wadi require targeted gypsum amendment (≥6 t ha − 1 ) and subsurface drainage. Sensitivity tests with ±20% perturbation in factor weights confirmed the robustness of the model, with changes in suitability class below 15%. Overall, this GIS–TOPSIS approach is not merely diagnostic; it provides a transparent, scalable, and investment‑ready platform to guide oilseed expansion under climate stress. By combining quantitative rigor with actionable spatial outputs, it supports Algeria's agricultural self‑sufficiency agenda and offers a transferable template for dryland suitability planning across the region.

Abstract
Humid-tropical ultisols supporting smallholder horticulture in Southeast Asia are widely recognised as degraded agroecosystems whose biogeochemical functioning has been disrupted by accelerated cation leaching, sulfur depletion following reduced atmospheric S deposition, and decades of chloride-loaded fertilisation. From an ecological engineering perspective, restoring the productive capacity of these soils requires interventions that simultaneously address nutrient cycling, soil-microbiome connectivity, and avoidance of off-site externalities, rather than yield optimisation alone. This study evaluated potassium sulfate (K₂SO₄) as an ecologically engineered substitute for potassium chloride (KCl) in a degraded humid-tropical ultisol of Indralaya Selatan, Ogan Ilir, South Sumatra, Indonesia, using shallot ( Allium cepa var. aggregatum L.) as a sentinel crop. An OLS model (R² =.827) is proposed as a non-destructive ecological monitoring tool for in-field state assessment. The findings advance an ecologically engineered nutrient-pathway framework chloride-free, dual K–S supply, dose-bounded by ecosystem self-organisation, and coupled to non-destructive monitoring for the restoration of productive function in degraded humid-tropical ultisol agroecosystems.

Abstract
The use of copper in many technologies inevitably leads to environmental issues due to pollution by Cu2+, whose harmfulness to living organisms at high concentrations is well documented. Detection methods as well as strategies for Cu2+ removal in aqueous media are important to reduce its environmental impact. A spectroscopic method based on the cupric reducing antioxidant capacity (CUPRAC) assay is proposed for Cu2+ quantification in aqueous media. This method involved Cu2+ complexation with neocuproine followed by its reduction to the orange Cu(I) complex by ascorbic acid. Abstract
Soil organic carbon (SOC) pools reflect the net balance of plant- and microbial-derived inputs, transformation, and loss, yet a global-scale assessment of distribution patterns, relative contributions, and hierarchical environmental correlates of microbial necromass carbon (MNC) and plant-derived carbon (PdC) remains incomplete. Based on 1167 site-level observations (1235 pool-level records) across four ecosystem types, we applied variance partitioning, random forest, linear mixed-effects models, and structural equation modeling to quantify global patterns and associative pathways of the two carbon pools. Unadjusted pooled comparisons showed higher MNC concentration and SOC contribution than PdC. However, covariate-adjusted linear mixed-effects models revealed significantly higher PdC. Paired observations showed the same directional trend, although statistical significance was not reached due to limited paired sample size (concentration: p = 0.104; contribution: p = 0.140).
