TJBe-GS: Rigid Trajectory-Guided Bézier Gaussian Splatting for RGB-D Dynamic ReconstructionCORE10
Authors: Wei Wei, Chao Li, Xiang-Hui Pan, Jia-Yuan Du, Cheng-Ju Liu...
Venue: IEEE Sensors Journal
Published: 2026-11-15
Abs · Code1
Abstract
Dynamic scene reconstruction via vision sensors is a crucial basis for accurate spatiotemporal environment perception and geometric modeling. Against this backdrop, accurately modeling the continuous pose variations of dynamic objects over time is crucial for applications such as simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), autonomous driving, and robot imitation learning. However, existing 3-D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) methods couple object-level rigid motion with local deformation, which increases motion ambiguity and may cause trajectory drift, dragging artifacts, or blurred local geometry under complex RGB-D dynamics. To address this, we propose TJBe-GS, a novel 4-D reconstruction framework for precise dynamic modeling. Unlike traditional mesh- or anchor-based methods, we formulate the continuous evolution of dynamic objects as a superposition of global rigid displacements and local nonrigid deformations. Specifically, we leverage synchronized RGB-D streams, and instance masks are used to separate dynamic objects from the static background, acquiring precise point-prior trajectories for the dynamic elements by a geometrically guided initialization and filtering module (GIFM). We then establish static and dynamic Gaussian primitives: static primitives are enhanced via temporal accumulation and random downsampling, while each dynamic Gaussian is assigned a learnable Bézier curve to model continuous local deformation. Furthermore, we introduce a motion smoothness constraint to enforce physical plausibility and temporal coherence. Extensive experiments and ablation studies conducted on the TUM, BONN, and custom datasets demonstrate that TJBe-GS reconstructs dynamic objects with superior quality and precise geometric details compared to existing methods. Demos and code are available at https://github.com/wei17861101975-hash/TJBe-GS
Anatomy-Aware 3D GAN with Local Mask Refinement for Brain MRI InpaintingECO2
Authors: A. F. Rahimi, H. M. Rai, S. Y. A. Mushtaq
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-11-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Brain tumor MRI inpainting aims to synthesize plausible healthy-appearing tissue in regions removed from pathological scans. We present an anatomy-aware three-dimensional generative adversarial network for Task 4 of the BraTS 2026 challenge. The method combines mask-aware gated convolutions, mirrored contralateral context, attention-modulated skip fusion, boundary-aware refinement, and a local masked-region refiner, each adapted from established components in the image synthesis and inpainting literature. The model is trained with a two-stage objective that first emphasizes voxel-wise and structural reconstruction and then applies low-weight adversarial fine-tuning. On a deterministic local held-out split, the proposed method substantially improves over a simple 3D AutoEncoder baseline but does not surpass the challenge's Pix2Pix3D baseline. On the official BraTS 2026 Task 4 evaluation server, the submitted model obtained a mean SSIM of 0.658 and a mean PSNR of 14.81~dB over 219 evaluated cases, lower than the local held-out figures. We report these results transparently, analyze likely contributing factors, and identify the controlled ablations (removal of the mirrored-context channel and of the two-stage schedule) required to isolate the contribution of each architectural component.

Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-11-15

Abstract
Although multimodal recommendations have shown the ability to learn user preferences and item properties by leveraging explicit multimodal information, they remain constrained by two critical limitations: low-quality representation learning and modality fusion. For the former, existing solutions primarily focus on multi-granularity representation learning; however, we argue that training such representations is difficult due to the inherent data sparsity problem in recommendation scenarios. For the latter, existing solutions often rely on contrastive learning to align representations of different modalities to facilitate modality fusion, yet alignment-only objectives may over-compress valuable modality-specific information. To this end, we suggest \underline{``Take a Step Back"} and propose a \textbf{D}ual-granularity raw feature empowered \textbf{I}ntermediate fusion \textbf{G}raph \textbf{C}onvolutional \textbf{N}etwork (\textbf{DIGCN}) for multimodal recommendation. DIGCN leverages the multimodal understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to construct dual-granularity raw features directly. Furthermore, DIGCN introduces an intermediate fusion graph convolutional network equipped with dynamic cross-modality weight correction, designed to capture cross-modality signals and achieve effective modality fusion. Comprehensive experiments on four real-world datasets validate that DIGCN consistently outperforms a variety of strong baselines. Additional analyses confirm its superiority under varying levels of data sparsity and cold-start scenarios. Notably, DIGCN demonstrates high extensibility, capable of further benefiting from additional contrastive learning-based alignment tasks.
Geo2Gcode: From 3D Geometry to CNC Programs through Structured Machining PrimitivesECO1
Authors: Sang-Min Hong, D. Cho, Mohsen Yavartanoo, Kyoung Mu Lee
Venue: Pattern Recognition
Published: 2026-11-15
Abs
Abstract
Turning a 3D shape into a Computer Numerical Control (CNC) program is still largely manual. For every part, a machinist chooses setups, tools, step-downs, and toolpath patterns. We present Geo2Gcode, which converts a target mesh directly into standard G-code through structured machining primitives. Each primitive couples an indexed machining direction, a closed per-view reconstruction of the target, and explicit tool-radius, layer-height, and clearing-pattern parameters, and compiles deterministically into layerwise toolpaths via signed-distance queries. Most of the fidelity comes from this compiler. A lightweight selector trained with a voxel stock simulator fills in primitive parameters without per-part manual settings, approaching the finest fixed setting at about a quarter of its toolpath length. In simulation on ABC parts, Geo2Gcode reaches roughing fidelity comparable to an expert-tuned commercial CAM setup with a moderately longer toolpath, and clearly improves on a default CAM configuration. Generated programs run in a commercial G-code simulator, and one part is machined on a physical CNC mill. These results demonstrate that roughing programs can be generated directly from geometry without per-part CAM tuning.

Abstract
A two-stage pipeline is widely used in visual place recognition (VPR) for mobile robotics to improve robustness under challenging conditions. It typically consists of two steps: <italic>descriptor generation</italic> and <italic>re-ranking</italic>. In descriptor generation, a backbone network generates descriptors for the query and database images. Then, re-ranking performs three steps: 1) top-<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$k$ </tex-math></inline-formula> candidate descriptors are selected the database; 2) these candidates are aggregated into a global descriptor; and 3) a <italic>re-ordered</italic> list of candidates is returned according to similarity scores against the global descriptor. However, current re-ranking methods suffer from two critical limitations: 1) <italic>false positive aggregation</italic>: Under texture variations, positive and negative database samples may produce similar descriptors. As a result, some negative samples may be selected into re-ranking, contaminating the aggregation and hence, incorrect top-<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$k$ </tex-math></inline-formula> similarity scores; and 2) <italic>limited similarity correction</italic>: Most re-ordering is typically nondifferentiable, so that <italic>no</italic> matching error can be back-propagated to train the re-ranking stage to learn calibrated similarity scores during network training. To address these issues, we propose a novel re-ranking framework called dynamic graph attention optimal transport (DGAOT), comprising two key re-ranking modules built upon optimal transport (OT) theory. First, a dynamic graph aggregation (DGA) module is designed to convert the pairwise affinities of the top-<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$k$ </tex-math></inline-formula> query–candidate graph into an OT-constrained joint coupling, down-weight the falsely selected candidates, and aggregate the descriptors under consistent distributional patterns, improving robustness to texture variations. Second, a differentiable re-ordering layer (DRL)–a differentiable score calibration layer—is designed to perform differentiable score re-allocation via Sinkhorn iterations. Finally, a new coarse-to-fine average precision (CFAP) loss is proposed to supervise DGA and DRL, enabling end-to-end re-ranking. Extensive experiments show that DGAOT achieves high retrieval performance, with about <bold>5.3% relative</bold> and <bold>3.35% absolute improvement</bold> in R@1 on average.
