Revitalization of Ultisol Using Biochar from Cassava Harvest Waste Combined with Organic FertilizerCORE6

Venue: Journal of Multidisciplinary Applied Natural Science
Published: 2026-11-03

Abstract
This study aimed to evaluate the effects of biochar produced from fresh cassava stem and trunk waste, as well as the application of manure, on soil fertility and cassava yield in Ultisols. A factorial randomized block design with three replications was used, testing 4 biochar rates (0, 5, 10, and 15 t/ha) and 3 manure treatments (no manure, cattle manure at 10 t/ha, and chicken manure at 10 t/ha). The initial properties of the Ultisol were characterized by acidity (pH 4.58), low organic carbon (1.80%), total nitrogen (0.12%), total phosphorus (209.90 ppm), exchangeable potassium (0.27 cmol(+)/kg), and cation exchange capacity (4.47 cmol(+)/kg). Application of biochar at the highest rate increased soil pH to 5.5, organic carbon to 2.07%, and cation exchange capacity to 9.82 cmol(+)/kg. There was no significant interaction between biochar and manure treatments for cassava growth and yield, indicating that their effects were independent. Both biochar and manure applications improved plant growth and yield, with the highest yield (124.93 kg/plot) obtained from the combination of 15 t/ha biochar and chicken manure, representing a 13.36% increase over the control (110.20 kg/plot). The combination of the highest biochar rate and chicken manure produced the greatest tuber weight (2.63 kg/plant) and shoot biomass (3.85 kg/plant). The tallest plants (284.60 cm at 8 months) and largest stem diameter (2.70 cm) were observed with the highest biochar rate. Chicken manure consistently improved plant growth across all treatments, with the greatest effects observed at the highest biochar rate. The results indicate that both biochar derived from cassava waste and manure are effective in improving Ultisol fertility and cassava productivity, with the greatest improvements achieved when both amendments are applied together. These findings highlight the importance of targeted nutrient input combinations to enhance crop yield in acidic soils.

Abstract
Forest canopy serves as the first interface for atmospheric deposition into ecosystems, thereby playing a crucial role in regulating hydrological processes and associated water chemical cycles by means of canopy exchange processes. However, it remains poorly understood how canopy exchange processes alter chemical processing of rainwater, such as the acid-neutralizing capacity (ANC) in acidic areas. This study examined the hydrological partitioning and associated chemical composition in a typical subtropical evergreen broad-leaved forest in southern China. Hydrological components, including rainfall, throughfall, and stemflow, were measured and sampled from January 2020 to December 2021, and the canopy exchange process was then estimated using a canopy budget model. Results showed that pH and ANC increased in throughfall but decreased in stemflow. During the dry season, throughfall exhibited significantly higher neutralization capacity (ΔpH = 1.11 ± 0.89; ΔANC = 0.11 ± 0.13 meq L-1), whereas in the wet season, the neutralization capacity of throughfall was substantially weaker (ΔpH = 0.07 ± 0.85; ΔANC = 0.03 ± 0.09 meq L-1). In contrast, stemflow showed persistent acidification across both seasons (dry-season ΔpH: -1.29 ± 1.01; wet-season ΔpH: -1.18 ± 0.69), with its ΔANC significantly below zero in the wet season (-0.07 ± 0.09 meq L-1, p = 0.009). The canopy budget model indicated that most solutes (except F-) were leached from the forest canopy, with K+ exhibiting the most pronounced canopy leaching effect. In addition, canopy exchange contributed positively 83% to acid-neutralizing capacity (ANCNTF) of net throughfall flux. Among ions mediating canopy processes, Ca2+, K+, Mg2+, and F- all made positive contributions to the acid neutralizing capacity of canopy exchange (ANCCE) in both dry and wet seasons, with K+ contributing the most (31.43% in the dry season and 25.52% in the wet season). In contrast, NO₃- and Cl- exhibited negative contributions: the negative effect of NO₃- was stronger in the wet season (-20.69%) than in the dry season (-0.63%), whereas Cl- showed the opposite pattern (dry: -12.03%-; wet: -12.03%). Our results suggest that the forest canopy may play a significant role in mitigating the effects of atmospheric acid deposition. This finding contributes to a better understanding of forest eco-hydrological processes and could have important implications for forest management in similar subtropical regions globally.
Illumination selectively strenghens performances of nitrification inhibitor in reducing mixed pesticide residues and nitrous oxide emissions: a tradeoff effect.CORE5

Venue: Environmental research

Abstract
Pesticides and nitrous oxide (N2O) emissions are critical concerns in agricultural systems. Soil nitrogen management could promote pesticide degradation and reduce N2O emissions, and dicyandiamide (DCD) is one of the most widely used nitrification inhibitors. The illumination can serve as a crucial mechanism for degrading various pesticides, and the effects of nitrification inhibitors on the soil nitrogen cycle might vary with different illumination conditions. This study investigated the combined effects of DCD and illumination on mixed pesticide residues (carbendazim and paclobutrazol) and N2O emissions in soil. Under illumination, DCD application significantly reduced soil carbendazim residues. Meanwhile, the DCD application substantially reduced the N2O emission rates by 27% and 13% under dark and illuminated conditions on day 28, respectively. Paclobutrazol residues did not show a detectable response to DCD or illumination during the incubation. Lower carbendazim residues in DCD-treated soil co-occurred with higher soil pH and microbial biomass carbon, greater chitinase activity, and shifts in bacterial taxa and the relative abundances of N-metabolism genes, including norB/C, gdhA/B, and gltS. The illumination augmented DCD's ability to accelerate carbendazim dissipation but impaired the DCD's ability to inhibit the N2O emission rates. Meanwhile, illumination concided with higher relative abundances of nitrification-related amoA/B/C and hao genes and a lower proportion of N2O-reduction gene nosZ. Under the tested experimental conditions,the illumination could selectively strenghen the performances of nitrification inhibitor in reducing mixed pesticide residues and N2O emissions. The tradeoff between carbendazim dissipation and N2O emissions should be considered when utilizing the illumination to strengthen the performance of nitrification inhibitors to promote pesticide dissipation.
Soil quality in the plains and piedmont areas of Valle del Cauca, Colombia: impact of heavy metal concentrationsCORE5

Abstract
This study evaluated the quality of agricultural soils in Valle del Cauca, Colombia, through an integrated analysis of physicochemical properties and heavy metal concentrations. The research was conducted in the plains and piedmont areas of the region. The Soil Quality Index (SQI) ranges from 0 to 1, with values between 0.20–0.39 indicating low quality; 0.40–0.59, moderate; 0.60–0.79, high; and 0.80–1.00, very high. The results show that the evaluated agricultural soils exhibit moderate quality (SQI = 0.57), providing suitable conditions for agricultural productivity. Soil quality was positively influenced by saturated hydraulic conductivity, porosity, pH, and cation exchange capacity. The impact of heavy metals such as Cr, Ni, Pb, and Co on soil quality was moderate. Electrical conductivity contributed less to soil quality, mainly due to the established threshold values rather than actual salinity issues. It is recommended that agricultural practices that may alter soil properties and compromise its quality be avoided. The proposed soil quality index proved useful for monitoring soil conditions, ensuring sustainable agricultural productivity, and mitigating the effects of potentially toxic trace elements. Keywords: heavy metals in soils, quality index, soil quality, soil properties.

Abstract
The Jiamusi section of the lower Songhua River is an important commodity grain production area in China, and the environmental quality of its cultivated soils is directly linked to regional food security and ecosystem stability. To clarify the status of heavy metal pollution in soils in this area, identify pollution sources, and assess potential risks, this study established sampling units in typical cultivated land in the Jiamusi section in 2023, collected 276 topsoil samples (0-20 cm), and determined the contents of eight heavy metals, namely As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Hg, Ni, Pb, and Zn, as well as physicochemical indicators such as soil pH. Correlation analysis and principal component analysis were used to identify heavy metal sources; the single-factor pollution index method, Nemerow comprehensive pollution index method, and geo-accumulation index method were used to evaluate pollution levels; and a human health risk assessment model was used to assess carcinogenic and non-carcinogenic risks for adults and children. The results showed that the average soil pH in the study area was 6.51. The average concentrations of eight heavy metals (As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Hg, Ni, Pb and Zn) were 10.38, 0.23, 53.77, 22.83, 0.09, 21.63, 29.17, and 83.89 mg/kg, respectively, all of which are below the soil background values of the region. Compared with the soil pollution risk screening values for agricultural land, Cd exceedance was the most prominent, with 63 sampling sites exceeding the standard. As, Cr, Cu, Pb, and Zn each had 1-6 sites exceeding the standard, while Hg and Ni had no exceedance sites. Correlation and principal component analyses showed that soil heavy metals mainly originated from natural parent materials (Cr, Ni), industrial activities (Zn, Cu), traffic activities (Pb, Cd), and a composite source associated with other anthropogenic activities (Hg). Pollution assessment results indicated that the soils in the study area generally ranged from unpolluted to mildly polluted: 72.82% of the sampling sites were unpolluted, 11.23% were at the warning level, 11.59% were slightly polluted, and 4.34% were moderately to heavily polluted. Health risk assessment showed that oral ingestion was the main pathway of heavy metal exposure, and the non-carcinogenic risk to children (mean HI 0.87, 19.93% of sampling sites with HI>1) was higher than that to adults (mean HI 0.15). The carcinogenic risk indices at all sampling sites were less than 10‒4, within the acceptable range. These results provide a scientific basis for the prevention and control of heavy metal pollution in cultivated soils, green agricultural development, and ecological and environmental protection in the Jiamusi section of the lower Songhua River.

Abstract
Livestock systems in high-Andean environments can modify vegetation structure and soil conditions through changes associated with grazing management and land use. Silvopastoral systems established under retained tree cover represent an alternative management strategy in these landscapes; however, ecological responses associated with different stages of farmer-managed silvopastoral establishment under high-Andean conditions have received comparatively less attention. This study evaluated soil physicochemical properties, vegetation composition, and pasture productivity across a silvopastoral chronosequence established under retained tree cover in the Amazonas region of Peru. The chronosequence consisted of three silvopastoral systems representing one, four, and seven years since establishment (SPS-1, SPS-4, and SPS-7), while a ten-year open-field grazing system (OF-10) was included as a contrasting grazing condition. Soil properties, vegetation composition, diversity indices, and pasture productivity under dry- and rainy-season conditions were assessed. Significant differences among systems were observed for pH, organic matter, bulk density, nitrate, calcium, magnesium, zinc, and boron concentrations (p < 0.05). Organic matter decreased from 17.87% in SPS-1 to 5.98% in OF-10, whereas bulk density increased from 0.97 to 1.15 g cm−3. Silvopastoral systems presented greater vegetation heterogeneity and higher richness values than the open grazing condition. Floristic analyses indicated compositional differentiation among systems, while pasture productivity was greater in SPS-1 and SPS-4 than in OF-10 under both seasonal conditions. These responses indicate that silvopastoral establishment under retained tree cover are associated with soil characteristics, vegetation diversity and promote a more stable forage production relative to the open grazing condition.

Abstract
Magnesite production negatively impacts forest ecosystems; yet, the impact of such pollution on vegetation remains poorly understood. This study aimed to investigate interspecific differences in the accumulation of macro- and microelements in the vegetation organs of 43-year-old experimental forest stands of Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris L.) and silver birch (Betula pendula Roth.) exposed to emissions from magnesite production. The study area comprised experimental plots established in 1983 under similar forest growth conditions, with varying anthropogenic loads near a magnesite production source. In each experimental site, 15 model trees per species were selected across pollution zones and background conditions. Samples of shoots, bark, needles (pine), and leaves (birch) were collected. Increasing soil pH to alkaline levels (9.2–8.9 units) altered exchangeable cation ratios (Mg2+, Ca2+), deteriorated soil structure, and induced solonetz properties. Species showed significant differences in Na, Zn (p < .001), Fe, Cd (p < .01), Ca, and Mn (p < .05). Organ-specific variation was significant for Ca, Mg, K, Na, Mn, Zn, and Cd, with the greatest variation in K, Mg, Na, and Mn (p < .001). Approaching the emission source, concentrations of Ca, Mg, K, Mn, and Zn changed significantly, with Mn (p < .001) and Mg (p < .01) showing strong distance dependence. Scots pine is a preferred bioindicator species for monitoring magnesite pollution. At the same time, silver birch shows greater resistance and is recommended as planting material for reforestation in magnesite-contaminated areas. Abstract
This study investigated pollution release control, substrate amendment and phytoremediation of electrolytic manganese residue (EMR) via combined red mud (RM) and phosphogypsum (PG) amendments. A pot trial with gradient EMR-RM/EMR-PG ratios using Pennisetum giganteum explored substrate properties, enzyme activities, plant growth and metal accumulation, with principal component analysis distinguishing RM and PG functions. RM and PG lowered substrate conductivity and buffered pH to weak alkalinity. ERM35 (EMR:RM = 3:5) and EPG35 (EMR:PG = 3:5) were optimal, with total fresh weights of 90.07 and 93.30 g versus 13.27 g in ERM62. ERM35 boosted urease and sucrase; ERM53 raised phosphatase by 1381%, and EPG35 increased urease by 130.60%. The total substrate Mn concentration decreased greatly with increasing RM or PG proportion. Such reduction originates partly from material dilution effect; meanwhile, pH and Eh modulation by solid-waste mixtures further alters Mn environmental behavior. EPG35 achieved a 71.90% reduction in total Mn content. RM elevated substrate Fe, while PG promoted Cu accumulation. Mn, Fe and Zn mainly accumulated in plant roots. RM governed plant growth and metal enrichment, whereas PG regulated substrate properties and suppressed Mn release. This work offers theoretical and technical guidance for multi-source solid waste recycling, heavy metal pollution control and rapid soilless restoration of EMR yards.
Soil amendments reshape alpine meadow microbial communities across degradation gradients by modulating nutrient availabilityCORE3
Authors: Na Wang, Zhong-Hua Zhang, Hua-Kun Zhou, Yan-Bin Wen, Xin Wang...
Venue: PLOS One
Published: 2026-11-03
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Climate change and overgrazing have led to the continued degradation of alpine meadows on the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, thereby severely impairing the ecosystem services provided by these grasslands. Soil amendments are an important strategy for restoring degraded grasslands; however, the regulatory effects of different amendment measures across varying degradation gradients and the underlying microbial response mechanisms remain unclear. This study systematically investigated the effects of three soil amendments (humic acid, superabsorbent polymer, and Bacillus subtilis) on soil physicochemical properties and microbial community structure in mildly, moderately, and severely degraded alpine meadows. The results indicated that the regulatory effects of soil amendments are significantly dependent on the degradation gradient: In the severe degradation stage, amendments significantly increased available nutrients, lowered pH, and alleviated alkalization, whereas their regulatory effects were limited in the mild degradation stage. Amendments selectively altered the abundance of microbial groups involved in organic matter degradation, nitrogen cycling, and stress-tolerant symbiosis. Humic acid demonstrated the optimal regulatory effect in the mild degradation stage, whereas superabsorbent polymers and Bacillus subtilis were more suitable for restoration in the moderate and severe degradation stages. Redundancy analysis indicated that bacterial communities were primarily subject to deterministic selection by soil pH, available potassium, and organic matter gradients, with the intensity of environmental selection increasing with increasing degradation severity; fungal communities exhibited significant environmental responses only in the moderate degradation stage, while stochastic processes dominated community assembly in the mild and severe degradation stages. In summary, alpine meadows at various stages of degradation require differentiated remediation strategies. Precise, stage-specific remediation tailored to degradation severity can facilitate synergistic restoration of the soil–microbial system, providing a theoretical basis and practical guidance for restoring degraded alpine meadows on the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau.

Abstract
Solitary trees such as oak (Quercus spp.) play a central ecological role in wood pastures. They alter the surrounding soil and abiotic conditions, with largely unclear consequences for the soil fauna such as earthworms. We investigated how solitary oak trees alter soil properties and whether those alterations impact earthworm communities. Soils and earthworms were sampled across 30 oak-based traditional wood pastures in Transylvania, Romania. At each of the 30 wood pastures, a southern transect was established from the trunk of the focal tree. Four soil profiles were opened along each transect at distances of 1, 3, 7, and 24 m from the tree trunk, resulting in a total of 120 soil profiles, which were described and sampled at three depths: 0–5 cm, 5–25 cm, and 25–45 cm. At each profile location, earthworms from the top 25 cm were hand-sorted from a 25 × 25 cm soil block, providing paired soil and earthworm data. We analysed the contents of soil organic carbon (SOC) and total nitrogen (TN), soil pH, soil moisture, and bulk density for all three depths. The cover of plant functional groups and litter was visually recorded at all sites. We found the highest SOC content (23.4 ± 7.2 g kg−1) and the widest C:N ratio (10.2) at 1 m from the tree trunk compared with those further away. Soil pH, soil moisture content, and bulk density were similar at all distances from the tree trunk. Earthworm abundance was lowest at 1 m (173.3 ± 31.8 ind m−2) and highest at 24 m (225.1 ± 30.8 ind m−2) distance from the tree trunk, but did not differ significantly with distance from the tree trunk or with any of the analysed soil properties. Earthworm composition, however, varied along the distance gradient, with anecic species occurring closer to the tree trunk and endogeic species dominating the open grassland, though these patterns were only marginal after correction for multiple testing. Our results show that solitary oak trees create distinct chemical fertility islands in wood-pasture topsoils, but this enrichment does not ensure greater earthworm abundance. The findings suggest that organic matter quality and accessibility, rather than quantity alone, may shape the observed functional-group patterns.

Abstract
Himalayan grasslands are ecologically sensitive ecosystems facing accelerating degradation from intensive livestock grazing. This study investigated the capacity of soil physicochemical properties to classify grazing disturbance intensity in the bugyal grasslands of the Chakrata region, Uttarakhand, India. Ninety soil samples were collected during a single pre-monsoon campaign in April 2026 from three disturbance classes (non-disturbed, moderately disturbed, and highly disturbed) across two high-altitude grassland sites (Moila Bugyal and Koti Kanasar Bugyal), with thirty samples per class. Six parameters were measured: soil organic carbon, available nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, bulk density, and soil pH. The data were analysed using two-way analysis of variance, Tukey’s post-hoc test, and permutational multivariate analysis of variance; three machine learning classifiers (Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Gradient Boosting Machine) were evaluated using five-fold cross-validation. Soil organic carbon declined most sharply from non-disturbed to highly disturbed sites (5.71% to 3.39%; -40.7%), available nitrogen fell by 34.5%, and bulk density increased by 19.5%. The disturbance gradient explained 42.5% of the total multivariate soil variance (PERMANOVA). Random Forest achieved the highest classification accuracy (86.3 ± 7.5%; kappa = 0.795), with soil organic carbon identified as the most discriminative feature (36.7% of feature importance). The results indicate that soil physicochemical properties can support machine learning-based classification of grazing disturbance in Himalayan alpine terrain.

Abstract
As endocrine-disrupting chemicals, plasticizers have become an increasing concern in agricultural environments and food systems due to their widespread use and environmental persistence. Due to their persistence in the environment and migration capacity, plasticizers can be absorbed and accumulated by plants, and may pose health risks to humans through the food chain. This study investigated the pollution characteristics of 16 priority phthalate esters (PAEs) in peppers from pepper planting bases in Chongqing. The occurrence of PAEs in soils and different pepper tissues was characterized, and their enrichment, translocation behavior, and associated human health risks were evaluated. The results revealed that DBP, DIBP, and DEHP were the predominant PAE congeners detected in both soil and pepper samples. The accumulation pattern of PAEs in roots, stems, leaves and fruits followed the order of DBP > DIBP > DEHP. According to the enrichment and translocation coefficients, the roots and above-ground parts of peppers had the strongest enrichment capacity for DBP, and DBP, DIBP and DEHP were all easy to migrate from roots to above-ground parts. Although the estimated dietary exposure risks of PAEs through chili pepper consumption were within acceptable levels in the investigated cultivation systems, the widespread application of agricultural plastics and their potential contribution to long-term PAE accumulation highlight the importance of continuous monitoring and sustainable plastic management strategies. This study highlights the combined influence of soil contamination and atmospheric deposition on PAE accumulation in fruit vegetables, hence providing scientific evidence to support environmental risk assessment and management of chili pepper cultivation in plastic-intensive agriculture systems.

Abstract
Sericulture generates large volumes of organic residues that are rarely reintegrated into production systems. We evaluated a closed-loop system in which silkworm (Bombyx mori) frass, uneaten leaves and larval litter were composted for 2.5 months in an aerated, moisture-controlled vessel and applied (1.74 kg/m2) to a mulberry (Morus alba L.) plantation following a 2024 baseline period, with compost amendments and subsequent assessments carried out over the 2024–2026 seasons, in a randomized paired-plot field comparison design (one control and one compost-amended plot). The preliminary results showed that compost amendment was associated with an increase, compared to the control group, in soil humus by 44% (5.20% vs. 3.61%), organic carbon by 44% (3.01% vs. 2.09%) and total nitrogen by 60% (0.32% vs. 0.20%), while lowering the C/N ratio from 10.37 to 9.36 (all p ≤ 0.011), without altering pH (p = 0.153); sodicity indicators remained low (exchangeable sodium percentage ≤ 2.3%). Exchangeable K+ increased 2.6-fold (5.85 vs. 2.28 me/100 g soil). In leaves, compost amendment correlated with a significant increase in the experimental group compared to the control group for Ca by 51% (3.05% vs. 2.02%), Mg by 19% or 1.19-fold (0.31% vs. 0.26%), Cu by 36% (8.38 vs. 6.14 mg/kg) and Mn by 96% (59.20 vs. 30.28 mg/kg) (p ≤ 0.02), while chlorophyll A and carotenoids increased by 12% (20.78 vs. 18.61 mg/100 g FW) and 31% (10.76 vs. 8.19 mg/100 g FW), respectively. Fifth-instar larvae fed leaves from amended plots ingested 14% more dry matter (524.63 g vs. 459.64 g) and 8% more nitrogen (26.16 mg/day vs. 24.29 mg/day) and 33% more ether extract (8.88 vs. 6.69 mg/day) relative to the control group (p ≤ 0.001). These preliminary observational results suggest that the application of sericulture waste compost is linked to trends of enhanced soil fertility and higher leaf nutrient content. Unlike previous studies that evaluated sericulture by-products as isolated soil amendments, this work provides an associative assessment of parameters measured across the soil–plant–silkworm–compost loop, offering evidence for the potential benefits of diverting sericulture by-products away from landfills and converting them into high-quality organic compost, although final crop yield and sericultural performance endpoints remain to be quantified.
