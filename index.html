Pasture Management and Mineral Fertilization on Soil Properties, Plant Nutrition, and Biomass Production of Urochloa decumbens in Tropical LixisolsCORE7
Authors: Tiago de Carvalho Pessoa, T. A. F. de Souza, V. da Silva Fraga, João Henrique Melo, F. D. de Oliveira...
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Pasture management and mineral fertilization are key factors influencing soil fertility and pasture yield. However, their effects on the soil properties of Lixisols in the Brazilian semi-arid region, and their subsequent impact on Urochloa decumbens biomass production, remain unclear. We compared soil properties between short- and long-term pastures and evaluated how fertilization affects the growth of U. decumbens. The grass was cultivated in pots with soil subjected to four treatments: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), combined NP fertilization, and an unfertilized control. We assessed shoot dry biomass, plant responsiveness to fertilization, plant N and P concentrations, and the fertilization effect on nutrient uptake. Long-term managed pasture exhibited higher levels of K+, soil pH, available P, and total organic carbon, whereas short-term degraded pasture had higher total nitrogen, Mg2+, H+, Al3+, and cation exchange capacity. NP fertilization significantly enhanced U. decumbens growth in both pasture conditions, resulting in greater shoot dry biomass, higher responsiveness to fertilization, and improved N and P uptake compared with the control. In addition, N and P fertilization applied individually promoted the accumulation of their respective nutrients in plant tissues, with N fertilization resulting in the highest plant N content and P fertilization resulting in the highest plant P content. Our findings suggest that (i) short-term degraded pasture management altered soil nutrient status, which may be associated with changes in nutrient cycling and soil chemical properties, and (ii) adequate fertilization in short-term degraded pastures can enhance U. decumbens biomass production.
Nitrogen deposition does not alter the temperate forest soil carbon pool: A 14-y experiment on varied nitrogen fertilization.CORE6
Authors: Yun-Yu Zhang, S. Hobbie, W. Schlesinger, Tao Sun
Venue: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil carbon (C) pools in nitrogen-limited temperate forests are critical to the global C cycle, but there are no long-term experiments examining soil C, with various nitrogen (N) forms. Here, a 14-y experiment (100 kg N ha-1 y-1), with added N simulating atmospheric deposition proportions (~70% inorganic, ~30% organic) did not affect soil C pools, whereas organic N (ON) and inorganic N (IN) individually significantly increased values by 2.7-fold and 1.8-fold, respectively. Although no treatment significantly affected soil pH, treatment effects on pH were negatively associated with soil C pools. In addition, treatments containing ON forms increased mineral-associated organic C fractions, indicating enhanced C stability vs. IN. These findings enhance our understanding of N deposition-soil C pool interactions.
Tillage-Driven Shifts in Rhizosphere Microbial Communities and Their Associations with Soil Chemical Properties in Dryland Maize of Western LiaoningCORE4
Authors: Yu-Cong Miao, Shi-Yu Zhang, Qian Cai, Wei Bai
Venue: Agriculture
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Tillage is an important agronomic practice that regulates soil quality and microbial ecology. Nevertheless, in-depth evidence on how tillage shapes rhizosphere microbial diversity remains limited. To evaluate the long-term effects of tillage on microbial community structure and diversity in the spring maize rhizosphere four treatments with three replicates have been set up since 2009—rotary tillage (RT), subsoiling (ST), deep ploughing (DT), and no-tillage (NT). Rhizosphere soil samples were collected at the maize harvest stage in 2025 after 17 years of long-term experiment. High-throughput sequencing and soil chemical analyses were used to compare bacterial and fungal diversity and to identify the principal drivers of community differentiation. Compared with the other treatments, NT decreased soil pH by 4.78–7.90%, available phosphorus (AP) by 8.03–22.53%, and available potassium (AK) by 20.43–48.61%, while increasing total carbon (TC) by 8.15–13.29%. Compared with RT, DT increased AP and AK concentrations by 18.72% and 54.84%, respectively. NT significantly decreased the Chao1 and Shannon indices of both bacteria and fungi relative to RT, ST, and DT. For bacteria, the Chao1 index under NT was 18.86–40.18% lower, and the Shannon index was 5.36–8.58% lower than the other three tillage treatments. For fungi, the reduction was more pronounced: the Chao1 index decreased by 35.88–51.32%, and the Shannon index decreased by 15.43–30.70%. ST and DT increased rhizosphere microbial richness and diversity, with similar diversity levels between ST and DT. Redundancy analysis further revealed that soil pH, TC, AP, AK and AN were key factors driving rhizosphere microbial community distribution. Overall, compared with NT, ST and DT improved rhizosphere nutrient status and enhanced microbial richness and diversity.
Synergistic effects of biochar and organic fertilizer on the regulation of microbial carbon and nitrogen metabolism in paddy soil to reduce net greenhouse gas emissions.PROXY4
Authors: Xiao-Qiang Cao, Zhen-Nan Zang, Ji-Long Liu, Guo-Long Zhan, Guo-Xin Shi...
Venue: Journal of environmental management
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs
Abstract
Biochar and organic fertilizer have been widely applied to improve soil carbon sequestration and mitigate greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in rice production systems. However, the synergistic mechanisms through which these amendments regulate microbial carbon/nitrogen (C/N) metabolism, net GHG emissions, and crop productivity in dry direct-seeded rice (DDSR) systems remain insufficiently understood. In this study, a two-year field experiment was conducted with combined treatments of organic fertilizer substitution (0% and 20%) and biochar application (0, 5, and 10 t/hm2) to evaluate their integrated effects on GHG emissions, soil properties, rice yield, and microbial functional genes involved in C/N cycling. Biochar and organic fertilizer significantly reduced CH4 and N2O emissions, leading to lower global warming potential (GWP), greenhouse gas emission intensity (GHGI), and net greenhouse gas emissions (NGHGE), while simultaneously increasing rice yield. The combined application of 20% organic fertilizer substitution with 5 t/hm2 biochar achieved the optimal performance, increasing yield by 11.64%-15.44% and reducing GWP and GHGI by 7.58%-16.56% and 17.21%-27.72%, respectively. Metagenomic analysis revealed that the combined application modified microbial community structure while regulated microbial C/N metabolism by reshaping key functional pathways. The enhancement of Calvin-Benson-Basham (CBB) pathway was related to the increased abundance of PRK and prkB under biochar conditions; meanwhile, reduced abundances of aclA, aclB, and ccsA were associated with suppressed reductive tricarboxylic acid (rTCA) cycle. Concurrently, denitrification and nitrification pathways showed positive correlations with increased abundances of nirK, norB, and nosZ. Redundancy analysis (RDA) and mantel analyses indicated that soil organic carbon (SOC), pH, ammonium nitrogen (NH4+-N), and nitrate nitrogen (NO3--N) were the primary drivers shaping microbial functional gene structure. Overall, the synergistic application of biochar and organic fertilizer effectively coordinated carbon sequestration, emission reduction, and yield enhancement, providing a feasible strategy for low-carbon and sustainable management of DDSR.
Gradient Organic Fertilizer Substitution Reshapes Soil Microecology and Microbial Carbon Utilization in Tea PlantationsCORE3

Abstract
Organic fertilizer substitution is widely used to improve acidified tea plantation soils, but the coupled effects of substitution gradients on soil aggregates, microbial carbon utilization, and microbial C–N–P limitation remain insufficiently quantified. This study aimed to determine how gradient organic fertilizer substitution regulates soil physicochemical properties, aggregate-size distribution, extracellular enzyme stoichiometry, and microbial carbon-source utilization in a subtropical tea plantation, and to identify a suitable substitution range under the experimental conditions. A long-term field experiment was conducted in an acidic red-soil tea plantation in Wuyishan, Fujian Province, China. Five treatments were established: pure chemical fertilizer (CF) and organic fertilizer substituting 33% (OF33), 50% (OF50), 67% (OF67), and 100% (OF100) of chemical fertilizer nitrogen. Tea growth indices, soil physicochemical properties, aggregate mechanical stability, microbial biomass C, N, and P (MBC, MBN, and MBP), extracellular enzyme activities, and BIOLOG-ECO carbon-source utilization profiles were measured. Results showed that OF33 and OF67 increased soil pH by 0.46–0.60 units relative to CF. OF33 generally improved available N and K and aggregate stability indices, whereas OF50 increased 100-bud weight and microbial carbon-source richness. OF100 increased MBC but also increased vector length and vector angle, indicating stronger relative C and P limitation. BIOLOG-ECO profiles separated treatments, and moderate substitution (OF33–OF50) broadened the carbon-source utilization niche. However, different indicators peaked under different treatments; therefore, 33–50% substitution should be regarded as a suitable range under the conditions of this experiment rather than a universally optimal rate. These findings provide a site- and cultivar-specific basis for reducing chemical fertilizer use in red-soil tea plantations.
Cropping System-driven Changes in Phosphorous Fractionation and Soil Fertility in Acid SoilsCORE3

Venue: Journal of Advances in Biology &amp; Biotechnology
Published: 2026-10-16

Abstract
Phosphorus (P) availability in acidic soils is predominantly influenced by its transformation into various inorganic and organic forms, which are further affected by cropping systems and associated nutrient management. The present study was conducted at the ICR farm, Assam Agricultural University, to assess the distribution of different P fractions in acid soils under seven intensive cropping systems, with three replications in a randomised block design, following five consecutive years of cultivation. Soil samples (0–30 cm) collected from each cropping sequence were analysed for major soil properties, available nutrients, total P, organic-P and different inorganic P fractions, including saloid-P, Al-P, Fe-P and Ca-P. Continuous cultivation improved soil pH, organic carbon and available N, P2O5 and K2O compared with the nearby uncultivated soil (virgin soil). The different phosphorus fractions varied significantly across the cropping-system soils. The inorganic P distribution was dominated by Fe-P, followed by Al-P, Ca-P and saloid-P, irrespective of the cropping system. The contributions of the P fractions to total P followed the order Fe-P > Al-P > Org-P > Ca-P > Sa-P. Correlation analysis indicated strong associations between soil pH and Fe-P and Al-P, whereas organic-P was positively associated with soil organic carbon. Thus, the findings demonstrated that five consecutive years of intensive cultivation and associated nutrient management substantially affected soil phosphorus pools and their distribution, highlighting the significance of balanced nutrient management and diversified cropping systems in sustaining different P pools in acidic soils.

Abstract
Selenium (Se) is an essential trace element for the human body, and farmland soil is the primary source of Se in the food chain. Ximeng County is a typical Se-rich plateau region in Yunnan, China. The study area was selected from a representative region of Ximeng County. A total of 136 farmland soil samples were collected in the study area to determine and analyze the effects of soil factors (soil pH, organic matter, clay content, sand content, silt content), topographic factors (elevation, slope, aspect), climatic factors (runoff, rainfall, temperature), and natural factors (land use type, soil type) on soil Se concentration. Using Geodetector analysis, the study assessed the extent to which these factors influence soil Se concentration and the impact of interactions among them. The results indicate that the average total Se content in the study area was 0.48 mg·kg−1, exceeding the background values for soil elements in Yunnan Province. In terms of spatial distribution, total Se and available Se were primarily concentrated in the southeastern and western parts of the study area; using the factor explorer in Geodetector, it was found that elevation, rainfall, runoff, temperature, and silt content significantly influenced the concentrations and spatial distribution of total Se and available Se, with elevation having the greatest impact. Interaction analysis revealed that all pairwise interactions among the 13 influencing factors exhibited nonlinear two-factor enhancement. Among these, the interaction between slope and elevation provides the strongest explanation for the spatial variation in total Se and available Se. A combined correlation and Geodetector analysis revealed that the Se enrichment in the study area is primarily influenced by the combined effects of elevation, climatic factors (runoff, rainfall, temperature), and organic matter content. This study is of great significance for land resource management in Se-rich areas, the development of Se-rich agricultural products, and the implementation of targeted policies.

Abstract
This study examined the hydrobiological characteristics of Ped Lake in Tasgaon tehsil, Sangli district, Maharashtra, alongside surrounding soil properties, vegetation patterns and standing-tree carbon estimates. The field investigation was conducted during 2024–2025. Water samples were collected from six sites, while soil properties were assessed at five sites. Water pH ranged from 8.14 to 8.80, electrical conductivity from 0.39 to 0.42 dS/m, total dissolved solids from 255 to 266 mg/L, dissolved oxygen from 5.3 to 5.9 mg/L and biochemical oxygen demand from 3.8 to 4.9 mg/L. Towards predictive ecological biogeography: predicting vegetation type using fine-scale measurements of the abiotic environmentCORE3
Authors: Karl H. Palmquist, S. B. Fedeli, Nicolas Lenner, S. Leibler
Venue: bioRxiv
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
One of the goals of ecological biogeography is to accurately predict where major vegetation types, such as forests or grasslands, can establish themselves. Alas, our current understanding of the mechanisms by which environmental variables jointly influence vegetation types does not allow for such predictions. Here, we compile a database of local measurements of soil, weather, topography, and bedrock lithology for 6404 sites across the continental United States, which we complement with information about the vegetation around the measurement sites. By applying machine learning methods to a subset of the sites, we are able to train a machine that predicts - using only information about abiotic variables - the presence of forests or grasslands with nearly 90% accuracy on the rest of the sites. We find that while weather variables are statistically important for these predictions, a few soil variables prove to be the most powerful predictors. By separating the combined contributions of weather and soil, we show that soil texture is a better predictor under dry conditions than under wet conditions, while pH is important predictor regardless of the weather. Furthermore, from 136 sites undergoing grassland-to-forest succession, our analysis indicates that soil pH changes with a certain lag relative to vegetation change. Sites that have recently undergone a transition exhibit a higher pH, closer to that of grasslands, despite the presence of trees. The pH then decreases by about one unit over a 30-year period of forest development. Ultimately, our results indicate that fine-scale environmental measurements and historical ecological data can serve as a basis for quantitative bio-geographical predictions, which could eventually guide targeted conservation interventions.
Two decades of conservation agriculture enhances soil structure, carbon sequestration, and water retention in Mediterranean soilsCORE3
Authors: Jennifer Alvarez-Sagrero, A. Berhe, S. Chacon, Jeffrey P. Mitchell, T. Ghezzehei
Venue: SOIL
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Conservation agriculture offers a pathway for enhancing soil health with climate co-benefits in Mediterranean agricultural systems. This study examined long-term impacts of combining reduced tillage with cover cropping over 20 years in California's Central Valley, providing insights into soil system response under sustained conservation management. We assessed soil physical, chemical, and structural properties comparing reduced tillage with cover crops (CTCC) to standard tillage without cover crops (STNC), employing density fractionation and spectroscopic analysis to understand carbon protection mechanisms. After two decades, conservation agriculture produced soil conditions consistent with the approach toward a management-induced steady state described for systems managed for 15–20 years (Caruso et al., 2018; West and Post, 2002), characterized by shifts in carbon stabilization pathways. Water-stable aggregate analysis revealed the most pronounced management effects, with CTCC exhibiting 136 % greater stability than STNC, indicating substantial improvements in soil structural integrity. These structural enhancements corresponded with a reorganization of carbon protection mechanisms: CTCC disproportionately enriched the occluded light fraction (44.1 % vs. 35.4 % of total recovered carbon in STNC), indicating that aggregate-mediated physical protection becomes a dominant carbon stabilization pathway under long-term conservation management. Mineral-associated organic carbon saturation analysis revealed that both management systems remained well below theoretical maximum capacity (11.5 % vs. 7.4 % saturation for CTCC and STNC, respectively), indicating that mineral surface availability is not the primary constraint on carbon storage in this Mediterranean system. Physical property improvements under CTCC included 15 % lower bulk density and 13 % greater water retention at field capacity, though benefits were concentrated in the surface horizon. Our findings demonstrate that two decades of conservation agriculture substantially altered soil functioning through aggregate-mediated physical protection, while creating measurable improvements in soil structural integrity and water retention capacity. These mechanism shifts are consistent with a soil system that maintains enhanced functionality and continued carbon sequestration potential in Mediterranean agricultural systems.

Abstract
Wearable colorimetric pH-sensing textiles provide a power-free, noninvasive platform for healthcare and environmental monitoring, yet their sustainable deployment was hindered by short service life, dye leaching-induced aquatic pollution, and substrate-dependent manufacturing with redundant process development. Herein, a universal two-step reactive polymer coating strategy is developed to fabricate robust pH-sensing textiles independent of inherent fiber chemistry, with catalyst-free ambient fabrication and covalent dye immobilization as core sustainable advances. An amine-rich cross-linked interlayer was first formed in situ on textile surfaces via aza-Michael addition between dipentaerythritol pentaacrylate (5Acl) and branched polyethylenimine (BPEI), offering abundant covalent anchoring sites and a tunable microenvironment to modulate the chromophore acid–base response. Acrylate-functionalized nitrazine yellow (GMA-NY) was then covalently grafted through a secondary Michael addition, imparting reversible pH-sensing capability. The sensor exhibits reversible yellow–green–blue trichromatic transitions over pH 1.0–8.0, only a 6% color strength loss after 10 washing cycles, and a tailored apparent pKa (3.13 ± 0.65) for early bacterial infection warning. Compatible with diverse fibers and extendable to rewritable information recording and vapor detection, this substrate-agnostic platform establishes a scalable, sustainable design paradigm for wearable sensors.
Economic Trade and Atmospheric Transport in Shaping China’s Nitrogen Deposition PatternsPROXY2
Authors: Jing-Xu Wang, Zheng-Zhong Liu, He-Ran Zheng, Li-Fang Sheng, Yuan-Hong Zhao...
Venue: Environmental Science &amp; Technology
Published: 2026-10-16
Abs
Abstract
Atmospheric nitrogen deposition in China is driven not only by local emissions but also by global economic demand and transboundary atmospheric transport. Unraveling these dual influences is critical for effective environmental governance. Here, we integrate an economic-emission analysis with a high-resolution atmospheric chemical transport model to quantify, for the first time, the distinct roles of domestic consumption, international trade, and atmospheric transport in shaping China’s nitrogen deposition patterns. We find that domestic consumption is the dominant contributor, responsible for 67.6% of the total deposition, involving a substantial interprovincial transfer of emissions from affluent coastal provinces to less developed inland regions. International trade attributes 7.8% of China’s deposition to foreign consumption, primarily driven by demand from the USA and Western Europe. In contrast, trans-national atmospheric transport directly accounts for 12.8%, with a disproportionately high impact on ecologically sensitive western China. Natural sources contribute the remaining 11.8%. Our results reveal a complex web of drivers extending beyond China’s borders. Mitigating nitrogen deposition, therefore, requires moving beyond local emission controls to embrace consumption-based accountability and enhanced international cooperation on both trade and atmospheric pollution to protect vulnerable ecosystems.

Abstract
Background: Rivers are essential freshwater resources, although increasing urbanisation and industrial activities have led to significant water quality degradation. The Kalundre River in Panvel, Maharashtra, India, flows through diverse land-use areas, including residential, agricultural, and urban zones, making it susceptible to pollution from domestic sewage, industrial effluents, and urban runoff. Aim: This study examined physicochemical conditions along the Kalundre River, Maharashtra, at four sampling sites representing different levels of human influence. Methods: A cross-sectional observational study was conducted at four sampling sites along the river, representing upstream (minimal impact), midstream (moderate impact), and downstream (high urban/industrial influence). Water samples were collected at a depth of 20–30 cm using sterilized bottles and analysed according to APHA standard methods. Parameters measured included pH, electrical conductivity (EC), total dissolved solids (TDS), dissolved oxygen (DO), biological oxygen demand (BOD), chemical oxygen demand (COD), alkalinity, and major ions (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, HCO₃⁻, Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻). Upstream S1 showed good water quality, S2 was moderately polluted due to domestic discharge, and S3–S4 exhibited poor to very poor conditions influenced by urban sewage and industrial effluents. Major ions, such as Cl⁻ and SO₄²⁻ highlighted anthropogenic contributions from sewage and industrial sources. Conclusion: The study demonstrates the strong impact of urbanisation and industrialisation on river water quality. The downstream degradation emphasises the need for effective wastewater management, pollution control, and sustainable water resource management to protect river ecosystems.

Abstract
Understanding mineral dissolution in heterogeneous porous media is critical for predicting the evolution of subsurface systems during geological CO2 storage. Here, we integrate time-resolved synchrotron techniques, 4D X-ray microtomography (μ-CT), X-ray fluorescence (TR-XRF), and X-ray diffraction (TR-XRD) to investigate acid-induced dissolution in cemented sandstone from the Rio Bonito Formation in the Paraná Basin (South America) across multiple spatial scales. The results reveal that dissolution is strongly controlled by mineralogical heterogeneity and fluid accessibility, resulting in spatially localized, temporally evolving reaction fronts. Carbonate cement (calcite) dissolves rapidly under acidic conditions, as evidenced by a sharp depletion of the Ca signal (TR-XRF) and a continuous reduction in diffraction intensity (TR-XRD), driving early-stage porosity enhancement observed by μ-CT. In contrast, silicate phases (microcline) exhibit slower, surface-limited dissolution accompanied by partial structural modification, while quartz remains comparatively unreactive under the investigated conditions and experimental time frame. Dissolution rates show an exponential dependence on pH, confirming the dominant role of proton activity in controlling reaction kinetics. This behavior has direct implications for predicting injectivity evolution and geochemical buffering during the early stages of CO2 injection. Integrating structural, chemical, and mineralogical observations establishes a mechanistic link between phase-specific reactivity and pore-scale evolution. These findings highlight the importance of reservoir mineralogy, petrophysical properties, and diagenetic history in governing reactive transport processes. The results provide experimental constraints for reactive transport models addressing permeability evolution and early-stage geochemical alteration in CO2 storage systems.

Abstract
The recovery of silicon from End-of-Life photovoltaic panels represents a fundamental step toward a sustainable energy transition, significantly reducing environmental impact, minimizing the production of electronic waste, and fostering new economic prospects. This study evaluates two distinct chemical purification strategies for silicon cells obtained from decommissioned solar panels, aiming to assess their effectiveness in removing silver and aluminum. Specifically, we compare a conventional multi-step NaOH/HNO3 treatment with a novel, patented ultrasound-assisted citric acid approach. Importantly, these two methods were also selected for their contrasting safety, operation conditions, and environmental profiles. Our findings reveal that the traditional base/acid leaching using NaOH/HNO3 is the most effective, achieving almost complete removal of aluminum (96%) and silver (100%). In contrast, the ultrasound-assisted citric acid treatment, while offering a safer and simpler alternative, exhibits a lower impurity removal rate (69% for aluminum, 83% for silver). This result presents a clear trade-off between final purity and process safety: the standard base/acid treatment using NaOH/HNO3 yields a higher purity material (92.6 wt% for Si, 0.3 wt% for Al), while the citric acid-based approach produces a powder with a slightly lower purity (89.4 wt% for Si, 2.3 wt% for Al, 0.2 wt% for Ag). Nevertheless, the citric acid-based strategy is highly promising due to citric acid's superior safety, cost-effectiveness, and eco-friendly nature, coupled with a single-step purification process. Furthermore, the use of citric acid allows for the metallic/solid recovery of leached silver and aluminum, eliminating the need for reduction and/or electrodeposition processes. Industrial applications suitable for the two powder compositions will be briefly discussed on the basis of published literature.

Abstract
One of the most significant problems facing different ecosystems worldwide is the pollution of soil and water resources with heavy metals, which are harmful to biota. Metals are absorbed by plant roots through soil-plant interaction, leading to accumulation in plants. The plant's growth phase is one of the most important factors in heavy metal uptake and accumulation. Among heavy metal elements, nickel is one of the serious environmental pollutants and plant growth inhibitors due to its toxicity and persistent nature at high concentrations, as well as its bioaccumulation in ecosystems. This study aims to investigate how nickel accumulation in plant organs (roots, stems and leaves) and morphological characteristics (plant height, stem thickness, dry weights of roots, stems and leaves and root length) of silage maize seedlings at different growth stages (0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 days) change when exposed to different nickel (Ni) concentrations (0, 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 mg kg-1). Results showed that the highest Ni accumulation  was obtained in the 20-day-old seedlings, while the lowest value was observed in the 10-day-old seedlings. The highest plant height, stem thickness, dry leaf weight, and dry stem weight were mostly observed in 5-day-old seedlings. In the study, the treatment of 500 and 600 mg kg-1 Ni to the plants resulted in plant death, while the treatment of 200 mg kg-1 Ni led to improvements in morphological characteristics.  In terms of animal feed, 10-day-old seedlings are considered more advantageous because they contain less Ni compared to those grown from seed and other age groups, whereas 20-day-old seedlings are not recommended due to both their high Ni accumulation and weaker morphological characteristics.  Considering the remediation capacity, we can say that 20-day-old seedlings may have the potential to be evaluated in areas contaminated with 300 and 400 mg kg-1 Ni.
