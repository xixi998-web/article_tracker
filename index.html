Sulfur, organic, and inorganic soil acidification effects on HLB-affected citrus tree productivityCORE10
Authors: Duplicate Sambani, Lorenzo Rossi, I. T. Ayankojo, Hossein Ghoveisi, D. Bright...
Venue: Frontiers in Plant Science
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil amendments are important for improving soil quality and increasing nutrient availability, especially in citrus production systems affected by Huanglongbing (HLB). This study evaluated the effects of humic acid (HA), fulvic acid (FA), and sulfuric acid (SA), applied with or without elemental sulfur (S), on soil pH, nutrient availability, and the performance of HLB-affected citrus trees at two Florida sites. A factorial experiment arranged in a randomized complete block design was used with S application and acid amendment (control, HA, FA, or SA) as experimental factors. HA acid with S produced more pronounced time-dependent acidification. At Fort Meade, root-density responses were more evident during the earlier study years under selected FA and SA treatments, whereas root density at Lake Alfred was comparatively unresponsive to the amendments. Yield responses were inconsistent. At Fort Meade, S increased yield in 2023 when averaged across treatments, although this response was largely concentrated in the control without sulfur and was not observed when S was combined with HA, FA, or SA. In other site-year combinations, S reduced yield, including within FA at Fort Meade in 2024 and within selected treatments at Lake Alfred. Fruit quality responses likewise depended on site, year, and amendment combination. At Fort Meade, sulfur altered total soluble solids (TSS), titratable acidity (TA), TSS: TA ratio, pounds solids, and juice percentage only in certain treatment-year combinations, with no detectable treatment differences in 2023. At Lake Alfred, S improved some fruit quality traits in selected combinations during 2022 and 2023, but worsened others in 2024. Overall, soil amendment responses were conditional rather than uniform, highlighting the importance of evaluating amendment strategies under specific soil and production conditions when managing HLB-affected citrus.
N, P, and K Fertilization Differentially Regulates Catalpa bungei Growth and Soil Greenhouse Gas FluxesCORE4
Authors: Wen-Wen Yuan, Li-Fen Ji, Zi-Yi Zhang, Xiao-Min Ma, Ding-Gen Ji...
Venue: Plants
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Fertilization can enhance plantation productivity but may also alter soil greenhouse gas (GHG) fluxes. We conducted a two-year field experiment in a Catalpa bungei plantation to evaluate the effects of individual and combined nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) additions on tree growth, soil microbial properties, and soil CH4 and CO2 fluxes. Eight treatments (CK, N, P, K, NP, NK, PK, and NPK) were applied. Fertilization enhanced diameter at breast height (DBH) growth but did not significantly affect height growth. NP and NPK increased annual DBH increment by 37.5–59.4%, and soil N availability was an important predictor of DBH growth. Fertilization increased the corresponding soil nutrient pools and microbial biomass N, P, and K, whereas soil pH, soil organic carbon, and microbial biomass C showed no significant responses. NP increased methane monooxygenase (MMO) level by 20.9%, while P and NPK increased β-glucosidase activity by 16%; No significant treatment effect was detected for the immunoreactive MCR level. NP increased soil CH4 uptake by 59.7%, whereas P increased soil CO2 efflux by 68.4%. Path analysis suggested an indirect association linking available P, microbial biomass P, and β-glucosidase activity to CO2 efflux. These findings highlight contrasting growth and soil gas responses to nutrient addition and suggest potential microbial pathways associated with these responses. Evaluating fertilization strategies should therefore consider tree growth together with soil C exchange.
Land-Use Type Regulates Soil Macrofaunal Community Structure via Soil Properties in a Reclaimed Coastal Area, ChinaCORE4

Venue: Animals
Published: 2026-11-19

Abstract
Coastal reclamation creates a mosaic of land uses whose long-term consequences for soil biodiversity remain poorly resolved. We examined how land-use type shapes soil macrofaunal communities through soil physicochemical properties in a century-old reclaimed coastal area of Jiangsu Province, eastern China. Four land-use types sharing a common reclamation history were selected: young and mature dawn redwood forests, a ginkgo forest, and a dryland–woodland system. Soil properties and hand-sorted macrofauna were sampled in September 2022 (five replicate plots per land use); differences were assessed by analysis of variance, principal component analysis, Mantel tests, and redundancy analysis. Soils differed markedly: pH, available phosphorus, and available potassium were highest in the dryland–woodland; electrical conductivity and soil organic matter were highest in the young forest; and water content and available nitrogen were highest in the ginkgo forest. A total of 1352 individuals in 26 taxonomic groups were collected. The dryland–woodland supported the greatest taxonomic richness and diversity, the ginkgo forest the highest abundance, and the mature forest the lowest. Total carbon was the only soil property that significantly explained community variation. Land use, acting through carbon- and water-related soil properties, modulates the reassembly of soil macrofaunal communities, supporting their potential utility as bioindicators in reclaimed coastal landscapes.
Assessing Agricultural Land Capacity as a Proxy for Drought Resilience Under Climate Change in MongoliaCORE4
Authors: Temuulen Tsogbayar, Altangerel Tsogtsaikhan, B. Odgerel
Venue: Mongolian Geoscientist
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF
Abstract
This study assesses agricultural land capacity as a proxy indicator of drought resilience under climate change in Mongolia's central agricultural region. Increasing drought frequency and climate variability have intensified environmental constraints on agricultural production, highlighting the need for spatially explicit approaches to identify resilient agricultural areas. Despite increasing climate risks, parcel-level assessments that integrate multiple environmental factors to evaluate agricultural land capacity and drought resilience remain limited. To address this gap, the study develops an integrated Geographic Information System-based Multi-Criteria Analysis framework to evaluate agricultural land capacity across 16,895 agricultural parcels covering 918,360.5 hectares. Nine environmental variables-precipitation, temperature, soil moisture, irrigation availability, elevation, slope, soil organic matter, soil pH, and the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index-were standardized and integrated to develop a Land Capacity Index, which was used as a proxy indicator of drought resilience. The results show that 77.5% of agricultural land falls within the low and very low land-capacity categories, suggesting limited environmental capacity to withstand drought across much of the study area. Areas classified as having relatively high or high land capacity account for only 9.5% of the total agricultural land area and are concentrated mainly in Selenge Province, followed by Khentii. Precipitation, soil moisture, and vegetation productivity emerged as key factors influencing land capacity and its potential as a proxy for drought resilience. These findings suggest that agricultural land capacity may serve as a practical biophysical proxy for drought resilience in data-scarce environments. The proposed framework offers a replicable tool for climate-adaptive land-use planning, agricultural risk management, and drought risk reduction, supporting evidence-based decision-making for sustainable agricultural development in Mongolia.

Abstract
Artisanal and small‐scale gold mining in the southeastern Amazonian department of Madre de Dios, Peru, has caused severe soil degradation characterized by extreme sand dominance, nutrient depletion, and disrupted microbial communities. Conventional restoration approaches have often resulted insufficient, highlighting the need for evidence‐based strategies. This study evaluated five legume cover crops— Stylosanthes guianensis , Pueraria phaseoloides , Canavalia ensiformis , Centrosema macrocarpum , and Crotalaria juncea —and a control as nature‐based solutions for soil health recovery. Plots were arranged in a completely randomized block design with three replicates, comparing treatments and post‐establishment years (11 and 22 months). Soil Quality Index (SQI) integrating 15 biological, physical, and chemical indicators through principal component analysis was developed to quantify soil recovery. All cover crops improved soil quality relative to bare controls (SQI = 0.070), reaching values between 0.092 and 0.197, although still below the reference forest benchmark (0.461). Stylosanthes showed the highest SQI (0.197), associated with greater aggregate stability and increased actinobacterial abundance, while Pueraria promoted strong fungal proliferation and enhanced potassium availability; Canavalia also improved potassium levels. Microbial respiration increased across all treatments compared with the control, and biomass production was highest under Stylosanthes , suggesting greater potential for organic matter inputs. Overall, the SQI framework captured multidimensional recovery patterns and revealed complementary ecological functions among species. Although limitations in organic matter content and cation exchange capacity persisted, these findings demonstrate the short‐term potential of legume cover crops to initiate soil recovery processes and support long‐term ecological restoration in Amazonian ecosystems affected by gold mining.

Abstract
Biological invasions are major drivers of ecological change, often altering soil nutrientcycling and ecosystem functioning. This study investigated the effects of the invasive alienplant Conyza canadensis on soil properties and nutrient dynamics across several sites inthe Kashmir Himalaya. At each site, paired invaded (IN) and uninvaded (UN) plots withcomparable environmental conditions were selected. Soil samples were analyzed forphysicochemical properties and nutrient concentrations. Significant differences (P < 0.05)were observed between invaded and uninvaded plots for all measured soil variablesacross the study sites. Invaded plots exhibited significantly higher soil pH, organic carbon,and total nitrogen, whereas electrical conductivity, available phosphorus, andmicronutrients, including iron, copper, manganese, and zinc, were significantly lower.These patterns were consistent across all sampled locations, indicating a strong influence of C. canadensis on soil conditions. Effective management of C. canadensis,together with restoration of altered soil conditions, is therefore essential for conservingnative ecosystem integrity in the Kashmir Himalaya.
Short-Term Variation in Soil Organo-Mineral Associations in Alpine Forest–Shrub Transitional Zone on the Eastern Tibetan PlateauCORE3

Abstract
Soil organo-mineral associations (OMAs) play key roles in soil organic carbon (SOC) long-term persistence. However, OMAs may be sensitive to biotic inputs, such as litter and root exudates, as well as to soil physicochemical properties, including pH and cation concentrations. In alpine forest–shrub transitional zones, pronounced seasonal changes in environmental conditions may drive biogeochemical processes that alter OMAs over short timescales. Nevertheless, the short-term dynamics of OMAs remain poorly understood. Here, we combined field sampling across three seasons with an in situ vegetation-removal incubation to examine seasonal and post-winter changes in extractable carbon associated with different Al- and Fe-bearing fractions in an alpine forest–shrub transitional zone on the eastern Tibetan Plateau. Sodium pyrophosphate (PP), hydroxylamine hydrochloride (HH), and dithionite-HCl (DH) extractions were used to operationally quantify organo-metal complexes, SOC associated with short-range-order (SRO) mineral phases, and SOC associated with crystalline mineral phases, respectively. In the vegetation-removal incubation, DH-extractable fraction (CDH) exhibited a significant, albeit small, decrease (approximately 7.7%), which coincided with an increase in soil pH. Under natural seasonal conditions, the highest concentration of HH-extractable carbon (CHH) occurred in October, consistent with the seasonal pattern of SOC, whereas CDH and PP-extractable carbon (CPP) showed little variation among seasons. Extracted C-to-metal molar ratio (C/M; C relative to Al + Fe) and the proportions of extracted C to SOC also varied among sampling periods. The results demonstrate short-term variation in extractable OMA-associated C fractions and their ratios to SOC. These findings highlight the short-term variability of operationally defined OMA-associated carbon pools in alpine soils and improve our understanding of SOC cycling in alpine ecosystems.

Abstract
Aims: To characterise extractable phosphorus and zinc pools and related properties in acidic tea-estate soil following incubation with nine combinations of triple superphosphate (TSP) and zinc sulphate. Study Design: A completely randomised 3 × 3 treatment-combination experiment with three replicate soil containers per combination (27 experimental units). Place and Duration: Surface soil from Birganj Tea Estate, Dinajpur, Bangladesh, was incubated for 90 days at 25 °C. Methodology: A sandy loam soil with initial pH 5.02 received TSP at 40, 80 or 120 kg product ha⁻¹ together with ZnSO₄·7H₂O at 5, 10 or 20 kg product ha⁻¹. Post-incubation measurements comprised pH, electrical conductivity (EC), organic matter, total nitrogen, exchangeable potassium, available sulfur, Olsen-extractable phosphorus and DTPA-extractable zinc. Results: Olsen-extractable phosphorus ranged from 11.32 to 32.25 mg kg⁻¹ and was highest in treatments T8 and T9, both receiving 120 kg TSP ha⁻¹. DTPA-extractable zinc ranged from 1.45 to 3.13 mg kg⁻¹; within each TSP group, the highest zinc means occurred at 20 kg ZnSO₄ ha⁻¹. Soil pH was lower under the higher-input combinations, whereas EC, exchangeable potassium, organic matter and available sulfur exhibited distinct treatment patterns. Total nitrogen varied within a narrow interval. Conclusion: The combinations yielded differentiated post-incubation phosphorus and zinc extraction profiles in a single acidic tea-plantation soil. The results identify treatment patterns for evaluation in planted-soil and field experiments.

Abstract
The use of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) has the potential to improve agricultural land management in plantations, but currently there are no digital maps that analyse the physical properties and soil fertility of crop blocks at Stiper Edu Agro Tourism (S.E.A.T) Ungaran. This study aims to create a digital map of the plantation and determine the location points and sample collection based on each crop block in the Stiper Edu Agro Tourism (S.E.A.T) Ungaran plantation. The method used in this study was to collect soil samples using purposive sampling in crop blocks with depths of (0-20 cm), (20-40 cm), and (40-60 cm). Abstract
Sesbania cannabina is a salt-tolerant legume with potential for forage production and associated changes in saline–alkali soil properties. However, its stage-specific agronomic performance and associated soil responses under field conditions remain unclear. This study evaluated the effects of phenological stage and stubble height on forage yield and quality, soil physicochemical properties, and rhizosphere microbial communities in mildly saline–alkali land in Xinjiang, China. A field experiment was conducted using three phenological stages (budding, full flowering, and pod setting) and five stubble-height treatments (10-50 cm). Plant samples were collected to determine biomass yield and forage nutritional quality, and rhizosphere soil samples were analyzed for soil physicochemical characteristics and microbial community composition. Phenological stage and stubble height significantly affected biomass yield and forage quality (P < 0.05). The highest cumulative biomass yield, 3966.07 ± 6.44 kg ha-1, was obtained at full flowering with a 30-cm stubble height, indicating a favorable balance between biomass accumulation and multi-cut utilization. With advancing phenology, crude protein and relative feed value declined, whereas fiber fractions increased, reflecting a maturation-driven trade-off between forage yield and nutritional quality. S. cannabina cultivation was associated with changes in soil pH, electrical conductivity, soluble ion concentrations, soil porosity, and moisture during plant development, indicating coordinated changes in soil physicochemical properties. Rhizosphere microbial communities exhibited distinct phenology-dependent responses. Bacterial communities showed stronger compositional variation than fungal communities, with phenological stage accounting for 49.0% and 11.6% of the variation in Bray–Curtis dissimilarity, respectively. (PERMANOVA, R2 = 0.490 and 0.116). Bacterial diversity declined at full flowering and partially recovered at pod setting, whereas fungal communities maintained relatively stable α-diversity but underwent selective taxonomic turnover. Several microbial taxa showed significant associations with soil properties and forage traits.The preferred utilization strategy of S. cannabina depends on management objectives. Under the conditions tested, full flowering combined with a 30-cm stubble height provided a relatively balanced strategy for forage production, whereas later phenological stages were associated with stronger changes in soil salinity-related properties. These findings provide stage-specific insights into the management of S. cannabina in saline–alkali agroecosystems, while further multi-year and multi-site studies are needed to evaluate the persistence and broader applicability of these effects.
Source-Resolved Ecological Risk of Soil Heavy Metals in an Industrial–Mining Town of South China: From Source Apportionment to Spatial Risk AttributionECO1
Authors: Yu-Chen Zhu, Xiang-Ke Kong, Ying Chen
Venue: Geosciences
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Conventional ecological risk assessment based on total heavy metal concentrations can identify high-risk areas but cannot determine which pollution sources dominate the risk or which elements drive source-specific risk. We developed a source-oriented framework integrating positive matrix factorization (PMF), GeoDetector-assisted source interpretation, source-resolved ecological risk assessment, and spatial attribution. The framework was applied to 191 topsoil samples from an industrial–mining town in South China for Cr, Zn, Pb, Cd, As, and Hg. PMF resolved three sources: an As-dominated mixed source (F1), an anthropogenic mixed source characterized by Cd–Pb–Zn (F2), and a Cr-rich factor with geological-background-related characteristics (F3). GeoDetector supported source interpretation, with F2 showing the clearest association with human-activity-related environmental stratification. Under the selected three-factor solution, F1, F2, and F3 accounted for 7.8%, 74.5%, and 17.7% of the summed ecological risk across the 191 sampling sites, respectively. Importantly, source composition and risk composition were not equivalent: F2 was compositionally dominated by Zn but its ecological risk was mainly driven by Cd, whereas F3 was dominated by Cr in composition but by Hg in risk. Source dominance became increasingly concentrated in F2 as total risk increased; all high- and very-high-risk sites were F2-dominated. Spatially, F2 showed the strongest clustering (Global Moran’s I = 0.2960), close to that of total risk (0.2711). Of the 12 stable high–high clusters of total risk, 11 were dominated by F2 and 11 by Cd. These results show that source-resolved risk attribution can identify priority sources and risk-driving elements obscured by conventional total-risk assessment, providing a direct basis for differentiated risk management in mixed-contamination areas.
