Multi-View Relighting with Mergeable Radiance FieldsCORE10
Authors: Yinan Wang, Rohan Kadkol, Hyunjin Yoo, Jean-François Lalonde, James J. Clark
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose MergeableRF, a method for radiance field relighting through model parameter merging. Given multi-view images of a scene under known basis lights, our goal is to relight the scene by directly combining the parameters of radiance fields trained under these lights. This is motivated by the linearity of light transport: the image under a mixture of lights is the same weighted combination of the images under each light. However, combining the parameters of independently trained radiance fields does not produce the corresponding combination of images. To address this, we represent a scene with shared geometry and a separate appearance network for each basis light. We start these networks from the same initialization and introduce merge-aware training, which teaches their parameter combinations to reproduce the corresponding combinations of images. Given a target HDR environment map, we compute its basis-light coefficients and use them to merge the appearance networks into a single relit model. Our method supports both Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), without material estimation, additional relighting networks, or target-specific optimization. Unlike image-space blending, it requires only one rendering pass per viewpoint. Experiments on synthetic Neural Gaffer objects and real OLATverse captures demonstrate relighting quality competitive with methods trained on the same multi-light observations.
AGSR: Aperture-Guided Super-Resolution for Gaussian SplattingCORE7
Authors: Ayman Alkhateeb, Hadi Amirpour, Christian Timmerer
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables real-time novel view synthesis, but Gaussian rasterization attenuates fine-scale scene structure through the projected covariance of each primitive. We show that contracting these projected covariances at inference time can mitigate the effective bandwidth limitation induced by finite Gaussian footprints, motivating a multi-aperture rendering framework for super-resolution. Based on this insight, we propose AGSR (Aperture-Guided Super-Resolution), a lightweight inference-time framework that progressively fuses multi-aperture renderings using uncertainty-guided feature aggregation and geometric conditioning from transmittance and depth cues. AGSR operates as a plug-in enhancement for pre-trained 3DGS models. Experiments on the Mip-NeRF 360 benchmark show that AGSR outperforms lightweight image-space super-resolution baselines, with gains increasing as the underlying Gaussian representation deteriorates. The results provide evidence that covariance contraction exposes bandwidth-sensitive structural cues attenuated by native Gaussian rasterization. AGSR achieves these improvements while maintaining real-time performance with up to 139k parameters.

Abstract
We propose an original, end-to-end approach to generate and refine the onboard 3D shape model of irregular small bodies, such as asteroids, using only low-cost Sun sensor eclipse data and coarse a priori knowledge, tailored for resource-constrained missions like CubeSats. Leveraging EclipseNets, a differentiable neural model of eclipse boundaries, our pipeline first refines 2D silhouettes by learning from real-time entry and exit events during spacecraft orbits, avoiding the limitations of traditional imaging or lidar in bandwidth-starved environments. In simulations of the body Eros under realistic orbital dynamics, the method increases silhouette IoU from 0.70 to 0.87 and produces coherent 3D reconstructions, suggesting our approach could find a place in future autonomous architectures for small-body exploration.
Eco3R: Pinned-Key Inference for Efficient and Streamable Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction from Long VideosCORE6
Authors: Hong-Sen Cao, Shan-Xin Yuan, Mona Jaber, Ahmed Sayed
Venue: Computational Visual Media
Published: 2026-11-13
Abs
Abstract
Feed-forward multi-view transformers such as VGGT recover the cameras and geometry of a video in one pass, but the cost of their cross-frame self-attention grows quadratically with the frame count, and their accuracy drops on long videos. Most remedies approximate attention over all frames under a fixed computational budget or select a few keys by their content. We hypothesize that the keys of a long video can be selected independently of video content, and our experiments on VGGT and $\pi^3$ support it: a small number of uniformly sampled frames can serve as the keys of every frame. We present Eco3R, a training-free inference framework whose keys form a Pinned Key Set of 31 such frames. A pin pass stores their tokens in a Scene Cache, and the other frames read it in independent batches that reproduce joint inference with these keys exactly, at a constant cost per frame. An Online Stream updates the pinned frames during recording. Six middle global blocks align the frames and form a reusable VGGT module: the Alignment Score reads their attention to flag failures, and Alignment Refocusing reduces them by fine-tuning only these blocks (3.6 GPU hours). Eco3R ($\pi^3$) matches $\pi^3$'s success rate on 1,000-frame ScanNet videos while running 13.8 times faster. With refocusing, Eco3R (VGGT) raises VGGT's success rate from 70% to 82% while running 8.5 times faster on 500 frames. Eco3R can reconstruct sequences of up to 4,000 frames on a single A100 GPU, while Eco3R ($\pi^3$) streams 1,000 frames at 18.6 fps, achieving the lowest online absolute trajectory error.

Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Editing a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) capture of a person so that they wear a new garment requires every rendered view to agree on the same garment details. Existing 3D virtual try-on (VTON) pipelines, however, edit training views largely independently and fuse them with hand-designed heuristics such as manually chosen reference views and fixed attention-blending weights. The resulting inconsistencies are baked into the 3D representation and surface as texture drift, blurred prints, and implausible geometry in novel views. We present GS-VTON-MVT, a three-stage framework that combines implicit and explicit 3D reasoning for consistent 3DGS try-on. (i) A multi-view inpainting transformer jointly edits a set of training views with full cross-view self-attention, so that cross-view correspondence is learned rather than imposed. (ii) A geometry-guided multi-modal ControlNet conditions the diffusion editor on the garment image together with depth and surface normals rendered from the current 3DGS state, supplying explicit structure where visual evidence is weakest. (iii) A pose-adaptive persona-aware attention replaces the fixed blending weight between current-view and reference-view features with weights derived from camera-pose proximity. On the 3D-VTONBench benchmark, GS-VTON-MVT improves FID, KID, LPIPS-based cross-view consistency, and user preference over general-purpose 3D editors and prior 3D try-on methods.

Abstract
Efficient and accurate 3D surface reconstruction from multi-view images remains a fundamental challenge in Gaussian splatting. Many existing methods rely on sparse geometric initialization and iterative densification, resulting in substantial optimization overhead and difficulties in balancing rendering quality and geometric accuracy, particularly in reflective and weakly textured regions. To address these limitations, we propose RAPID, a geometry-first framework that uses multiview polarization cues to recover dense surface priors. We integrate surface normals into depth maps using sparse geometric anchors. These depth maps are then fused across views. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of RAPID in accelerating Gaussian optimization from polarimetric multi-view inputs.

A Pairwise-Cascade Distributed 3D Reconstruction Method for Multi-View Industrial Robot ScanningCORE5
Authors: Yun-Qi Yang, Cheng-Zhi Su, Xing-Xing Yang, Zhe Yang
Venue: Journal of Imaging
Published: 2026-11-13
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Multi-view industrial robot scanning requires efficient reconstruction of large point clouds while controlling onboard memory use and maintaining geometric and stitching quality. This paper presents a pairwise-cascade distributed reconstruction framework that addresses serial processing dependencies and onboard memory pressure through layer-wise parallel pairwise merging on edge nodes. Within each merge, octree pruning reduces correspondence search costs, and Voronoi-region classification determines closest points on candidate triangles for point-to-plane iterative closest point (ICP) registration. Distance-weighted fusion consolidates redundant samples in overlapping regions and controls the fusion transition. Dynamic programming optimizes the boundary correspondence path to reduce reliance on local greedy stitching decisions. Boundary interpolation addresses uneven boundary sampling, while edge-length and normal-deviation checks reject invalid candidate faces and trigger local re-solving. Experiments on three industrial components show mean cloud-to-mesh (C2M) distances of 1.88–2.72 mm relative to a centralized reconstruction baseline and stitching anomaly rates of 4.1% at most. The distributed speedup reaches 4.57× on two edge nodes and 7.80× on four nodes, while onboard memory stays within 0.5–0.8 GB for the tested distributed workloads containing 14–34 frames. On the free-form component, the cumulative C2M increase over six merging layers is 0.74 mm. These results demonstrate the framework’s efficiency and onboard memory benefits while characterizing its geometric agreement with centralized reconstruction.
TetraCoder: Adaptive Tetrahedral Encoding for Neural FieldsCORE5
Authors: Daniele Della Pietra, Antonella Rech, Nicola Conci, Nicola Garau
Venue: Computers and Graphics
Published: 2026-11-13
Abs
Abstract
Implicit and explicit representations of geometry are central in computer graphics applications and come with their pros and cons. With surface sampling being faster on explicit representations and spatial queries being faster on implicit representations, finding efficient conversions between them is a highly desirable property. We present TetraCoder, a single resolution hash-free adaptive tetrahedral encoding that links an explicit tetrahedral spatial encoder with an underlying implicit signed distance field (SDF). The resulting hybrid implicit-explicit space is continuous piecewise affine (CPWA) by design, allowing to obtain an exact correspondence between the implicit SDF and the explicit analytic mesh. TetraCoder's design comes with additional perks, in particular: (a) it scales better than multi-resolution encoding methods due to its single resolution optimizable grid, (b) unlike hash-based methods, it is not affected by collisions, (c) it enables fast tetrahedral ray-tracing, geometry and appearance editing, directly through its adaptive tetrahedral scaffold. We demonstrate the versatility of TetraCoder by showing an improvement over state-of-the-art methods on multiple tasks: 2D/3D fitting from images and point clouds, 3D novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and geometry/appearance editing.
PaveSplat: Resolution-Robust Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting with Residually Corrected Surface-Aligned CovariancesCORE3
Authors: Janghun Kim, Hyun-Cheol Kim, Eunbin An, Myeonghun Oh, Hyon-Gon Choo
Published: 2026-11-19
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
We present PaveSplat, a feed-forward Gaussian splatting method that adds a learned residual to the analytic surface-aligned covariance, keeping surfels paving the visible surface across rendering resolutions. Pixel-aligned feed-forward Gaussian splatting predicts one primitive per pixel from a few images in a single pass, and its rendering quality depends on how well these primitives cover the surface. Recent surfel-based methods compute the surface-aligned covariance analytically from predicted depth. However, this covariance has no way to correct the surfel orientation where the depth-derived frame is ill-posed. The surfels then cover the surface loosely; at the training resolution each surfel's one-pixel minimum footprint covers the gaps, but they show up as holes when the same prediction is re-rendered at a higher resolution. To address this, we keep the analytic covariance as an initialization, with its axes aligned to the surface tangents, and add an identity-initialized unit-quaternion residual that reorients a surfel only where this improves rendering, turning surfels that the analytic frame mis-aims back onto the surface. On RealEstate10K, PaveSplat achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS among single-Gaussian-per-pixel methods at every scale (1x-8x). The native-resolution gain is small; the benefit appears when the same prediction is rendered at higher display resolutions without re-running the network. On zero-shot DL3DV, it leads PSNR within this group at every scale and ranks first or second on SSIM/LPIPS. A coverage-hole analysis shows fewer uncovered pixels than both baselines under high-resolution re-rendering.

Abstract
Roadside LiDAR detectors are expensive to train on real labels, and digital twins promise to replace these labels, but a detector trained on a twin meets a different sensor, different objects and different annotation conventions on the real site. Abstract
GeoPillars is a pillar-based 3D detector for single-sweep LiDAR under a hard budget of eight points per 0.4 m pillar, a budget that discards 46.6% of in-range points. Abstract
Hybrid quantum-classical learning architectures provide a promising framework for integrating quantum feature transformations into complex data-processing tasks. Among these transformations, the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) offers a structured approach to feature processing, motivating an investigation of how its circuit configuration influences predictive performance, quantum resource requirements, and noise robustness. We present a hybrid PointNet-quantum neural network architecture for LiDAR point-cloud classification, combining classical feature extraction, amplitude encoding, and a variational quantum circuit. Abstract

Abstract
Unsupervised 3D point clouds anomaly detection aims to identify and localize anomalous geometric regions only using normal training samples. Existing methods predominantly rely on feature matching and geometric reconstruction, or implicit shape modeling, where anomalies are indirectly identified through task‐specific deviation measures, such as feature discrepancies, reconstruction errors, or geometric deviations. Different from previous works, in this paper, we propose a new flow‐based framework (PCAD‐Flow) for 3D point clouds anomaly detection, which directly evaluates the anomaly scores from the point clouds by utilizing point‐wise features based on exact likelihood estimation. Specifically, PCAD‐Flow employs conditional normalizing flows to learn invertible mappings from point‐wise feature representations to a tractable latent distribution, allowing anomaly scores to be computed directly from negative log‐likelihoods under the learned normal distribution.
