Reduction of mineral fertilizer use through municipal solid waste compost application in potted spinach ( Spinacia oleracea L.)CORE4
Authors: Francesco Manicone, Adriano Sofo, Rui Manuel de Almeida Machado, Rosangela Addesso
Venue: Agrosystems, Geosciences &amp; Environment
Published: 2026-10-18
Abs · PDF
Abstract
The overuse of mineral fertilizers can harm the environment and reduce crops quality. The combined use of N fertilizers with organic amendments may provide a more sustainable fertilization strategy. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of source‐separated organic fraction of municipal solid waste (OFMSW) compost application on crop yield and soil physicochemical properties in potted spinach. Four compost doses (10.38 t ha −1 [T1]; 20.75 t ha −1 [T2]; 30.19 t ha −1 [T3]; and 41.51 t ha −1 [T4]) combined with reduced mineral N fertilization (0.63 g N pot −1 ) were compared with a full mineral fertilization treatment receiving 1.25 g N pot −1 (M) and an unfertilized control (C). Chlorophyll content (measured as SPAD [Soil Plant Analysis Development] Index) and soil electrical conductivity (EC) were monitored over time; moreover, pH, EC, organic matter, and nitrate content were measured before and after harvest. Yield and plant growth response (leaf area, petiole, and leaf blade dry matter percentage) were analyzed after harvest. Treatment T4 showed the highest yield values (146.7 g FW pot −1 ) and soil organic matter content accumulation (1.69%), outperforming the full mineral fertilization treatment. EC values remained below soil spinach salinity tolerance thresholds, without any salt stress symptoms. The highest EC value was 0.62 mS cm −1 , measured in T4 at 21 days after transplant. The lowest compost dose produced a yield comparable to that of full mineral fertilization (M), while reducing mineral N input by 50%. The results support the application of high doses (≥40 t ha −1 ) of OFMSW compost in spinach, with a significant reduction of mineral N fertilizer input. In this crop, the combined use of OFMSW compost and reduced mineral N fertilization may represent an advantageous farming practice from an environmental and economic point of view.
Analysis of the Multi-Factor Mechanism of High-pH Saline-Alkali Paddy Fields Restricting Growth and Yield in RiceCORE3
Authors: Yang Xu, Guan-Ru Lu, Yu-He Piao, Miao Liu, Ming-Ming Wang...
Venue: Agriculture
Published: 2026-10-18
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Global soil salinization threatens crop productivity, food security, and ecosystems. In Northeast China’s Songnen Plain, high-pH saline-sodic soils impose severe alkali stress on plants. The yield of rice (Oryza sativa) is severely restricted. Our study established gradient saline-alkali paddy fields with three different pH values (7.81, 8.52, and 9.23) to analyze the correlations among plant growth, soil microorganisms, and yield. After three years’experiments, the results revealed that increasing pH reduced the growth indicators of rice. Severe saline-alkali paddy fields experienced damaged rice root systems, and the Malondialdehyde (MDA) content and Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) accumulation significantly increased. In the moderate saline-alkali paddy field, the root system enhanced the activity of antioxidant enzymes to alleviate the damage caused by the alkaline stress. High pH enhanced the CO32−, HCO3−, and Na+ levels in the soil, but the soil nutrient indicators were reduced. The abundance and diversity of soil microorganisms also decreased with increasing pH and were strongly correlated with soil physical and chemical properties. Moreover, the theoretical yield and its constituent factors significantly decreased with increasing pH, along with reduced seed testing indicators. These findings provide a complete theoretical and practical basis for an in-depth and comprehensive clarification of the plant-soil-microbe synergistic mechanism affecting rice yield from multiple perspectives.
Assessment of soil variability in the East Coastal Region for Crop Growth in Tamil Nadu, IndiaCORE3

Venue: International Journal of Drug Delivery Technology

Abstract
: A detailed soil investigation was carried out in a part of Chengalpattu district, Tamil Nadu, representing the East Coastal Plains region to characterize, classify, and evaluate the major soils in relation to their agricultural potential. Five representative soil pedons (P1–P5) were studied for their morphological, physical, and chemical properties and their relationship with major crops grown in the region. The soils were developed under the influence of diverse parent materials, including granite, alluvium, and lacustrine deposits. The soils were generally deep (>150 cm), moderately well to well drained, and occurred on level (0–1%) to very gently sloping (1–3%) landscapes. Soil reaction ranged from slightly to strongly alkaline (pH 7.5–9.6), with an increasing trend in pH with depth. Electrical conductivity ranged from 0.15 to 0.41 dS m⁻¹, indicating non-saline conditions. Organic carbon content varied from 0.26 to 0.88%, with relatively higher values in surface horizons and a gradual decrease with depth. Clay content ranged from 24.5 to 66.4%, with P2, P4, and P5 showing higher clay accumulation within the profiles. Bulk density ranged from 1.05 to 1.33 Mg m⁻³, particle density from 2.47 to 2.69 Mg m⁻³, and total porosity from 46.4 to 59.1%. Cation exchange capacity varied from 15.5 to 24.5 cmol (p⁺) kg⁻¹, with the exchange complex dominated by calcium and magnesium. Available nitrogen was low, phosphorus was low to medium, and potassium was medium to high, with nutrient availability generally higher in surface horizons. Based on soils variabilities were classified under the orders Inceptisols and Vertisols. The soils supported major crops such as paddy, vegetables, cashew, and mango. Among the studied pedons, P2 and P4 exhibited comparatively favourable soil properties and better crop performance. The study provides valuable information for soil resource assessment and sustainable crop growth.
Rapeseed Biofumigation Combined with Biochar Mitigates Continuous-Cropping Obstacles in Chrysanthemum via Soil Improvement, Rhizosphere Microbial-Community Modification, and Wilt Disease ReductionCORE3
Authors: Zhong Yao, Gao-Chuang Shi, Kai-Peng Xi, Yu-Rong Qiu, Min-Gang Tao...
Venue: Microorganisms
Published: 2026-10-18
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Long-term monoculture degrades soil ecological conditions and is associated with severe wilt disease in Chrysanthemum morifolium. The effects of combining rapeseed biofumigation and biochar to alleviate these continuous cropping obstacles remain poorly understood. A field experiment compared a control (CK), rapeseed biofumigation alone (R), and biofumigation combined with corn-straw biochar (RB) in soil under eight years of continuous chrysanthemum cultivation. Soil properties, enzyme activities, wilt disease severity, chrysanthemum yield, and rhizosphere microbial communities were assessed. Compared with CK, RB significantly increased available phosphorus (+40.7%), available potassium (+16.4%), total nitrogen (+4.9%), cation exchange capacity (+8.6%), selected enzyme activities, plant height, and flower yield (p < 0.05). The disease index decreased from 8.67 in CK to 2.67 under R and 2.33 under RB, with both reductions significant but no significant difference between R and RB. Both treatments altered microbial community composition, reduced bacterial richness, and enriched MND1; RB additionally reduced bacterial diversity, enriched Acidobacteriota, and decreased the abundances of Firmicutes and Bacteroidota relative to CK. Exploratory Mantel tests indicated that available phosphorus and cation exchange capacity were associated with variation in rhizosphere microbial community structure. Overall, RB improved selected soil fertility parameters and flower yield, accompanied by microbial community changes and lower wilt severity. Broader application requires multi-site and multi-season validation.
Fabrication of Nb 2 O 5 @IrO 2 Core–Shell Catalysts for Enhanced Oxygen Evolution Reaction in Acidic MediaPROXY2
Authors: Soomin Kim, Y. Tak
Venue: ChemElectroChem
Published: 2026-10-18
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Iridium oxide (IrO 2 ) is the benchmark catalyst for the acidic oxygen evolution reaction (OER) in proton exchange membrane water electrolysis (PEMWE), but its cost and scarcity require improved Ir utilization. Herein, Nb 2 O 5 @IrO 2 core–shell catalysts were developed using ball‐milled Nb 2 O 5 supports with uniform IrO 2 shell formation. Ball‐milling at 300 rpm for 8 h produced a rounded and relatively uniform Nb 2 O 5 morphology with a high defect‐related oxygen contribution, providing favorable support characteristics for homogeneous IrO 2 deposition. Electron microscopy and elemental mapping supported an approximately 3 nm IrO 2 shell on the Nb 2 O 5 core. Among catalysts prepared from supports milled for different durations, BM‐8h‐Nb 2 O 5 @IrO 2 exhibited the best OER performance in 0.1 M HClO 4 , with an overpotential of 307 mV at 10 mA cm −2 and an IrO 2 mass activity of 304.17 A g IrO2 −1 , outperforming unsupported IrO 2 . Although the 18 h ball‐milled catalyst exhibited the largest electrochemically active surface area, its lower OER activity indicates that optimized support morphology and defect structure, rather than surface area alone, govern IrO 2 utilization. These results demonstrate that ball‐milled Nb 2 O 5 support provides an effective platform for Ir‐efficient acidic OER catalysts relevant to PEMWE anodes.
Effects of Reclaimed Water Irrigation on Nutrient Management and Fruiting in Blueberry Production SystemsPROXY2
Authors: J. Odogwu, Mary G. Lusk, D. Kadyampakeni
Venue: Water
Published: 2026-10-18
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Increasing pressure on freshwater resources has heightened interest in reclaimed water (RW) as an alternative irrigation source for agriculture, yet its elevated salinity and variable nutrient composition may influence nutrient dynamics and crop performance in salt-sensitive species such as blueberry (Vaccinium corymbosum L.). This study assessed the short-term effects of RW irrigation on substrate nutrient status, nutrient leaching, and fruit production in a 10-month greenhouse experiment using groundwater (GW) and RW from two treatment plants (RW1 and RW2) applied to mineral soil, pine bark, and a 1:1 soil–pine bark mixture in a split-plot randomized complete block design. RW1 and RW2 were diluted 1:1 with GW; all water sources were adjusted to pH 5.5 before application. Relative to GW, NH4+–N in soil increased by 203% under RW1, and B and Mo concentrations in pine bark increased by 23% and 35%, respectively. Fruit number and weight were influenced by the substrate × water source interaction, although Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons did not identify significant differences among treatments. Irrigation water source did not affect cumulative NH4+-N, NO3−-N, or PO43−-P leaching losses. This study evaluated diluted RW in containerized plants under greenhouse conditions; therefore, findings should not be directly extrapolated to field production systems.
