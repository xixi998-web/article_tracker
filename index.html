Venue: NeurIPS 2026

Abstract
Multi-View Stereo (MVS) and Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) provide complementary cues for dense 3D reconstruction. While MVS leverages explicit geometry for metric depth, it struggles in challenging regions where feature matching is unreliable. Conversely, MDE provides robust structural priors but typically yields scale-ambiguous results. Existing hybrid methods mainly exploit monocular cues in a unidirectional manner, leaving the mutual enhancement between MDE and MVS insufficiently explored. To address this limitation, we propose BridgeMVS, a unified cascaded multi-stage MVS framework that bridges monocular depth features and multi-view cost volume representations through Bidirectional Dynamic Fusion (BDF). At each stage, BDF performs dynamic mono-to-cost and cost-to-mono updates, where monocular structural features guide cost volume refinement for more robust MVS depth estimation, while multi-view geometric evidence recalibrates monocular representations to improve their metric-scale consistency. Furthermore, the fused monocular and multi-view representations are progressively propagated from coarse to fine stages and constrained by stage-wise relative-scale MDE supervision and metric-scale MVS supervision, enabling cross-branch interactions to benefit subsequent high-resolution depth estimation. In addition, BDF is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be conveniently integrated into existing cascaded MVS frameworks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that BridgeMVS achieves state-of-the-art performance on the DTU, Tanks-and-Temples, and ETH3D benchmarks, while simultaneously boosting the accuracy of monocular depth estimation. Notably, BridgeMVS ranks first on both the Tanks-and-Temples Intermediate and Advanced benchmarks. The source code is available at \href{https://github.com/JianfeiJ/BridgeMVS}{https://github.com/JianfeiJ/BridgeMVS}.

Abstract
Persistent object maps must incorporate new surfaces while retaining valid identities and correcting earlier associations. We present a map-revision module that separates historical identity changes from new-surface admission. Proposed merges are tested against shared and separate mask explanations across views; a merge is allowed only if its evidence margin remains positive after removing any one observed frame. New measured points are assigned through local identity competition, with a mask witness and a seed-frame stability check. On two preselected KITTI-360 windows, the fixed method increases mean building/car instance AP50:90 from 17.259 to 18.762 under a local evaluation protocol. Controlled output interventions attribute the gains to fragment consolidation, protection against harmful overwrites, and local assignment of new points. The extra seed-frame check yields no AP gain over nearest-seed assignment in either set. A 330-frame garage recording demonstrates offline integration with LiDAR--inertial--visual odometry. These results identify where observation-supported revision improves object maps and where conservative decisions still limit recovery.

Abstract
Video generation models are widely used as 3D priors for novel view synthesis and 3D scene generation. Fine-tuned for perception, generation models also excel at dense prediction such as depth estimation. However, methods that apply video generation models to 3D reconstruction either predict depth without cameras or generate geometry and estimate cameras through many iterative denoising steps, which makes them slower. In this paper, we present $\textbf{VG3R}$, which turns a pretrained video generation model into a feed-forward 3D reconstruction model. Abstract
Adversarial object attacks pose a threat to BEV-based 3D object detectors in autonomous driving. Existing attacks typically target a single modality, coordinate camera and LiDAR observations at the current timestamp, or model temporal persistence mainly in camera streams. Without a unified construction of current and historical observations, attacks can leave part of the detector's accumulated evidence unsuppressed, limiting their effectiveness. In this paper, we propose AdvMTO, a 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) framework for suppressing detection of persistent adversarial objects. Its oriented surfels encode appearance, geometry, and opacity in one object state, from which camera images and scanline LiDAR returns are generated. AdvMTO then constructs current and historical inputs through detector-native processing, regenerating LiDAR returns in each native source sweep for fusion detectors. We optimize bounded appearance perturbations for each detector, propagating gradients through historical BEV memory for temporal camera detectors. For fusion detectors, bounded geometry refinement along local normals further perturbs both sensing modalities. Experiments on nuScenes demonstrate effective suppression of traffic-cone detection across three BEV detectors. Temporal ablations further highlight the importance of detector-native history modeling for both attack construction and optimization.
Refin3R: Geometry-Guided Refinement for Feed-Forward 3D ReconstructionCORE7
Authors: Jongmin Lee, Jaeyeon Kim, Seungryong Kim
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-11-17
Abs
Abstract
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models predict dense point maps from multiple images in a single pass. Without explicit geometric constraints, however, their predictions are often inconsistent across views and lack fine-grained precision. Recent methods incorporate geometric priors such as camera pose or depth, but use them merely as auxiliary inputs, process each view independently, or refine the geometry only where the priors are available. We present Refin3R, which refines feed-forward point maps with Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points, propagating SfM geometry over the image grid. In its dual-branch architecture, a 2D branch extends SfM geometry beyond its coverage using geometry-aware backbone features, while a 3D branch reasons over the SfM points of all views, capturing multi-view structure that the image grid alone cannot represent. The two branches exchange information bidirectionally to jointly refine each view, yielding dense and accurate point maps. Across ScanNet++, ETH3D, Tanks\&Temples, and out-of-domain human and surgical scenes, Refin3R consistently outperforms feed-forward and geometry-guided methods in point map accuracy while running faster than the strongest geometry-guided baseline.
Complementary Geometry in Gaussian SplattingCORE6
Authors: Mingle Jiang, Jikui Zhou, Chenghan Lyu, Rui Xu, Yunke Wang...
Published: 2026-11-22
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Gaussian Splatting has achieved high visual quality in novel view synthesis, but the accuracy of its rendered geometry remains a challenge. In particular, the rendered depth is computed as an opacity-weighted average along each ray, which may not correspond to the actual scene surface. In this paper, we observe that depth predictions from different views of the same frozen Gaussian representation exhibit complementary errors, allowing us to improve novel-view geometry using information from other views without modifying the original representation. Based on this observation, we propose REVEAL, a general framework that selectively refines novel-view depth using complementary geometric information. Specifically, we first reproject depth predictions from source views to the target view and combine them using robust multi-view consensus. We then introduce a learned selection mechanism to determine whether the candidate depth is more accurate than the original prediction, based on the expected reduction in depth error. In addition, we introduce RIG, a real-world benchmark captured with synchronized RGB-D cameras, which provides ground-truth depth for evaluating both static and dynamic reconstruction models. Experiments on 6 Gaussian Splatting backbones across 3 benchmarks show that REVEAL consistently improves depth accuracy while maintaining comparable rendering quality.
Not All Directions Are Flat: Decomposed-Gradient Flatness for Sparse-View 3D Gaussian SplattingCORE6
Authors: Ungbae Yoon, Kwanyoung Kim
Published: 2026-11-21
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many applications of novel view synthesis, such as augmented reality and robotics, can afford only a few input views, since dense capture is costly. With such sparse views, however, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) overfits the training views and degrades at unseen viewpoints. Among the approaches to this problem, flat-minima optimization treats the Gaussians as trainable parameters and steers training toward flat minima, which generalize better, by perturbing the Gaussians with random noise or along the training gradient. However, these perturbations ignore their direction relative to the other training views. Interestingly, we find that the direction of the perturbation determines whether it improves generalization. Specifically, we decompose the gradient of each training view into a component parallel to the average gradient of all views, on which the views agree, and an orthogonal component, on which they disagree. Perturbing along the orthogonal component improves generalization, whereas perturbing along the parallel component does not. We further find that the flatness correction often opposes the reconstruction gradient, shrinking or even reversing the reconstruction step. Based on these findings, we propose DecoFlat, which perturbs each Gaussian along the orthogonal component together with random noise. Furthermore, we introduce a conflict-aware correction, which removes the part of the flatness correction that opposes the reconstruction gradient, and a rescheduling of the spherical-harmonics color, which suppresses the overfitting of view-dependent appearance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DecoFlat, as a plug-in optimizer, consistently improves vanilla 3DGS and a wide range of sparse-view 3DGS methods.

Authors: Guanyu Jiang, Junjie Cao, Jinshan Pan, Zhixun Su

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Detecting unexpected objects is essential for autonomous driving safety. Most existing LiDAR anomaly detectors rely on semantic discrimination, which primarily captures category-discriminative features but may overlook deviations from broader feature relationships in normal scenes. Reconstruction offers a complementary perspective by assessing consistency with learned normal patterns. However, pointwise reconstruction errors alone struggle to distinguish anomalies from normal variability in complex outdoor point clouds. We propose Structured Residual Reasoning (SRR) to extract additional anomaly evidence from residual structure across feature channels, time sequences, and geometric space. We further establish that semantic discrimination and normal-pattern reconstruction capture fundamentally different yet complementary anomaly evidence: category compatibility and consistency with learned normal patterns, respectively. At matched background false-positive rates, SRR recovers more objects missed entirely by the semantic branch while producing fewer false positives in normal-only scenes. Building on this finding, we propose Reconstruction-Guided Dual-Branch (RGDB) learning, in which SRR-derived normal-pattern consistency cues guide a semantic-conditioned anomaly decoder to complement reconstruction-based predictions during training. At inference, the two branches jointly exploit their complementary anomaly evidence. Experiments show that standalone SRR establishes a strong reconstruction-based baseline without semantic labels from the target-scene training data, while RGDB achieves state-of-the-art performance on the STU validation set.

Abstract
Dense collaborative mapping is essential for search and rescue, building inspection, and warehouse automation, where panoramic cameras capture the entire surroundings in a single frame. Abstract
Atomic electron tomography (AET) computationally reconstructs the 3D atomic structure of materials from a series of 2D projection measurements acquired at different tilt angles. Inspired by Gaussian splatting techniques from computer vision, we introduce an atom-centric parameterization for modeling AET samples. Instead of a conventional volumetric representation with a grid of voxels, we model the sample as a list of atoms, where each atom is a 3D Gaussian characterized by learned location, scale, and amplitude. The Gaussian model is a natural fit for atoms that allows us to easily incorporate prior information grounded in physics; we impose isotropy of atoms, a minimum interatomic distance, and realistic atomic radii. We show in simulation that our method is more robust than volumetric baselines to real-world imaging challenges, including a limited number of measurements and low electron dose. We further show that our method yields a more physically plausible reconstruction of an experimental 23,000 atom iron-platinum nanoparticle than a previously published volumetric reconstruction. Beyond tomography, our representation is a promising tool for any atomic resolution imaging modality (e.g. multislice electron ptychography).

Abstract
Oriented object detection has advanced with the development of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers. However, modern detectors still rely on predefined object candidates, such as anchors in CNN-based methods or queries in transformer-based methods, which struggle to capture spatial information effectively. LMnOP: Pruning 3D Gaussian Splatting via Leave-Many-Out ImportanceCORE5
Authors: Sunjae Jeong, Jaeseung Kim, Hyoseok Hwang
Published: 2026-11-23
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting renders photorealistic views in real time. However, a single scene often needs millions of Gaussians, so these models are costly to store and render. Pruning reduces this cost by keeping a subset of the Gaussians. A common score is the leave-one-out importance, which is the loss increase when a Gaussian alone is removed. At high pruning ratios, many Gaussians are removed together, so Gaussians that are redundant alone but essential together are lost. We present LMnOP (Leave-Many-Out Pruning), which estimates the importance of each Gaussian under the removal of the other Gaussians. This leave-many-out importance is the average loss increase when a Gaussian is removed while the others are also removed at random. We compute the exact mean and variance of each pixel's color under random removal in closed form, and approximate the importance from them without sampling removal patterns. We refine each Gaussian's probability of remaining with its importance and prune once at the end. Without fine-tuning, LMnOP achieves the best results in 16 of 18 metrics on three datasets at 90% and 99% removal. It improves SSIM and LPIPS over GaussianPOP in all 13 scenes.

Abstract
Terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) enables 3D reconstruction of trees and quantitative structure models (QSMs) for estimating woody volume and structural traits. However, occlusion in single-station or sparsely sampled TLS causes incomplete branch observations and biased volume estimates. Existing point-cloud completion methods recover plausible geometry but do not explicitly preserve branch topology, cylinder radii, or woody volume. We propose ArborNet, a volume- and skeleton-aware framework for completing tree structures in the QSM domain. Each tree is represented as a TreeQSM cylinder graph encoding cylinder geometry, branch level, parent-child topology, and branch identity. A multi-relation graph encoder propagates information across parent, child, and intra-branch connections. An anchor-conditioned decoder generates missing cylinders through terminal-branch continuation and new-branch generation. Geometry, existence, branch, radius, axis-smoothness, and volume losses encourage structural consistency. During inference, generated cylinders are selected under predicted level-wise missing-volume budgets. We evaluate ArborNet on DART-simulated TLS trees and urban TLS trees from Hong Kong, using single-station QSMs as partial inputs and multi-station QSMs as references. Under budget-constrained inference, ArborNet achieves mean total-volume relative error of 0.081% and mean level-wise volume error of 0.676% against reference QSMs, while reducing skeleton geometry error relative to evaluated point-cloud completion baselines. These errors quantify agreement with reference QSMs, not accuracy against destructively measured woody volume. Ablation results show that joint volume supervision is particularly important for volume control. These results demonstrate the potential of QSM-domain completion to recover explicit woody structure while constraining geometry and volume.

Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is commonly optimized with multiple reconstruction losses, which naturally forms a multi-objective optimization problem. This issue becomes more pronounced when perceptual losses are introduced to improve visual quality, as different objectives may impose competing update directions on Gaussian primitives and make it difficult to simultaneously improve fidelity, perceptual quality, and representation efficiency. Based on this view, we reformulate 3DGS training as a multi-objective optimization problem and derive selective conflict-aware position updates that reduce interference between competing objectives while preserving the contribution of each loss. Abstract
Autonomous exploration is widely used to map unknown environments, yet maintaining both path efficiency and low online computation remains difficult as environment scale increases.
