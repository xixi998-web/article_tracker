Authors: J. Duarte, J. D. de Brito, E. F. de Mesquita, Vicente Victor Lima de Andrade, Cristiano dos Santos Sousa...
Venue: Horticulturae
Published: 2026-11-04
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil organic carbon fractions and fertility attributes may differ among land-use conditions in the Brazilian semiarid region due to environmental heterogeneity, soil properties, and land-use history. This study compared soil attributes in degraded, forest, and restoration areas across four desertification hotspots located in Ceará (CE), Paraíba (PB), Pernambuco (PE), and Piauí (PI), Brazil. Surface soil samples (0–10 cm) were collected from four independent plots per land-use condition within each hotspot and analyzed for particle-size distribution, pH, total organic carbon (TOC), humic fractions, available phosphorus, exchangeable cations, and potential cation exchange capacity (CEC). The magnitude and direction of differences among land-use conditions varied across the investigated hotspots. Forest areas exhibited 12% and 57% higher TOC contents than degraded areas in PB and PI, respectively. In PB, humic acid carbon contents were approximately 85–95% higher in forest and restoration areas than in the degraded area, while humin carbon content in the restoration area was 136% higher than in the degraded area. Available phosphorus was markedly higher in restoration areas in PB and PI. In PI, the restoration area exhibited high Ca2+ and Mg2+ contents and potential CEC, whereas soil pH ranged from approximately 5.1 in the forest area to 8.0 in the degraded area. These findings reveal substantial spatial variation in soil carbon fractions and fertility attributes among land-use categories, potentially associated with differences in soil texture, pedogenic characteristics, and previous land management. The results highlight the importance of considering local edaphic conditions when assessing soil quality across contrasting land-use categories in semiarid environments.

Progressive shifts in soil respiration response to long-term nitrogen enrichment across global forests and grasslands.CORE6
Authors: A. Xing, Meng-Ying Zhao, Shang-Shi Liu, A. Broadbent, E. Du...
Venue: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Published: 2026-11-04
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil stores the largest pool of actively cycling carbon (C) on land, and its dynamics under global changes are critical for projecting climate feedbacks. Global nitrogen (N) deposition has more than doubled since the 1860s; however, the temporal dynamics of soil C emissions under prolonged N deposition are poorly understood. Here, using a global time-series dataset compiled from 38 long-term experiments in forests and grasslands, we find that the response of soil respiration (Rs) to N addition systematically shifts from a transient increase persisting for approximately 2 y to a sustained suppression over time (mean effect = -6%; 95% CI = [-10%, -3%]). Rs in forests and grasslands followed consistent nonlinear trajectories, characterized by a rapid initial decline in the magnitude of the response, which progressively leveled off. Notably, suppression of Rs emerged when cumulative N inputs exceeded a threshold of ~100 kg N ha-1. Consequently, the suppression was both stronger and occurred more rapidly under high-level N additions (>50 kg N ha-1 y-1). These temporal dynamics were associated with N-induced soil acidification and related changes in microbial properties. Overall, these findings indicate that accurate prediction of land-atmosphere C fluxes under global change requires models to capture the reported temporal dynamics.

Venue: Scientific Reports

Abstract
Hainan Island is China’s largest tropical crop-production base, making its agricultural soil quality important for national food security. We determined total concentrations of eight heavy metals (Hg, As, Cd, Pb, Cr, Cu, Zn, Ni) in 300 surface soils collected across the island in late 2025. Soils were predominantly acidic (mean pH 5.18; 77% below pH 5.5), and mean concentrations were low to moderate (e.g., Cd 0.21, Pb 58.19, and Ni 28.50 mg kg⁻¹). Against pH- and land-use-stratified GB 15618 − 2018 screening values, single-metal exceedance was most frequent for Pb (28.0%) and Ni (14.7%); separately, the Nemerow comprehensive index exceeded its pollution threshold ( P n > 1.0) at 23.7% of sites. The geo-accumulation index ( I geo = log 2 [ C i /(1.5 × B i )]) showed the strongest enrichment for Cd (mean 1.48; 99.3% above 0), moderate enrichment for Pb (0.33), and at or below background levels for the remaining metals. Soil pH correlated negatively with total Cd, Cr, Hg, and Pb ( r  = − 0.47 to − 0.28). Parent material, land use, and pH jointly explained about 45% of the variance, separating lithogenic Ni-Cu-Zn from mixed Cd-Pb-Hg-Cr associations. Together, these results show that fixed-threshold assessment understates contamination in acidic tropical soils, providing an island-wide baseline for targeted risk management.

Abstract
Regeneration of catalysts through arsenic and alkaline rare earth removal can not only solve the environmental problems caused by the stacking of deactivated catalysts and arsenic pollution, but also reduce the economic investment required to produce new catalysts. After analyzing the structure and composition of the spent V2O5–WO3/TiO2 catalysts obtained from a power factory, their existence, form and content were determined for As and Ba. NaClO was used as both an oxidant and a base source in leaching As. It first oxidizes the highly toxic and adsorbed stable As(III) into the more water-soluble As(V). Meanwhile, the OH− produced by hydrolysis provides an alkaline environment, promoting the conversion of As5+ to soluble AsO43− and facilitating the desorption and dissolution of As from the catalyst. Taking into account the removal rate of arsenic and the loss of active ingredients, the optimal process conditions were determined. The BaSO4 was removed using the EDTA complexation method and the optimal conditions were determined. The elution and regeneration process not only removed arsenic but also effectively unblocked the pore structure of the catalyst. The redox capacity of the regenerated catalyst was significantly enhanced, and the number and strength of acid sites and the denitrification performance were also effectively restored.
Effects of Soil Amendment Application on Soil Quality in Saline–Alkali Land: A Meta-AnalysisCORE4
Authors: Han-Jing He, Ming Hong, Alimu Abulaiti, You-Wei Jiang, Fan-Feng Dang
Venue: Agriculture
Published: 2026-11-04
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil salinization and alkalization are major forms of land degradation, and soil amendments are commonly used to improve the quality of saline–alkali soils. This study aimed to quantify the effects of different amendments on soil physicochemical properties in saline–alkali soils in China, compare single and mixed applications, and identify environmental and management factors associated with variations in their effects. A systematic literature search and screening process identified 223 eligible datasets from studies conducted in China. Data from amended treatments and corresponding unamended controls were synthesized using meta-analysis. The amendments included organic and inorganic materials, such as humic acid, phosphogypsum, and desulfurization gypsum, applied individually or in mixtures. Compared with unamended controls, amendment application significantly decreased soil pH, salt content (SC), exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP), sodium adsorption ratio (SAR), and bulk density (BD) by 2.8%, 12.4%, 27.2%, 44.4%, and 6.6%, respectively; soil nutrient levels increased significantly: available nitrogen (AN), available phosphorus (AP), available potassium (AK), and soil organic carbon (SOC) increased by 33.9%, 42.3%, 20.7%, and 20.5%, respectively. Mixed applications showed greater improvements in six of the nine evaluated soil properties, whereas single applications produced greater increases in available nitrogen and greater reductions in salt content and SAR. Among amendments applied individually, organic amendments generally showed larger increases in soil nutrient availability and organic carbon, whereas inorganic amendments generally showed larger reductions in ESP and SAR. Additionally, random forest analysis identified precipitation as the most important predictor of salt-content responses under single applications and application amount under mixed applications. Application amount was also the most important predictor of soil organic carbon responses under both application strategies. Overall, farmyard manure is a promising option for increasing nutrient availability and SOC and reducing BD, whereas inorganic–organic treatments merit consideration where excessive exchangeable sodium coincides with poor soil fertility. Amendment selection should therefore be based on specific soil conditions and local environmental characteristics.

Abstract
The large-scale application of livestock and poultry manure in protected agriculture is common; however, research on the spread of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) from this practice remains limited. Herein, 16 S and metagenomic sequencing methods were used to investigate the effects of eight consecutive years of pig manure application on tetracycline (TC) antibiotics in protected soil and to evaluate the ecological consequences. Soil environmental factors collectively accounted for 72.27% of ARG variation, making them the most important factor affecting ARGs. Our research will support manure management in protected agriculture to reduce ARG spread.

Abstract
A field experiment was conducted during the kharif season of 2025 at the Wheat and Maize Research Unit, Vasantrao Naik Marathwada Krishi Vidyapeeth, Parbhani, Maharashtra, to assess the influence of graded nitrogen application and plant growth promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) on soil fertility, plant nutrient content and uptake, and rhizospheric biological activity in maize. The experiment was laid out in a randomised block design with eight treatments and three replications. Treatments comprised an absolute control, PGPR alone, and 50, 75 and 100% of the recommended nitrogen dose (RDN), each applied with and without PGPR. The PGPR consortium consisted of Azotobacter chroococcum, Azospirillum lipoferum and Bacillus subtilis. Soil observations were recorded at tasselling and harvest, while plant nutrient content and uptake were assessed at harvest. Soil pH, electrical conductivity and calcium carbonate were not significantly affected by the treatments, whereas organic carbon, available N, P and K and DTPA-extractable Fe, Mn, Zn and Cu generally increased with increasing nitrogen levels and PGPR inoculation. Plant N, P and K contents and uptake increased progressively, with 100% RDN + PGPR recording 1.95% grain N, 0.40% grain P and 0.41% grain K and total uptake of 246.86, 66.57 and 136.95 kg ha⁻¹, respectively. Micronutrient content and uptake also increased, with 100% RDN + PGPR recording the highest Fe, Zn, Mn and Cu accumulation. Bacterial, actinomycetes and fungal populations and dehydrogenase, acid phosphatase and alkaline phosphatase activities were significantly enhanced by integrated nutrient management. The findings indicate that combining the recommended nitrogen dose with PGPR improved plant nutrient acquisition and rhizospheric biological activity without appreciable changes in soil reaction or salinity during one cropping season.
Spatial assessment of soil fertility in high-Andean potato systems using a multivariate index in the Pasco region, PeruCORE3

Venue: Frontiers in Soil Science
Published: 2026-11-04

Abstract
Potato cultivation in the central Andes of Peru occurs under highly heterogeneous environmental conditions, where soil fertility is influenced by interactions among lithology, geomorphology, climate, and vegetation. This study evaluated the spatial variability of soil fertility in high-Andean potato systems across three river basins of the Pasco region, Peru, through the development of a multivariate Soil Fertility Index (SFI). A total of 573 soil samples were collected and analyzed for soil, climatic, and environmental variables. Principal Component Analysis was applied to construct a minimum dataset and assign indicator weights according to their contribution to the explained variance. The final SFI integrated 11 indicators, with pH, exchangeable Mg, precipitation, and cation exchange capacity identified as the most influential variables. The SFI was used to compare soil fertility among three river basins and across lithological, geomorphological, and life zone classes. Significant differences in SFI were observed among lithological, geomorphological, and life zones (p < 0.001). Clastic sedimentary and igneous lithologies exhibited the highest fertility median values (0.50), whereas metamorphic and carbonate sedimentary formations showed lower median values (0.42–0.46). Among geomorphological units, hills exhibited the highest SFI median values (0.51), while depositional slopes and terraces showed the lowest (0.45). Andean shrublands exhibited the highest fertility median values (0.53), whereas tropical dry forests showed the lowest (0.44). Districts with higher SFI values generally exhibited greater potato yields, although this relationship was influenced by additional environmental and management factors. Spatial interpolation using RK and GWRK enabled the characterization of soil fertility patterns across the three basins and the identification of areas with contrasting fertility conditions. Although model performance was moderate (R² = 0.10–0.33), the resulting maps provide a useful framework for soil fertility assessment, site-specific management, and sustainable agricultural planning in Andean potato production systems.
Beyond Backscatter: Leveraging Diverse Sentinel-1 Radar Features for Improved Soil pH PredictionCORE3

Venue: Land
Published: 2026-11-04

Abstract
While the application of Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) in soil pH prediction has been explored, its predictive capacity has been underestimated because the radar indicators employed are mostly mathematical combinations of backscatter observations. We combined soil pH observations from the LUCAS 2018 topsoil dataset across the Iberian Peninsula with Sentinel-1 backscatter, texture, and interferometric coherence features to develop random forest models and compared their performance with models based on Sentinel-2 optical data. Coherence values decreased and spatial details diminished as the temporal baseline increased, while shorter baselines provided better modeling performance. Models incorporating multiple temporal baselines outperformed those using a single baseline. Among models using a single type of radar information, coherence-based models incorporating all temporal baselines achieved the highest accuracy (R2 = 0.54–0.55), followed by texture-based models, while backscatter-based models performed the poorest (R2 = 0.46–0.48). Notably, most radar-based models achieved higher accuracy than those using Sentinel-2 optical data alone (R2 = 0.44–0.46). Integrating diverse Sentinel-1 radar features further improved prediction accuracy, achieving R2 = 0.59 for pH in H2O and R2 = 0.58 for pH in CaCl2, while adding Sentinel-2 data only slightly enhanced accuracy. Coherence was the most influential variable group, particularly at short temporal baselines. These results highlight the substantial potential of diverse Sentinel-1 radar information for soil pH mapping.

Abstract
The one-way effect of moss diversity on soil properties was investigated using belt transects in two established 1-ha plots located at 920–1,160masl in the montane forest of Mt. Hamiguitan Range Wildlife Sanctuary, San Isidro, Davao Oriental. Moss species were identified, and diversity, dominance, and relative abundance were determined. Soil properties were assessed based on soil type, temperature, moisture, pH, and NPK macronutrient levels. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was used to determine relationships between moss diversity and soil physicochemical parameters. A total of 28 moss taxa from six orders and ten families were recorded, with 20 species identified in Plot 1 and 21 in Plot 2. The mean Shannon diversity index was 1.68, indicating moderately high diversity, while the mean Pielou’s evenness and McIntosh dominance indices were 0.55 and 0.83, respectively. Trismegistia calderensis and Braunfelsia sp. were the most abundant species. Most mosses occurred on epigeous or tree-based substrates rather than on the ground. Both plots had clay loam soil. Mean soil temperatures were 19.7°C and 21.5°C, while soil moisture values were 1.05% and 1.07%, and pH values were 5.4 and 5.3. NPK levels varied between plots. Correlations between moss diversity and soil parameters differed between plots, particularly for NPK. Temperature showed no correlation in either plot. Overall, the findings suggest that moss diversity influences certain soil properties through ecosystem functions rather than solely through direct substrate contact, supporting the ecological and functional importance of mosses in montane forest ecosystems.

Abstract
As a strategically important groundwater source in Shanxi Province, the Xinzhou Basin contends with localized fluoride and nitrate exceedances whose hydrogeochemical controls remain insufficiently characterized. This work compares the unconfined and confined aquifers of the basin on the basis of 40 samples collected in July 2025, combining hydrogeochemical diagramming, multivariate statistics and the USEPA non-carcinogenic risk model. Both systems are mildly alkaline, with pH means of 7.96 and 7.95. Ca2+ and Mg2+ dominate the cations and HCO3− the anions, and the two groups share the same facies spectrum: 27 of the 40 samples are HCO3-Ca type and seven are Na-HCO3 type, while no sample reaches the Cl-Na corner. Water–rock interaction is the overarching control. Combined carbonate and silicate weathering supplies most of the solute, and evaporite dissolution is restricted to three samples in which sulphate exceeds bicarbonate. Calcite and dolomite are at or above saturation in 39 and 38 samples, whereas gypsum and fluorite remain undersaturated throughout (SI −4.17 to −0.32 and −2.64 to −0.12) and continue to dissolve. The seven Na-HCO3 samples carry the lowest Ca2+ fractions and the most negative chloro-alkaline indices, which identifies forward cation exchange as the process that removes Ca2+ and sustains fluorite dissolution. Nitrate has a mixed provenance, combining synthetic fertilizer, manure and domestic effluent, and soil organic nitrogen, and its imprint is stronger in the unconfined aquifer. Nitrate carries the larger hazard burden (HI up to 4.34, against 2.35 for fluoride), and unconfined groundwater records higher HI values than its confined counterpart, with ingestion as the dominant pathway. The two contaminants peak in different places: fluoride risk in the southern Xinfu–Dingxiang sector, and nitrate risk in Yuanping in the central basin. These results provide an evidence base for groundwater quality management, fluorosis mitigation and drinking-water security planning in the Xinzhou Basin.

Abstract
Polyethylene agricultural mulching films can increase crop yields; however, long-term residue leads to the accumulation of plastic fragments and microplastics in soil, threatening soil health and food security. In this study, we conducted a field-simulated experiment in a rapeseed (Brassica napus L.) system to compare plastisphere and rhizosphere soils with respect to soil properties, soil multifunctionality (MF), and bacterial and fungal community characteristics. Our results indicate that compared with the rhizosphere, the plastisphere exhibited a significantly higher pH and lower water content. In addition, inorganic nitrogen, dissolved organic nitrogen, total nitrogen, ammonium nitrogen, net nitrogen mineralization rate, and available phosphorus were all lower in the plastisphere, while total carbon was higher. These nutrient availability shifts translated directly into ecosystem function. Soil MF was substantially lower in the plastisphere than in the rhizosphere (p < 0.001). Both bacterial and fungal richness and diversity (Chao1 and Shannon indices) were markedly reduced in the plastisphere (p < 0.01). The plastisphere also showed reduced microbial diversity and richness in both bacteria and fungi (Chao1 and Shannon indices, p < 0.01), with strong compartmental separation across communities (bacteria R2 = 0.85; fungi R2 = 0.78; p < 0.01) and simplified co-occurrence network cohesion. Random forest analyses indicated that fungal community traits were more important than bacterial traits in predicting MF. Overall, residual mulching film drives nutrient limitation and restructures microbial interaction networks, leading to decreased soil multifunctionality and potential downstream impacts on nutrient cycling and plant nutrient supply.

Abstract
Diatomaceous earth (DE), or diatomite, is a natural sedimentary material derived from the fossilized remains of diatoms, microscopic algae that have accumulated over millions of years. This study explores the structural characteristics and multifunctional applications of DE, particularly in environmental protection and sustainable agriculture.  DE’s high porosity and large surface area contribute to its exceptional adsorption capacity, enabling the removal of contaminants such as heavy metals and pollutants from water. Analytical techniques, including X-ray fluorescence (XRF), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM), were employed to examine the physical and chemical properties of DE, revealing its crystalline structure and surface morphology. The results indicate that DE's primary component, amorphous silica, can be effectively utilized as a natural filter, insulating material, and adsorbent in industrial and environmental contexts. Additionally, this study highlights the potential of DE in agriculture, where it enhances soil aeration, retains moisture, and serves as a natural pest control agent. DE’s non-toxic nature makes it a favorable alternative to synthetic chemicals in farming, contributing to sustainable agricultural practices. DE’s efficiency in specific applications, such as heavy metal adsorption and pollution control, can be optimized through surface modification. This research underscores the versatile nature of DE and its vital role in promoting sustainability in various industries while advocating for further exploration into its surface chemistry and environmental benefits.
