Thermally Treated Oyster Shell Exacerbates Phosphorus Immobilization during Acidic Soil AmendmentCORE11
Authors: Yu-Qing Liu, Jian-Feng Xu, Yi-Qian Zhou, Tao Liang, Min Tang...
Venue: ACS Sustainable Chemistry &amp; Engineering
Published: 2026-11-10
Abs
Abstract
Soil acidification constrains crop productivity, and oyster shell (OS) waste is increasingly used as a sustainable liming material. Phosphorus (P), one of the three essential macronutrients for crops, can react with calcium (Ca)-containing materials during liming and thereby affect its bioavailability. However, cooking-related thermal treatments alter the physicochemical properties of OS, and whether this alteration affects phosphorus bioavailability during liming remains unclear. We hypothesized that thermal loss of the OS organic matrix strengthens calcium (Ca2+)-phosphate (HnPO4(3–n)–) interactions, promotes calcium phosphate (CaP) nucleation, reshapes soil microbial P-cycling responses, and thereby reduces plant-available phosphorus despite effective acidity neutralization. To test this, we compared raw (ROS), steamed (SOS), and grilled (GOS) OSs via organic matrix characterization, binding energy analyses for interfacial mechanisms, and pot experiments with maize in acidic soil for phosphorus bioavailability. Thermal treatment progressively degraded the organic matrix, particularly carboxyl-rich proteins, and subsequently enhanced phosphate binding on CaCO3. These stronger interactions accelerated heterogeneous CaP nucleation and promoted the formation of apatite-like CaP phases on thermally treated surfaces. In pot experiments, ROS increased maize shoot biomass and phosphorus uptake relative to SOS and GOS, and maintained higher soil-available phosphorus with lower Ca-bound phosphorus fractions, despite all treatments raising soil pH to a similar extent. At the microbial level, thermally treated OS was associated with a predicted phosphorus-starvation response and reduced soil phosphatase activities, whereas ROS sustained a more active microbial phosphorus-mineralizing community. Together, the abiotic interfacial reactions and the coupled microbial responses acted in concert to reduce phosphorus bioavailability under thermally treated OS, while ROS preserved both the organic-rich surface and microbial phosphorus turnover. Collectively, these results show that thermal treatment of OS enhances phosphate binding and CaP immobilization, reducing phosphorus bioavailability despite comparable acidity neutralization, and identify raw oyster shell as the preferred amendment for preserving phosphorus availability in acidic soils.

Abstract
Coastal soil salinization driven by sea-level rise and changing climate patterns is a serious threat to agricultural productivity and wetland ecosystems worldwide. This study evaluated temporal variations in soil salinity across seven coastal locations, namely low saline (Sites 6 and 7), moderately saline (Site 3), very saline (Sites 4 and 5), and strongly saline (Sites 1 and 2). Monthly topsoil samples (0–15 cm) were collected from May 2024 to April 2025 using GPS to assess climate-induced processes influencing salt accumulation, leaching, and redistribution. Soil pH of soil saturation paste (pHe) extract ranged from slightly acidic (6.58) to moderately alkaline (8.49), while the electrical conductivity of soil saturation paste extract (ECe) varied from 5.68 to 65.29 dS m–1, with peak salinity observed during dry months across all the sites. Elevated concentrations of soluble sodium and chloride ions in very saline and strongly saline sites showed significant intrusion of creek water. There was a higher content of potassium, calcium, magnesium, chloride, sulphate, carbonate, and bicarbonate during dry periods, whereas wet seasons promoted salt leaching and reduced Na⁺ levels. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) showed that PC1 explained 38% of the overall variance (eigenvalue 8.36), which was associated strongly with ECe, exchangeable sodium, calcium, exchangeable sodium percentage, sodium adsorption ratio, water-soluble potassium, calcium, magnesium, chloride, sulphur and available sulphur, and described the major salinity variation. However, rainfall, pHe, exchangeable potassium, water-soluble exchangeable percentage, and sodium adsorption ratio showed strong negative loadings, highlighting their major role in salinization dynamics. Spearman’s correlation confirmed that rainfall showed negative relationship with ECe and soluble salts because of leaching, whereas higher temperatures increased salinity through evaporation. These results provide a scientific basis for a management approach tailored to particular sites, such as salt-tolerant crops, enhanced drainage, residues management, and organic amendments, to support the resilience of coastal agro-ecosystems within varying climatic environments.
Biochar–Dolomite Co-Application Alleviates Soil Functional Decline Under Continuous Ganoderma lingzhi Cropping and Promotes Quality FormationCORE4

Abstract
Soil degradation caused by continuous cropping has become a major constraint on stable Ganoderma lingzhi production, while biochar–mineral amendments are commonly used to restore degraded soils. However, the specific effects of continuous cropping on Ganoderma lingzhi production and soil functions, as well as the potential of co-application to restore its yield and quality, remain unclear. A Ganoderma lingzhi microcosm system was established with first-cropping, continuous-cropping, and different biochar–dolomite treatments. Soil physicochemical properties, enzyme activities, microbial communities, yield, quality traits, and heavy metals were evaluated. Continuous cropping significantly reduced soil pH, organic carbon, total nitrogen, available nutrients, microbial diversity, and Ganoderma lingzhi yield. Fruiting-body dry weight and triterpenoid content declined by 20.09% and 24.62%, respectively. Biochar–dolomite co-application effectively alleviated the adverse effects of continuous cropping. Compared with other co-application treatments, the 30% biochar + 70% dolomite treatment showed strong overall recovery capacity. Compared with continuous cropping, this treatment increased fruiting-body biomass by up to 54.81% and triterpenes by up to 75.57%. Different co-application ratios produced distinct ecological regulation patterns: the dolomite treatment favored soil pH elevation, available nutrient and S-NPH accumulation, and sporulation; the biochar treatment showed advantages in S-UE activity and heavy metal control; and the 70% biochar + 30% dolomite and 50% biochar + 50% dolomite treatments regulated carbon transformation processes and microbial community recovery, respectively. Co-application also increased microbial richness and diversity and reshaped community structure. Structural equation modeling indicated that the amendments strengthened positive links among soil nutrients, the ionic environment, microorganisms, enzyme activities, and Ganoderma lingzhi quality, revealing a potential nutrient and ionic environment-microorganism-enzyme-Ganoderma lingzhi quality pathway. In summary, biochar–dolomite co-application alleviates the continuous cropping obstacle of Ganoderma lingzhi and improves its yield and functional quality by improving the soil physicochemical environment, promoting humus accumulation, and reshaping microbial community structure, providing a theoretical basis for quality improvement of medicinal fungi and soil ecological regulation under continuous cropping.

Abstract
A novel dual-mode optical sensor and controlled-delivery hydrogel system (CNF-PEI-AMX-Urea) was successfully engineered using biomass-derived carbon nanofibers (CNFs) decorated with active functional groups and co-entrapped with urea and amoxicillin (AMX). Morphological and structural evaluations confirmed an interconnected fibrous network with ultrafine widths ranging tightly between 2.40–4.76 nm and an amorphous-to-nanocrystalline sp2-hybridized graphitic framework. FTIR spectroscopy verified an intensely hydrophilic boundary layer rich in amine (3471 cm−1), hydroxyl (3372 cm−1), carbonyl (1669 cm−1), and graphitic carbon (1408 cm−1) functional groups, achieving a high operational degree of substitution of 0.52. The fabricated platform demonstrated striking dual-mode optical performance, functioning simultaneously as a daylight halochromic sensor exhibiting pronounced color difference (ΔE) values reaching 4.50 at pH 3 and 8.08 at pH 9 for blank beads, and 2.54 (pH 3) and 3.15 (pH 9) for the urea-loaded test system and as a pH-responsive fluorogenic probe under UV illumination with distinct emission shifts across acidic, neutral, and alkaline conditions. Furthermore, in vitro release studies conducted at a simulated soil pH of 6 demonstrated a slow, sustained, and diffusion-controlled delivery profile governed closely by the Higuchi model (R2 = 0.9747 for urea, R2 = 0.9811 for AMX), avoiding burst release and achieving 24-hour cumulative releases of 4.08% for urea and 15.58% for AMX. Overall, this multifunctional hybrid platform successfully unifies smart optical pH sensing with regulated agricultural delivery, presenting exceptional potential for intelligent real-time environmental monitoring and sustainable agricultural applications.

Groundwater Quality Assessment in Akoko District, Southwestern Nigeria: A GIS-Based Hydrogeochemical and Statistical AnalysisPROXY2

Abstract
The practice of exclusive rain-fed agriculture may not guarantee food security in today’s rapidly growing and urbanizing Nigerian society due to seasonality of rainfall regimes. This study employed index-based analytical and statistical techniques in GIS environment to evaluate the hydrogeochemical processes influencing groundwater quality in the Akoko area of Nigeria with a view to determining the suitability of the groundwater for drinking and irrigation purposes. On-site pH, TDS, and EC were measured in situ using Hanna meter. While SO4 2+ was determined using a colorimeter, Ca2+, Na+ , and K+ were measured using a flame photometer. Titrimetric approach was adopted for estimating HCO3 - , Cl-, and TH. The predominance of the cations and anions followed Ca2+>Mg2+>Na+>K + and HCO3 ->Cl->SO4 2->NO3 - order respectively. Alkali earth metals (Ca2+ + Mg2+) dominated over alkali metals (Na+ + K+ ), and (CO3 - + HCO3 - ) exceeded (SO4 2- + Cl- ), suggesting continental environment. Negative chloro-alkaline indices suggested exchange of (Ca2+ + Mg2+) in groundwater for (Na+ + K+ ) in the aquifer. Ionic ratios exhibited depletion of Na+ against Cl- , and prevalence of weathering processes. The pH values ranged from 6.4 to 7.8 (slightly acidic to alkaline). The groundwater was moderately hard to hard, signifying a strong influence of bicarbonate ions. The most prevalent water type was Ca-HCO3, indicating silicate minerals dissolution consistent with Gibbs plot. From PCA results, strong correlations existed between DWQI and EC, TDS, Cl- , SO4 2- , Na+ , and K+ . Generally, the groundwater is drinkable and largely suitable for irrigation following WHO standards and FAO benchmarks with possible salinity management.

Abstract
Soil contamination by heavy metals represents a significant environmental challenge, primarily due to the persistent and toxic nature of these inorganic species and their detrimental effects on the resident soil microbial communities. This study investigates the impact of heavy metal exposure on bacterial growth and diversity in contaminated soil from two distinct sites in Tikrit city: an agricultural field (S1) and an industrial‐adjacent area (S2). The core of the analysis utilizes the macromolecular biopolymer, 16S ribosomal RNA (rRNA), as a molecular marker for bacterial identification and diversity assessment. Soil samples were challenged with varying concentrations of lead (Pb) and cadmium (Cd) (0.5, 1, and 2 mg/L), and cobalt (Co) and molybdenum (Mo) (0.1, 0.2, and 0.5 mg/L) to evaluate their concentration‐dependent effects on bacterial proliferation. The 16S rRNA gene was extracted from the microbial consortia and amplified via polymerase chain reaction, followed by sequencing and comparative analysis against the NCBI rRNA database. Results demonstrated a clear inhibitory effect of heavy metals on bacterial growth, which intensified with increasing metal concentration. Specifically, lead (Pb) exhibited a stimulatory effect at the lowest concentration (0.5 mg/L) before becoming inhibitory, while cadmium (Cd) showed a strong, dose‐dependent inhibition, leading to a complete absence of growth at 2 mg/L. Cobalt (Co) and molybdenum (Mo) presented varied effects, with Mo notably supporting growth in the S2 sample at its highest tested concentration (0.5 mg/L).

Abstract
Deciphering the intricate correlation between polymer backbone chemistry and electrochemical performance remains a pivotal challenge in the rational design of ion-exchange membranes for flow batteries. Herein, we design and synthesize two sulfonated poly(arylene) membranes with identical ion-exchange capacity yet divergent backbone architectures, and systematically interrogate their performance in redox flow batteries. We unveil that the electronic nature of the bridging groups critically dictates thermal stability, ionic conductivity, area resistance, and ion permselectivity. Impressively, the SPAEKES membrane affords an energy efficiency of 86.6% at 200 mA cm-2 in AZIFBs, sustaining stable operation up to an ultrahigh current density of 540 mA cm-2, while its acid-treated counterpart (SPAESEK-H) exhibits superior proton conduction and effectively suppresses vanadium crossover in VRFBs, delivering an energy efficiency of 74.5% at 200 mA cm-2 and a peak power density of 500 mW cm-2. Both membranes demonstrate exceptional durability under prolonged cycling. Collectively, our findings establish a molecular-design strategy for ketone- and sulfone-modified sulfonated poly(arylene) membranes, in which tuning the electronic nature of the bridging groups addresses the distinct demands of acidic versus alkaline systems, offering a rational pathway toward high-performance, application-tailored ion-exchange membranes.

Abstract
Poorly managed urban waste-disposal sites may affect environmental water and soil quality. The objective was to describe the microbiological and chosen physicochemical characteristics of borehole water and soil at six urban locations associated with waste disposal in Abia State, Nigeria. A descriptive environmental study was used to summarise June 2025 laboratory certificates containing 18 coded borehole-water entries from six locations, three reported soil readings per site-depth group (12 groups), and accompanying physicochemical measurements. Site and site-depth means were used for descriptive analysis. The water microbiology certificate reported mean (SD) total heterotrophic bacteria and total coliforms of 95.83 (62.16) and 55.50 (42.59) CFU/100 mL, respectively, across six location means; all 18 entries contained positive faecal-coliform values. Borehole-water pH ranged from 5.77 to 6.40, and mean lead concentrations across locations ranged from 0.049 to 0.223 mg/L. Mean soil moisture was 7.95%, and site-depth mean total petroleum hydrocarbons ranged from 9.10 to 16.37 mg/kg. The observations identified variations in microbial and chemical characteristics of borehole water and soil that warrant integrated environmental-health surveillance. Quantitative regulatory water-quality classification and attribution to individual dumpsites were outside the scope of this descriptive study.

Abstract
Heavy metal soil pollution is still a serious environmental issue as the risk assessment relies not only on total metal concentration but also on chemical speciation and mobility. DOM impacts the metal transport via complexation, competitive adsorption, and colloid-facilitation; however, its effect varies depending on molecular structure and concentrations. The column experiments of soil for Cd, Cu, and Pb were carried out with the application of control, low- and high-concentration straw-derived DOM, and low- and high-concentration humic-rich DOM. The BCR sequential extraction, ICP-MS, UV-Vis spectroscopy, EEM-PARAFAC, and XGBoost-SHAP techniques were combined to associate DOM properties with the speciation and mobility of metals. Simulation results which fit the experimental setting demonstrated that high straw-derived DOM increased the Cd acid-soluble fraction up to 61.3% and cumulative migration up to 31.4%; however, for high humic-rich DOM, the parameters equal 39.5% and 11.6%. The R² of XGBoost models was higher than that of Random Forest and SVR models and equal to 0.93, 0.90, and 0.88 for Cd, Cu, and Pb migration, correspondingly. According to SHAP analysis, acid-soluble fraction and DOC played the dominant role for the migration of Cd; however, humic-like fluorescence and SUVA254 had a greater impact on Cu and Pb.

Abstract
The “Korzeniowy Dół” gully, located on the Nałęczów Plateau within Kazimierz Landscape Park (SE Poland), is a distinctive loess landform surrounded by agricultural land, where surface runoff may contribute to the accumulation of fertiliser-derived radionuclides and heavy metals. This study presents the first integrated assessment of natural and artificial radioactivity, heavy-metal concentrations, and selected physicochemical properties of soils from this site. Eight surface soil samples (0–10 cm) were analysed using high-resolution HPGe gamma-ray spectrometry, X-ray fluorescence (XRF), and standard soil-science methods. Relationships among the measured parameters were investigated using correlation analysis, hierarchical cluster analysis (HCA), and principal component analysis (PCA). The mean activity concentrations of ⁶Ra, Th, and ⁴⁰K were 24.6, 23.5, and 370 Bqkg⁻, respectively, all below the corresponding UNSCEAR world averages. The mean activity concentration of ⁷Cs was 1.07 Bqkg⁻, indicating low-level residual fallout. Radiological hazard indices were also low, with a mean external hazard index (Hₑₓ) of 0.23, an absorbed gamma dose rate of 41.0 nGyh⁻, and an outdoor annual effective dose of 0.050 mSvy⁻. Heavy-metal concentrations were generally within the range reported for Polish loess soils. Correlation and multivariate analyses indicated that the distribution of natural radionuclides and several trace elements was predominantly controlled by lithogenic factors, particularly the clay fraction and the carbonate-rich soil matrix. Overall, the results indicate a low external radiological hazard and provide baseline data for future environmental monitoring of loess landscapes in the Lublin Upland.
