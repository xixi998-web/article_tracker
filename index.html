Environmental determinants of phenazine- and 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol-producing Pseudomonas in the wheat rhizosphere across a precipitation gradientCORE8
Authors: O. Mavrodi, Alexander Panchenko, Oleksandr Dykhovychnyi, Ian Leslie, Lyudmyla Barannyk...
Venue: Microbiology Spectrum
Published: 2026-10-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
ABSTRACT The Columbia Plateau of the Inland Pacific Northwest (IPNW) encompasses one of North America’s largest dryland wheat production regions, where soil moisture strongly influences plant growth, health, and rhizosphere microbial communities. Two ecologically and genetically distinct groups of the Pseudomonas fluorescens complex naturally suppress soilborne wheat pathogens in IPNW fields by producing either phenazine-1-carboxylic acid (Phz+) or 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol (Phl+). To determine how precipitation and associated soil factors shape the abundance of these bacteria, we analyzed wheat root samples from commercial fields spanning a precipitation gradient across the Columbia Plateau. We quantified pairwise relationships among environmental variables using Spearman correlations and constructed a correlation network to visualize patterns of association. Network analysis revealed that precipitation influences soil properties indirectly, with organic matter, clay content, cation exchange capacity, and pH forming an interconnected cluster of correlated variables. Wheat rhizosphere colonization frequency by Phz+ pseudomonads was primarily associated with soil pH, which correlated with precipitation through its effects on weathering and organic matter accumulation, whereas colonization frequency of Phl+ pseudomonads showed weaker associations with abiotic variables. Phz+ pseudomonads were favored in neutral to alkaline, coarse soils typical of the low-precipitation zone, whereas Phl+ populations thrived in wetter, organic-rich soils with lower pH. These findings highlight soil pH as a key environmental filter mediating precipitation-driven differences in Pseudomonas populations and suggest that biotic interactions and plant selection further modulate root colonization. Understanding how these agriculturally important microbial functional groups respond to moisture regimes provides insight into the sustainability of disease-suppressive microbiomes in the IPNW. IMPORTANCE Dryland wheat-based production systems of the Inland Pacific Northwest are highly vulnerable to changing precipitation patterns that affect crop productivity, soilborne pathogens, and soil microbial ecology. This study reveals how long-term rainfall gradients influence the balance between two groups of beneficial Pseudomonas that protect wheat from root diseases. We show that soil pH, closely linked to rainfall, organic matter, and mineral weathering, is a key abiotic factor controlling the presence of phenazine-producing bacteria in dryland wheat grown in low-precipitation zone soils. In contrast, 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol producers dominate in wetter, organic-rich environments. These insights help explain how beneficial microbiomes adapt to environmental constraints and identify soil conditions that promote natural disease suppression. Understanding these mechanisms will aid in predicting microbiome resilience and guiding management strategies to maintain wheat productivity under changing weather conditions. Simulated Environmental Aging Increases Cadmium Extractability in Acidic Soil Amended with Cadmium-Containing Rice-Straw BiocharCORE6
Authors: Xian-Xiang Li, Yu-Chao Fan, Hong-Biao Cui, Li-Ya Liu, Rui Xia...
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-10-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Biochar is widely used to remediate acidic soils, but the long-term risks from its endogenous heavy metals remain unclear. We hypothesized that simulated environmental aging would increase cadmium (Cd) extractability in biochar-amended acidic clean soils. Two rice-straw biochars derived from clean (CB) and polluted (PB) sites were prepared and Cd extractability were investigated in the two biochars and their amended acidic soils before and after accelerated simulated aging. Environmental aging was simulated by combination of the cycles of wetting–drying, freezing–thawing, acid rain, and fertilizer application. Results showed that aging significantly reduced biochar pH by 1.05–2.02 units and increased oxygen-containing functional groups, while decreased aromaticity and graphitization. These changes may have contributed to endogenous Cd release, as indicated by increase in water-soluble and CaCl2-extractable Cd by 19.9–150.7% and 26.1–109.9%, respectively. Biochar application at 5% rate raised soil pH by 1.12 units but increased total soil Cd by 81.3–120.3%. The 1–8 years of aging increased water-soluble (17.19–281.6 µg·kg−1) and CaCl2-extractable (37.07–279.78 µg·kg−1) Cd by 2.53–56.8-fold and 1.76–18.9-fold in amended soils, but only decreased the acid-soluble Cd by 13.7–24.0% for CB-treated soils. Collectively, the accelerated simulated aging equivalent to 8 years weakened pH amelioration, but increased Cd extractability. Our findings suggest that biochar application at such a high rate offers short-term pH benefits in acidic clean soils, but amplifies soil Cd extractability, highlighting the need for long-term field risk assessment.
Spatial Variability and Influencing Factors of Soil P and K in a High-Altitude Mining Area: A Case Study in Southwest ChinaCORE6

Venue: Polish Journal of Environmental Studies

Abstract
The spatial variation and influencing factors of various forms of P and K in soil from a high-altitude mining area were assessed using a combination of traditional statistical and geostatistical methods. The primary factors affecting the distributions of P and K were identified using correlation analysis. The mean total phosphorus (TP), available phosphorus (AP), inorganic phosphorus (IP), total potassium (TK), quick-release potassium (AK), and slow-release potassium (SAK) were 1280.78 mg kg-1, 8.99 mg kg-1, 554.04 mg kg-1, 15358.60 mg kg-1, 215.14 mg kg-1, and 1381.70 mg kg-1, respectively. The coefficients of variation ranged from 44.79% to 80.89%, indicating that all parameters exhibited strong variation intensity. The block-to-base ratio of semivariance varied from 0.00% to 24.53%, indicating strong spatial autocorrelation. Moran’s I analysis revealed significant spatial clustering for TP and TK. However, the spatial distributions of AP, IP, and SAK may be random. Soil pH was negatively correlated with TP and AP, positively correlated with IP, and not correlated with TK, AK, or SAK. SOM was positively correlated with TP and AP and significantly negatively correlated with IP and TK, but not with AK or SAK. Cation exchange capacity was significantly positively correlated with TP, but significantly negatively correlated with TK. Except for IP, there was no significant correlation between soil particle composition and the various forms of P and K. Elevation and aspect had no effect on the distribution of various forms of P and K. NDVI was significantly positively correlated with TP and AP and negatively correlated with IP and TK. Mining activities can strongly affect the availability of P and K. These results have practical significance for soil management and ecological restoration in high-altitude mining areas.
Comparative Assessment of Soil Physicochemical Properties across Active Mining, Logistics, Peripheral and Agricultural Zones in the North Karanpura Coalfield, Jharkhand, IndiaCORE5

Venue: Asian Research Journal of Agriculture

Abstract
This study evaluates differences in topsoil physicochemical properties among four techno-ecological settings in the North Karanpura Coalfield, Jharkhand, India: an active opencast mining site (Amrapali), a coal-logistics zone on reclaimed land (Piparwar village), a mining-peripheral site (Binglath village), and an agricultural/low-disturbance reference site (Honhe village). Composite topsoil samples (0–30 cm) were assessed for soil texture, bulk density, porosity, specific gravity, pH, electrical conductivity (EC), organic matter, available phosphorus, available sulphate, and moisture content. Descriptive comparisons were complemented by exploratory Pearson correlation analysis, principal component analysis (PCA), and a Minimum-Data-Set-informed relative Soil Quality Index (SQI). Because the dataset contains one composite value per site rather than independent replicated observations, the multivariate analyses are descriptive and hypothesis-generating; they are not used for significance testing or causal inference. Available sulphate was excluded from the multivariate and index calculations because its reported values require verification against the original laboratory records. Using eight indicators—pH, EC, bulk density, porosity, clay, available phosphorus, moisture content, and soil organic matter—the recalculated relative SQI values were 0.33 for Amrapali, 0.41 for Piparwar, 0.40 for Binglath, and 0.63 for Honhe. The observed site differences are consistent with a gradient of physical and chemical disturbance, including textural coarsening, lower moisture content, acidic pH, and lower available phosphorus at the mining-influenced sites. However, the single composite observation per site prevents the separation of site effects from sampling variability. The study therefore provides a preliminary comparative baseline and identifies specific measurements that should be replicated and verified in future monitoring rounds.
Laccase and Tyrosinase Activities Reveal Contrasting Responses Associated with Soil C and N Pools Across Italian Land-Use SystemsCORE4
Authors: M. Mazzon, Andrea Ciurli, C. Marzadori, L. Cavani
Venue: Journal of Soil Science and Plant Nutrition
Published: 2026-10-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Soil oxidative enzymes play a key role in the transformation of complex organic compounds and the regulation of soil organic matter (SOM) dynamics. This study investigated laccase and tyrosinase activities across five Italian sites representing contrasting land-use systems and management regimes. Enzyme activities were evaluated in relation to soil pH, soil organic carbon (SOC), total nitrogen (TN), dissolved organic carbon (DOC), total dissolved nitrogen (TDN), and C:N stoichiometric ratios. Soil biochemical variability was primarily structured by site, whereas management effects were evident mainly where contrasting practices substantially altered C and N availability and substrate inputs. Laccase and tyrosinase displayed contrasting responses to soil C and N gradients. Laccase activity was positively associated with DOC and the DOC:TDN ratio and decreased with increasing pH, whereas tyrosinase increased with TDN and pH and was negatively associated with SOC:TN and DOC:TDN. These relationships suggest that the two enzymes were associated with distinct oxidative pathways involved in SOM transformation. Stoichiometric indicators were associated with broad-scale variability in enzyme activity across contrasting sites, but their predictive power was weaker within individual sites, where the narrower range of C variation may increase the relative importance of local factors. Management effects were mostly evident in the tomato system, where conventional, organic, and agroecological practices altered the relationships between oxidative enzymes and C:N ratios. The findings highlighted the functional divergence between laccase and tyrosinase and support their combined assessment, together with soil C and N pools, as complementary indicators of SOM transformation across contrasting land-use systems.

Abstract
Soil salinization is a global challenge for sustainable agricultural development. Composite modifiers have the potential to boost the agricultural productivity of salinized soils. However, it’s still unclear whether it outperforms single modifiers in regulating rhizosphere soil properties, microbial communities, and forage yield and quality of different oat varieties ( Avena sativa L.) and the mechanisms. In this field experiment in 2023–2024 in the arid western China, five modifier treatments, namely CK (control group without any modifier), TJ (microbial agent), TF (organic fertilizer), TG (silica powder), and TJFG (microbial agent + organic fertilizer + silica powder), were designed for two oat varieties (TY 60, Tianyan 60; MST 2, Musite 2). Then, their effects on salinized soil properties, rhizosphere microbial communities, and the interaction between soil nutrient cycling and microbial composition were explored. The crude protein (CP) content of the two oat varieties peaked under the TJFG treatment. The rhizosphere soil NH 4 + -N, available phosphorus (AP), available potassium (AK), organic matter (SOM), and water content (SWC) of the TJFG treatment were significantly higher than those of CK, while the soil pH and NO 3 − -N content were significantly lower. In addition, the AP content of the TJFG treatment was 70.17, 44.91, and 66.93% (average over 2 years) higher than that of the TJ, TF, and TG treatments, respectively. The application of TJFG improved the diversity of bacterial and fungal communities as well as the activities of phosphatase (ATP), urease (UE), and catalase (CAT), thereby promoting soil nutrient transformations and enhancing soil fertility, oat yield, and quality. Chloroflexota and Ascomycota were the key microbial phyla to regulate oat yield. Organic matter, AK, AP, and CAT activity were the main soil factors affecting yield. Overall, MST 2 has greater growth potential and more value for promotion and application than TY 60. This study provides an important reference for salinized land use, ecological health maintenance, and sustainable agricultural production in arid regions.
Remobilization risk of Cd in aged pig manure biochar under rice rhizosphere influence: Evidence from six-season cultivation.PROXY3
Authors: Xiao-Qing Zhang, Fei Chen, Ze-Ning Yu, Rong-Liang Qiu, Xin-Xian Long
Venue: Journal of environmental management
Published: 2026-10-15
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Biochar is widely used to immobilize cadmium (Cd) in contaminated paddy soils; however, the long-term stability of biochar-adsorbed Cd under rice rhizosphere influence remains poorly understood, raising concerns about its potential secondary release. Herein, we investigated the remobilization behavior and speciation evolution of Cd from Cd-loaded pig manure biochar (Cd-PMB) using a six-season rice pot experiment, combined with a sequential simulated aging procedure designed to decouple the contributions of soil solution chemistry, root exudates, and oxidative processes. Two rice cultivars with contrasting Cd accumulation capacities were employed. Total Cd in Cd-PMB decreased by 84.3%-89.2% after six seasons, with >50% of the loss occurring during the first season, driven predominantly by cation exchange and rhizosphere acidification. Speciation analysis revealed that the ion-exchangeable Cd fraction was almost completely released (>95%) irrespective of planting treatment, while physically adsorbed and hydrogen-bonded Cd were preferentially remobilized under rhizosphere influence. Notably, complexed Cd exhibited a dual-phase dynamic, which initially increased by 8.1%-19.3% through secondary complexation onto newly formed carboxyl sites and subsequently declined during prolonged aging as these sites underwent protonation and oxidative degradation, as confirmed by Fourier-transform infrared and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy analyses and further supported by significant Pearson correlations between COOH/ CO32- and Qcom-Cd (r = 0.801, p < 0.05). The simulated aging experiments confirmed that soil solution, root exudates, and oxidative aging synergistically promote Cd release from distinct binding pools. Our findings demonstrate that biochar-adsorbed Cd undergoes substantial, binding form-dependent remobilization during aging, posing a secondary risk that necessitates explicit consideration in long-term safety assessments of biochar-amended soils.

Abstract
High-Andean drylands are environmentally heterogeneous ecosystems, yet soil bacterial communities in southern Peru remain poorly characterized. This exploratory study examined bacterial communities in 23 soil samples collected from five sites in Candarave, Tacna, representing herbaceous–shrubland, succulent shrubland, and agricultural environments that also differed in elevation, soil pH, moisture, and historical aridity descriptors. Bacterial communities were characterized by 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing. Actinobacteriota, Chloroflexota, and Proteobacteria predominated across the sampled sites. Alpha diversity differed among sites (Kruskal–Wallis, p ≤ 0.009) and was highest at the two agricultural sites (P4 and P5; Shannon index, 11.33 ± 0.34 and 11.35 ± 0.17) and lowest at the herbaceous–shrubland site P1 (9.52 ± 0.75). ANCOM-BC indicated higher abundances of Azospirillales at both agricultural sites and of Blastococcus and Solirubrobacter mainly at P4, whereas Candidatus Udaeobacter and Conexibacter were more strongly represented at P3, the succulent shrubland site. Unweighted UniFrac analysis showed marked differences in bacterial community composition among the five sampling sites (PERMANOVA, pseudo-F = 4.67, p = 0.001), while multivariate dispersion did not differ significantly (PERMDISP, F = 0.686, p = 0.153). These results provide a descriptive baseline for soil bacterial diversity in an understudied high-Andean region and document substantial community heterogeneity among sites differing simultaneously in environmental conditions, vegetation, and land use.

Abstract Micronutrient availability in carbonate-rich sierozems of dissected foothill landscapes is controlled by both vertical profile differentiation and lateral redistribution of soil material along erosion-deposition catenas. This study aimed to characterize the radial and lateral distribution of Cu, Zn, Mn, B, and S in non-eroded upper-slope, moderately eroded midslope, and depositional footslope typical sierozems formed on Neogene deposits and to evaluate cotton responses to mineral, sulfur-containing, micronutrient, and organomineral fertilization. Abstract
This study investigated the effect of pH on the adsorption capacity of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) during the fortification of watermelon rind (Citrullus lanatus) using potassium ammonium phosphate KNH₄PO₄) for potential application as a soil amendment. Watermelon rind was processed into a powdered adsorbent and subjected to batch adsorption experiments at different pH levels (2, 4, 6, 8, and 10). The adsorption capacities of N, P, and K were determined after equilibration using standard analytical methods. Results showed that pH significantly influenced nutrient adsorption, with adsorption capacity decreasing as pH increased. The highest adsorption capacities were recorded at pH 2, with values of 5.81 mg g⁻¹, 5.87 mg g⁻¹, and 5.91 mg g⁻¹ for nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, respectively. The enhanced adsorption under acidic conditions was attributed to the protonation of surface functional groups and increased electrostatic attraction between nutrient ions and adsorption sites on the watermelon rind. The findings indicate that acidic conditions favor nutrient loading and retention, thereby enhancing the suitability of fortified watermelon rind as a slow-release soil amendment for sustainable agriculture.

Abstract

Abstract

Published: 2026-10-15

Abstract
Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a multifactorial neurodegenerative disorder characterised by progressive cognitive decline, amyloid-β (Aβ) deposition, tau hyperphosphorylation, synaptic dysfunction, and chronic neuroinflammation. While rare familial cases are genetically driven, most AD is sporadic, underscoring the critical role of environmental determinants. Emerging evidence from epidemiological, experimental, and exposomic studies identifies environmental neurotoxicants, including air pollutants, heavy metals, pesticides, and emerging contaminants such as micro- and nanoplastics, as significant accelerators of brain ageing and AD pathogenesis.These toxicants access the central nervous system via systemic circulation, olfactory translocation, and gut-brain axis interactions, converging on shared pathogenic mechanisms such as oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired proteostasis, and neuroimmune activation. Together, these processes promote amyloidogenic processing of amyloid precursor protein, enhance Aβ aggregation, and drive tau pathology, accelerating neurodegenerative cascades. Notably, early-life exposures induce persistent epigenetic and metabolic reprogramming, increasing lifelong vulnerability, particularly in genetically susceptible markers such as APOE ε4 carriers. Although preclinical models robustly demonstrate pollutant-induced AD-like phenotypes, translation into clinical interventions remains limited. Integrating environmental exposures into AD pathogenesis frameworks highlights the exposome as a modifiable risk factor and emphasizes the urgent need for longitudinal human studies, mechanistic validation, and preventive public health strategies targeting environmental risk reduction.

Abstract
Nitrogen (N) use efficiency is critical in ensuring food production while reducing nitrate (NO3--N) to ammonium (NH4+-N) is widely recognized as a key for reducing N loss from paddy fields. Microbial involvement has long been regarded as the indispensable step for NO3--N reduction to NH4+-N in nature; it remains largely unknown whether abiotic processes can drive this. Here, using laser confocal fluorescence microscopy and thermodynamic laws, we find that Fe(II) ions in the rice rhizosphere exist on iron plaque in a specific manner with strong reducing capability. These specifically adsorbed Fe(II) ions (Fe(II)SA) could mediate the abiotic reduction of NO3--N to NH4+-N, accounting for 57% of the total NO3--N reduction. When cultured in a sterile nutrient solution containing only 15NO3--N and Fe(II) ions, over 90% of N in the xylem of rice plants exists as 15NH4+-N, directly proving the occurrence of NO3--N reduction to NH4+-N and taken up by rice. Theoretical calculations elucidate the Fe(II)SA-induced local electron depolarization enhances interfacial charge transfer which drives this reaction with the conversion of *NO to *NOH/*NHO identified as the rate-limiting step. We then propose a regulatory strategy to establish a stable Fe(II)SA/Fe(III)SA cycle utilizing FeC for controlled-release of Fe(II) ions. Two-year field experiments confirmed this strategy can increase rice yield by 16.1% while cutting half of the N input. Our work enables the design of novel pathways in improving N use efficiency for sustainable rice production.

Abstract
Abstract— Mine waste heaps in the Eastern Donetsk basin (Donbass) are long-term sources of heavy metal pollution of adjacent ecosystems. This study assessed the efficiency of induced bioremediation of Technosol (Spolic Technosol (Toxic)) using Elytrigia repens and Medicago sativa, citric acid as a chelating agent, and the arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus Glomus sp. in the waste heap impact zone in the Rostov region. In a model pot experiment with separate and combined application of the chelating agent and mycorrhiza, changes in the physicochemical properties of the soil, plant morphological traits, and the accumulation and distribution of heavy metals (Mn, Zn, Cd, Ni, Pb, Cu, Cr) in plant organs were assessed. The translocation factor and accumulation coefficient were used as biogeochemical indicators. The application of citric acid decreased pH, increased the measured organic carbon content (via dichromate oxidation) and the mobility of metals, while mycorrhization stimulated plant growth and root system development, which together produced a synergistic enhancement of phytoremediation. The greatest effect was observed with the combined use of amendments: the total removal of heavy metals reached 13.8 kg/ha for M. sativa and 3.7 kg/ha for E. repens. The heavy-metal accumulation coefficient in the roots of E. repens reached 15.8 for Cd and 3.7 for Pb, while the translocation factor did not exceed 0.9, indicating the predominant immobilization of pollutants in the root system. For M. sativa, on the contrary, the translocation factor increased to 3.5 (for Cu) and 2.5 (for Cd, Cr), indicating the more efficient transfer of these metals to the shoots. These results indicate the potential of E. repens as a phytostabilizer and M. sativa as a phytoextractor in combination with citric acid and mycorrhiza for restoring heavy-metal-contaminated soils on mine waste heaps.
