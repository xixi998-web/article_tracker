Effects of Food Waste Digestate and Mineral Fertilization on Soil Fertility and Maize GrowthCORE9
Authors: B. E. L. Sousa, R. Magalhães, F. Evangelista, J. Barroso, Thamires Nascimento de Oliveira...
Venue: AgriEngineering
Published: 2026-10-10
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Digestate is a byproduct of the anaerobic digestion of food waste and can be used as a fertilizer in agricultural production. Combining mineral fertilizers with food waste digestate represents a promising strategy to improve soil fertility and enhance plant nutrition and growth. Therefore, this study evaluated the effects of different proportions of food waste anaerobic digestate combined with mineral fertilization on soil fertility, growth, and the nutritional status of maize (Zea mays L.) plants. The experiment was conducted under greenhouse conditions using a completely randomized design with six treatments: unfertilized control (C0), 100% mineral fertilizer (M100), 75% mineral fertilizer + 25% digestate (M75D25), 50% mineral fertilizer + 50% digestate (M50D50), 25% mineral fertilizer + 75% digestate (M25D75), and 100% digestate (D100), with four replicates. The chemical characterization of the digestate revealed concentrations of 0.70 g kg−1 total N, 0.60 g kg−1 P, 0.60 g kg−1 K, and 1.30 g kg−1 Na. The 100% mineral treatment (M100) supplied 120 kg ha−1 urea, 80 kg ha−1 triple superphosphate, and 70 kg ha−1 potassium chloride, while digestate application rates were calculated based on total N supply, with mineral P and K adjusted proportionally. M25D75 significantly increased cation exchange capacity, base saturation, and soil pH and resulted in higher soil P and Mg concentrations; however, it also increased soil Na concentration and exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP). M25D75, M50D50, M75D25, and M100 promoted higher chlorophyll index, stem diameter, number of leaves, plant height, and shoot dry biomass, as well as higher P, K, Ca, and Mg concentrations in plant tissues. In contrast, the exclusive application of digestate (D100) increased electrical conductivity, exchangeable sodium percentage, and Na concentrations in both soil and plant tissues, resulting in reduced plant growth. Therefore, high doses of digestate may increase the risk of soil sodicity. Overall, the combined application of mineral fertilizers and food waste digestate improved soil fertility, plant nutrition, and maize growth. These findings support the recycling of food waste through its agricultural use as digestate, contributing to circular nutrient management while reducing dependence on synthetic mineral fertilizers.
Crop-Specific Heavy Metal Transfer in Tropical Agricultural Soils: Soil Drivers and Management Implications Across Hainan IslandCORE9
Authors: Lei Su, Zhen-Liang Wang, Zhi-Zhuo Liu, Jian-Zhou Yang, Xue-Miao Du
Venue: Agriculture
Published: 2026-10-10
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Heavy metal transfer from agricultural soils to edible crops is an important concern for food safety and sustainable land management in tropical regions, where intensive weathering and heterogeneous parent materials can decouple total soil metal concentrations from plant uptake. In this study, 1120 soil samples and 1093 crop samples representing 55 crop species and five operational crop groups were collected across Hainan Island, China. Geological background and 38 soil physicochemical properties, including available micronutrients such as B, Cu, Fe, Mn, Mo, S, and Zn, were integrated into Random Forest models to evaluate their associations with the bioaccumulation factors (BCFs) of Pb, Cr, Cd, As, and Hg. Mean BCFs on a dry-weight basis were 1.144 for Cd, 0.111 for Hg, 0.057 for Cr, 0.046 for As, and 0.009 for Pb. Cd transfer was relatively higher in vegetables, whereas woody fruits generally showed lower transfer. Five-block spatial validation with a 5 km training buffer yielded BCF R2 values of 0.045 for Cd, 0.122 for Cr, 0.060 for Hg, −0.079 for Pb, and −0.061 for As, which were substantially lower than the corresponding OOB estimates. Removing soil Cr reduced the spatial R2 for Cr BCF from 0.122 to 0.002, whereas removal of soil Cd had little effect on Cd BCF performance. Excluding crop group also reduced predictive performance for Cd, Cr, and Hg. Geological background was associated with total soil metal distributions, whereas soil pH, Fe/Al-related variables, exchangeable cations, and crop group showed associations with variation in soil-to-crop transfer. Accumulated local effects analysis showed nonlinear fitted relationships between BCF and several key soil properties within the observed data range. Overall, total soil metal concentrations alone provided an incomplete indication of crop accumulation. These results highlight the value of considering soil acidity, exchangeable base-cation status, geological background, and crop type together when evaluating heavy-metal transfer in highly weathered tropical agricultural soils.

Abstract
Land Suitability for Lowland Rice (*Oryza sativa* L.) Cultivation in Bilah Hulu and Bilah Barat Districts, Labuhanbatu Regency, North Sumatra. Land suitability evaluation was conducted using the FAO (1976) framework. This study utilized data from soil sample analyses taken from three representative land observation points in each district. Evaluated parameters included climatic factors (temperature, humidity), soil physical properties (texture, effective depth, drainage, slope), soil chemical properties (pH, organic C, total N, P₂O₅, K₂O), and nutrient retention properties (CEC, base saturation). The results indicate that actual land suitability in Bilah Hulu District is dominated by Class S3 (marginally suitable), covering 866.8 ha (40.6%), whereas Bilah Barat District is dominated by Class S2 (moderately suitable), covering 704.25 ha (33.0%). The primary limiting factors in both districts are total nitrogen content (T), drainage (Dr), texture (S), slope (E), and water availability (W). Based on potential improvement measures, there is a possibility of upgrading land suitability classes from S2 and S3 to S1 (highly suitable) in several locations, particularly through soil fertility management involving the application of organic and inorganic fertilizers, as well as drainage improvements. This study provides recommendations for the sustainable development of lowland rice cultivation in Labuhanbatu Regency

Abstract
The purpose of this study was to analyze the physical properties of rice field soil affected by seawater intrusion in Ulujami Subdistrict, Pemalang Regency. Soil samples were collected from Limbangan Village and analyzed in the laboratory in November 2024, focusing on electrical conductivity (EC), salinity, and soil pH. The EC values obtained were 7.8 mmhos/cm at the center, 7.4 mmhos/cm on the right, and 7.1 mmhos/cm on the left, exceeding the plant tolerance threshold of 4 mmhos/cm. These results indicate high dissolved salt content, classifying the area as saline soil with salinity class 2, where only salt-tolerant plants can grow and productivity may decline. The soil also showed alkaline characteristics, with pH values of 8.7 (center), 8.5 (right), and 8.2 (left). The elevated pH is associated with sodium chloride ions displacing essential soil cations such as calcium and magnesium, thereby reducing microbial water absorption and potentially disrupting nutrient balance. Overall, the EC, salinity, and pH measurements indicate that the soil fertility in this area is low, making the land less suitable for optimal rice cultivation.
Chemical and Biological Mobilization of Soil Phosphorus by ppk-Expressing Transgenic Rice (Oryza sativa L.)CORE3

Abstract
Phosphorus (P) availability in alkaline soils is constrained by the immobilization of legacy P, particularly through calcium phosphate precipitation. We used a confined soil–plant microcosm to examine how expression of the polyphosphate kinase gene (ppk) is associated with legacy P utilization in rice (Oryza sativa L.). We compared ppk-expressing transgenic rice (ETR) carrying single-copy (ETRS) and double-copy (ETRD) insertions with their isogenic wild type (WT), without P fertilization. Both transgenic lines had higher root organic acid release and acid phosphatase activity on day 30 and greater plant P acquisition by day 60 than WT; soil pH declined to a greater extent. On day 30, transgenic roots released more oxalate and malate, and their exudates mobilized more soil P than WT exudates in a separate incubation assay. These traits corresponded to greater depletion of the operationally defined HCl-P and NaOH-P pools. Bacterial profiles and PICRUSt2 predictions were descriptive because one pooled library per genotype precluded tests of genotype effects. Diffusive gradients in thin films (DGT) measurements revealed greater rhizosphere labile P in the ETRD early in plant growth, followed by a decline as plant P concentrations increased. This pattern was consistent with an early increase in P supply followed by plant-driven depletion. Net P acquisition in ETRD was 2.55 times that in WT, and ETRD exhibited 53% greater root biomass, while ETRS showed intermediate responses. Collectively, these results link enhanced plant P acquisition to greater P release in exudate incubation assays and higher acid phosphatase activity within this confined system.
Production activity and resulting pollution, not depth, driven microbial community structure, function, and assembly mechanisms in an aged petroleum hydrocarbon contaminated soil.CORE3

Venue: Journal of environmental management
Published: 2026-10-10

Abstract
The degradation activity of soil microorganisms remains a crucial pathway for the reduction of petroleum hydrocarbons (PHs) pollutants in industrial site soils. Stimulating the metabolic activity of indigenous soil microorganisms to achieve pollutant removal is a promising remediation strategy in remediation practice. A vertical and horizontal investigation covering four production areas, four pollution levels, and five sampling depths at a PHs contaminated site from a long-term coking plant was launched to study soil PHs and bacterial community structure, function potential, and assembly. The results showed that both surface and subsurface soils (up to 6 m deep) at the contaminated site exhibit PHs contamination, with the most severe levels found between 4.5 and 5 m below the surface. The total petroleum hydrocarbon (TPH) concentrations in the soil ranged from 20 to 34,000 mg/kg. Potential PHs degrading bacteria, specifically the phylum Proteobacteria and the genera Pseudomonas and Sphingomonas, demonstrated positive correlations with TPH levels. Non-metric multidimensional scaling (NMDS) results showed that microbial community structure significantly differed among different production area and TPH pollution level. Redundancy analysis (RDA) results indicated that total nitrogen, moisture content, and available potassium in the soil exert the most substantial influence on the microbial community, while TPH, pH, soil organic carbon (SOC), sulfate content, and available phosphorus have comparatively minor effects. Analysis of community assembly mechanisms revealed that, with the exception of soils at the crude benzene, cooling tower (LQT), and L3 pollution levels-which were predominantly shaped by stochastic processes-most soil microbial communities were primarily influenced by deterministic factors such as environmental conditions and biotic interactions. Thus, production activity and the resulting pollution overwhelmed soil depth in shaping the structure, function potential, and assembly mechanisms of the microbial community in the aged, PHs polluted soil of the coking plant site. The relevant research results can provide theoretical support for the decision-making of PHs contaminated site remediation technologies and the regulation of remediation projects.
Mechanistic Evaluation of Polyvinyl Alcohol (PVA) Coarse-Grained Soil Stabilization and Ecological Restoration on Slopes of Sejila Mountain Section of the G318 HighwayCORE3
Authors: Yu-Yun Zhang, Qi Wang, Ao-Hui Guo, En-Long Liu, Yue-Feng Li
Venue: Applied Sciences
Published: 2026-10-10
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Focusing on coarse-grained soil slopes along the Sejila Mountain section of the G318 highway in China, this study adopts the environmentally friendly polymer polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) as an improvement material. Through a combination of laboratory tests, field tests, and microscopic analyses, the improvement patterns, reinforcement mechanisms, and ecological adaptability of PVA-modified coarse-grained soils are systematically investigated. A natural soil control group and five PVA mix-ratio groups (4%, 6%, 8%, 10%, and 12% by weight) were designed. The results show that PVA can significantly increase soil cohesion and internal friction angle, optimize the stress–strain relationship, and inhibit dilatancy, with 8% determined as the optimal content. PVA forms a continuous reticulate cementation structure among soil particles, effectively filling pores, strengthening interparticle bonding, and enhancing the integrity and stability of the soil. However, excessive PVA may wrap soil particles and weaken frictional interlocking, resulting in strength reduction. Indoor and field ecological planting tests (using highland barley and alfalfa) indicate that an appropriate amount of PVA can increase seed germination rate and plant height, while soil pH and nutrient contents remain stable, showing no ecological inhibition. Thus, the modified soil meets the requirements of slope ecological restoration.

Abstract
The fungal communities within biological soil crusts (BSCs) of alpine sandy lands remain poorly understood, particularly regarding their associations with long-term plantation restoration. Four types of sand-fixing plantations (Salix psammophila, Salix cheilophila, Caragana korshinskii, and Populus simonii) in the Gonghe Basin were chosen as study sites, and bare sand, algae crust, and moss crust samples were obtained from each plot. Soil physicochemical properties and extracellular enzyme activities were measured, and the fungal community was characterized by Illumina high-throughput sequencing of the ITS1 region. We systematically examined how key predicted guilds of fungal communities and their environmental factors changed across crust types, and characterized the differentiation of predicted guilds. Our main results are summarized below: (1) Ascomycota and Basidiomycota dominated at the phylum level, while Preussia, Alternaria, Paraphoma, and Endocarpon dominated at the genus level. (2) Moss crust had the highest fungal richness and evenness, followed by algae crust, whereas bare sand had the lowest values. Furthermore, PCoA-ANOSIM further revealed marked differences in BSC fungal community structure among the plantations. (3) Spearman’s correlation analysis, CCA, and the Mantel test indicated that the plantations were associated with fungal community differentiation through variation in edaphic physicochemical properties and extracellular enzymatic activity. Furthermore, pH, AK, and AP were associated with fungal community composition in both crust types, with ALP and SUC additionally important in algae and moss crusts, respectively. (4) FUNGuild predictions indicated that Undefined Saprotroph was the dominant predicted guild and that saprotrophy was the dominant trophic mode in the BSC fungal communities of the study area. This study revealed the fungal community composition and organization of BSCs across various crust types, and identified the factors associated with these communities in different artificial sand-fixing plantations in alpine sandy land. These findings may offer a microbiological foundation for restoring alpine sandy land ecosystems in the future.
Seasonal and Interannual Microbial Community Dynamics in Dacrydium pierrei and Vatica mangachapoi Tropical RainforestCORE3

Abstract
Elucidating plant-microbiome temporal dynamics is essential to forecast plant vulnerability under climate change, particularly for endangered tropical trees. Therefore, we investigated the assembly mechanisms of phylloplane and rhizoplane microbial communities in two endangered tropical rainforest tree species in Hainan—Dacrydium pierrei and Vatica mangachapoi—across seasonal and interannual scales. We sampled the phylloplane and rhizoplane microbiomes of these two tree species across seasons and years and combined high-throughput sequencing with physical and chemical measurements to analyze their community assembly mechanisms. The main findings are as follows: (1) Both phylloplane and rhizoplane bacterial communities exhibited significantly stronger interannual variation, whereas phylloplane fungal communities showed significant seasonal variation but no significant interannual variation. Most dominant taxa exhibiting significant changes were mainly driven by nutrient factors. (2) Bacterial communities and phylloplane fungal communities exhibited significant interannual variation in α-diversity, whereas rhizoplane fungal communities showed significant seasonal variation, with temperature serving as an important driver of these diversity changes. β-diversity analyses confirmed that interannual variation was the dominant force restructuring phylloplane and rhizoplane bacterial communities, and community structure changes were mainly influenced by pH, temperature, and water content (WC). (3) Network analysis showed that both phylloplane and rhizoplane microbial community complexity exhibited stronger interannual than seasonal variation. Phylloplane bacterial complexity was influenced by temperature and leaf water content (WC) and fungal complexity by leaf pH, soil pH, and soil total potassium (TK), while rhizoplane fungal complexity was jointly affected by leaf pH, leaf total nitrogen (TN), leaf total potassium (TK), soil water content (WC), and soil available potassium (AK). Overall, our results clarify how environmental factors differentially govern microbiome assembly at distinct temporal scales, laying the mechanistic groundwork for predicting rare tree species’ responses to current climate trends and informs the design of microbiome-assisted protection strategies.

Abstract
Layered double hydroxides (LDHs) are promising amendments for simultaneous cadmium (Cd) and arsenic (As) immobilization, but pristine materials often require relatively high application rates. Here, MgFe LDHs were functionalized with two bio-derived modifiers, cysteine and humic acid, using calcination–reconstruction or mechanochemical ball milling. The resulting materials were evaluated in separate aqueous Cd(II) and As(III) adsorption experiments and in soil incubation and pot experiments using Cd–As contaminated soil. In separate aqueous adsorption experiments, ball-milled humic acid–functionalized LDHs (M-H-LDHs) exhibited the strongest performance, with fitted maximum Cd(II) and As(III) adsorption capacities of 443 and 93.3 mg g−1, respectively, representing increases of 243% and 104% relative to pristine LDHs. When applied at 5 g kg−1 soil in soil incubation experiments, M-H-LDHs reduced extractable soil Cd and As by 67.3% and 50.4%, respectively. Pot experiments further demonstrated decreases in Cd and As concentrations in Chinese cabbage shoots by 34.5% and 13.1%, respectively. This enhanced immobilization may be associated with humic acid functional groups and mechanochemical changes to the material surface, as well as changes in soil pH and microbial community structure. These findings provide a promising strategy for efficient co-stabilization of Cd and As in agricultural soils.

Abstract
Effective remediation of hexavalent chromium [Cr(VI)] requires materials that perform well not only in aqueous systems but also in heterogeneous soils exposed to fluctuating water flow and environmental disturbance. Here, phosphoric acid–modified wood biochar (P-BC) was evaluated against pristine biochar (BC) through physicochemical characterization, pH-dependent aqueous retention tests, soil water-retention measurements, repeated-infiltration leaching experiments, and accelerated drying–rehumidification cycles. Across the tested pH range (pH 2.0–4.0), P-BC showed slightly higher mean apparent Cr(VI) retention than BC at each tested pH. Batch experiments showed higher measured apparent Cr(VI)-derived retention for P-BC under the tested conditions: at 25 °C and an initial Cr(VI) concentration of 106.06 mg L−1, the 360-min mean apparent retention values were 8.46 ± 0.03 mg g−1 for BC and 10.10 ± 0.10 mg g−1 for P-BC. At 55 °C, the model-derived Langmuir qmax values were 18.08 and 28.18 mg g−1 for BC and P-BC, respectively. P-BC showed higher late-stage water-retention values, while a 3.5% layered application reduced cumulative Cr(VI) leaching by 82.9% relative to the unamended control. Under the accelerated drying–rehumidification protocol, low mean water-extractable Cr(VI) endpoints were observed at the higher P-BC application rates, with the 2.5% P-BC treatment yielding the lowest mean cycle-12 concentration among the tested P-BC treatments. Taken together, these findings show that phosphoric acid modification was associated with modestly higher aqueous Cr(VI)-derived retention and lower measured Cr(VI) mobility under several dynamic soil conditions. The results further indicate that amendment rate and spatial configuration are important determinants of remediation performance, supporting further evaluation of P-BC as an engineered amendment for aqueous Cr(VI) treatment and soil mobility control.
Selective Nickel Leaching from Cu-Ni Sulfide Ores Using Deep Eutectic SolventsPROXY2
Authors: Biki T. Kalake, S. Ndlovu, M. Mapolelo
Venue: Minerals
Published: 2026-10-10
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Traditional hydrometallurgical lixiviants such as sulfuric and hydrochloric acid pose environmental and safety risks due to their corrosive and toxic nature. To advance greener extraction technologies, this study evaluated deep eutectic solvents (DESs) as potential sustainable alternatives for the selective extraction of nickel (Ni) from polymetallic sulfide ore concentrates. Three choline chloride (ChCl)-based DESs, ethaline, reline, and maline, were compared using a factorial experimental design approach. All of the DESs demonstrated selectivity for Ni, indicating their potential for targeted metal extraction with minimal co-dissolution of copper (Cu) and cobalt (Co). Ethaline achieved the highest Ni recovery of 56.3%, while maline achieved moderate Cu recovery (19.6%). The Co recoveries were low (~16%) across all solvents. Factor screening identified the pulp density as the dominant parameter influencing Ni recovery, with lower solid loading enhancing the leaching efficiency. Under optimized conditions (0.5% w/v, 600 rpm, 25 °C, 1 h), a peak Ni recovery of 59.1% was achieved, consistent with the response surface methodology (RSM) model prediction of 58.1%. The recovery ceilings below industrial benchmarks were attributed primarily to the locked nature of pentlandite (<30% liberation), which likely restricted the reactive surface area accessible to the DES lixiviant. The study highlights the potential of ChCl-based DESs as selective, energy-efficient, and environmentally benign lixiviants for nickel extraction.
