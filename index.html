Anchor3DGS: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting with Compact Anchor-Based RepresentationCORE11
Authors: Sheng Ye, Jiangke Lin, Fudong Wang, Yi Yuan, Zhen-Hui Dong...
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
Published: 2026-10-15
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods have enabled effective multi-view 3D reconstruction by predicting Gaussian primitives in a single forward pass. However, existing approaches predominantly adopt a pixel-aligned paradigm that predicts one Gaussian per input pixel, coupling the Gaussian count to the input resolution and number of views. This also leads to redundant primitives in textureless regions and insufficient coverage in geometrically complex areas. We present Anchor3DGS, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that replaces pixel-aligned prediction with a compact anchor-based representation. Guided by information entropy, our method places a set of 3D anchors in the scene, concentrating them in regions of higher visual complexity while maintaining broad spatial coverage. Each anchor performs occlusion-aware aggregation of multi-view image features, exchanges information with other anchors, and is decoded into a set of local Gaussians. A lightweight feature enhancement module further refines the coarse RGB output from Gaussian splatting and improves rendering fidelity. Extensive experiments on diverse benchmark datasets demonstrate that our Anchor3DGS achieves state-of-the-art novel view synthesis quality while only using an order of magnitude fewer Gaussian primitives.

Venue: Computational Visual Media

Abstract
Feed-forward geometric foundation models (GFMs) directly predict dense geometry and camera poses from batches of images. Emerging visual SLAM methods chain together consecutive GFM batches to form dense point cloud maps. A core challenge is how to address the ambiguous geometric scale of GFM predictions: independent predictions are locally consistent up-to-scale but not constrained by any direct physical measurements. Models trained on data with known scale improve the consistency of GFM reconstructions but again do not directly measure it. Consequently, trajectories and 3D reconstructions from GFM-enabled SLAM methods inherit this issue. Motivated by parallels with classical visual-inertial odometry, we introduce FoundationVIO which integrates inertial sensing with GFM predictions to recover metric scale reconstruction using a factor-graph based approach. We demonstrate odometry estimates competitive with classical state-of-the-art VIO methods and accurate dense reconstructions that outperform GFM-enabled SLAM methods. We also add a minimal GFM-based loop-closure mechanism to create a full Visual-Inertial SLAM system capable of creating globally accurate reconstructions and pose estimates. We evaluate our method on three datasets with well known sensor systems and demonstrate its compatibility on ubiquitous camera and IMU sensors. Abstract
Feed-forward transformer models have recently achieved strong performance in multi-view 3D reconstruction, but they still rely primarily on appearance-based attention and often struggle in dynamic scenes with weak geometric cues. In this work, we introduce a motion- and gravity-aware framework under the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm. During training, residual optical flow and gravity direction are provided as privileged signals to guide geometric representation learning; during inference, the model uses standard visual inputs only. We integrate these priors into transformer backbones with lightweight architectural changes, preserving feed-forward efficiency. Experiments on standard benchmarks show consistent gains in reconstruction accuracy and robustness, especially in scenes with dynamic objects, ambiguous structure, and challenging camera motion. Our analysis further shows that the optimal injection stage is backbone-dependent: middle-layer integration is generally preferable for VGGT, while late-stage integration is more effective for π3. These results suggest that LUPI offers a practical way to inject motion and physical priors into feed-forward 3D reconstruction without increasing test-time complexity.

Abstract
Distilling vision-language features into 3D Gaussian Splatting yields a language map that can be queried with arbitrary text. In deployment, however, a small set of user-specified targets must be reliably located, which current distilled maps often miss, while open-vocabulary queryability for other words must remain intact. We propose a training-free, target-aware refinement of an already-distilled language map. Guided by a text-promptable segmenter, the visual embedding inside each detected target region is blended with the target text embedding, a convex combination whose coefficient sets the amount of injected prior, while non-target regions are left untouched. Injecting too much overwrites appearance cues and general queryability, so we seek, for each target, the smallest coefficient that still lets it win, and we obtain every coefficient in closed form, leaving no hyperparameters to tune. We further identify a failure mode of injection in language-goal localization. Text embeddings share a common component, and injecting them makes the standard cosine read-out query-agnostic, which a centering step in the read-out removes. Across three benchmarks, the refinement consistently improves open-vocabulary segmentation, roughly doubling the base mIoU on Replica, and localizes language goals more reliably than the base map. It also transfers as a plug-in to off-the-shelf field-based and object-level language maps and supports a real-world demonstration in which a legged robot navigates to natural-language goals. Abstract
We introduce Edit4D, an editable text-to-4D scene generation framework that constructs 4D scenes from separate background and dynamic character representations. Recent advances in generative models have enabled text-driven dynamic scene synthesis, but editable 4D scene generation remains challenging due to the need to preserve spatial geometry and temporal dynamics. Existing approaches typically generate holistic scene videos, where the background and dynamic characters are coupled, making object-level editing and flexible camera control difficult. Edit4D addresses these limitations through a stage-specific generation pipeline. Given a user instruction, Edit4D first decomposes it into stage-specific prompts for background, dynamic character, and scene-character composition. Abstract
Sparse-view Cone-Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) reconstruction is essential for reducing radiation dose and scan effort, but remains underconstrained due to limited projection information. Recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)-based CBCT reconstruction methods rely on gradient-driven densification, which becomes unstable under sparse acquisition and often leads to over-smoothed structures. We propose AD-GS, an Anatomical Density-guided 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that formulates Gaussian densification as an attenuation-aware Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling process. By interpreting Gaussians as samples from an underlying anatomical attenuation distribution, AD-GS adaptively concentrates representation capacity in high-attenuation regions, enabling sharper structural boundaries under sparse projections. Abstract
Vision-language models still struggle to understand 3D spatial relations across views and update spatial judgments as the observer moves. A common approach fuses 3D reconstruction features into visual tokens, but aligning these features with pretrained semantic representations remains challenging under limited spatial supervision. We propose DK-VLM with Dual-Kernel Attention, which computes attention from both a visual token's input position and its geometric position in the scene. A semantic kernel retains the pretrained model's native attention and positional encoding, while a separate geometric kernel uses estimated 3D points and camera poses to directly modulate attention through point displacements and relative view transformations. Abstract
Accurate motion prediction is critical for safe autonomous driving, yet even strong forecasters leave residual errors in their multimodal proposals. Existing refiners compress complete proposals or update their segments sequentially, preventing the full proposed future from serving as explicit relational context for correction. We introduce FutureLift, a backbone-agnostic refiner that represents each multimodal proposal as a heterogeneous future graph, with spatially grounded temporal nodes connected to agent histories, map elements, alternative modes, and, for joint prediction, other agents future trajectories. Bidirectional temporal edges allow later evidence to revise earlier segments in a single pass, and the updated nodes predict residual coordinate and confidence corrections. Requiring only proposals, scores, and scene context, FutureLift provides a common refinement interface for structurally different forecasting backbones. On Argoverse2 and the Waymo Open Motion Dataset, it consistently improves frozen proposals and outperforms native and specialized refiners across marginal and joint forecasting. On the WOMD motion-prediction leaderboard, FutureLift ranks first in minADE (0.5049m) and minFDE (0.9942m) among the compared standard-input methods, without LiDAR or ensembling, while achieving a competitive 0.4874 Soft mAP.

Abstract
High-fidelity closed-loop simulation for autonomous driving increasingly relies on dense multi-view 3D reconstruction. While recent feed-forward geometric foundation models have revolutionized per-scene optimization, applying them to continuous, long-horizon driving sequences is computationally intractable due to GPU memory limits and the quadratic complexity of global attention. Existing scaling strategies are fundamentally limited, as causal streaming suffers from compounding scale drift and trajectory deviation, while rigid"chunk-then-align"heuristics fragment global context and produce trajectory discontinuities. To address these limitations, we introduce Drive3R, a block-wise streaming geometric foundation model. Its confidence-aware re-anchoring further mitigates long-range drift by detecting unreliable streaming states, re-initializing the KV cache, and stitching refreshed segments through SE(3) alignment. Authors: Vahid Yazdnian, Yasaman Ghasempour

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Knowing the layout of the physical world matters for many applications, yet the sensors we rely on for it (e.g., cameras and LiDAR) can fail in darkness, in adverse weather, or behind walls. Wireless signals do not face these limitations: every link between a base station and a user is shaped by the buildings it passes, reflects from, or is blocked by, leaving information about the surrounding scene embedded in the wireless channel measured at the endpoints. However, mapping channel measurements to the environment is a severely ill-posed problem: a measurement records only the structures that interact with the transmitted electromagnetic waves along its propagation paths, and even these observations can often be explained by multiple plausible scenes. We therefore formulate wireless scene reconstruction as posterior inference and develop a hierarchical framework with two complementary stages to approach this problem. A wireless foundation model, pretrained to respect propagation physics, transforms each channel measurement into a representation of the scene structure that shaped the observation. A diffusion model then jointly integrates representations from multiple measurements to model and sample the posterior over building occupancy, with consensus over samples producing the final reconstruction. We demonstrate that the framework can accurately recover building footprints from wireless channel measurements alone and show that its performance depends critically on both design components: physics-grounded representations expose scene-relevant information, while joint posterior inference coherently integrates complementary observations.

Abstract
Recent advances in 3D reconstruction enable the extraction of temporally consistent 3D motion from RGB observations; however, current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models still predominantly rely on 2D image-text priors. Therefore, they do not accurately represent the fundamental dynamics of the 3D world associated with physical actions. This over-reliance on static 2D visual cues limits the capacity of VLA models in achieving robust robot manipulation. To address these limitations, we introduce FlowVLA, a reconstruction-guided, pluggable 3D flow learning framework aimed at empowering VLA models with an inherent understanding of action-relevant dynamics. Instead of requiring explicit 3D sensor inputs or altering the inference-time architecture, our method is designed to utilize temporally aligned 3D flow only during training. This allows the model to learn the dynamic evolution of the physical scene while preserving the computational efficiency of conventional 2D VLA backbones. To bridge scene-level dynamics with robotic manipulation, we propose a modular 3D flow learning framework with dual-level flow supervision. Specifically, point-level flow guides the model to forecast the future displacements of task-critical 3D points, while TCP-level flow aggregates these motions to align with the robot's TCP trajectories. This dual-level flow supervision encourages the model to focus on semantically meaningful dynamic regions, effectively coupling scene-level motion with precise control signals. Extensive evaluations across simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that FlowVLA consistently improves manipulation performance, zero-shot robustness, and optimizes data efficiency. FlowVLA adds only lightweight auxiliary heads during training and discards them at inference. Therefore, FlowVLA introduces no additional 3D sensor inputs, architectural modifications, or inference-time computation.

Generalist visual-navigation policies tell a robot which direction to go but not how far. Model-Based Trailer-Sway Suppression for Autonomous Passenger VehiclesCORE3
Authors: Alec McFadyen, Pablo Covarrubias De La Torre, Connor McGovern

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Trailer sway is a persistent contributor to severe crashes among light passenger vehicles towing trailers, yet it remains largely unaddressed by the autonomous vehicle systems now entering this new era of automation. Improper cargo placement rearward of the trailer axle reduces, and can invert, the tongue load transmitted through the hitch. The resulting loss of vertical load at the tow vehicle's rear axle diminishes the lateral tire force available there and degrades yaw stability of the coupled system, admitting a divergent lateral oscillation of the trailer known as trailer sway. Under adverse combinations of speed and loading, this oscillation can progress to jackknifing, in which the articulation angle diverges rapidly and directional control is lost. This work develops a linear state-space model of the yaw-plane dynamics of a light vehicle-trailer combination, parameterized to expose tongue load as an explicit stability-determining quantity. From this model a linear quadratic regulator is synthesized to suppress articulation-angle divergence. Because consumer trailers carry no sensors, the two trailer states the controller needs, trailer yaw rate and articulation angle, are estimated by a Kalman filter from measurements taken only on the tow vehicle. The controller is then implemented and evaluated in a MuJoCo multibody simulation across a range of longitudinal speeds and tongue-load conditions, including the negative-tongue-load regime. Results show a significant decrease in trailer oscillation when controlling a given vehicle's steering with an LQR using tow-vehicle IMU and LiDAR data. These findings indicate that LQR controlled anti-sway systems are a viable control method for future autonomous vehicles.

Abstract
Set-based variational autoencoders (VecSet VAEs) represent 3D shapes as compact sets of latent tokens. Although each token originates from a query at a sampled surface location, whether these latents truly align with geometry remains unclear. We investigate this alignment and identify four issues: (i) attention-sink-like tokens that attract queries from across the shape; (ii) spatially periodic repetitions in decoder attention responses; (iii) mismatched normal information in encoder and decoder query features; and (iv) missing supervision of the intrinsic relation between signed distance fields and surface normals. These changes align latent retrieval and field prediction with geometry without enlarging the per-shape code.
