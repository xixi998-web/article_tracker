Suitability Evaluation of Selected Soils for Rice Cultivation in Riggi, Lau Local Government Area, Taraba State, NigeriaCORE7
Authors: A. Muhammad, Yaseer Khallah Ibrahim, James Mono Galadima
Venue: FUDMA Journal of Sciences
Published: 2026-10-13

Abstract
This study evaluated the suitability of selected soils for rice cultivation in Riggi, Lau Local Government Area, Taraba State, Nigeria. Two representative pedons were examined for their morphological, physical, and chemical properties to determine their potential for sustainable rice production. Results revealed significant variability between the pedons. Pedon 3 exhibited a near-neutral slightly acidic pH (6.00-6.20), moderate organic carbon, and very high cation exchange capacity (95.49-99.10 cmol/kg), but was severely constrained by critically low available phosphorus (1.18-3.41 mg/kg), dominance of exchangeable acidity (over 95% of ECEC), and high bulk density (1.87-1.97 g/cm³). Pedon 6 slightly showed alkaline pH (7.23-7.38), very low organic matter and total nitrogen, low available phosphorus (1.54-7.37 mg/kg), and severe compaction (bulk density up to 2.10 g/cm³). Electrical conductivity values were low across both pedons, indicating no salinity constraints. Based on the FAO framework, both pedons were classified as marginally suitable (S3) for rice cultivation due to multiple physical and chemical limitations. The study concludes that while the soils have potential for rice production, they require site-specific management interventions including phosphorus fertilization, organic matter incorporation, liming for Pedon 3, and amelioration of alkalinity for Pedon 6 to achieve sustainable productivity of the crop.

Abstract
Okra [Abelmoschus esculentus (L.) Moench] is an economically important vegetable crop, but conventional flood irrigation with blanket fertilizer application restricts nutrient-use efficiency to 40-60%. Such practices also accelerate nutrient leaching, soil degradation and environmental pollution. Fertigation with water-soluble fertilizers (WSF) can improve efficiency by matching nutrient supply to crop demand, yet region-specific dosage recommendations and their effects on soil nutrient dynamics remain poorly documented. This study evaluated fertigation levels for growth, earliness, yield and soil nutrient dynamics in okra cv. Arka Nikita. Field experiments were conducted during late kharif 2025 and summer 2026 at the University of Horticultural Sciences, Bagalkot, Karnataka, on moderately alkaline soil with low available nitrogen, high phosphorus and medium potassium, in a randomized complete block design with nine treatments and three replications. Treatments comprised conventional soil application at 100% of the recommended dose (RDF; 125:75:63 kg NPK/ha), WSF fertigation at 60, 80, 100 and 120% RDF, conventional-source fertigation treatments, farmers' practice and an absolute control. Soil pH, electrical conductivity (EC), organic carbon and available N, P2O5 and K2O were determined before planting and after harvest using standard methods, and changes from baseline were calculated. Growth, earliness and yield data were pooled across seasons and analysed statistically. Fertigation with WSF at 120% RDF (T5) produced the tallest plants (203.44 cm) and heaviest fruits (10.68 g). Fertigation at 100% RDF (T4) recorded the earliest 50% flowering (34.42 days), earliest fruit initiation (37.42 days) and most fruits per plant (43.55) with a 20% lower dose than T5. The absolute control showed the poorest performance across the measured parameters. Post-harvest soil N, P and K declined under T4 and T5 relative to pre-planting levels (N by 2.4–8.9%, P by 10.3–15.6%, K by 1.5–4.6%), reflecting crop uptake, while pH, EC and organic carbon were unaffected. Fertigation with WSF at 100% RDF is recommended for okra under semi-arid conditions, combining earliness, yield and resource efficiency without impairing soil fertility.

Abstract
This study aims to address this gap by using spatiotemporal mapping and monitoring of heavy-metal concentrations and assessing their corresponding pollution levels and ecological risks to evaluate the environmental quality of oil-degraded sites. This study investigates the presence of metals, their pollution levels, and the associated ecological risks at an oil-spill site in Eleme, Nigeria. Twenty-six soil samples were analysed, comprising twenty-two from the spill site and four from reference sites. The concentrations of cadmium (Cd), chromium (Cr), copper (Cu), iron (Fe), nickel (Ni), lead (Pb), and zinc (Zn) were determined during two sampling phases at a six-month interval. The geoaccumulation index (Igeo) showed values < 2 after the first sampling phase, indicating moderate contamination, and values < 1 after the second phase, signifying uncontaminated levels. The metal pollution index (MPI) decreased from 1.01 to 0.59, indicating a significant reduction in pollution associated with post-remediation activities such as metal immobilisation and leaching, as well as environmental processes such as rainfall, weathering, and groundwater movement. The contamination factor (CF) primarily indicated low contamination for most heavy metals across the samples, except Cd, which remained persistent, while the potential ecological risk index (PERI) revealed a shift from moderate to low ecological risk. The findings showed progressive contaminant reduction and improved environmental quality, with heavy-metal concentrations spatially concentrated in the southern parts of the site and significant second-phase reductions, particularly for Cr and Pb, which fell within permissible limits of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (USEPA). However, Cd, Zn, and Fe remained persistent in certain areas, reflecting ongoing attenuation dynamics that may affect heavy-metal behaviour and mobility and highlighting the need for targeted remedial efforts and public health protection.

Abstract
Permafrost wetlands are important components of the global carbon cycle, functioning as carbon stores while also releasing methane (CH4). Although biochar amendment has been proposed as a strategy to mitigate greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, its effectiveness in permafrost wetland soils under contrasting moisture regimes remains uncertain. We conducted a 100-day incubation experiment to examine the effects of pine-sawdust biochar produced at 300 or 800 °C and applied at five rates (0%, 1%, 3%, 5%, and 8%, w/w) on soil properties, enzyme activities, and CO2 and CH4 emissions under non-flooded and waterlogged conditions. Biochar addition altered soil pH, carbon and nitrogen pools, and enzyme activities, with responses varying between moisture regimes. Under non-flooded conditions, 300 °C biochar reduced soil pH and increased nitrate nitrogen (NO3−-N), whereas under waterlogged conditions, biochar treatments increased soil pH and were associated with higher ammonium nitrogen (NH4+-N) and lower NO3−-N concentrations. Biochar generally reduced CO2 emissions under non-flooded conditions and CH4 emissions under waterlogged conditions. The 8% amendment of 800 °C biochar under waterlogged conditions yielded the lowest reported global warming potential (GWP; 5.49 ± 0.15 g·kg−1). Enzyme responses also depended on moisture regime: dehydrogenase and urease activities generally declined under waterlogged conditions, whereas 300 °C biochar increased catalase activity. Pearson correlation and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analyses identified NH4+-N (29.3%) and catalase activity (17.4%) as the two leading predictors of GWP in the fitted model. These findings suggest that biochar amendment can reduce GHG emissions during laboratory incubation, although the magnitude and direction of the response depend on pyrolysis temperature, application rate, and moisture regime. Among the treatments tested, the 8% amendment of 800 °C biochar under waterlogged conditions produced the lowest GWP.

Abstract
The present study investigated the effects of cow manure compost and biochar on the dissipation and half-life of Othello and Atlantis herbicides in wheat cultivation. A greenhouse experiment was conducted as a completely randomized design (CRD) with five treatments and four replications at the Faculty of Agriculture, Lorestan University, during the 2019–2020 crop year. The treatments consisted of a control without any organic amendment (NA), cow manure compost at 25 and 50 g kg−1 soil (CMC1 and CMC2), and cow manure-derived biochar at 25 and 50 g kg−1 soil (CMB1 and CMB2), all tested under the application of Othello and Atlantis herbicides in wheat cultivation. Soil samples were collected at 3, 15, 30, 55, and 110 days after herbicide application. Herbicide residues were determined using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), while soil pH and plant growth parameters were also measured. The results showed that the addition of organic amendments significantly improved the growth and yield components of wheat compared to the control. The application of CMB2 produced the highest plant height at all growth stages. Soil pH gradually decreased over time, with the highest acidification observed in the CMB1 treatment. Herbicide residues decreased over time in all treatments, with biochar treatments showing the lowest residual levels compared to compost and control. The nutrient content (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, Fe, and Zn) of wheat tissues was significantly affected by the organic amendments, with their highest concentrations observed in the CMB2 treatment. Overall, the application of compost and biochar derived from cow manure reduced the persistence of herbicides in the soil and simultaneously improved nutrient uptake and wheat crop growth.

Abstract
The relationship between soil properties and weed distribution across two geological formations were evaluated on morphological, physical, and chemical properties, and their influence on weed distribution along two toposequences in different geological formations, Minna (Basement Complex) and Bida (sedimentary formation) in Niger State.. Six soil profile pits were excavated across the two toposequence three from each toposequence at upper, middle, and lower slope positions, and samples were collected from identified genetic horizons for laboratory analyses using standard procedures. Weed assessment was conducted using 1 m² quadrants, and species were identified using standard taxonomic guides. The results showed that soils across the two toposequences were predominantly loamy sand, with sand content ranging from 800–840 g kg⁻¹, indicating coarse texture. The soil structure varied from weak granular in surface horizons to moderate subangular blocky in subsoils. The soil pH ranged from slightly acidic to near neutral (6.00–6.91) in the two toposequence, while organic carbon and total nitrogen decreased with depth, indicating surface accumulation of organic matter. Exchangeable bases were moderate, suggesting a moderate fertility status. Lower slope positions exhibited relatively higher organic matter and nutrient accumulation due to erosion–deposition processes. Weed distribution varied markedly across slope positions, with drought-tolerant species dominating the upper slopes and moisture-loving species prevalent on the lower slopes, particularly under Basement Complex soils. The study demonstrates that topography and parent material jointly influence soil variability and weed distribution. These findings highlight the importance of site-specific soil and weed management strategies for improved and sustainable agricultural productivity.

Abstract
In order to explore immobilization characteristic and its possible mechanism of various feedstocks-based biochars, deriving from chicken manure (CMB), rice straw (RSB) and sewage sludge (SSB), the effect of three biochars on speciation transformation of Cd in soil was compared, and soil properties, microbial community diversity were also examined. The treatment of CMB and RSB reduced significantly the content of acid-extractable Cd by 53.62% and 41.18%, respectively, but this available fraction was increased by 23.44% after SSB treatment. Simultaneously, CMB and RSB treatment significantly increased the soil pH, CEC, TOC, AN, AP, TK, microbial biomass carbon, dehydrogenase, phosphatase, catalase and urease enzyme activity, while SSB treatment slightly increased AP and TK content, only decreased urease enzyme activity. Lower bacterial alpha diversity was observed at the initial sampling of day 0, whereas treatment differences in bacterial Shannon diversity were not significant at day 225, it suggested that their effects on microbial alpha diversity were dependent on sampling stage and microbial kingdom. Partial least squares modelling (PLS-PM) speculated that CMB and RSB treatment decreased available Cd through direct effect of biochar and indirect effect of soil, whereas SSB treatment elevated available metal by indirect effect. This increase might be due to the decrease of urease activity indirectly affected by SSB, thus decelerating Cd precipitation and enhancing available Cd. These results deepen our understanding on the degree of immobilization and underlying mechanisms for Cd in soil that is critical for evaluating the application of effective biochar treatments.

Abstract
Mineral nitrogen (N) losses from tropical soils are often inferred from texture. We evaluated how contrasting soil types and N application rates affect N losses via ammonium (NH4+) and nitrate (NO3−) leaching and nitrous oxide (N2O) emissions. One-meter columns packed with a sandy (28 g kg− 1 clay) or clayey (626 g kg− 1 clay) soil received ammonium sulfate at 0 (control), 600, 1200, or 1800 mg N column− 1 (0–240 kg N ha− 1) and 1200 mm simulated rainfall over 112 d. We recovered every leachate, monitored N2O flux and water-filled pore space, and sectioned profiles at termination. Cumulative NH4+-N leaching was greater from the sandy soil at all fertilized rates but showed no consistent rate response. Cumulative NO3−-N leaching increased linearly with N rate only in the sandy soil, yet was greater from the clayey soil at each fertilized rate. The sandy soil drained 6.7% more water, whereas flow-weighted NO3−-N concentration was greater in the clayey soil, showing that its larger NO3− export under fertilization was concentration-driven. Nitrogen-induced acidification was strongest near the surface in the clayey soil but extended through 10–70 cm in the sandy soil. N2O-N emission was approximately sixfold greater from the clayey soil (emission factor 0.17% versus 0.03%). NO3−-N dominated mineral-N leaching from both soils. Weak NH4+ retention favored its mobility in the sandy soil, whereas greater background N availability and wetter conditions in the clayey soil favored NO3− export and N2O production. These contrasts cannot be attributed to clay content alone.

Abstract
Over the past six decades, billions of cubic feet of gases associated with crude oil exploration have been flared. Unfortunately, gas flaring has continued despite successive legislative interventions, with limited implementation of effective remediation strategies in the Niger Delta region of Nigeria. Gas flaring involves the combustion of associated gases produced during oil extraction, which contain particulate matter (soot), carbon dioxide, sulfur oxides (SOx), nitrogen oxides (NOx), and volatile organic compounds. These components have been linked to significant detrimental effects on diverse ecosystems and public health in the Niger Delta region. This study provides an updated overview of the impacts of gas flaring on public health in both human and animal, the local and national economies, and the Niger Delta region environment matrices, including air, soil, and water. In addition, it examines the implementation and effectiveness the Petroleum Industry Act (PIA) [98] in relation to gas flaring. The novelty of the study lies in its integration of recent data with an analysis of the PIA’s enforcement gaps and its alignment with Nigeria’s commitments to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs). Current evidence indicates that gas flaring, particularly acidic oxides, contributes to acid rain, leading to loss of fertile farmland, biodiversity decline, reduced agricultural productivity, and loss of livelihoods. It is also associated with adverse health outcomes, including respiratory diseases, cancer, skin irritation, and mental health challenges. Economically, gas flaring costs both host communities and the Nigerian economy trillions of Naira annually. Given the extensive environmental, public health, and socioeconomic consequences of gas flaring, there is an urgent need to strengthen the enforcement of existing environmental laws, policies enshrined in the Petroleum Industry Act (PIA), to ensure the capture and utilization of associated gases. Effective implementation of the PIA would mitigate the adverse impacts of gas flaring on the Niger Delta region’s ecosystem and communities, while enhancing national economic growth. In addition, it would support Nigeria’s efforts to achieve its NDCs and advance the attainment of the SDGs 3, 7 13 and 15. Future research should prioritize long-term environmental, epidemiological, ecological, and multidisciplinary studies to evaluate gas flaring impacts, healthcare burden, ecosystem resilience, and SDG implementation.
Salt marsh porewater alkalinity varies with plant species identity and growth stagePROXY2
Authors: Nienke Mueller, Ella L. Logemann, G. Reithmaier
Venue: Frontiers in Marine Science
Published: 2026-10-13

Abstract
Salt marshes are increasingly recognized as important sources of total alkalinity (TA), contributing to coastal carbon sequestration. While hydrological drivers are well studied, plant-mediated effects on TA export remain poorly constrained. Here, we assess how plant species identity and diversity influence TA production in a marsh mesocosm experiment including eight species and two- and five-species mixtures. On average, porewater TA concentrations were nearly twice as high in vegetated sediments (~4200 µmol kg - ¹) compared to unvegetated controls (~2400 µmol kg - ¹). However, responses varied strongly among species, with monocultures ranging from values comparable to unvegetated sediments (e.g., Puccinellia maritima ) to >5000 µmol kg - ¹ ( Triglochin maritima ). TA increased with plant growth stage, belowground biomass, sediment water content, and more reducing soil conditions, indicating tight coupling between plant traits and sediment biogeochemistry. However, TA did not increase consistently with plant species richness, as mixtures generally converged toward intermediate values. Lower TA production was often associated with higher TA:DIC ratios, suggesting a greater relative buffering capacity despite lower absolute alkalinity generation. These findings suggest that species identity and development may be important factors influencing salt marsh alkalinity production, with implications for coastal carbonate dynamics and blue carbon budgets.

Abstract
This study assessed whether long-established monospecific stands of Eucalyptus camaldulensis (EC), Eucalyptus occidentalis (EO), Pinus halepensis (PH), and Cupressus sempervirens (CS), established approximately 70 years ago, were associated with distinct patterns of soil microbial community structure and biochemical functioningin a semi-arid Mediterranean gypsiferous soil. Specifically, we examined differences in soil organic matter pools, microbial community structure, and potential enzyme activities related to C, N, and P cycling, and explored their associations with soil resource status and leaf/needle chemical traits. Mineral topsoil, leaves or needles, and litter were sampled from monospecific stands established on the same Gypsic Haploxerept near San Cataldo, Sicily. Soil chemical and biochemical properties were assessed, including microbial biomass C and N, respiration, microbial community structure, and potential enzyme activities. In addition, the elemental composition and fiber fractions of leaves and litter were analyzed. Differences among stands were assessed by one-way ANOVA, while exploratory stepwise discriminant function analyses were used to integrate soil, microbial, enzymatic, and plant-trait variables. Long-established monospecific stands were associated with distinct soil microbial community structure and biochemical profile in this gypsiferous Mediterranean soil. Integrating enzyme activities, phospholipid fatty acid profiles, and plant-residue chemistry helped characterize these differences, which should be interpreted as associations rather than replicated causal species effects.

Abstract
Contamination of water and soil by heavy metal ions is a major global concern because these ions are toxic and persist in the environment for long periods. A new, effective fluorescent sensor based on a fluoroquinolone was developed for detecting zinc ions (Zn2+) with a turn-on signal. When Zn2+ ions are gradually added to the fluoroquinolone solution, the fluorescence intensity increases due to an internal charge transfer mechanism. We evaluated the detection of Zn2+ ions using the fluorescence probe in various solvents and across different pH conditions. The key results showed that the fluorescent probe demonstrated a strong preference for detecting Zn2+ ions in ethanol, maintaining accuracy despite the presence of other metal ions, and exhibited enhanced sensitivity. The developed sensor achieved a remarkably low detection limit of 1 nM for Zn2+ ions, outperforming earlier reported sensors. Fluorescence measurements exhibited a consistent linear correlation for Zn2+ concentrations between 1 nM and 15 µM. Furthermore, spectrophotometric data showed increased absorbance peaks upon addition of Zn2+ to the fluoroquinolone solution, confirming the creation of a fluoroquinolone–Zn2+ complex. The suggested probe was successfully applied to detect Zn2+ ions in wastewater and drinking water samples, achieving recovery rates ranging from 97.0% to 102.75%. More interestingly, binding to Zn2+ leads to significant changes in the fluorescence lifetime of the proposed probe, which can be used to visualize Zn2+ ions in A549 cells via Confocal fluorescence microscopy. The reported chemosensor with fluoroquinolone is an effective and economical system for real-time monitoring of Zn2+ ions.

Abstract
Climate change-associated environmental pollution has intensified heavy metal contamination, particularly cadmium (Cd), posing a serious threat to agricultural productivity and food safety. The present study investigated the potential role of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) in mitigating Cd-induced toxicity in squash plants cultivated under Cd-stressed soil conditions (100 µM Cd). For this purpose, four treatments were performed, i.e., Group I- Control; Group II- Cd (100 µM); Group III- AMF; Group IV- AMF + Cd (100 µM). Exposure to Cd markedly suppressed squash growth and physiological performance, as evidenced by reductions in root biomass (fresh: 18.18% and dry: 20.92%), shoot height (11.32%), root length (12.32%), chlorophyll contents (19.05%) and Carotenoids (19.32%) accompanied by excessive Cd accumulation in both roots and shoots. Enhanced accumulation of secondary metabolites, particularly total phenolics and flavonoids, was also observed in AMF-inoculated plants under Cd stress. Overall, AMF effectively mitigated Cd-induced phytotoxicity in squash plants through modulation of antioxidant defense mechanisms, osmolyte accumulation, secondary metabolite biosynthesis, and limitation of Cd translocation. These findings highlight the promising potential of AMF as an eco-friendly and sustainable strategy for improving plant tolerance and productivity under heavy metal-contaminated conditions.

Abstract
The problem of cadmium (Cd) pollution in soil has attracted much attention. Although Pennisetum glaucum has developed tolerance mechanisms to heavy metals, the accumulation of excess Cd through the food chain threatens human health. Silicon (Si) has a beneficial role in alleviating environmental stress. However, the underlying physiological and molecular mechanisms for alleviating Cd toxicity in P. glaucum are still largely unknown. Therefore, experiments were conducted to explore the effects of Si on the photosynthetic system, antioxidant defense system, and the expression of in P. glaucum under Cd stress.
