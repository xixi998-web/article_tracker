Soil quality index as a predictor of wetting-front dynamics under surface drip irrigationCORE7
Authors: O. Faloye, A. Oluwadare, O. Akintola, O. Olaleye, J. Adeyeye...
Venue: Frontiers in Soil Science
Published: 2026-11-16
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Efficient soil water movement is essential for improving crop water-use efficiency under drip irrigation, yet the role of Soil Quality Index (SQI) in predicting wetting-front under surface drip irrigation has not been comprehensively studied. This study investigated five land-use systems—bare fallowing for 2 years (BF2), bare fallowing for 3 years (BF3), monocropping (MOC), crop rotation (CR), and fallowing with vegetation (FV)—on soil quality and wetting-front development under surface drip irrigation. Soil hydro-physical and chemical properties at 0–30 cm depth were assessed, including bulk density (BD), water-holding capacity (WHC), pH, effective cation exchange capacity (ECEC), available phosphorus (P) and potassium (K), organic carbon (OC), total nitrogen (N), and base saturation (Bsat). Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to derive the Soil Quality Index (SQI), while wetting width and depth were measured after 15, 30, and 60 min of irrigation. Land-use management significantly (P < 0.05) affected soil properties and wetting-front dimensions. SQI ranged from 0.33 (bad) under BF3 to 0.99 (very good) under FV, while values of 0.43 (fair), 0.83 (very good), and 0.89 (very good) were obtained at BF2, MOC and CR, respectively. FV produced the greatest wetting width and depth, followed by CR, whereas BF3 recorded the lowest values. Strong positive relationships occurred between SQI and wetting dimensions, with correlation coefficients generally > 0.70 and reaching 0.984 between SQI and wetting depth at 60 min. The principal component regression model explained 92.75% of SQI variability. These findings demonstrate that vegetation-based fallowing and crop rotation enhance soil quality and water movement, supporting sustainable irrigation water management.
Multi-Indicator Coupled Response Mechanism of Soil Degradation Improvement in Greenhouses Through Tomato Straw Mulching Under Plastic FilmCORE6

Venue: Soil Systems

Abstract
In arid irrigation districts of Northwest China, long-term continuous cropping of greenhouse tomatoes under plastic mulch has led to soil degradation issues such as secondary salinization, structural deterioration, and depletion of organic matter. The in situ return of tomato straw to the field as a resource is a green approach to improving degraded soils. In this study, a two-season field experiment was conducted in solar greenhouses, consisting of four tomato straw coverage gradients under plastic mulch (0.42, 0.83, 1.25, and 1.67 kg/m2, designated as S1–S4) and a control treatment with plastic film alone (CK). This experiment aimed to systematically investigate the coupled regulatory mechanisms by which different straw application rates affect soil aggregate structure, pH and salinity, organic matter, and soil enzyme activity. The results showed that the introduction of tomato straw under plastic film promoted the disintegration of large-particle-size, mechanically stable aggregates, significantly increasing the proportion of water-stable aggregates in the 5–0.5 mm size class, the content of water-stable aggregates larger than 0.25 mm, and the mean weight diameter (MWD) of wet sieved samples. The ameliorative effects exhibited a consistent increasing trend across the two consecutive crop seasons, suggesting a potential cumulative effect that requires longer-term verification; the synergistic effect of straw and plastic mulch effectively leached soluble salts from the soil; the low-straw treatment (S1) showed a prominent short-term advantage in desalination, but all straw treatments slightly increased soil pH, suggesting a potential risk of alkalization that requires further investigation. Soil organic matter and the activities of urease, sucrase, cellulase, and catalase increased continuously with rising straw coverage rates, but the marginal effects of soil improvement tended to plateau at a coverage rate of 1.25 kg/m2. Correlation analysis confirmed that straw coverage rate was extremely significantly positively correlated with medium-sized water-stable aggregates, organic matter, and the activity of various soil enzymes. Based on a comprehensive analysis of soil physicochemical and biological indicators, the optimal straw coverage rate under plastic mulch for greenhouse tomatoes in the Ningxia Yellow River Irrigation District is 1.25–1.67 kg/m2. In summary, tomato straw mulching under plastic film synergistically restores degraded greenhouse soils through aggregate restructuring, salt leaching, organic carbon sequestration, and microbial activation. This study provides theoretical support and quantifiable technical parameters for the resource utilization of vegetable straw and the targeted improvement of continuous-cropping soils in arid irrigation areas of Northwest China.

Abstract
Watershed soils and vegetation may differ from those of adjoining uplands, making site-specific assessments important for environmental management. This study compared soil physicochemical properties and plant diversity in the Ediene watershed and adjoining upland in Abak, Akwa Ibom State, Nigeria. Sixteen composite soil samples were collected from the two landscape positions at depths of 0–15 and 15–30 cm. Laboratory analyses assessed particle-size distribution, pH, electrical conductivity, organic carbon, organic matter, total nitrogen, available phosphorus, exchangeable bases, exchangeable acidity and effective cation exchange capacity (ECEC). Plant taxa and individual abundances were recorded to assess richness and Shannon–Wiener diversity. Both locations were predominantly sandy. Sand content ranged from 90.83% to 93.79% in the watershed and from 93.89% to 96.00% in the upland. Soil pH ranged from 5.30 to 5.61, while electrical conductivity ranged from 0.06 to 0.07 dS m⁻¹. Available phosphorus ranged from 30.931 to 35.366 mg kg⁻¹ in the watershed and from 50.071 to 54.166 mg kg⁻¹ in the upland. Corresponding ECEC ranges were 2.730–2.821 and 3.470–3.511 cmolc kg⁻¹. Three plant taxa comprising 27 individuals were recorded in the watershed, compared with five taxa comprising 83 individuals in the upland. Recalculated Shannon indices were approximately 1.09 and 1.23, respectively. The watershed had comparatively lower organic carbon, available phosphorus, ECEC and observed plant richness. These differences are descriptive and do not establish causal relationships.

Abstract
Ligusticum jeholense is a traditional Chinese medicinal herb whose quality varies among production regions, yet the relationship between rhizosphere soil factors and bioactive component accumulation remains unclear. In this study, rhizosphere soil and root samples were collected from six major production regions in Northeast China. Soil physicochemical properties and enzyme activities were determined, rhizosphere bacterial and fungal communities were analyzed by high-throughput sequencing, and the contents of seven representative bioactive components (covering phenolic acids, phthalides, and coumarins) were quantified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The results revealed significant differences in all measured parameters among production regions. Benxi Manchu Autonomous County (DY) exhibited the highest contents of soil organic matter (SOM), ammonia nitrogen (AN), available phosphorus (AP), and available potassium (AK), while Huanren Manchu Autonomous County (HR) had the highest pH (6.15) and urease (URE) activity. Microbial diversity indices (ACE, Chao1, and Shannon) were highest in HR. Redundancy analysis indicated that pH, SOM, AN, and URE were key factors driving microbial community structure, with the fungal community (91.49% variance explained) appearing more responsive to environmental factors than the bacterial community (83.04%). Vicinamibacter was positively correlated with ligustilide and coniferyl ferulate, whereas Paraburkholderia was negatively correlated with both. Ligustilide content was positively correlated with SOM and AK, while ferulic acid was positively correlated with pH and URE. These findings reveal the soil–microbe–quality relationship in the rhizosphere of L. jeholense and provide a theoretical basis for quality-oriented cultivation.
Crop-Specific Microbial Responses and Soil Quality Improvement Under a Bio-Based SBT-Crop System in Saline Desert SoilsCORE4

Abstract
Saline desert soils limit microbial activity and crop establishment. This study evaluated microbial and soil-quality responses to a soil bioactivation technology (SBT)-crop system in Sandy Desert and Gobi Desert soils of northwestern China planted with maize, cotton, or alfalfa. Here, SBT refers to a soil amendment composed mainly of marine active chitosan, plant fermentation liquid, natural oils, cellulose, and lignin, applied to the topsoil at 3000 kg ha−1 before sowing. Soil properties, bacterial and fungal marker-gene abundances, microbial richness, and the activities of five soil enzymes—sucrase (SC), cellulase (CL), urease (UE), alkaline phosphatase (AKP), and alkaline protease (ALPT)—were assessed. A minimum data set (MDS) approach was used to calculate the soil quality index (SQI), a composite indicator integrating multiple soil properties to assess overall soil quality, and partial least squares path modeling (PLS-PM) was used to examine its statistical associations with soil physicochemical and biological indicators. Compared with the corresponding SBT-only controls, maize cultivation significantly increased maximum water-holding capacity from 175 g kg−1 to 213 g kg−1 in Sandy Desert soil (21.7%) and from 195 g kg−1 to 225 g kg−1 in Gobi Desert soil (15.4%). In Gobi Desert soil, total salt content under maize decreased from 12.31 g kg−1 to 5.56 g kg−1, a reduction of 54.8%. In Sandy Desert soil, bacterial and fungal marker-gene abundances under maize were 61.63 and 85.81 times those in the control, respectively, while bacterial abundance under alfalfa in Gobi Desert soil was 77.93 times that in its control. In Gobi Desert soil, Chao1 and observed species richness were significantly higher under maize and alfalfa than in the SBT-only control (p < 0.05). The soil quality index calculated using a linear scoring function and minimum data set (SQI-L-MDS) increased from 0.29 to 0.40 under maize in Sandy Desert soil (37.9%) and from 0.34 to 0.62 under maize in Gobi Desert soil (82.4%); the value under maize in Gobi Desert soil was significantly higher than that under the other treatments. PLS-PM identified nutrient availability as the strongest positive correlate of soil quality, whereas total salt content and pH showed negative path coefficients. These findings demonstrate that soil-quality and microbial responses to SBT-amended crop management depend on both soil type and crop species. The results provide quantitative evidence to guide crop selection and soil-improvement strategies for saline desert agricultural systems, while longer-term field validation is needed to establish the persistence of these benefits.
Co-Contamination of Heavy Metals and Microplastics in Facility Soils of a Karst Region, South China: Characteristics, Coupling Mechanisms, and Ecological RisksPROXY3

Abstract
Heavy metals (HMs) and microplastics (MPs) co-contamination in karst facility soils may pose a potential risk to groundwater, but their coupling and ecological risks remain unclear. This study investigated spatiotemporal HMs, acidification, and salinization in facility soils of Guangxi, China, and assessed HMs–MPs coupling and ecological risks. Only Pb, Hg, and Zn exceeded background levels. Mean pH was 5.34, water-soluble salt (WSS) was 0.99 g/kg, and 62.0% of sites were acidified. After 20 years, Cd and WSS increased 3.7- and 5.1-fold, respectively. 15-year soils reached pH 4.80. HMs were highest in the karst-dominated northwest, followed by the central, northeast, southeast, and southwest, which had the highest acidification and salinization. Mean MP abundance was 1947.0 n/kg, reaching 2584.4 n/kg after 20 years. Small MPs (<1 mm) dominated (72.05%) and increased with cultivation duration. Soil MPs primarily comprised polyethylene terephthalate and polyethylene (>59.69%). MP abundance correlated positively with HMs, confirming MPs as HM carriers; only MPs < 0.1 mm showed stronger transport capacity. Cadmium risk was highest in the northwest and increased with cultivation duration, consistent with MP risk. Intensive karst farming causes combined pollution of HMs, MPs, acidification, and salinization, synergistically amplifying groundwater contamination risk. These findings help predict soil pollutant leaching into water in karst agricultural areas.

Abstract
Pruning and grafting are important measures for renovating degraded Hippophae rhamnoides plantations, but their effects on soil microbial communities and ecological associations remain insufficiently understood. This study investigated changes in soil physicochemical properties, microbial activities, bacterial and fungal communities, and co-occurrence networks following pruning and grafting in a 25-year-old Hippophae rhamnoides plantation on the northeastern Qinghai–Tibet Plateau. Soil samples from pruning and grafting (PG) and untreated control (CK) treatments were collected, and soil properties, enzyme activities, microbial biomass, and microbial communities were analyzed using physicochemical measurements and metagenomic sequencing. The results showed that PG significantly increased soil pH by 5.4% and soil bulk density by 17.8%, while decreasing soil field capacity, organic carbon, total nitrogen, total phosphorus, available phosphorus, and dissolved organic nitrogen by 24.2%, 30.0%, 33.3%, 21.3%, 35.5%, and 44.0%, respectively. Microbial biomass nitrogen and alkaline phosphatase activity also decreased significantly. Bacterial and fungal communities exhibited distinct responses to PG. Bacterial Chao1 richness decreased, although no significant difference in overall bacterial beta diversity was detected between treatments. In contrast, fungal beta diversity changed significantly, indicating greater compositional shifts in the fungal community. Co-occurrence network analysis further revealed that the proportion of negative associations increased from 7.81% to 28.5% in bacterial networks and from 7.17% to 20.9% in fungal networks. Bacterial network modularity increased from 0.79 to 0.88, whereas fungal network modularity decreased from 0.89 to 0.86. These findings demonstrate that pruning and grafting alter soil environmental conditions and are associated with responses in bacterial and fungal community structures and co-occurrence patterns. This study provides insights into the soil microbial responses to plantation renovation and offers a scientific basis for the sustainable management of Hippophae rhamnoides plantations on the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau.

Abstract
Microfliers hold considerable application potential as compact, lightweight platforms with expandable functionality, yet their flight behavior is typically constrained by device size, mass, and limited onboard power. Here, we report a humidity-driven origami microflier that integrates a hygroscopic composite film with a bioinspired autorotating architecture, thereby enabling passive aerodynamic reconfiguration. The film combines rapid humidity-triggered actuation with mechanical robustness, allowing reversible reconfiguration of the wing pitch angle under ambient humidity gradients. At low humidity, the microflier maintains stable autorotating descent, with a terminal velocity of 0.69 m s-1 and a spinning rate of 17.05 rad s-1. Upon entering a humid region, the microflier initiates wing pitch angle reconfiguration within 0.44 s and transitions to a faster descent state, thereby shortening its airborne residence time. The origami architecture also supports modular payload integration, enabling the delivery of seeds and fertilizer as well as post-landing soil pH indication. This work demonstrates a power-free strategy for adaptive descent regulation and payload delivery, with potential applications in ecological restoration, agricultural seeding, environmental monitoring, and other environment-interactive scenarios.

Abstract
Water and nutrients are two important factors affecting the growth and quality of greenhouse vegetables. Precision irrigation and fertilisation not only improve the quality of produce but also save water and nutrients. An IoT-based fertigation system for greenhouse-grown capsicum (Volante F1 variety) was developed and evaluated in a climate-controlled greenhouse at the Centre for Protected Cultivation Technology, Indian Council of Agricultural Research–Indian Agricultural Research Institute, New Delhi, for 210 days after transplantation. Fertigation was carried out based on soil moisture, soil pH and soil electrical conductivity. The soil EC- and pH-based fertigation system was developed, calibrated and installed in the greenhouse capsicum field, with three capacitive soil moisture sensors at depths of 15, 30 and 45 cm. In this system, irrigation was applied from the lower threshold of 50% maximum allowable deficit to 100% field capacity (15% and 22% volumetric water content, respectively), and fertigation was conducted between the specified electrical conductivity thresholds. The pH of the soil was maintained between 5.5 and 7 for efficient uptake of nutrients. The sensors were connected to the microcontroller through RS-485 Modbus interfaces and linked to the IoT platform; data were stored in a MySQL database. The manual system produced 5226 kg of fruit per 1000 m², whereas the IoT-based system produced 5137.2 kg. The automated system saved 4.83% water and 5.43% nutrients compared with the manual fertigation system.

Abstract
Contamination of corn with fumonisin mycotoxins (FUM) caused by the fungus Fusarium verticillioides is a recurrent issue in Texas corn production that results in significant economic losses for corn producers. Both prediction and management of FUM outbreaks are challenging due to a lack of information regarding region-specific environmental and biological drivers of contamination. We analyzed 683 historical FUM contamination events for 12 years that were categorized as low (<5 mg kg −1 ; <5 ppm) or high (≥5 mg kg −1 ; ≥5 ppm) and evaluated geo-spatial, environmental and biological drivers of outbreaks using a machine learning approach. We applied Ratkowsky-based growth and toxin production models for five genetically diverse F. verticillioides strains and found strain-specific predicted growth and Fumonisin Risk Index (FRI) profiles. Using FRI (strain-specific) or weather-only feature sets, each combined with soil biogeochemistry properties, we developed Random Forest (RF) and Neural Network (NNET) models. Historical balanced accuracy averaged ~74% for RF and ~70% for NNET models. Feature importance revealed that RF models consistently ranked barometric pressure and latitude among top predictors, while NNET models emphasized soil moisture and FRI. Among the overlapping drivers between RF and NNET were FRIs at the beginning of February and mid-October. Critical influential weeks for soil moisture (May–August), barometric pressure (July–October), and FRI (August–November) overlapped with corn developmental stages susceptible to F. verticillioides infection and FUM accumulation in a region-dependent manner due to differences in planting dates across the state. Soil biogeochemical properties such as organic matter, electrical conductivity, and clay content were also influential in models’ predictions indicating region-specific relationships with historical outbreaks. This study highlights the combined roles of weather patterns, fungal strain diversity, soil properties, and crop phenology in shaping FUM outbreak risk across Texas and underscores the importance of integrating strain-specific biological responses and regionally relevant environmental factors into predictive modeling frameworks for improved mycotoxin risk assessment. In addition, results suggest that region specific management of soil physico-chemical properties could reduce FUM risk, offering insights into new mitigation strategies for on-farm prevention of mycotoxin contamination and the potential to develop a novel early-warning system as well as mitigation tools for FUM outbreaks in Texas.

Abstract
Soybean, a major protein and oil source, is severely constrained by saline-alkaline stress. Despite the role of sucrose in energy and signaling, its molecular mechanisms in stress tolerance remain unknown. Here, we characterize the function of soybean sucrose transporter 4 (GmSUT4) and molecular mechanisms controlling saline-alkaline stress in soybean. Function analysis showed that GmSUT4 overexpression (OE) enhances stress tolerance by improving sucrose allocation in roots, reducing electrolyte leakage, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation. X-ray computed tomography analysis further revealed that GmSUT4-OE lines exhibited increased root volume, root surface area, and reduced root-soil distance. Interestingly, accumulated sucrose was associated with increased transcription and protein levels of H+-ATPase, thereby enhancing root H+-ATPase activity under saline-alkaline stress. Noninvasive microtest analysis revealed that H+ efflux was significantly increased in GmSUT4-OE roots. Root acidification assays further demonstrated that GmSUT4-OE lines exhibited stronger acidification capacity. Moreover, we demonstrate that interaction with GmCyt b5-2 enhanced GmSUT4-mediated sucrose transport efficiency, H+ efflux, and improved saline-alkaline tolerance, whereas this synergistic effect was abolished in GmSUT4-KO lines. Together, this study demonstrates that the GmSUT4-GmCyt b5-2 module improves the H+-ATPase abundance and H+ efflux, thereby protecting roots from saline-alkaline injury, advancing our understanding of sugar signal-proton pump, and providing needed genetic resources for breeding saline-alkaline-tolerant soybean.

Abstract
In modern industry, flue gas desulfurization is widely used as a method for removing sulfur dioxide to prevent environmental problems such as acid rain, smog, and air pollution. The development of green, high-performance absorbents is a major challenge currently facing the field. In previous studies, deep eutectic solvents have been regarded as highly promising green solvents for sulfur dioxide absorption. Therefore, this study proposes a deep eutectic solvent system based on tetraethylammonium chloride (TEACl) and 4-methylimidazole (4-MI). Experimental results show that under conditions of 298.15 K and 1.0 bar, the TEACl+4-MI (1:2) system exhibits an absorption capacity as high as 20.5 mol/kg (1.31 g/g), demonstrating its exceptional SO2 absorption efficiency. Furthermore, the system displays excellent reversible absorption performance (over 10 cycles) and outstanding stability. Finally, electrostatic potential (ESP) sites on the molecular surface were calculated based on Gaussian 16W simulation results. Combined with 1H NMR and FT-IR spectroscopy, the binding sites between the solvent and SO2 were identified. Quantum chemical simulations revealed that π-hole interaction exists between the positively charged sulfur site in the SO2 molecule and the abundant negatively charged sites in the DES. This study employed various quantum chemical calculations to elucidate the absorption mechanism of SO2 by deep eutectic solvents at the molecular level, providing a novel theoretical basis for the design of efficient, low-cost, and renewable flue gas desulfurization absorbents.

Abstract
Heavy metals in soil pose significant environmental and sustainability challenges, requiring cost-effective and circular approaches for remediation. Although the term “heavy metal” lacks universally accepted scientific definition, it is used throughout this study in accordance with its widespread application in the environmental and phytoremediation literature, including for nickel (Ni) as a potentially toxic metal. This study evaluates the phytoremediation potential of Tagetes erecta on nickel polluted soils by assessing growth performance, biomass allocation, and nickel uptake under varying soil nickel concentrations (0–4000 mg/kg d.m.) through the comparison of two cultivation methods: direct germination and transplantation. Because all plants were grown under controlled climatic chamber conditions, the observed differences between cultivation methods may be related to transplantation-induced root disturbance and the timing of Ni exposure rather than climatic stress. Successful establishment under direct sowing suggests that Ni had only limited effects on Tagetes erecta germinations at low and moderate concentrations, whereas establishment failed at the highest tested concentration. The observations suggest that plant responses may be associated with both pollution level and developmental stage at exposure. Directly germinated plants showed more favourable growth measurements, maintaining higher biomass, more stable morphological traits, and enhanced nickel accumulation compared to transplanted individuals. In contrast, transplanted plants showed pronounced stress responses, including reduced vegetative growth and increased allocation to reproductive structures, possibly reflecting a survival strategy under metal stress. Plant survival was observed up to 2000 mg/kg, while severe toxicity and mortality occurred at 4000 mg/kg. Optimal phytoremediation performance was identified at intermediate pollution levels, where biomass production and metal uptake were balanced. The findings suggest a possible role of early-stage exposure and indicate that direct sowing warrants further evaluation as a strategy for in situ applications. The findings indicate that Tagetes erecta may have potential for the phytoremediation of moderately Ni-polluted managed urban soils, although its field performance and the treatment of harvested biomass require further investigation.
Response of Ascorbic Acid and Piriformospora Indica in Improving Arsenic Stress Tolerance in Tomato ( Solanum lycopersicum )ECO1

Venue: Journal of Qassim University for Science

Abstract
Heavy metal (HM) pollution, especially arsenic contamination, has become a growing concern for agriculture because of its direct impact on crop yield and food quality. Since arsenic does not break down easily, it tends to remain in soil and water for long periods, eventually entering the food chain and posing risks to both the environment and human health. Tomato ( Solanum lycopersicum ), which is widely cultivated and consumed, is quite sensitive to such stress conditions. When exposed to arsenic, plants often show reduced growth, poor germination, and disturbances in basic processes like photosynthesis and nutrient uptake. At the same time, the stress leads to the formation of harmful reactive oxygen species, which can damage cellular components. Plants naturally rely on internal defense systems to cope with such stress, and compounds like ascorbic acid are important in this process. As an antioxidant, it helps in maintaining cellular balance and protecting plant tissues from oxidative damage. Alongside this, beneficial root-associated fungi such as Piriformospora indica have gained attention for their ability to support plant growth under stressful conditions. This fungus is known to improve nutrient absorption and strengthen the plant’s tolerance against environmental challenges, including heavy metal stress. A key idea in this study is the possible combined (synergistic) effect of ascorbic acid and Piriformospora indica . While each one helps plants deal with stress on its own, using them together may give better results because they act in different but related ways—ascorbic acid supports antioxidant defense, while Piriformospora indica improves plant responses through its interaction with roots. Based on this, it is expected that arsenic stress will harm plant growth and normal functions. However, applying ascorbic acid and Piriformospora indica , especially together, may help plants cope better by improving growth, strengthening defense systems, and possibly lowering arsenic buildup. This could offer a simple and eco-friendly way to manage heavy metal stress in crops.

Abstract
The spatial evaluation of soil functions and soil-based ecosystem services (SES) is a key challenge and demand nowadays. SES can provide a scientific background to the decision and policy-making. We define them as benefits that humans and the environment derive from soils and their functions. In our studies we presented the first maps of SES on a national scale for Poland. The research was focused on evaluation of regulating SES, which plays a crucial role in the crop production and circulation of matter and processes in the entire environment. Regulating SES contribute to CO 2 fluxes and sequestration, nutrient cycling, water cycle regulation, control of erosion and mass movements, among others. In this research we develop the multi-indicators index, grouping four SES and 11 separate indicators. For this reason, we used geometric means for calculating regulating SES and their final integrated index. For the nutrients provision, and carbon sequestration we used digital soil mapping approach with quantile regression forest algorithm, while for the water regulation and erosion control, mechanistic and empirical models were applied. Our results showed that regulating SES in Polish agricultural areas is relatively low, indicating serious limitations for Polish agriculture. In particular, 84% agricultural soils were classified as having very low or low status of regulating SES especially due to natural limitations, whereas only 2% showed high status, mainly due to the limited capacity for erosion control and high water retention capacity. This is an important step in the agricultural studies indicating the main soil health problems and areas of improvement to increase the natural potential of soils. The presented approach, due to the applied statistical and point bonitation methods, can be applied to other SES evaluation, and also allows to implement different indicators that may be more important for different regions in the world.
