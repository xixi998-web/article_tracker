From Point Clouds to Engineering Models: Benchmarking Agentic 3D Reconstruction in the Built EnvironmentCORE5
Authors: Chang-Yu Du, S. Nousias, Syed Salman Ahmed, Kai Dietmair, Mohammad Alrabab'h...
Venue: Information Fusion
Published: 2026-11-20
Abs
Abstract
Coding agents can translate 3D measurements into CAD geometry, but reconstructing engineering models for the built environment demands semantic interpretation and consistent construction constraints beyond surface fitting. We formulate scene-level Scan-to-BIM (Building Information Modeling) as an agentic 3D coding task and introduce S2Bench, comprising public and proprietary scans of 128 real-world scenes across five domains, paired with expert-curated reference models spanning diverse spatial scales. A shared framework lets agents measure scans in Python, build class-labeled components in a live CAD/BIM kernel, and iteratively refine their code. We also propose parallel asynchronous collaboration, where a lead delegates versioned tasks, continues modeling while workers execute, and integrates revision-bound proposals. Evaluating five agents and a collaborative team across 1,028 reconstructions shows that scan-to-model residuals directly guide geometric repair, whereas inferring semantic classes and construction relations requires joint geometric, visual, and engineering reasoning that agents struggle to ground and verify. Consequently, the leading agent fits scan geometry closely ($96\%$ coverage within $5\,\text{cm}$) yet reaches only $63\%$ mIoU, and no agent passes all engineering checks on more than $38\%$ of scenes. Coverage and engineering conformance drop with scene scale, whereas running a smaller model as a team improves all three axes with greater coverage gains on larger scenes. Linear-Time Online Visual Geometry Transformer with Hybrid ArchitectureCORE3
Authors: Haoyu Kang, Pingzhi Li, Ruichen Zhang, Tianlong Chen
Published: 2026-11-25
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Transformer-based 3D reconstruction models such as VGGT and $π^3$ predict cameras, depth and point maps directly from many input views. Causal attention models such as StreamVGGT extend them to online video but cache all past tokens, so their total compute grows quadratically with the stream length. Recurrent models such as CUT3R and TTT3R keep a fixed-size state at constant cost, but they progressively forget early geometry. We present LinearVGGT, a hybrid model distilled from StreamVGGT, which uses Mamba2 layers for efficient sequence modeling in three of every four global layers. The remaining layers use register-guided sparse attention to retrieve a few relevant past frames, compensating for the limited exact long-range recall of Mamba2. Our distillation needs only 9\% of the GPU hours and 31\% of the training frames that StreamVGGT uses to learn from VGGT. LinearVGGT keeps an almost constant latency of 27 ms per frame over 1,024 frames and is on average $12 \times$ faster than StreamVGGT. It outperforms its teacher and is competitive with the state-of-the-art streaming model LingBot-Map, while running $2.5\times$ faster and using $2.7\times$ less memory.
Synchronizing Cameras via 3D Object RelationshipsCORE3
Authors: Sujong Shin, Sang-Il Choi, Kwang Moo Yi
Published: 2026-11-24
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we synchronize multiple videos of the same dynamic event through object relationships. Driven by dynamic reconstruction and novel view synthesis, many recent synchronization methods rely on pixel-level evidence, such as point correspondences across views or the photometric losses of a reconstructed scene. In dynamic scenes, however, fast motion and occlusions make the moving regions that carry much of the timing information hard to match across views. We thus propose to bring back the traditional wisdom that objects are reliable cues for global reasoning about cameras. Measured in 3D, the relationships between objects, such as the distance between two people, are stable across all views up to scale; they can serve as cues for alignment. To compare these relationships across cameras, we only need the identity of each object in every view, as the cues are relational. We obtain these identities by giving a vision-language model a few frames of every camera and prompting it to identify the objects in the scene. We then segment and track each identified object through every video with an open-vocabulary segmentor. To obtain the 3D locations of objects, we use a pretrained depth estimator. Where depth or segmentation fails, we complement these 3D cues with 2D motion from point tracks. Finally, we synchronize the cameras by finding the temporal offsets at which the cues of different cameras agree best, first for each camera pair and then for all cameras jointly with robust optimization. We evaluate our method on four standard benchmarks, outperforming the state of the art.

Tornado4D: Physics-Guided 4D Gaussian Reconstruction of a Laboratory Tornado from Sparse One-Sided VideosCORE3
Authors: Yucheng Zhu, Maria J. Molina, Christopher Metzler
Published: 2026-11-23
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Characterizing how tornadoes evolve over space and time is critically important for understanding and preparing for these highly destructive weather events. Towards this end, we develop algorithms capable of forming time-resolved 3D reconstructions of laboratory smoke tornadoes from sparse one-sided videos. Even in a controlled environment, reconstruction is severely underconstrained: appearance is view dependent, motion is non-rigid, and the unobserved volume admits many geometries. We present Tornado4D, a physics-guided 4D Gaussian framework capable of reconstructing laboratory tornadoes from sparse, one-sided videos. Tornado4D uses knowledge of the enclosure geometry to introduce rotational airflow field priors that constrain how the 3D structure evolves over time. These rotational priors allow us to reconstruct otherwise unobserved structure. We evaluate reconstruction accuracy by computing novel-view synthesis accuracy on a held out camera view: Tornado4D outperforms frame-by-frame 3DGS and video-based 4DGS by a considerable margin.
Enhancing Sparse-View Gaussian Splatting with Feed-Forward Priors via Spectral Fusion and DistillationCORE3
Authors: Weikai Lin, Jun Dai, Sheng Zhao, Junjie Zhao, Yuhao Zhu
Published: 2026-11-23
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Gaussian splatting from sparse training views renders high-quality images near training views, but reconstruction quality degrades at distant regions, which are undersampled by the training set. Feed-forward novel view synthesis (NVS) models use strong learned priors to resample the scene from substantially different viewpoints, but they take more than ten times as long to render and produce high-frequency artifacts on out-of-domain scenes. We propose a plug-and-play method that enhances an existing sparse-view Gaussian model with a pretrained feed-forward NVS model. We observe that the two are complementary in the frequency domain: the prior is more accurate at low frequencies, and the Gaussians at high frequencies. The reliability of the prior relative to the Gaussians is inversely proportional to the image frequency, so the optimal fusion weight is a first-order low-pass filter with one parameter that can be fitted. Our key idea is then to intelligently fuse the reliable part of each of the two methods. To remove the slow feed-forward model at render time, we use these fused images as teachers and distill them back into Gaussians, balanced with supervision from input views. Our method improves the quality of various sparse-view Gaussian methods without sacrificing rendering speed, and our analysis works on different prior models. FGSCalib: Targetless Camera-LiDAR Extrinsic Calibration from Foreground SegmentationCORE3
Authors: Marcell Kégl, Csaba Benedek
Published: 2026-11-23
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Camera-LiDAR pairs installed as fixed infrastructure need periodic recalibration, but a calibration target takes the installation out of service. We present FGSCalib, a targetless method that calibrates a stationary camera-LiDAR pair from the objects moving in its view. We only assume that arbitrary moving objects are present in the scene: each sensor separates the moving foreground from the static background, without relying on object classes. With the correct extrinsic, the LiDAR foreground points project onto the camera foreground. FGSCalib turns the camera mask into a boundary-proximity field and finds the extrinsic by two-stage simulated annealing over selected frame pairs. It needs no measured initial extrinsic (random starts give similar results) and works with both rotating and non-repetitive LiDARs. We evaluate it on 21 simulated scenarios with exact ground truth and on 72 real ones (nine camera-LiDAR pairs in eight scenes), with a reference averaged from 43 manual calibrations. In simulation the median error is 5.3 cm and 0.19°. On real data we use a tolerance of 18 cm and 1.8°, met by 93% of single manual calibrations. Keeping the lowest-cost of five runs, FGSCalib meets it in 51 of the 72 scenarios: in 20 of 24 with the Livox, where the strongest of five baselines reaches 11, and in 31 of 48 with the two rotating LiDARs, where only one baseline estimate does. Its median error is 9.5 cm and 0.9°, against 7.3 cm and 0.7° for a single manual calibration. The code and the dataset are available online.
One Latent World for Heterogeneous LiDARs: A Scan Pattern Agnostic 4D World Model via Direct Ray DecodingCORE3
Authors: Minwoo Jung, Dongjae Lee, DongGuw Lee, Yeong Sang Park, Maurice Fallon...
Published: 2026-11-23
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LiDAR world models generate and predict metric 3D observations in the form of LiDAR scans. They typically synthesize scans following the scan pattern of their training sensor or render them from an intermediate 3D surface or field. However, following one scan pattern limits transfer to LiDARs with other patterns, and rendering from intermediate geometry can cause incorrect depths and false returns. In this paper, we propose a scan pattern agnostic 4D LiDAR world model that decouples the scene from sensor observation, so one spatial latent can be observed by heterogeneous LiDARs at the same mounting position without regeneration. Our decoder predicts the hit probability and depth of each ray directly from the latent, combining global scene context with local features retrieved at a preliminary depth. Without an intermediate surface or field, it reduces incorrect depths and false returns, and as each ray is decoded independently from one latent, LiDARs that share a ray receive the same return and observe one consistent scene. Finally, a diffusion transformer generates scenes in this latent and predicts 4D sequences from observed latents warped by the given ego and object motion. On KITTI-360 and nuScenes, our model generates realistic scenes and reduces the registration error between predicted frames by about 70% relative to the best compared world model. Its decoder yields 19 to 28% lower depth error than the rendering used by other world models, and generated scans benefit several downstream tasks. Our code will be open-sourced. Ref2Light: From One Image to Interactive Glasses-Free 3D CharactersCORE3
Authors: Hanzhong Zhang, Haoyang Wang, Siyang Song
Published: 2026-11-22
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Animating character illustrations on light-field displays demands coherent binocular parallax, real-time pose articulation, and fine-grained expression control. However, existing 2D animation and 3D reconstruction pipelines cannot combine these with rapid character switching. We present Ref2Light, a feed-forward framework that maps a single A-pose illustration and continuous pose and expression controls to display-native light-field animation, without per-character 3D reconstruction, rigging, or test-time optimization. Our key insight is to decompose light-field synthesis into static identity, view-dependent parallax, and control-driven articulation. A shared encoder caches multi-scale identity features once per character for rapid switching. A conditional decoder then synthesizes ordered angular anchors from these features via backward texture transport and direct color synthesis. A global-local control scheme deforms the anchors with head-body controls and uses the updated correspondence fields to track facial regions across views for masked local facial refinement. Finally, calibrated differentiable subpixel interlacing converts the anchors into the native panel raster, enabling end-to-end native supervision without rendering all views at full resolution. On held-out VRoid identities, Ref2Light reduces head-body LPIPS by 20.1%-55.6% over 2D and 3D baselines and lowers non-target change MAE in mouth closure by 95.6%-96.5% versus monolithic decoding, while running at 34.0-40.1 FPS for 1440x2560 native rasters on a consumer GPU. Abstract
High-resolution images contain rich visual details that require fine geometric structures for faithful 3D reconstruction. However, generating extremely detailed 3D geometry from images requires substantial computational resources and training data, making high-resolution geometry generation challenging for existing state-of-the-art models. In this paper, we present SuperFine3D, an efficient framework for generating extremely detailed 3D geometry through global-local recursive refinement. SuperFine3D learns a shared refinement operator from paired coarse-to-fine representations and recursively reuses it to progressively synthesize finer geometry beyond the training resolutions, without requiring additional paired supervision at target resolutions. To make recursive refinement computationally efficient, we introduce spatial-cell attention, which restricts high-resolution interactions to local spatial neighborhoods while maintaining bounded communication across neighboring cells. We further propose spatially anchored cross-scale guidance, which leverages globally contextualized coarse geometry to guide local detail synthesis and preserve structural consistency. In addition, geometry-aware training objectives encourage the refinement process to preserve stable geometric structures while introducing fine-grained surface details. Experiments demonstrate that SuperFine3D generates geometry at a $4096^3$ decoder-grid resolution, corresponding to a sparse $256^3$ latent lattice, using a shared refinement operator trained only on $512^3$--$1024^3$ representations. Compared with a full-attention recursive baseline, SuperFine3D achieves a $9.4\times$ speedup in end-to-end inference and reduces peak GPU memory usage by $23.0\%$, while improving cross-scale structural consistency and producing cleaner surfaces with finer geometric details.

Abstract
Recovering globally consistent 3D human motion from monocular videos remains challenging under dynamic camera motion. Although SLAM and human mesh recovery (HMR) provide complementary camera and body estimates, directly combining them often leads to inconsistent orientations and accumulated trajectory drift. We present GravHMR, a training-free, plug-and-play framework that couples SLAM and HMR through a shared gravity-aligned reference. By allowing gravity to be estimated independently of the HMR backbone, GravHMR accommodates world-grounded HMR models with or without explicit gravity-view representations. Specifically, Yaw-Aware Orientation Optimization (YAOO) resolves heading inconsistencies by correcting body and velocity orientations about the shared gravity axis. Conditioned on these corrected orientations, Gravity-Aware Trajectory Optimization (GATO) anchors root trajectories in the metric SLAM world by jointly leveraging 2D reprojection, the metric scale implicit in the human body, and the body's own motion. On EMDB-2, GravHMR achieves state-of-the-art performance with 154.0 mm W-MPJPE100 using ViPE and 189.8 mm using masked DROID-SLAM, outperforming TRAM (222.4 mm) under the same masked DROID-SLAM camera estimates. It further reduces W-MPJPE100 by 44.3% over GVHMR (276.5 mm) and by 20.0% over GVHMR + HTD-Refine (192.4 mm) with the same HMR backbone.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Unsupervised spectral super-resolution aims to recover spectral information from RGB images without paired hyperspectral data. However, the limited spectral information in RGB observations makes learning an effective spatial-spectral representation challenging, requiring the incorporation of structured spectral modeling with sensor imaging. In this paper, we propose SpectralGS, a spectral Gaussian splatting framework comprising two components: Shared Spectral Gaussian Representation and Spectral Response Projection Rendering. The representation module constructs a continuous spatial-spectral representation via shared spectral and local spatial Gaussian primitives, exploiting the low-rank spectral structure of hyperspectral images. The rendering module utilizes the spectral response function (SRF) to apply integral projection to the continuous representation, blending it to generate an RGB image. Consistency between the rendered and observed RGB images provides training supervision. Our main contribution is a Gaussian splatting framework that integrates structured continuous spatial-spectral representation with imaging response-based rendering, enabling unsupervised spectral reconstruction. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method outperforms current state-of-the-art methods.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cooperative perception with roadside LiDARs (RSLs) extends the sensing range of autonomous vehicles and resolves onboard-sensor occlusions, particularly at curves and intersections. Because infrastructure deployment is costly, existing work optimizes RSL configurations once, against road geometry and aggregated traffic statistics, and treats them as fixed thereafter. Traffic flow, however, is inherently dynamic, and a placement optimal on average can be far from optimal at any given moment. We introduce RSLaA (Roadside LiDARs as Agents), a framework in which each RSL adaptively adjusts its pose to the evolving traffic scene over decision-time windows. Since ground truth is unavailable at decision time, the framework centers on surrogate metrics computed solely from a probabilistic occupancy grid of historical observations, which predicts the next-window perception performance of candidate poses. We construct a co-simulation benchmark pairing five candidate surrogates with detector-agnostic ground-truth perception labels at every decision window, identifying the multi-view coverage metric J_mv as the most reliable predictor. Upon this validated surrogate we build two adaptive configuration methods, a receding-horizon (MPC) optimizer and a lightweight MAPPO-based multi-agent reinforcement learning scheme with centralized training and decentralized execution. Across 10 sites and 89 six-hour traffic scenarios (890 evaluation runs), adaptive configuration consistently improves the composite perception score over static deployment. Our results indicate that RSLs could be benefitted from being treated as agents.

Abstract
Spatial analysis of the tumour microenvironment with multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) typically relies on single thin tissue sections, which misrepresent the 3D tissue organisation and bias spatial cell statistics in ways that remain largely unquantified. We reconstruct 24 breast and lung tumour tissue microarray cores in 3D, each from 15-20 serial mIF sections, and compare spatial cell statistics in 3D with those from individual sections. Our pipeline combines affine pre-alignment with dense deformable refinement using RoMa v2, achieving a median target registration error of 1.79 $μ$m, well below nuclear size, which allows nuclei to be linked across sections. Tissue composition can thus be reliably estimated from individual sections, whereas cell-cell interactions require volumetric analysis.

Abstract
Streaming 3D reconstruction must preserve geometry under limited memory, yet STAC's fixed-resolution spatial key-value (KV) cache grows as scenes expand. We propose STAC++, a plug-and-play, training-free framework that compresses and reuses spatial memory for long-term reconstruction in unbounded scenes. Adaptive Spatial KV Cache (ASKC) organizes KV states into an octree with adaptive resolutions across regions and layers. Response-Aware Cache Compression (RACC) guides KV merging by measuring attention-output changes, balancing storage savings with geometric preservation. Integrated into four pretrained streaming backbones, STAC++ reduces spatial memory consumption while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality on long sequences and large scenes, with further quality gains from refinement at limited computational overhead. CAR3T: Generalizable Calibration-Guided Reference-View Attention Reuse for Multi-View 3D TransformersCORE3
Authors: Lan Wei, Haosong Peng, Sicheng Tian, Tianyu Qi, Wenchao Xu...
Published: 2026-11-21
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Feed-forward multi-view 3D reconstruction relies on global attention for cross-view aggregation, but the attention cost grows quadratically with the number of views, making inference increasingly expensive for larger inputs. Existing acceleration methods compress tokens or sparsify attention, potentially losing geometric detail or restricting cross-view aggregation while leaving redundancy among attention outputs underexploited. We observe similar cross-view attention distributions in certain layers, revealing an opportunity to reduce repeated global aggregation through output reuse. However, the layers suitable for reuse vary with the input, and reusing one layer alters the features received by later layers. Similarity at individual layers therefore cannot characterize the combined effect of multiple reuse decisions on output drift. To address these challenges, we propose CAR3T, a training-free framework combining reference-view attention output reuse with offline risk calibration and online dynamic layer selection. Calibration models cumulative reuse risk, while online selection adapts reuse to each input under a calibrated budget. CAR3T retains all input tokens and attends to the keys and values of all views, reducing the core attention computation in selected layers from quadratic to linear. Experiments on VGGT, DA3, and $π^3$ show forward speedups up to $1.41\times$ with at most 100 views and $1.75\times$ on longer sequences, with accuracy trade-offs across backbones and datasets.

Abstract
Online surface reconstruction from LiDAR scans requires observation models that respect the sensor's physical measurement geometry. Unlike unordered point sets, LiDAR returns carry anisotropic range noise along known rays, which also implicitly identify free space. We introduce the Occupancy Planar Patch (OPP), an incremental bounded planar patch representation that evaluates surface likelihoods and free space directly in range-space, allowing sensor noise, plane uncertainty, and ray clearance to be explicitly modelled during reconstruction. A single ray-to-patch association weight jointly updates plane geometry, refines surface boundaries, and blends camera imagery. Geometric reconstruction operates in real time, requiring 81 ms per frame on a representative sequence, with texture projection adding 134 ms.

Abstract
Accurate road surface reconstruction is essential for autonomous-driving mapping and simulation, but existing methods trade reconstruction quality for cross-scene inference. Optimization-based methods recover detailed surfaces through costly per-scene optimization, while feed-forward methods lack road-specific constraints and often produce large elevation errors. We propose \textbf{BReD}, a feed-forward framework for reconstructing road elevation, appearance, and semantics from calibrated six-camera sequences. Saddle Buckling of a Multicellular Alga Driven by Differential GrowthCORE3

Venue: bioRxiv
Published: 2026-11-20

Abstract
During development of many simple multicellular organisms there is a complex interplay between cell growth and generation of an extracellular matrix that confers structure. We study this problem quantitatively using Gonium pectorale, a 16-cell alga that transforms from a flat to a buckled shape during growth without cell division or rearrangement. Cell-level measurements and 3D reconstructions show that the 12 peripheral cells grow faster than the central 4, producing hoop stress that selects the saddle mode, whose amplitude displays a continuous bifurcation with classical square root singularity. These results are consistent with a continuum model of differential growth within Föppl–von Kármán theory, which yields information on the mechanical properties of the ECM. Implications of this transformation in evolutionary transitions to multicellularity are discussed.

Abstract
Intelligent cleaning of chemical reaction vessels is an inevitable trend in equipment operation and maintenance, and its implementation highly depends on high-precision perception of the residual amount and spatial distribution of pharmaceutical deposits on vessel inner walls. However, conventional inspection methods suffer from three major limitations: high labor intensity, low efficiency, and weak morphology reconstruction capability. Therefore, these methods are insufficient for supporting refined, demand-driven, and quantitative cleaning strategies. Although point cloud deep learning has been widely applied to 3D semantic segmentation, specialized networks for reactor inner-wall pharmaceutical deposit detection remain limited. Existing general-purpose models are poorly adapted to the small scale, weak boundaries, and irregular adhesion patterns of deposits, resulting in insufficient local detail representation and incomplete boundary delineation. To address these problems, this study proposes LAR-Net, a lightweight adaptive receptive field network for point cloud segmentation of pharmaceutical deposits on reactor inner walls.
