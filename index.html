3D Scene Completion via Fusion of Diffusion Model Priors and Point Clouds.CORE16
Authors: Yu-Hang Hong, Zhen-Feng Gu, Ze-Yu Gong, Yao Wang, Bo Tao
Venue: IEEE transactions on visualization and computer graphics
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Three-dimensional (3D) scene reconstruction, essential for applications such as virtual reality, digital twins, and robotics, has been significantly advanced by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). However, when visual information is missing or erroneous, it often results in incomplete scene reconstruction, featuring holes and artifacts in unseen regions. To address this, we aim to leverage point cloud priors covering unseen regions as reliable geometric cues. We propose a framework that combines diffusion priors with point cloud data for 3D scene completion under limited input conditions. In first stage, by leveraging the spatial information from point clouds combined with geometric optimization strategy, we effectively enhance the structural clarity of 2DGS scenes while significantly reducing rendering artifacts. Sparse depth maps projected from point clouds are used to train an image inpainting U-Net diffusion model, guiding the completion of unseen regions with geometrically consistent and stylistically accurate images. The proposed progressive completion strategy employs inpainted images to successively optimize the 2D Gaussian scenes. Experiments on multiple datasets show that our method reliably reconstructs unobserved regions with accurate scene structures and globally consistent styles under sparse input conditions. This approach extends the applicability of 3D reconstruction to real-world scenarios with limited visual data.
MatrixSplat: Matrix-Parallel and Feature-Scalable 3D Gaussian Splatting Rendering on GPUsCORE10

Venue: NeurIPS 2026
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a mainstream representation for real-time novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction. However, existing 3DGS rendering pipelines are poorly aligned with modern GPU architectures: depth-sorted alpha compositing introduces strong per-pixel sequential dependencies, limiting parallelism and resulting in scalar FMA-dominated execution that underutilizes high-throughput matrix units such as Tensor Cores. This mismatch becomes more severe for multimodal and semantically enriched 3DGS, where high-dimensional features must be accumulated from millions of Gaussians, leading to rapidly increasing computation and memory costs. We propose MatrixSplat, a matrix-parallel rendering framework for feature-scalable 3DGS on modern GPUs. We first identify the sequential dependency of alpha compositing as a key obstacle to efficient high-dimensional feature rendering, and adopt an order-independent weighted accumulation formulation that transforms feature rendering into matrix-oriented computation. Our framework features three key designs: (i) a tile-based splatting pipeline that maps feature accumulation to warp-level matrix-multiply-accumulate execution on Tensor Cores; (ii) a warp-granularity Gaussian packing strategy that mitigates visibility irregularity and improves feature reuse; and (iii) a lazy channel-batched feature-fetching pipeline that reduces shared-memory pressure, maintains high warp occupancy, and overlaps memory access with matrix execution for high-dimensional features. On Mip-NeRF 360, LERF, and 3D-OVS, MatrixSplat achieves up to 3.8$\times$ speedup over 3DGS and 2$\times$ over TC-GS while maintaining rendering quality and semantic accuracy, and further reduces render-kernel time by up to 36.7$\times$ for high-dimensional features. Integrated into existing fast renderers, it provides an additional 1.69-2.84$\times$ speedup.

Abstract
Thermal infrared imaging is vital for applications like search-and-rescue and autonomous navigation, yet trans- lating it into 3D scene representations remains a critical bottleneck. The low-texture nature and view-dependent emissivity of thermal images cause standard pose estima- tors to fail catastrophically, collapsing standard 3D Gaus- sian Splatting pipelines under sparse-view constraints. Fur- thermore, existing thermal reconstruction methods rely heavily on dense capture and computationally expensive per-scene optimization. Critically, many of these ap- proaches depend on aligned RGB-thermal datasets to ob- tain reliable camera poses and initial point clouds, ren- dering them unusable in purely thermal, unposed envi- ronments. To break this bottleneck, we present Thermal- Splat, the first generalizable, feed-forward Gaussian splat- ting framework that reconstructs 3D scenes directly from sparse thermal images without requiring known camera poses, RGB guidance, or per-scene training. We over- come thermal-specific challenges using a soft census loss for brightness-invariant structural matching and a bound- ary structure loss to explicitly supervise temperature discon- tinuities. Trained on an aggregated multi-source dataset, ThermalSplat synthesizes sharp reconstructions of unseen scenes in a single feed-forward pass. Extensive experi- ments demonstrate that ThermalSplat holds a strict advan- tage over scene-specific thermal splatting methods in the sparse regime. Furthermore, even compared against these baselines operating under their ideal dense capture set- tings, ThermalSplat achieves superior perceptual fidelity. By eliminating the need for computationally expensive per scene optimization, ThermalSplat offers an efficient and generalizable approach to thermal 3D reconstruction.
DepthGS: Depth-Guided Gaussian Splatting with Adaptive Regularization for High-Quality Surface Reconstruction.CORE9
Authors: Xiao Ren, Yu Liu, Ning An, Jian Cheng, Xin Qiao...
Venue: IEEE transactions on pattern analysis and machine intelligence
Published: 2026-11-06

Abstract
Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as an efficient representation for novel view synthesis, offering fast differentiable rendering and high visual fidelity, and has increasingly been extended to surface reconstruction. However, the relationship between depth information and reconstruction error remains insufficiently understood, limiting the principled design of geometric optimization strategies. To address this issue, we propose DepthGS, a depth-guided Gaussian splatting framework. We derive theoretical relations between depth discrepancies and reconstruction error under single-view and multi-view conditions. For single-view optimization, we propose a depth-difference-guided adaptive normal regularization strategy that modulates the normal constraint strength using a geometric reliability weight computed from local depth differences. For multi-view optimization, we introduce a cross-view depth consistency regularization strategy that enforces consistency between neighboring-view depth maps and the corresponding depth predictions transformed from the current view, thereby promoting multi-view geometric consistency. Extensive experiments on DTU, Mip-NeRF360, and Tanks and Temples demonstrate that DepthGS achieves competitive performance in both surface reconstruction and novel view synthesis.
OfficeTwin: Realizing 3D Reconstruction of Redundant Scenes through Partial Viewpoint PerceptionCORE8
Authors: Quan-Yun Wu, Kyle Gao, Daisy Li, Kshitij Goyal, Da-You Mao...
Venue: Science Robotics
Published: 2026-11-06
Abs
Abstract
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction turns an uncalibrated monocular stream into a dense point cloud in a single inference pass, orders of magnitude faster than classical structure-from-motion (e.g., COLMAP), and reliable on the weakly textured, repetitive surfaces where keypoint matching fails outright. However, raw point clouds lack the object identity, physical properties, and watertight volume required for robot learning. This gap widens under robot-native capture. A camera mounted on a robot constrained to a few degrees of freedom sees each object only partially, and the repetitive, weakly textured structure of an office makes distinct workstations nearly indistinguishable, leaving redundant and partial observations that downstream reconstruction cannot resolve. Existing image-to-3D generation methods struggle with these conditions, hallucinating geometry under incomplete views and confusing repeated instances. We present OfficeTwin, a robot-native framework that converts these incomplete observations into simulation-ready digital twins. OfficeTwin first imposes metric and structural constraints, then exploits the office's structural redundancy by registering repeated cubicles into a shared canonical frame, where complementary observations recover geometry occluded in any single view without hallucination. On a real office scan, OfficeTwin recovers every cubicle as an individually watertight, metric solid, outperforming feed-forward reconstruction and generative baselines that fail to produce simulation-ready assets.
IncR-3DSGG: Incremental Posed RGB Image Based 3D Scene Graph Generation via Privileged Depth SupervisionCORE5
Authors: Mrunmai Phatak, Daniel Kienzle, Roman Küble, Jörg Hähner, Rainer Lienhart
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Scene graphs provide a compact, structured representation of environments by capturing objects and their spatial or semantic relationships in a form suitable for embodied agents. Existing 3D scene graph generation methods often rely on depth sensing at inference, either to associate object instances across views via point cloud overlap or to compute inter-object relations from geometric cues, limiting deployment to platforms with depth sensors. We propose IncR-3DSGG, which builds 3D scene graphs incrementally from posed\footnote{RGB images with camera extrinsics estimated by an off-the-shelf visual SLAM system.} RGB images, eliminating the use of depth sensors at inference. Our method leverages a Parametric Relation Predictor (PRP) that learns to estimate pairwise distances and relative angles between objects from 2D visual features and 2D geometric cues, using 3D geometric supervision available only during training. These learned pairwise measurements are fused across views through Pose Graph Optimization (PGO)~\cite{grisetti2010tutorial} to recover metrically grounded 3D object positions and build a globally consistent 3D scene graph incrementally. On the real-world 3RScan~\cite{wald2019rio} dataset, our method attains $52.2$ mean object recall and $30.8$ mean predicate recall under the 3DSSG~\cite{wald2020learning} evaluation protocol, against $27.0$ and $17.6$ for MonoSSG~\cite{wu2023incremental}, while using no depth or reconstructed geometry at inference. This shows that relations and metrically grounded 3D object representations can be predicted from posed RGB images alone.
Adapting Monocular Depth Estimation in Novel Scenes through Disparity Refinement and Robot Ego-MotionCORE5
Authors: Dimitrios Chatziparaschis, Georgios Georgakis, Adnan I. Ansar, Shehryar Khattak, Konstantinos Karydis
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The geometric understanding of a scene is a crucial component for safe and efficient robotic operations in novel and unstructured environments. The recent emergence of foundation models for Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) has provided an opportunity to enrich robotic perception capabilities with dense long-range depth predictions, replacing expensive onboard hardware (e.g., LiDARs and stereo cameras). However, these offline models are primarily trained on affine-invariant depth representations, and their generalization to novel scenes remains unclear. In this work we present EM-ViT, a monocular depth estimation model that learns to adapt online relative depth inputs from a foundation model prior to the metric scale with observations collected during robotic operations. Motivated by the stereo camera parameterization, our method decomposes depth prediction via dense disparity refinement to recover the scene's relative structure and an effective baseline scalar prediction to determine the missing metric scale. The learning process leverages sparse LiDAR points and, optionally, the robot's Ego-Motion (EM) to rapidly train a lightweight Vision Transformer (ViT), while an internal gradient matching loss preserves the fine-grained semantic structure of the input disparity in real-time. We demonstrate the efficacy of our method on publicly available sequences, such as KITTI, and show that our online learning pipeline can adapt to novel scenes faster and with higher accuracy than existing MDE foundation models. The code and supplementary materials will be publicly available at https://emvit-mde.github.io.
RF3DGS: Reflection-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting with Self-PriorsCORE5
Authors: Qiang Wen, Guotao Meng, Junhong Ruan, Hongyu Liu, Chenyang Lei...
Published: 2026-11-10
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D reflection removal is crucial in applications demanding high-fidelity visualization, such as museum exhibitions. In this work, we propose Reflection-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting (RF3DGS), a novel method for superior reflection removal. RF3DGS exclusively leverages a widely-adopted photometric prior for reflection removal: that the minimum pixel intensity across multi-view images corresponding to the same 3D point strongly correlates with reflection-free information. However, generalizing this principle to a 3D Gaussian Splatting within a multi-view context is non-trivial. To address this, RF3DGS first optimizes a vanilla 3DGS model using a geometric prior to estimate a coarse, reflection-free depth map for each view. Leveraging these depth maps and photometric prior, a 3D warping process aggregates reflection-free information from auxiliary views to construct a clean transmission pseudo-label for a given reference view. This pseudo-label provides a robust self-supervised signal for optimization. We further introduce an Adaptive Twin Rendering (ATR) loss to synergistically utilize this pseudo-label while preserving high-frequency details from the original, reflection-degraded images. By adeptly fusing these internal 3D self-priors with the ATR loss, RF3DGS demonstrates state-of-the-art reflection removal performance across both synthetic and real-world scenarios. Furthermore, we establish the first 3D reflection removal benchmark, based on the Unity rendering engine, for rigorous quantitative evaluation.

Abstract
Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar offers a privacy-preserving and illumination-robust alternative for human mesh reconstruction (HMR). However, high-fidelity radar HMR remains challenging. Sparse point cloud-based methods suffer from information loss and missed detections, while raw radar tensor methods like RT-Mesh lack fine-grained semantics and explicit local body modeling. In this paper, we propose RadarDINO, a radar HMR framework that distills visual semantics from a DINOv3 teacher into 3D radar representations and introduces explicit geometric supervision. DINOv3 descriptors are transferred to radar voxels via calibrated RGB-radar geometry, providing voxel- and body-part-level supervision for part-aware radar representations. We further incorporate vertex- and joint-losses to directly constrain reconstructed geometry. On the M4Human dataset, our method outperforms the SOTA baseline across all three evaluation splits.

Venue: Sensors

Abstract
Vision-based 3D sensing provides an effective approach for constructing human-centric digital twins in applications such as ergonomic assessment and human–robot collaboration. However, reconstructing reliable dynamic human digital twins from visual sensor observations remains challenging due to large articulated deformation, which often causes surface discontinuities in existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)-based human avatars. Although current methods achieve high image-level rendering quality, their Gaussian primitives are not explicitly adapted to pose-induced surface deformation, resulting in cracks and holes during motion. In this work, we propose ElasticGS, a geometry-consistent human digital twin reconstruction framework for vision-based 3D sensing. ElasticGS introduces a training-free Pose-Aware Dynamic Covariance Adaptation strategy, which dynamically adjusts the rotation and anisotropic scale of Gaussian primitives according to local human surface deformation. Specifically, localized Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is performed on deformed SMPL-X mesh neighborhoods to estimate surface stretching directions and magnitudes, enabling Gaussian representations to maintain continuous surface coverage under complex poses. Furthermore, we propose the Avatar Surface Integrity Score (ASIS), a reference-free metric that evaluates structural completeness and surface compactness of reconstructed human avatars without requiring pixel-aligned ground truth. Experiments on the X-Humans, AvatarReX, and People-Snapshot datasets demonstrate that ElasticGS significantly improves surface integrity while maintaining competitive image-level sensing consistency. Compared with ExAvatar, ElasticGS maintains satisfactory rendering quality while improving ASIS from 0.741 to 0.961 on X-Humans, from 0.871 to 0.949 on AvatarReX, and from 0.874 to 0.952 on People-Snapshot. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of pose-aware covariance adaptation for robust human digital twin reconstruction in vision-based 3D sensing systems.

Abstract
Navigating off-road trails on a mountain bike requires diverse locomotion skills, including aerial maneuvers, cornering, and slip recovery, paired with sophisticated perception for scene understanding. Translating these capabilities to autonomous, two-wheeled robots remains a formidable challenge. We show that autonomous, high-speed trail navigation can be achieved through a single idea: encoding the trail as a semantic layer of the terrain. In simulation, this layer embeds the navigation task into the training environment; during deployment, it is reconstructed onboard from camera and LiDAR data. First, we introduce a unified learning framework that treats local navigation and locomotion as a joint optimization problem, bypassing the twist or pose command interface of classical control hierarchies. With fixed reward weights, the same framework produces both trail racing and agile parkour, shaped only through terrain semantics and episode length. Second, our perception pipeline combines a frozen vision foundation model with a lightweight dual-head decoder that predicts per-pixel terrain semantics and confidence scores, which are fused with LiDAR range measurements into an egocentric semantic elevation map at 30 Hz onboard an NVIDIA AGX Orin. We validate our framework on the Ultra Mobility Vehicle (UMV), a bicycle robot that autonomously raced across all sections of a kilometer-long, unstructured mountain bike trail at speeds exceeding 5 m/s and averaging 3.26 m/s. In simulation, our policy fails up to an order of magnitude less often than hierarchical baselines while driving faster.

Abstract
Predictive video encoders provide dense visual representations, but adapting them to 3D reconstruction requires accurate depth, camera poses, and cross-view geometry. We study supervised RGB-only multi-view reconstruction using an architecture that combines video-pretrained features, camera estimation, scene aggregation, epipolar transport, and residual pointmaps. Experiments compare global and spatial camera regression with joint relative objectives on CO3D, alongside an independently trained TUM RGB-D model. On 168 CO3D five-view windows, the spatial-camera configuration achieves 29.3% lower absolute trajectory error than the global-camera configuration, but performs worse than identity poses in trajectory error and worse than the global configuration in depth error. The joint relative-objective configuration improves normalized intrinsics error and similarity-aligned Chamfer distance from 0.6447 to 0.5573, but increases raw Chamfer distance from 3.5535 to 4.4374. On 163 indoor windows, camera error is 0.3063 m versus 0.3694 m for identity poses, while depth error is 0.4279 versus 0.2516 for constant median depth. Refinement worsens indoor camera error, and additional experiments show competing depth and pose effects. Overall, the results distinguish representation adaptation, aligned shape, and raw reconstruction, highlighting the need for isolated component and encoder comparisons before attributing reconstruction gains to predictive video pretraining.

Authors: Jingdong Zhang, Jiong-Hao Wang, Zhengming Yu, Yan-Cheng Cai, Wei Chow...
Venue: Science Robotics

Abstract
Frontier language models are increasingly deployed as agents, and how well an agent performs depends not only on its model but also on its harness: the prompts, tools, memory, and control loop through which the model acts. When an agent is applied to a new problem, a natural question is whether it can use the feedback of the task to improve the way it works, and not merely its answer. Most existing benchmarks keep the agent's harness fixed and score the solution it produces, or target large-scale model training. We introduce Flex-RSI, a benchmark for the recursive self-improvement of agents on vision and robotics tasks under a fixed budget. In each round, an executor improves the task solution, which is kept only if it scores higher on held-out validation data, and an improver then revises the harness, including the executor and itself. Every run starts from the same general-purpose harness, and the final solution is scored on hidden test data. Evaluating closed-source and open-weight models on tasks spanning perception, reasoning, robot action, 3D reconstruction, visual creation, and infrastructure, we find that self-improvement helps most when the agent builds its own solution, that feedback recording only success or failure holds back open-weight models, and that open-weight models fall furthest behind where outputs must pass strict checks of physical stability or exact correctness. Paired runs further show that editing the harness itself contributes to the gains.
Driving World Models Move the Camera, Not the SceneCORE3
Authors: Yuqiao Feng, Xiaoyu Ao
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Plan-conditioned driving world models are used to compare candidate plans, so their forecast should change with the plan as the real scene would, yet widely used control metrics check only whether the camera follows the command. Recorded drives already contain real outcomes of plans that were never driven: retiming a log gives pose-matched images and LiDAR for other speeds, and a stereo rig records a laterally shifted view. Against these references, released world models move their camera but barely move the scene: their scene response, the share of the real change in distance that the forecast reproduces, reaches at most about a third of real video's, and the trajectory score cannot tell an executed speed command from an ignored one. Interventions inside the generator trace the failure to the plan's channel. The forecast geometry follows the context frames; once they carry the planned geometry, plan tokens add little. ReTime-Compose therefore delivers the plan as geometry, without training. It transports the frames of one logged-speed rollout to the planned cameras, and the frozen model completes only what no frame covers. Frozen generators then follow a 0.54 m lateral shift nearly as the real scene does on KITTI, nuScenes and Argoverse 2, and respond more than re-sampling to slow-down plans in three generators and to speed-up plans in two.

The larynx is a complex anatomical structure central to human voice production, playing a critical role in clinical diagnosis, surgical planning, and rehabilitation. This study aims to develop an open-source, artificial intelligence-based three-dimensional (3D) laryngeal modeling pipeline using medical imaging data. Our approach integrates deep learning-based automatic segmentation (3D U-Net architecture), surface extraction (Marching Cubes), and multi-stage mesh optimization (Laplacian/Taubin smoothing, polygon decimation) within a unified framework, enabling high-accuracy modeling of the larynx's complex anatomy. This study provides a reproducible and accessible modeling pipeline in the field of computational anatomy.

Venue: Proceedings of the 35th ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management

Abstract
Generative recommendation has attracted widespread attention as a new paradigm in recommender systems. Its core idea is to discretize item semantic representations into semantic IDs and leverage the generative model's sequence modeling capability to predict the next item the user may prefer. Early approaches mainly focused on textual semantics, and recent multimodal generative recommendation methods incorporate additional modalities to provide richer semantics. However, existing methods typically handle modalities independently during both tokenization and generation, leading to insufficient modality fusion in semantic ID construction and inadequate cross-modal integration during recommendation. Moreover, the modality tokenizers are optimized with representation-level reconstruction or alignment objectives and lack recommendation-aware supervision, resulting in sub-optimal performance. To address these issues, we propose Accumulative Modality Tokenization for Multimodal Generative Recommendation, namely AMTGR. Specifically, we harness the Multimodal Large Language Model as the unified tokenizer to progressively accumulate and tokenize multimodal information, and design a collaborative-aware reconstruction task as the tokenizer's optimization objective to achieve recommendation-oriented multimodal semantic IDs, which will be jointly fed into the generative model to achieve effective cross-modal integration during recommendation. Experiments on public datasets demonstrate that AMTGR significantly outperforms baselines, and further analyses verify the effectiveness of high-quality multimodal semantic IDs and the unified modality integration.
