Long-term fertilizer-derived chloride inputs suppress nitrification and alter nitrogen supply patterns in a temperate agricultural brown soilCORE11
Authors: Xing-Tong Lv, Fang-Fang Cai, Yue Wang, Xing-Bin Liu, Jun-Mei Shi...
Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Chloride-containing fertilizers, especially those used as accompanying components in nitrogen fertilizers, are widely applied into the soil for agricultural production. However, the long-term impacts of this chloride input on nitrification and inorganic nitrogen supply remain poorly understood. Here, in order to determine whether long-term chloride-containing fertilization is associated with soil acidification, suppressed potential nitrification rate, altered ammonia-oxidizing microbial communities, and a shift in inorganic nitrogen supply patterns, we used a 35-year field experiment in Northeast China to compare chloride-free, low chloride, and high chloride fertilization regimes under equal total N, P, and K inputs. The results showed that High chloride treatments were associated with lower soil pH, although the direct effect of chloride on pH was not statistically significant in the path model, suggesting that ammonium-based fertilization itself is a major contributor to acidification in this system. Linear mixed models (LMM) further verified the significant effects of chloride addition and soil pH on PNR and the abundance of ammonia-oxidizing microorganisms. Our results suggest chloride may suppress soil nitrification via two hypothesized pathways: putative direct inhibitory effects of chloride, and an indirect pathway mediated by chloride-associated shifts in soil pH that may suppress ammonia-oxidizing microorganisms. Together, these two pathways facilitate the accumulation of ammonium-N relative to nitrate-N. High chloride treatments suppressed ammonia-oxidizing archaea (AOA) and ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB), and the abundance of AOA decreased by up to 72%. Amplicon sequencing of harvest-stage soil samples demonstrated that high chloride input altered the diversity and community composition of ammonia-oxidizing microorganisms, and reduced maize grain yield. Our findings reveal that combined application of potassium chloride and chloride-containing nitrogen fertilizers exacerbates soil acidification and substantially disturbs soil nitrification as well as ammonia-oxidizing microbial communities. Such disturbances reshape soil inorganic nitrogen supply patterns within this long-term brown-soil agroecosystem.

Abstract
The Teaching and Research Farm of the Faculty of Agriculture, Nnamdi Azikiwe University, Awka, is a source of crop trials, teaching and soil science research, but the horizon-level information of its soils is lacking. This study characterized and provisionally classified three pedons (P1, P2 and P3) selected across the farm’s soil–landscape units. The profiles were described according to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) guidelines and samples from each horizon were analysed for physical and chemical properties. The profile depths varied from 83 to 150 cm with the A–AB–Bt field-horizon sequences. The particle-size results indicated predominantly sandy-loam textures with the reported sand contents of 572–712 g kg⁻¹ and clay contents of 108–208 g kg⁻¹. However, the reported particle-size fractions for P1 Bt2 sum to 1,060 g kg⁻¹ and need to be verified. The bulk density varied from 1.19 to 1.76 Mg m⁻³, and pH in water from 5.44 to 6.10. Available phosphorus ranged from 0.96 to 4.27 mg kg⁻¹; the reported organic carbon and total nitrogen values and units need to be confirmed against the laboratory records. Effective cation exchange capacity ranged from 3.08 to 5.33 cmol(+) kg⁻¹, while ECEC-based base saturation was 65–84%, aluminum saturation 9.2–28.3%, and exchangeable sodium percentage below 4%. The low-chroma matrices and red mottles in the lower horizons of P1 and P3 suggest periodic redox conditions. Provisionally, P1 and P3 were considered possible Hapludults (Ultisols) and Acrisols in the World Reference Base (WRB), while P2 was considered an Inceptisol and Cambisol candidate. These placements are not definitive since the diagnostic-horizon criteria and classification-specific chemical data were not fully confirmed. The findings provide pedon-scale baseline information and identify priorities for verifying the soil data and assessing the nutrient, subsoil-strength and drainage constraints.
Mechanisms of Pb and As Enrichment by Native Vegetation in Mine Soils Under Controlled pH ConditionsCORE5
Authors: Zhen-Lang Yuan, Lin Xiao, Ge-Ge Jin, Nian Huang
Venue: Soil and Sediment Contamination: An International Journal
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs
Abstract
This study focused on two heavy metal-contaminated mine sites in Pingyuan County, Meizhou City, Guangdong Province. The four test soils had initial pH 5.6–6.0, Pb contents of 100.4–222.9 mg/kg, and As contents of 40.8–48.5 mg/kg. According to China’s Soil Environmental Quality—Risk Control Standard for Soil Contamination of Agricultural Land (Trial) (GB 15618-2018), for soil pH between 5.5 and 6.5, the risk screening values for Pb and As in “other” agricultural land (non-paddy) are 90 mg/kg and 40 mg/kg, respectively. Vegetation enrichment experiments were conducted using contaminated soil to delve into the enrichment mechanism of native vegetation for heavy metal pollutants, aiming to screen suitable remediation models for polluted mine sites. The results showed that pH regulation significantly affected the lead (Pb) and arsenic (As) enrichment capabilities and growth adaptability of three native plants: Kudzu (Pueraria lobata), Ramie (Boehmeria nivea), and Cattail (Typha orientalis). The optimal pH range was specific depending on the type of contamination. In single Pb-contaminated soil, Cattail exhibited the best comprehensive remediation efficiency in weakly acidic soil (pH 6.5), where the soil Pb content decreased from 222.9 to 204.0 mg/kg over 95 days (removal rate 8.24%), owing to its high biomass and translocation capacity. Under single As contamination, Cattail demonstrated both stability and a high As removal rate (6.7%) in acidic soil (pH 6.5), while Kudzu showed prominent enrichment capacity under alkaline conditions (pH 8.0). For Pb-As composite pollution, Kudzu in a neutral environment (pH 7.0) could efficiently co-enrich and translocate both heavy metals and significantly increase biomass. Considering plant tolerance and decontamination efficiency comprehensively, pH 6.0–7.0 is a universal and safe regulation range. Within the controlled conditions of this pot experiment, it is recommended to adopt a Kudzu-Cattail mixed planting model in composite pollution mining areas, controlling pH between 6.5–7.0, to synchronously remove Pb and As and reduce ecological risks; field validation is required before large-scale application.
The Impact of Irrigation on Soil Physico-chemical Properties in Toke Kutaye District, West Shoa, Oromia Regional State, EthiopiaCORE4
Authors: Belay Negera
Venue: American Journal of Ecology
Published: 2026-11-11

Abstract
Sustainable agricultural production and productivity depend on proper soil management and understanding how soils respond to agricultural practices over time. The objective of this study was to investigate the impact of small-scale irrigation on selected soil physico-chemical properties in comparison with cultivated and grazing land use systems in Toke Kutaye District. Three small scale irrigation schemes were selected from different directions of the district. A total of 60 composite soil samples were collected from irrigated, cultivated and grazing land use systems at two soil depths (0-15 and 15-30 cm) and analysed at the Debre Zeit National Soil Laboratory. The laboratory results were statistically analysed using SAS 9.4 software. The results indicated that soil pH (H 2 O), electrical conductivity (EC), soil organic carbon (SOC), available nitrogen (N), exchangeable potassium (K) and sodium (Na) showed no statistically significant differences among the land use systems across all irrigation schemes. However, variations were observed among land use systems, indicating the need for further investigation over longer cropping periods. In contrast, available phosphorus (P) and soil texture showed statistically significant differences among the land use systems and soil depths. However, small scale irrigation has not yet caused significant changes in most of the soil properties considered but continuous irrigation may influence soil quality over the longer cultivation periods. Therefore, long term monitoring over several cropping seasons is recommended to assess cumulative changes in soil properties, while alternative water sources and improved irrigation water management should be promoted to address irrigation water shortages and sustain crop production.

Abstract
Soil Cd contamination exhibits substantial spatial heterogeneity, making it challenging for conventional regional models to characterize Cd concentration patterns under complex environmental conditions. This study aimed to improve soil Cd concentration estimation by integrating explainable machine learning and spatial zoning to account for environmental heterogeneity. Using 188 soil samples and 36 environmental variables from a multi-source contamination area in the Chengdu Plain, southwestern China, we developed and compared global random forest (RF), SHAP-based feature-selected RF, spatial zoning-based local RF, and geographically weighted random forest (GWRF) models. SHAP analysis was used to identify important predictors, and variation-degree-based zoning was performed by integrating local gradients and spatial heterogeneity. The results showed that: (1) faults, stratigraphic lithology, population density, nitrate nitrogen (N2), and cation exchange capacity (CEC) were the important predictors of soil Cd concentrations in the study area; (2) In the global model, SHAP effectively reduced redundant variables, increasing the test-set R2 from 0.0374 to 0.2420; (3) In the local models, the effectiveness of the SHAP-identified important variables was limited within each local zone, whereas the all-variable model performed best, achieving an integrated test-set R2 of 0.3364; and (4) compared with the GWRF model, the local zoning-based random forest model demonstrated superior test-set performance and stability. This study demonstrates that a local zoning strategy integrating key factor variation degrees and spatial heterogeneity can effectively improve soil Cd estimation accuracy, providing technical support for soil heavy metal risk assessment and regional pollution prevention and control.

Abstract
Silicon (Si) depletion in intensively managed tropical soils may limit the performance of silicophilic crops such as sugarcane. This field study investigated granular and powdered Si-enriched compound fertilizers applied at a product rate of low (1000 kg·hm−2) and high (1250 kg·hm−2) doses, evaluating impacts on: (1) sugarcane agronomic traits and edaphic properties; (2) heavy metal accumulation in plant tissues and rhizosphere soil; and (3) rhizosphere bacterial communities via PacBio sequencing of full-length 16S rRNA genes. Our findings indicated that high-dose Si application (HP) significantly boosted sugarcane growth in the second cropping season (2022) compared with the control (CK), with stalk height increasing by 12.43%, stalk diameter by 8.93%, sucrose content by 21.41%, available stalk number by 21.33%, single stalk weight by 35.21%, and theoretical yield by 64.16%. High-dose powdered Si increased available nitrogen (33.89%), phosphorus (29.05%), potassium (43.42%), and soil Si (42.18%). Si amendments reduced toxic metals (Cd, Pb, Cr) by 56–75% in tissues and enriched putatively beneficial bacterial taxa (e.g., Aquabacterium, Candidatus Koribacter). Microbial community analysis revealed dose-dependent shifts in bacterial diversity, with the enrichment of putatively beneficial taxa, such as Candidatus Solibacter and Kitasatospora, in the high-dose treatment groups. A ternary plot analysis further revealed treatment-enriched genera distinct from the control. Moreover, redundancy analysis identified available potassium, soil pH, and Si as the key edaphic factors shaping rhizosphere bacterial community composition. This study offers a theoretical foundation for its application to sustainable farming towards food security. Further studies are required to explore the individual roles of these bacterial strains in the silicon-mediated plant growth.
Karst wetland reclamation increases the proportion of inorganic sulfur in total sulfur: associations with sulfate-reducing bacteria abundance and soil enzyme activityCORE3

Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-11-11

Abstract
Karst wetlands are fragile and critical ecosystems for soil sulfur biogeochemical cycling. In this study, six typical land-use types in the Huixian karst wetland of Guilin, southern China, were selected to investigate the variations in soil physicochemical properties, four inorganic sulfur fractions, soil enzyme activities, and sulfate-reducing bacteria (SRB) abundance across 0–40 cm soil profiles. The results showed that wetland reclamation significantly reduced soil pH, soil organic carbon (SOC), silt content, total nitrogen, and total sulfur, while increasing soil bulk density, electrical conductivity (EC), and redox potential (Eh). Reclamation reduced the content of total inorganic sulfur, while elevating its proportion in total sulfur; water-soluble sulfur dominated the inorganic sulfur pool. The composition of inorganic sulfur fractions differed markedly across land-use types. Reclamation increased SRB abundance but inhibited soil enzyme activities. Correlation analysis indicated that soil enzyme activities were negatively correlated with inorganic sulfur contents in abandoned land, whereas positive correlations were observed in other land-use types. Close internal correlations existed among different inorganic sulfur fractions. Soil pH and SOC were associated with greater inorganic sulfur accumulation, while Eh showed an inhibitory association. SOC, Eh, EC, pH, and silt content were the key environmental factors associated with SRB abundance and soil enzyme activities. Collectively, wetland reclamation reduced absolute concentrations of both total inorganic sulfur (TIS) and total organic sulfur (TOS), and preferential loss of TOS increased the TIS/TS ratio, altered sulfur transformation processes, and were accompanied by higher SRB abundance and lower soil enzyme activity. This study reveals the responses of soil sulfur biogeochemical processes to land-use conversion in subtropical karst wetlands and provides insights into sulfur cycle regulation under wetland disturbance.

Authors: J. Medo, R. Artimová, M. Gábor, M. Miluchová, J. Medová...
Venue: Horticulturae
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Compost application can improve soil properties while introducing complex microbial communities, yet the persistence of compost-associated microorganisms in soil and plant roots remains poorly understood. We compared industrial compost (IC) and long-matured compost (LMC) with contrasting production and maturation histories in a two-season field experiment with pepper followed by onion, using mineral fertilization at an equivalent nitrogen input as a reference. The composts differed markedly in physicochemical properties, biological activity, and prokaryotic community structure. Compost application caused transient changes in soil pH and dehydrogenase activity, while LMC produced more persistent increases in microbial biomass carbon and glucose-induced respiration. Soil prokaryotic communities were strongly affected by sampling time, treatment, and their interaction. SourceTracker estimated similar initial contributions of IC and LMC to soil communities approximately six weeks after application, but their subsequent trajectories differed: the IC-associated signature declined from about 12% to below 1%, whereas the LMC-associated signature remained detectable approximately one year later. Root-associated communities also responded to treatment, although direct compost contributions were limited. Crop yield was unaffected. These results show that compost effects in horticultural systems extend beyond nutrient supply and yield, affecting soil biological activity and microbial community trajectories over time.
Contrasting microbial and environmental associations link water availability to soil multifunctionality in the rhizosheath and bulk soil of Eremopyrum distansCORE3
Authors: Kang-Wei Jiang, Yu Wang, Si-Jie Ma, Zheng-Wei Heng, Zhi-Qing Zhang...
Venue: Frontiers in Microbiology
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Changes in water availability can alter soil resources and microbial communities, but whether association structures linking water availability to soil multifunctionality (SMF) differ between the root–soil interface and bulk soil remains unclear. We subjected Eremopyrum distans to five water treatments over one growing season and assessed dimension-weighted and threshold-based SMF, together with bacterial, fungal and protistan community attributes, in the rhizosheath and bulk soil. Dimension-weighted SMF generally increased across the imposed water-treatment gradient. At higher functional thresholds, W + 25 supported more functions at high levels, particularly in bulk soil. After accounting for water treatment, microhabitat and their interaction, C- and N-related functions were associated mainly with microbial diversity, whereas P-related functions and high-threshold SMF were more closely associated with network complexity and the community-stability proxy. Piecewise structural equation models indicated that, in the rhizosheath, soil moisture was associated with SMF primarily through modeled indirect associations involving integrated microbial diversity, network complexity and the community-stability proxy. In bulk soil, the positive direct association of soil moisture and the negative direct association of soil pH with SMF were more prominent. Formal paired-bootstrap comparisons showed that most corresponding paths did not differ between microhabitats, although selected path differences were detected across SMF thresholds. Water availability was associated with SMF through microhabitat-dependent association structures. Microbial community organization was more closely associated with SMF in the rhizosheath, whereas local environmental conditions retained stronger direct associations with SMF in bulk soil. These findings highlight the importance of distinguishing rhizosheath and bulk-soil microhabitats when evaluating soil functional responses to changing water availability.
Plasma membrane H+-ATPase enhances iron utilization in alkaline-grown rice.PROXY2

Abstract
High pH precipitates Fe as insoluble ferric oxides in alkaline/calcareous soils, limiting its availability. Plasma membrane H⁺-ATPase (PM H⁺-ATPase) drives rhizosphere acidification to promote nutrient uptake and is strongly induced by phosphorus deprivation. Fe deficiency is generally a weak inducer of root PM H⁺-ATPase activity compared with P deprivation, particularly in graminaceous species such as rice. This is likely because Fe acquisition mainly relies on Strategy II-related phytosiderophore secretion rather than Strategy I-like H⁺ secretion. Nevertheless, the mechanisms by which P deprivation induced PM H⁺-ATPase activity for Fe utilization in rice under alkaline conditions remains elusive. Here, we stimulated rice root PM H⁺-ATPase via P deprivation and assessed Fe uptake at pH 7.5 or pH 5.5. P deprivation alleviated chlorosis at pH 7.5, increasing leaf chlorophyll and soluble Fe, while downregulating Fe-homeostasis genes including OsIRT1, OsYSL2/15, OsIRO2 and upregulating OsIRT2. Proton efflux and PM H⁺-ATPase gene expression (OSA1, OSA8) were enhanced by P deprivation, alongside increased protein abundance and penultimate threonine (Thr) residue phosphorylation level, indicating improved enzymatic activity under alkaline conditions. Further, OSA1 overexpression lines showed reduced chlorosis under alkaline conditions. Conversely, the Fe homeostasis and transport-related gene mutants (osirt1, osirt2, osysl15, osiro2) exhibited chlorosis with lower chlorophyll and Fe contents in leaves under alkaline conditions. We conclude that strategy I-like proton extrusion significantly contributes to rice Fe uptake under alkaline conditions. This cooperative mechanism may play a vital role in Fe mobilization by rice plants in alkaline, mainly calcareous soils.

Abstract
Urban vegetable irrigation is increasingly associated with questions of water availability, soil quality, contaminant accumulation, environmental risk and food safety. However, research addressing these issues is dispersed across environmental science, agriculture, water resources, toxicology and public health, making the overall development of the field difficult to assess. This study examined the global research evolution and emerging frontiers in urban vegetable irrigation, soil and water quality, with emphasis on contamination pathways, environmental risks and food safety concerns. A bibliometric analysis was conducted on an evidence inventory of 90 unique records published between 1994 and 2026. Publication trends, geographical distribution, study-location countries, institutions, authors, journals, citation patterns, keyword co-occurrence, co-authorship and thematic evolution were examined. The literature covered 32 countries across Africa, Asia, Europe, North America, South America and Oceania/Australia. Africa contributed 23 records (25.6%), Asia 19 (21.1%) and Europe 18 (20.0%), while Pakistan had the highest country-specific output with 10 records, followed by Ghana (9), Nigeria and Ethiopia (7 each), and the United States (6). Publication activity increased markedly after 2020, reaching a peak of 10 records in both 2024 and 2025, equivalent to 11.1% of the total records in each year. Environmental Monitoring and Assessment was the most represented journal with five records. Hiller et al. (2022) received the highest citation count (97), followed by Tom et al. (2014) with 79 and Asirifi et al. (2021) with 64. Keyword analysis showed strong associations among urban agriculture, vegetables and irrigation, with 37 co-occurrences between urban agriculture and vegetables and 35 each between irrigation and vegetables and irrigation and urban agriculture. Heavy metals and health risk were prominent secondary themes, particularly in association with wastewater and vegetables. Co-authorship analysis revealed a fragmented field dominated by a small number of established regional research groups. Thematic evolution showed continued growth in urban agriculture, heavy metals, wastewater and health-risk research, with health risk emerging most strongly in the later period. The findings demonstrate a rapidly expanding but dispersed research field and highlight the need for stronger cross-regional collaboration and integrated research linking irrigation water, soil, vegetables, contamination and human health.

Abstract
This study investigated heavy metals (Cd, Pb, Cr, Cu, Zn) in the soil-rice system of Sanming, a typical industrial and rice-growing region in China, to analyze their spatial patterns, sources, and ecological and health risks. Standard deviation ellipses based on the geoaccumulation index (Igeo), single ecological risk index (Er) and comprehensive ecological risk index (RI) were used to visualize spatial disparities of pollution and risk. Cd was the primary pollutant with the widest distribution; its average (Igeo) reached 2.29, and roughly 80% of samples presented moderate to heavy pollution. The highly matched (Igeo) and (Er) ellipses of Cd resulted from its high toxicity and low background value. Zn and Pb exhibited local hotspots: Zn pollution clustered around Shaxian industrial sites, while Pb spread east–west along the Shaxi River basin. The RI centroid was at 26.334° N, 117.379° E. The hazard index (HI) of rice intake for children exceeded 1, indicating notable non-carcinogenic risks, whereas HI values for other populations were below 1. Zn, Cr and Cd dominated non-carcinogenic risks, and pH drove elevated Zn-related health risks. The Unmix model showed mixed industrial and agricultural sources contributed 55% of heavy metals. This study implies concentration-based spatial analysis may underestimate ecological and health hazards of low-toxic metals. These findings support effective soil management and the protection of local residents from dietary heavy metal exposure. Future studies should apply repeated standard deviational ellipse analyses to track pollution and risk centroid shifts and thus support adaptive management.
Syukuro Manabe’s Contributions to the Hydrologic Sciences: A Land Model Development PerspectiveECO1
Authors: K. Findell, P. Milly, S. Malyshev, E. Shevliakova
Venue: Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society
Published: 2026-11-11
Abs
Abstract
While Syukuro Manabe’s contributions to climate science are well known, his foundational contributions to the hydrologic sciences are equally noteworthy, though perhaps less frequently celebrated. In 1969 Manabe published the first computational representation of the coupled global water and energy cycles, derived from basic physical principles, with no external forcing other than solar radiation. Central to this achievement was a numerical depiction of the controls on the land water and energy balances—the first land model embedded in a global climate model. Through his simple soil moisture “bucket,” Manabe captured the essential role of land in the Earth system: regulating the partitioning of water and energy fluxes at the surface. Manabe and his colleagues needed this flux partitioning capability to depict the global hydrologic cycle so they could answer what is now recognized as the central climate question of the day: how does global mean temperature change with increasing atmospheric greenhouse gas concentrations? While that simple bucket remained at the heart of land models for decades, modern models have evolved to include surface heterogeneity, dynamic vegetation, and a growing list of human interventions in the Earth system (e.g., irrigation), providing today’s climate modeling community with the tools to answer a new suite of questions that require both a more nuanced treatment of the controls on water and energy partitioning and a fully coupled carbon cycle.
