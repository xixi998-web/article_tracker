Reclamation of Saline–Alkali Soils Using Fulvic Acid: Mechanism and Field EffectsCORE5
Authors: Gui-Qing Hu, Wen-Tai Zhang, Guang-Bin Zhang
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-11-08
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Fulvic acid is an organic soil amendment used to ameliorate salinealkali soils and to alleviate the negative effects of soil salinization on crops. However, scientific tests of many fulvic acid products have given very mixed results. A meta-analysis of 16 case studies on the field applications of fulvic acid was conducted. The results show that, compared with the control, fulvic acid generally reduced the soil electrical conductivity by 0.15 dS m−1. However, for fields with high groundwater tables and poor drainage, the effect of fulvic acid on promoting downward salt leaching was limited. Fulvic acid treatment reduced soil pH from 8.48 to 8.38, yet for fields with high CO32− and HCO3− contents, the soil pH may increase after fulvic acid addition. The mechanisms by which fulvic acid reduces soil electrical conductivity and pH are mainly colloid adsorption and ion exchange. Fulvic acid can promote the uptake of nutrients such as nitrogen and phosphorus by crops, and increase crop yield by 40.5%. However, both nutrient use efficiency and yield increasing rate decreased with increasing soil salinity. Fulvic acid was more effective in ameliorating moderately saline soils.

Venue: Land Degradation &amp; Development
Published: 2026-11-08

Abstract
This study investigates the influence of land use on total suspended solids (TSS) flux and nutrient leaching within a mixed land‐use catchment. The primary objective was to enhance predictive accuracy for soil erosion and water quality by assessing hydrologic drivers critical for managing degradation risks. Our findings demonstrate a significant land‐use impact: stream reaches adjacent to arable land exhibited the highest concentrations of nitrate nitrogen (up to 6.25 mg dm −3 ) and phosphates (0.89 mg dm −3 ), whereas forests and permanent grasslands showed significantly lower levels. Arable lands also recorded the highest transport intensity (1.02 m 3  s −1 ), reflecting increased surface runoff and diminished water retention capacity compared to forested reaches (0.47 m 3  s −1 ). Furthermore, the complete transverse mixing distance, a proxy for natural purification efficiency, was longest in arable‐dominated reaches (52.26 m), contrasting with shorter distances in forests (35.26 m) and grasslands (31.21 m). Spatial autoregression (SAR) modeling identified significant correlations between phosphate levels and agricultural zones, as well as nitrate nitrogen and grasslands. These results underscore the necessity of integrating land‐use management into forest engineering and maintaining permanent riparian grasslands to optimize nutrient retention and sediment control in erosion‐prone regions.

Source Analysis and Health Risk Assessment of Heavy Metals in Farmland Soil Based on Receptor Models and Machine Learning in a Typical Province of Northeastern ChinaECO1
Authors: Yue Chen, Jian-Ling Xu, Yi-Ning Liang, L. Sheng, Xin-Yu Wang...
Venue: Agronomy
Published: 2026-11-08
Abs · PDF
Abstract
Heavy metal pollution in farmland soils threatens food security and human health, particularly in industrially developed regions. This study investigates the sources and health risks of heavy metals in farmland soils of Liaoning Province by integrating receptor models and machine learning. We collected 168 surface soil and measured Cd, Pb, Zn, Cu, Ni, As, Cr, and Co. Source apportionment used APCS-MLR and EF-PMF models. A random forest (RF) model with eight environmental covariates (road network, annual precipitation, soil clay content, LUCC, DEM, vegetation cover, GDP, and population) predicted spatial distribution. Health risks were assessed using the US EPA model and Monte Carlo simulation. The results showed that Cd and Pb were significantly enriched, with enrichment factors of 2.51 and 4.50 indicating substantial anthropogenic input. Spatially, higher concentrations occurred in western and southern areas near coal mines and industrial activities. EF-PMF identified industrial activities (66.3%), atmospheric deposition (17.3%), agricultural activities (13.5%), and traffic emissions (2.9%). RF showed the highest R2 for Cr, Pb, and As (R2 = 0.535, 0.484, and 0.474) and revealed that industrial and mining activities were the dominant drivers of spatial heterogeneity. Health risk assessment indicated that children faced higher risks than adults, and Cr and Ni were identified as the elements posing the greatest health risks. Monte Carlo simulation confirmed that Cr and Ni are priority pollutants requiring control. The EF-PMF–APCS-MLR, RF, and Monte Carlo framework improves source apportionment and spatial characterization, supporting targeted heavy metal control in industrial agricultural regions.
